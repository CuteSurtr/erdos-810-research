PROOF AND LITERATURE AUDIT, 8 NOVEMBER 2026

This is a self-audit in the same session, not an independent agent review
or a formal proof. No historical-priority or publication-readiness claim.

MATERIAL CHECKS

1. Counting identity: every repeated opposite pair contributes TWO ordered
crossed transitions. A two-color C4 contributes four; a three-color C4 two.
Fixed transitions count actual edges exactly once only because the graph
is simple with unambiguous colors. The exact script checked 360 randomly
generated simple proper affine graphs, including 397 two-color and 3,688
three-color cycles. Thus the audit exercises both multiplicities.

2. Two-color exception: subtracting the two equations gives a+b=0.
The row involution has a unique fixed point because p is odd. A color pair
therefore supplies at most (p-1)/2 cycles; there are at most floor(d/2)
pairs. This conclusion would fail for unrestricted permutations and must
not be silently transferred there.

3. Robustness: the tested quadratic form, rather than a general operator
norm perturbation, charges each error at (a,x) at most rho_x times.
The masks must be in [0,1]. The second product error uses the involution
to interchange the two states. Cauchy-Schwarz is over d colors and then
p rows. These steps, including the positive-part lower bound, were tested
with exact rational masks. For fractional gamma_2<1, the displayed bound
retains max{1,gamma_2}; dropping it would be unjustified.

4. Exponents: (5+7/8)/3=47/24. Trace trimming produces
(3*(47/24)+2)/5=63/40 as the p exponent before replacing d by p, and
63/40+2/5=79/40 afterwards. Its zero-density threshold is again p^(1/16).
These arithmetic identities are checked with exact fractions.

5. Norm direction: normalized trace norm is at most gamma_2. That does
NOT transfer a gamma_2 lower bound to a trace-norm lower bound. The proofs
instead trim the SVD factors, preserve a specified positive fraction of
actual graph edges, and apply the gamma_2 theorem to that actual subgraph.

6. Dyadic factorization: at most 2k blocks per level, at most L levels,
and each column lies in one dyadic block per level. The two squared factor
norms are 2kL and L, giving sqrt(2k)*L, not sqrt(2kL).
All 8,190 binary rows of lengths 1 through 12 passed the decomposition
checks. The q-ordering construction enlarges the squared column norm by
q, giving sqrt(q) in gamma_2. The existing one-ordering interval result
has a better exponent for fixed k and is explicitly acknowledged.

7. Dense example: A=[1,floor(p/8)], X=[0,floor(p/4)-1], beta(a)=a^2.
The collision factor x+a+b is nonzero modulo p, so the graph really is
simple and proper. Its gamma_2 is one and it has no antipodal palette
pair. The three-color count is
|A|^3|X|^2/(2p^2)+O(p^(23/8)), hence p^3/16384+O(p^(23/8)).
An exact O(|A|^3) enumeration through p=1009 agrees with a separate
common-neighbor enumeration through p=251. At p=1009 the exact count is
60,983; this is a finite check, not an estimate of the asymptotic error
constant. Counts and script hashes are saved separately.

8. Arbitrary intercepts: only the archived common-support theorem is
used here, not the polynomial weighted operator bound. The extracted
all-one support rectangle is an actual subgraph on the original field.
There is no claim that the extracted vertex set is itself a field.
The matrix theorem's all-zero OR all-one alternative would not suffice;
the proof first sparsifies the COMPLEMENT and then applies its one-sided
zero-rectangle theorem. The palette and density losses are explicit.

9. The rank count: pivot-coordinate restriction is injective on a real
row space of rank r. Binary rows therefore have at most 2^r types. This
was checked on all 4,096 binary 3-by-4 matrices, although the short linear
algebra proof, not this finite check, establishes the general assertion.

10. Uniform trace bound for arbitrary intercepts: write delta=m/p^2 and
L=(log p)^(1/9). Nuclear/operator duality gives t>=m/(dp)>=delta. After
trimming, H'<=5t/delta and at least delta p^2/2 edges survive. The comparison
with the common-support bound gives
L<=C[t^3/delta^3+t/delta^2+log(C/delta)]. The three alternatives yield
delta<=C[t L^(-1/3)+sqrt(t)L^(-1/2)+exp(-cL)]. Thus the logarithmic
threshold is t=o((log p)^(1/27)); bounded t gives an explicit
O(p^2/(log p)^(1/27)) bound. No Boolean-to-fractional-mask extension is made.

QUADRATIC CHARACTER-OPERATOR AUDIT

11. Character weighting preserves the mean-zero completed-operator norm
for any intercept function and any palette. The diagonal factors depend
on which frequency coordinate is zero; using the nonzero-product formula
on the axes would be invalid. The exact modular-log audit checked 78,640
transitions covering both axes and all nonconstant frequency vectors.

12. For a full quadratic palette the nonzero-product block is a principal
compression of cubic convolution, up to unitary diagonal factors. The
two factors are not necessarily adjoints for a complex character; norm
invariance still holds. The audit checked 290,144 exact cubic identities.
The two frequency-axis blocks are compressed Fourier matrices with at
most two copies of each squared index, giving sqrt(2p); 24,384 exact
axis-phase cases passed. The constant block is removed before estimating.
The published Weil bound, rather than finite evaluations, establishes
the general 2sqrt(p) theorem.

13. The mask is COLOR-BY-COLUMN. Fourier coefficients use all p-1 colors,
with zero extension of unused colors, and max_y is inside the sum over
characters. Palette restriction costs M_A in the operator norm, not in
each mask. This gives M_A^(1/3) M_H^(2/3) after taking the cube root.
The binary mask ensures V>=1; fractional robustness retains max{1,V}.
The actual-support and positivity identities are the column versions of
items 1 and 3, obtained by exchanging the coordinate roles.

14. The quadratic-residue example already appears in the 18 October
common-potential notes. Its properness is essential: intersections have
column character chi_2(-1), while the selected columns have the opposite
character. Its V is exactly one, and the positive main term is
(p-1)^5/(64p^2). Exact counts reach p=1009 (N_3=15,872,976); direct
four-cycle enumeration independently agrees through p=61. The new part
being assessed is the counting application, not the example itself.

15. The October follow-up growing-index-wiener-palettes.md already has
p^(11/6) M_A M_H. The new character argument improves the powers of the
norms, not that p exponent. The old coset-union estimate ell*p^(5/3)
may still be stronger. The robust errors p^(3/2)R^(1/4) here and
p^(4/3)R^(1/3) there must not be described as a uniform improvement.
For some ROW-mask families, simplicity gives stronger bounds; the
column formulation has a genuinely dense proper example and avoids
mistaking such a vacuous row specialization for progress.

16. Six exploratory Lanczos runs at p=193,379 for proper common-potential
graphs returned small Ritz magnitudes. These are floating-point estimates
from 24 iterations, with no certified enclosure of the true spectrum.
They cannot establish a universal square-root bound for arbitrary masks.
The probe is kept separately from the written character-envelope theorem.

17. Random thinning: a three-color base cycle has three or four distinct
potential values, never two. Row collisions and opposite-pair collisions
give at most 3d(d-1) three-potential cycles. Two-color cycles have four
distinct potentials. These distinctions are needed for the below-threshold
first moment. The script exhaustively checked 332 masks through p=17,
including exact rational Bernoulli averaging, and 96 additional sampled
masks across the listed primes. Separate cycle and color-ratio counts agree.

18. The second moment counts graph cycles, including different cycles
sharing the same entire potential set. Maximum overlaps are bounded by
p^2,108p,216,72 for one through four specified potentials. The last bound
must not be replaced by one. A degree-nine elimination has nonzero leading
coefficient 1/u^3, while the triple-slot elimination is monic cubic.
These bounds give the threshold eta=p^(-3/4) and the relative asymptotic
N_3~eta^4 p^3/64 above it. Finite overlap bounds were checked through p=101.

19. The independent direct base count excludes at most six q parameters.
Its factors r,L,D,Q are squarefree and pairwise coprime over the algebraic
closure outside those exceptions; each of the three character conditions
has a private odd-multiplicity factor. This proves the hypotheses for
each of the seven multiplicative Weil estimates, including products in
which D occurs twice. The factor of two in counting ordered ratios was
checked against actual unordered cycles. This alternative proof makes
the random-threshold note independent of the new operator theorem.

20. Exact C potential-set counts through p=1019 agree with the Python
cycle totals on common inputs. Cube roots of unity act freely on the
three-color cycles while preserving every potential value. Multiplicities
are therefore divisible by gcd(3,p-1). No critical-window Poisson claim
is inferred from the numerical multiplicities alone.
The new Appendix B instead derives a law for DISTINCT sets from the
published Chen-Greenhill theorem with the exact normalization M_p.

21. The literature follow-up resolves the distinct-set count in the
companion potential-set-orbits.txt. The degree-five relation F is
necessary for every ordered cycle. Of its 24 slot permutations after
the ratio parametrization, exactly the identity and reversal vanish
identically. Each of the other 22 integer polynomials has a coefficient
equal to +1 or -1, hence is nonzero in every prime characteristic.
The exact coefficient certificate and sign pairing passed; independent
rectangle enumeration at p=13,23,31,61,101 checks generic orbit size and
the complete cube-root action. All four cases overlapping the previous
C enumeration agree. The count uses the elementary polynomial zero bound
to give at most 220p^2 exceptional cycles, not a statistical extrapolation.

22. Generic potential sets have exactly g=gcd(3,p-1) cycle realizations.
This yields M_p=N_4pot/g+O(p^2)=p^3/(64g)+O(p^(5/2)). At eta=c p^(-3/4),
exceptional four-potential cycles and three-potential cycles vanish with
high probability by their first moments. Consequently N_3=g X_p with
probability tending to one. Chen-Greenhill then gives N_3/g converging
to Poisson(c^4/(64g)) along either fixed class p mod 3. Two-color cycles
also vanish, giving rainbow probability exp(-c^4/(64g)). This application
does not claim a new general Poisson theorem or an ordinary Poisson law
for raw cycles when g=3. The scalar c is fixed and positive.

NOVELTY AND PAPER ASSESSMENT

Targeted searches for Erdos 810 / affine rainbow C4 supports combined with
factorization norms, hereditary discrepancy and supersaturation did not
establish historical priority. Absence of a search result is not a novelty
proof. The strongest new lead is the explicit quadratic character-operator
factorization and the resulting column-support theorem. Its only external
analytic input is the classical cubic Weil estimate. The accompanying
random-potential threshold is another concrete candidate: it strengthens
the archive's fixed-density random-thinning obstruction to a threshold
and a relative counting law, with an independent direct base count.
The fresh journal search shows that its general threshold step is already
covered by Chen-Greenhill (2021) and Kim-Lund-Pham-Yoo (2023). Its possible
original content is the algebraic configuration count and overlap
verification. The distinct-set Poisson extension is an explicitly
attributed corollary of the former, not a separate new probability method.
The additional generic-orbit count supplies an explicit leading constant
and an arithmetic distinction between the two prime residue classes.
This is a further concrete application candidate, with historical novelty
still unverified; it was derived after identifying the published framework.
See journal-literature-comparison.txt for the published bibliography and
the comparisons with the project's older fixed-formula theorem.
The broader package gives quantitative support-norm obstructions and
growing-complexity consequences for arbitrary intercepts.
The elementary rank and interval corollaries should remain supporting
observations, not headline claims. Searches for cubic convolution, affine
rainbow C4 operators, and the kernel rs(r-s) did not establish priority.

Before calling this paper-ready: independently review the archived weighted
and common-support inputs and the new applications, compare with the
relevant coloring literature beyond keyword searches, and decide whether
the quantitative scope is substantial enough for the intended venue.
No such external review or venue judgment has been completed here.
