POTENTIAL-SET MULTIPLICITIES AND AN EXPLICIT CRITICAL WINDOW
8 November 2026. Written deduction prompted by the journal literature audit.

Status: exact algebraic certificate, written counting proof, and independent
small-field checks. Historical novelty and external review remain unverified.
The Poisson step explicitly applies Chen-Greenhill's published theorem.

1. STATEMENTS

Use the random model in random-potential-threshold.txt: p is an odd prime,
A is the nonzero quadratic residues, J consists of nonzero h with quadratic
character -chi(-1), and H selects each element of J independently with
probability eta. The color-a edge of potential h is

                       (h/a^2-a, h/a).

Let N_4pot be the number of three-color cycles of the unthinned graph with
four distinct potentials. Let M_p count DISTINCT four-element potential
sets of those cycles, and put g=gcd(3,p-1). The preceding note proves

                      N_4pot=p^3/64+O(p^(5/2)).

THEOREM (multiplicity). All but O(p^2) of these graph cycles have a
potential set realized by exactly g graph cycles. In particular,

            0 <= N_4pot/g - M_p <= 220 p^2/g,              (1)
            M_p=p^3/(64g)+O(p^(5/2)).                     (2)

The constant in (1) is deliberately loose. This proves the multiplicity
asymptotic previously posed as a question in the random-potential note.

COROLLARY (critical window, using Chen-Greenhill). Fix c>0 and take
eta=c p^(-3/4), for all sufficiently large primes. Let N_3 be the sampled
graph's three-color cycle count. Along a prime sequence on which g is fixed,

                N_3/g  ->  Poisson(c^4/(64g))             (3)

in distribution, and

        P(every C4 is rainbow) -> exp(-c^4/(64g)).         (4)

Thus for primes p=2 mod 3 the raw count has limit Poisson(c^4/64).
For primes p=1 mod 3 its limit is THREE TIMES a Poisson(c^4/192) variable.
It is not an ordinary Poisson limit for raw cycles in the latter case.
The expected raw cycle count tends to c^4/64 in either residue class.
These are coarse-threshold critical-window statements, not a sharp
threshold in the technical (1+/-epsilon) sense.

2. THE RELATION SATISFIED BY A LABELED POTENTIAL QUADRUPLE

Label a three-color C4 as in the preceding note, with repeated color a,
other colors b,c, and ordered potentials h,k,u,v. Put q=b/a, r=c/a and
zeta=a^3. The equations are

        k=q h+zeta q(q-1),   h=r k+zeta r(r-1),
        u=qk,               v=rh.                        (5)

All potentials are nonzero. Properness gives q,r!=1 and q!=r; the earlier
calculation proves qr!=1. Solving the linear equations gives

 (h,k,u,v) = zeta/(1-qr) * (H,K,U,V),
 H=r(q^2-q+r-1),  K=q(r^2-r+q-1),  U=qK,  V=rH.          (6)

On the other hand, eliminating zeta directly from (5) gives

 F(h,k,u,v) := k(k^2-uh)v(v-h) - h(h^2-vk)u(u-k) = 0.    (7)

For example zeta=k(k^2-uh)/(u(u-k)); its denominator is nonzero since
u=qk, k!=0 and q!=1. The analogous expression using h gives (7).
Only necessity of (7) for an actual cycle is used below: no false
converse about arbitrary solutions is required.

The polynomial F is homogeneous of degree five and

                     F(k,h,v,u)=-F(h,k,u,v).             (8)

The reversal (h,k,u,v)->(k,h,v,u) simply changes the orientation of the
same cycle. For a permutation sigma of the four slots, define

                  R_sigma(q,r)=F(sigma(H,K,U,V)).         (9)

Each coordinate in (6) has total degree at most four, so R_sigma has
degree at most twenty. For the identity and reversal it is identically
zero. Every OTHER permutation gives a nonzero polynomial over EVERY
prime field. Here is an explicit coefficient certificate; the second
member of each sign pair is covered by (8):

 order supplied to F      coefficient in R_sigma          degree
 hkvu                    [q^12 r^3]  = -1                  17
 hukv                    [q^13 r^4]  =  1                  20
 huvk                    [q^13 r^1]  = -1                  18
 hvku                    [q^12 r^6]  =  1                  20
 hvuk                    [q^11 r^5]  =  1                  18
 kuhv                    [q^13 r^4]  =  1                  20
 kuvh                    [q^13 r^2]  =  1                  18
 kvhu                    [q^12 r^6]  =  1                  20
 kvuh                    [q^12 r^3]  =  1                  18
 uvhk                    [q^13 r^3]  =  1                  19
 uvkh                    [q^13 r^2]  =  1                  19

These coefficients follow by expanding (6)-(9) over the integers. The
complete coefficient lists and a standard-library exact polynomial
calculator are retained in potential-set-collision-audit.json and
audit_potential_set_collisions.py. In particular, this is a symbolic
certificate over Z, not an inference from finitely many prime evaluations.
As a separate arithmetic check, q=2,r=4 gives (H,K,U,V)=(20,26,52,80);
the displayed eleven orders all give nonzero integer values of F there.

3. COUNTING EXCEPTIONAL LABELINGS AND IDENTIFYING GENERIC ORBITS

A nonzero bivariate polynomial of total degree D has at most Dp zeros
over the p-by-p grid. One elementary proof writes it as a polynomial
of degree j in the first variable. Its leading coefficient has degree
at most D-j in the other variable: outside its at most D-j roots there
are at most j roots per fiber, and the remaining fibers have at most p.

Call a ratio pair (q,r) exceptional if one of the 22 nontrivial R_sigma
vanishes. The preceding certificate and root bound give at most 440p
exceptional ratio pairs. For each ratio pair and each a in A, equations
(5) determine at most one oriented cycle. Therefore at most

                     440p |A| <= 220p^2                 (10)

unoriented graph cycles have an exceptional ratio pair. Counting oriented
cycles as an upper bound only makes this inequality looser.

Consider a nonexceptional cycle C with ordered potentials (h,k,u,v).
Any other cycle C' with the same unordered potential set supplies a
permutation of these four distinct entries. It must satisfy (7). Because
the scale in (6) is nonzero and F is homogeneous, nonexceptionality forces
that permutation to be the identity or reversal. After reversing C' if
needed, both cycles have the SAME ordered potentials.

Then q=u/k and r=v/h agree, and

                       zeta=k(k^2-uh)/(u(u-k))

agrees as well. Their repeated colors a,a' consequently satisfy a'^3=a^3,
so a'=omega a with omega^3=1. Equations (5) and the edge definition give
precisely the common cube-root action

                 (x,y,a)->(omega x, omega^2 y, omega a).

Every cube root omega lies in A: its order divides the odd number three,
so it is a square. The action preserves all edge potentials and the
palette, and is free on three-color cycles because their unique repeated
color cannot be fixed by nonidentity omega. Thus this orbit contains
exactly g cycles, and these are all realizations of a generic potential set.

All potential sets, generic or otherwise, have multiplicity divisible by
g by the same free action. If m_S denotes that multiplicity, then

  N_4pot/g-M_p = sum_S (m_S/g-1).

Only exceptional cycles can contribute a nonzero term; (10) bounds the
sum by 220p^2/g. This proves (1), and the base count proves (2).

4. THE LIMIT LAW AND THE ROLE OF THE PUBLISHED THEOREM

Let X_p count distinct four-potential sets contained in H. The preceding
random-potential note verifies Chen-Greenhill Theorem 1.1 with q=p,n=1,
a=4,b=3,c=0; its overlap hypotheses do not require the new multiplicity
asymptotic. Equation (2) now makes the mean explicit:

                   E X_p=M_p eta^4 -> c^4/(64g).

The published theorem yields the corresponding Poisson law for X_p.
At this sampling rate the expected number of surviving exceptional
four-potential cycles is O(p^2 eta^4)=O(p^(-1)). The expected number of
three-potential three-color cycles is O(p^2 eta^3)=O(p^(-1/4)). With
probability tending to one neither occurs. On that event N_3=g X_p,
which proves (3). The O(p^2) two-color cycles each have four distinct
potentials and hence also vanish with high probability. This proves (4).

Source: Changhao Chen and Catherine Greenhill, Threshold functions for
substructures in random subsets of finite vector spaces, Journal of
Combinatorics 12(1) (2021), 157-183, Theorem 1.1.
https://doi.org/10.4310/JOC.2021.v12.n1.a6
https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03778

5. AUDIT AND NOVELTY SCOPE

The exact script verifies all 24 integer-polynomial expansions, the two
identically zero cases, the 22 unit coefficients, and the sign pairing.
Separate actual-graph rectangle enumeration at p=13,23,31,61,101 checks
the potential relation, generic multiplicity, every cube-symmetry orbit,
and the count deficit. Four of these cases also match the earlier C
enumerator's retained results. At p=101 the nongeneric sets include
multiplicity three even though g=1; the theorem correctly allows this.
The run took under one second on one of the already designated low-priority
CPU cores. No additional long-running search was started.

The generic labeling argument and its explicit arithmetic critical-window
consequence are additions to this project. They may be useful in a focused
research note. Their historical novelty has NOT been established. The
underlying Poisson theorem, polynomial zero bound, Weil estimate and
earlier quadratic-residue construction are all credited existing inputs.
