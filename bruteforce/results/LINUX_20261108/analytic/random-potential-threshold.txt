RANDOM POTENTIAL THINNING: A p^(-3/4) THRESHOLD AND A THREE-COLOR COUNT
8 November 2026. Candidate deduction from the quadratic character estimate.

Status: written proof with exact finite consistency checks. Historical
novelty and external review are unverified. This is a theorem for one
specified random family, not a bound for all graphs in Erdos #810.

1. THE RANDOM FAMILY AND THE CONCLUSIONS

Let p tend to infinity through odd primes. Write chi for the quadratic
character and put

 A={a!=0:chi(a)=1}, J={h!=0:chi(h)=-chi(-1)}, d=(p-1)/2.

Include each h in J in H independently with probability eta=eta(p),
0<=eta<=1. For every a in A and h in H retain the color-a edge

                     (h/a^2-a, h/a).                     (1)

This graph is always simple and properly colored, and has exactly
m=d|H| edges. Its unthinned version is the old quadratic-residue example
in the 18 October notes. Indeed a collision between distinct colors a,b
would require potentials -a^2 b,-ab^2, which have quadratic character
chi(-1), while all selected potentials have the opposite character.

THEOREM. The following statements hold for this distribution.

(i) If eta=o(p^(-3/4)), then with probability tending to one EVERY C4
    is rainbow.

(ii) If eta/p^(-3/4) tends to infinity, then, writing N_3 for the number
     of unordered three-color C4s,

                   N_3 / (eta^4 p^3/64) -> 1              (2)

     in probability. In particular the graph fails the rainbow-C4
     condition with probability tending to one.

Thus p^(-3/4) is a threshold scale for this random model. In its critical
range eta is of that order, m is of order p^(5/4) with high probability.
No limiting probability or Poisson law in the constant-factor critical
window is asserted. The sparse construction in (i) is not claimed to
improve the archive's best lower-bound constructions.

For every eta there is also the uniform expectation formula

 E N_3 = eta^4 p^3/64 + O(eta^4 p^(5/2)+eta^3 p^2),       (3)

with an absolute implied constant. The proof below uses elementary
counting and a second moment after a base counting input. The appendix
proves that input directly as well, independently of the spectral
argument. No unproved estimate for arbitrary masks is used.

2. THE UNTHINNED COUNT AND POTENTIAL COLLISIONS

The unthinned graph is equivalent to the column mask
F(a,y)=1_A(a)1_J(y), since a is a square. The companion note
quadratic-color-fourier.txt, Section 5, proves

                  N_3(base)=p^3/64+O(p^(5/2)).             (4)

Its ingredients are the exact actual-support identity, the classical
cubic Weil bound, and the completed-operator factorization. This is the
only asymptotic input in Sections 2-4. An independent character-sum proof
is supplied in the appendix below.

In a three-color C4, label the repeated color a and the other two colors
b,c, with positions

                     y             w
              x      a             b
              z      c             a.

Write h=ay, k=aw, u=bw, v=cy for the four edge potentials. Put q=b/a,
r=c/a and zeta=a^3. The row equations are exactly

 k=q h+zeta q(q-1),    h=r k+zeta r(r-1).                 (5)

Here q,r are nonzero, different from 1 and from each other. Also qr!=1:
if qr=1, consistency in (5) would give (q-1)^2(q+1)=0;
q=1 is forbidden and q=-1 gives q=r, also forbidden. Consequently the
two linear equations (5) determine h,k uniquely from a,b,c.

There are at least three distinct potential values in such a cycle.
The repeated pair h,k is distinct; adjacent edges in a column also
have distinct potentials because the column is nonzero and its colors
differ. Two values would therefore force u=h and v=k, implying qr=1.
This contradiction also shows that at most one potential equality occurs.

Let D count the cycles with three, rather than four, potential values.
They arise only in the following ways.

* Equal potentials on one row: for distinct colors a,b, equality gives
  (a+b)x+a^2+ab+b^2=0. If a+b=0 there is no solution, and otherwise the
  row is unique. Its two edges have at most two three-color completions:
  either a or b must be the repeated color, and then the other row is
  determined. There are at most 2*binom(d,2)=d(d-1) such cycles.

* Equal potentials on the opposite, differently colored pair: u=v.
  Using (5) and q!=r, this requires

                         q^2+r^2=q+r.                    (6)

  For each a,b there are at most two r values. Each color triple
  determines at most one cycle by qr!=1. This contributes at most
  2d(d-1) cycles, allowing overcounting of orientations.

Therefore

                  0<=D<=3d(d-1)=O(p^2).                  (7)

Let N_4pot=N_3(base)-D denote the number of three-color cycles with four
distinct potentials (this is NOT a count of four-color cycles). A base
cycle survives exactly when all its potentials are selected. Hence

 E N_3=eta^4 N_4pot+eta^3 D
       =eta^4 N_3(base)+(eta^3-eta^4)D.                   (8)

This proves (3), and N_4pot~p^3/64.

Two-color cycles cause no earlier obstruction. They require b=-a and
have columns y=a^2+ax, w=a^2-ax. Their potentials are
{ay,aw,-ay,-aw}. All four are distinct: zero is excluded, y!=w, and
y+w=2a^2!=0. There are at most floor(d/2)(p-1)/2=O(p^2) such cycles.

3. BOUNDED OVERLAPS OF THE FOUR-POTENTIAL CYCLES

For j=1,2,3,4, let Delta_j be the maximum number of three-color base
cycles with FOUR DISTINCT potentials containing a prescribed j-element
potential set. The following deliberately loose bounds suffice:

 j                 1        2         3        4
 Delta_j        <= p^2    <=108p    <=216     <=72.        (9)

For j=1, a given potential occurs on d graph edges. In any simple proper
affine graph a fixed edge belongs to at most p+d-2 nonrainbow C4s:
at most p-1 repeat that edge's own color, and at most d-1 repeat another
color on the other opposite pair. Properness determines each completion.
Thus Delta_1<=d(p+d-2)<=p^2.

For two prescribed values, assign them to two of the ordered slots
h,k,u,v above. There are at most 12 assignments. The possible slot types
are as follows, with the repeated color a fixed when used.

* h,k: at most d choices for a; these two edges determine the cycle.
* One row, such as h,u: for each a the first edge fixes x. The second
  color satisfies b^3+x b^2-u=0, with at most three roots; there are at
  most two three-color completions. This gives at most 6d cycles.
* One column, such as h,v: each a fixes the common column and hence c;
  at most two completions give at most 2d cycles.
* Opposite different colors, u,v: for each a, equations (5) give

       k^3=u h k+zeta u(u-k),
       h^3=v k h+zeta v(v-h),
       h=k^2/u+zeta-zeta u/k.                            (10)

  Substituting the last expression in the second and multiplying by k^3
  gives a polynomial of degree nine with nonzero leading coefficient
  1/u^3. There are at most nine nonzero k solutions. Each fixes h,b,c
  and all four vertices. Thus there are at most 9d cycles.

This proves Delta_2<=12*9p, including possible overcounts.

For three specified slots, if both h,k and one other value are fixed,
q or r is fixed and (5) determines zeta, giving at most three a values.
Otherwise take h,u,v fixed. Then r=v/h!=1 is fixed, and

                 zeta=(h-r k)/(r(r-1)).                  (11)

Substitution into the first equation of (10) gives a monic cubic in k.
It has at most three roots, each allowing at most three a values.
The case with k,u,v fixed is symmetric. With at most 24 slot assignments
this proves Delta_3<=216. If all four slots are fixed, h,k,u already
allow at most three a values; at most 24 assignments give Delta_4<=72.
No claim of uniqueness of a cycle from its potential set is made.

4. SECOND MOMENT AND THE THRESHOLD

Let Y be the number of surviving three-color base cycles with four
distinct potentials, counting graph cycles even if they share exactly
the same potential set. Its mean is

               mu=N_4pot eta^4~p^3 eta^4/64.

Disjoint potential sets give independent indicators. Two four-potential
cycles sharing j values have joint survival probability eta^(8-j).
By (9), summing covariances, including identical potential sets, gives

 Var(Y)<=C[p^3 eta^4+p^5 eta^7+p^4 eta^6+p^3 eta^5].       (12)

Consequently, when eta>0,

 Var(Y)/mu^2 <= C[1/(p^3 eta^4)+1/(p eta)
                       +1/(p^2 eta^2)+1/(p^3 eta^3)]     (13)

for all sufficiently large p. If eta>>p^(-3/4), every term tends to zero.
Chebyshev's inequality gives Y/mu->1 in probability. The other three-color
cycles have expected count at most 3p^2 eta^3, whose ratio to mu is
O(1/(p eta))=o(1). Markov's inequality shows that they do not change the
relative asymptotic. This proves (ii).

If eta<<p^(-3/4), the expected number of all nonrainbow cycles is at most

                   C[p^3 eta^4+p^2 eta^3+p^2 eta^4]=o(1).

The last term covers two-color cycles. Markov's inequality proves (i).
The graph is always proper, so no one-color or adjacent-repetition cases
are missing. Finally |H| is binomial with mean d eta and variance at most
d eta. If d eta tends to infinity, |H|/(d eta)->1 in probability, hence
m~eta p^2/4. This covers the threshold range.

5. COMPARISON AND AUDIT STATUS

The 18 October common-potential-negative-spectrum.md already proved that
fixed-density independent thinning of this proper family is not rainbow-C4
with high probability. It used a p^(7/8) lower spectral estimate and matrix
bounded differences. The present deductions add an explicit three-color
asymptotic, vanishing sampling probabilities, and a two-sided threshold
scale. The old example, the first/second moment methods, and the classical
Weil estimate are not claimed as new.

The proof does not require a small Fourier norm for the sampled H. It
also does not assert a deterministic spectral bound for every H or forbid
exceptional adversarial supports above the random threshold. Historical
novelty of this application remains unverified.

audit_random_thinning.py checks the potential identities, potential-count
classification, and overlap bounds at small primes. It also compares with
independent four-cycle enumeration and exhaustively averages all masks
through p=17 using exact rational Bernoulli weights. Finite checks support
the calculations; the written arguments establish the general statements.

6. CUBE SYMMETRY AND EXACT POTENTIAL-SET MULTIPLICITIES

If omega^3=1 in F_p*, then

        (x,y,a) -> (omega*x, omega^2*y, omega*a)

is an automorphism of every sampled graph, preserving each edge potential
ay. Such omega is a square, so it preserves the palette A. The group of
these roots has size g=gcd(3,p-1). It acts freely on three-color C4s:
their unique repeated color would otherwise satisfy omega*a=a. Thus the
number of graph cycles realizing any given potential set is divisible by
g. In particular, raw graph-cycle counts must not be modeled as independent
Poisson events when p=1 mod 3.

The local C enumerator potential_quadruples.c counts distinct four-value
sets with their exact cycle multiplicities. For example:

 p       four-potential cycles    distinct sets       multiplicities
 509          1,983,232             1,973,072             1,2
1009         15,773,184             5,254,032             3,6
1019         16,327,702            16,281,383             1,2.

Its total cycle counts agree with the separate Python methods on shared
inputs. The run used one low-priority CPU core, a 768 MiB memory limit,
and about 11.5 seconds. Source and binary hashes are in the manifest.

A concrete next question is whether the number M_p of distinct four-value
sets equals N_4pot/g+O(p^2), as these data suggest. This would require
bounding collisions between different cube-symmetry orbits; no such
asymptotic has been proved here. The threshold and counting theorems above
do not depend on it, or on an unproved critical-window limit law.

APPENDIX. A DIRECT PROOF OF THE BASE COUNT

This gives an independent mathematical check of the coefficient 1/64
and allows the random-threshold argument to stand without the completed
operator estimate. Only the classical multiplicative Weil bound is used.

For each repeated color a, put q=b/a,r=c/a. Both ratios lie in A, neither
equals 1, q!=r, and qr!=1. Solving (5), the columns divided by a^2 are

 Y=r(q^2-q+r-1)/(1-qr),
 W=q(r^2-r+q-1)/(1-qr).                                  (A1)

Conversely every such pair with chi(Y)=chi(W)=epsilon=-chi(-1) gives a
three-color cycle. The equations and q!=r ensure Y!=W. Each cycle is
counted twice, from (q,r) and (r,q). Thus if B counts the admissible
ordered ratio pairs,

                       N_3(base)=d B/2.                   (A2)

Fix q in A. Outside finitely many excluded r values, the conditions on
r are that the following three polynomials have prescribed quadratic
characters (1,epsilon,epsilon):

 P_1(r)=r,
 P_2(r)=(r+q^2-q-1)(1-qr),
 P_3(r)=(r^2-r+q-1)(1-qr).                               (A3)

The identities in (A3) use that q,r are squares; the initial condition
chi(P_1)=1 supplies the latter. Zero values, r=1,q, and qr=1 are removed
at a cost O(1) per q.

For all q outside the set

     {0,1,-1,5/4} union {q:q^2-q-1=0},                   (A4)

the four polynomials
r, L=r+q^2-q-1, D=1-qr, Q=r^2-r+q-1
are squarefree and pairwise relatively prime over the algebraic closure.
For clarity, Q has discriminant 5-4q; its value at zero is q-1, and L
vanishes at zero only if q^2-q-1=0. The other possible common roots are
excluded by

 q L(1/q)=(q-1)^2(q+1),
 q^2 Q(1/q)=(q-1)^2(q+1),
 Q(-q^2+q+1)=(q-1)^3(q+1).

Every nonempty product P_1^e P_2^f P_3^g, e,f,g in {0,1}, has a root
of odd multiplicity: P_1 has its private factor r, P_2 has L, and P_3
has Q. The shared factor D does not affect this conclusion. Hence none
is a constant times a square. Its degree is at most six.

Expand the three quadratic-character indicators into their eight terms.
The constant term contributes p/8+O(1); each nonconstant term is
O(sqrt(p)) by the multiplicative-character Weil bound. Therefore each
nonexceptional q in A supplies p/8+O(sqrt(p)) admissible r values.
The at most six exceptional q values contribute O(p) altogether.
Summing over d=(p-1)/2 choices yields

                       B=p^2/16+O(p^(3/2)).

Equation (A2) proves (4). This proof checks the same asymptotic through
a different Fourier expansion from the cubic-convolution operator proof.

Primary source for the standard character-sum input: Lucas Reis,
Arithmetic constraints of polynomial maps through discrete logarithms,
Theorem 3.2 (citing Lidl--Niederreiter, Theorem 5.41):
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.04114 . For a non-square polynomial the
quadratic-character sum is bounded by (z-1)sqrt(p), where z is the
number of distinct roots in its splitting field. Here z<=6.
The direct color-ratio count is also checked against the separate
cycle enumeration in random-thinning-audit.json.
