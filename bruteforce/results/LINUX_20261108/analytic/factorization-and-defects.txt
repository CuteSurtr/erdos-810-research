SUPPORT FACTORIZATION, DISCREPANCY, AND THREE-COLOR FOUR-CYCLES
8 November 2026. Research deductions from archived operator estimates.

Status: written proofs; the local exact identity audit passed. External review
and historical-priority checks remain outstanding.
These are restricted affine-coloring results, not a solution of Erdos #810.
The standard factorization ingredients are not claimed as new. Historical
priority of their applications here has not been established.

1. SETTING AND THE PRECISE INPUT

Let p be an odd prime, A subset F_p*, d=|A|, and beta:A->F_p. The two vertex
classes are separate copies of F_p. The actual color-a edges are

                  (x, ax+beta(a)) for F(a,x)=1,

where F is a binary d-by-p matrix. The selected colored graph must be simple
and proper; in particular, if two affine maps meet, at most one copy of that
edge is selected. Let m be the number of edges. N_j denotes the number of
unordered four-cycles using exactly j colors. Properness allows only j=2,3,4.

For counting-measure l2(F_p^2), define

 tau_a(x,y)=((y-beta(a))/a, ax+beta(a)), U_a f=f composed with tau_a,
 P f=p^(-2)(sum f)1.

Assume the following WEIGHTED completed-operator estimate, for real weights w:

       ||sum_a w(a)(U_a-P)|| <= Lambda max_a |w(a)|.          (1)

This is exactly Section 2 of the archived
research/local-progress/overnight-2026-10-15/
pass5-fourier-majorant-support-theorem.txt, which proves the stronger complex
weight version. For beta a polynomial of fixed degree 2<=D<p it gives

                     Lambda <= C_D p^(7/8).                 (2)

All results below through (12), before using (2), hold for any beta and any
valid bound (1). No assertion that (2) holds for arbitrary beta is made.
The archive proves (1) from fourth singular moments, including the d^3
axis-translation term. This note does not improve or reprove that input.

2. TWO STANDARD FACTORIZATION NORMS

For a real matrix B define

 gamma_2(B)=inf [max_a ||u_a||_2][max_x ||v_x||_2],
                         B(a,x)=<u_a,v_x>,

and the real separated norm

 Gamma(B)=inf sum_j ||s_j||_infinity ||t_j||_infinity,
                         B(a,x)=sum_j s_j(a)t_j(x).

The following elementary form of the Grothendieck factorization comparison
is enough here:

          Gamma(B)<=K gamma_2(B), K=sinh(pi/2).              (3)

Proof. Normalize any factorization by g=(max||u_a||)(max||v_x||), so B/g is
a matrix of inner products of vectors of norm at most one. Pad row and
column vectors in separate orthogonal directions to make them unit vectors.
Gaussian sign rounding gives a convex combination Z of rank-one sign
matrices with Z(a,x)=(2/pi)arcsin(B(a,x)/g). Every positive entrywise integer
power of Z is again such a convex combination: multiply independent copies
of the row and column signs. Entrywise sine inversion has total absolute
coefficient sum sinh(pi/2), hence Gamma(B/g)<=K. The convex hull of the
finitely many rank-one sign matrices is closed, so the convergent sine series
also has a finite representation. Infimize over factorizations; B=0 is
immediate. This is the argument in the previous low-rank note with its John
ellipsoid step removed, not a new comparison theorem.

Write M_b for multiplication by b(x) on functions of (x,y), and set

 T_B=sum_a M_Ba U_a M_Ba,       Q_B=sum_a M_Ba P M_Ba.

Real masks make Q_B positive semidefinite. Expanding a separated expression
for B twice in T_B-Q_B and applying (1) proves

              ||T_B-Q_B||<=Lambda Gamma(B)^2
                           <=K^2 Lambda gamma_2(B)^2.       (4)

The infimum causes no gap: the bound holds for every finite expansion, and
the right side decreases to the infimum. Neither bounded rank nor a common
Fourier envelope is needed in (4).

3. AN EXACT DEFECT IDENTITY

Let f=1_E for the actual uncolored edge set, as a subset of F_p^2. Then

               <f,T_F f>=m+2N_3+4N_2.                      (5)

Proof. A fixed transition tau_a(x,y)=(x,y) has y=ax+beta(a). The masks and
f make it exactly an actual color-a edge. Simplicity counts each edge once.
A nonfixed transition e=(x,y) -> e'=(z,w) with both masks and both edge
indicators nonzero has x!=z and y!=w. Its two closing edges (x,w),(z,y)
are actually present and have color a. Conversely any repeated opposite
pair in a proper four-cycle supplies precisely two ordered transitions,
from either edge of the other pair. A three-color cycle has one repeated
pair; a two-color cycle has two. This proves (5).

Put rho_x=sum_a F(a,x). Simplicity gives sum rho_x=m, rho_x<=p, and

 <f,Q_F f>=p^(-2)sum_a (sum_x F(a,x)rho_x)^2
            >=(d p^2)^(-1)(sum_x rho_x^2)^2
            >=m^4/(d p^4).                                (6)

For d>0, (4)-(6) imply the explicit supersaturation inequality

  2N_3+4N_2 >= m^4/(d p^4)-(1+K^2 Lambda gamma_2(F)^2)m.    (7)

For zero palettes all graph counts vanish; no division is needed.

In this affine setting two-color cycles are scarce for an elementary
additional reason:

              N_2<=floor(d/2)(p-1)/2.                      (8)

Indeed if opposite edges have colors a and b, subtraction of the two row
equations gives (a+b)(x-z)=0. Thus b=-a. For a fixed unordered pair {a,-a},
the rows of an alternating cycle satisfy x+z=(beta(-a)-beta(a))/a. This is
an involution with one fixed row and at most (p-1)/2 unordered distinct row
pairs, each determining at most one four-cycle. At most floor(d/2) palette
pairs are antipodal. Actual supports can only remove cycles. Therefore

 N_3 >= m^4/(2d p^4)
          -(1+K^2 Lambda gamma_2(F)^2)m/2
          -floor(d/2)(p-1).                               (9)

POLYNOMIAL SUPERSATURATION COROLLARY. Fix D and delta>0. If (2) holds,
m>=delta p^2, and gamma_2(F)=o(p^(1/16)), then

                 N_3 >= (delta^4/2-o(1))p^3.              (10)

The scale p^3 is intentional; it is smaller than the p^4 scale of all
four-cycles. In fact (4) gives an asymptotic COUNTING FORMULA, not only
a lower bound. Put

          C(F)=p^(-2)sum_a (sum_x F(a,x)rho_x)^2.

Then, with beta as in (2), (5) and (8) give

         N_3=C(F)/2+O_D(p^(23/8)gamma_2(F)^2).              (10a)

The error absorbs m/2 and 2N_2, each O(p^2), using gamma_2(F)>=1 for
a nonempty graph. If gamma_2(F)=o(p^(1/16)), the error is o(p^3).
The statistic C(F) uses actual row degrees; replacing it by a function
of the edge density alone generally loses information.

For a concrete nonvacuous example, take beta(a)=a^2,
A={1,...,floor(p/8)} and X={0,...,floor(p/4)-1}, retaining all A-by-X
incidences. For a!=b, a collision at row x would require x+a+b=0 mod p,
impossible because 0<x+a+b<p. Thus this is simple and proper. Its
support has rank and gamma_2 equal to one (when nonempty), with
m=|A||X|~p^2/32; it is not claimed to be rainbow-C4. There are no
antipodal palette pairs, hence N_2=0. Formula (10a) yields

 N_3=|A|^3|X|^2/(2p^2)+O(p^(23/8))
                         =p^3/16384+O(p^(23/8)).           (10b)

This demonstrates the p^3 scale with a concrete affine family. The
counting statement is an application of the existing spectral input,
not a new exponential-sum theorem.

Moreover making all four-cycles rainbow by edge deletions
while retaining the colors requires at least

                       (delta^4/4-o(1))p^2

deletions. To see this, a fixed edge e can belong to at most p+d-2 bad
cycles: at most p-1 from repeating its own color on the opposite edge,
and at most d-1 from a repeated other color on the other opposite pair.
For each other color, properness determines both adjacent closing edges
uniquely. Hence each deleted edge hits at most 2p bad cycles. Apply (10).
This is a lower bound on deletions in the restricted low-norm class, not
a general colored removal lemma.

4. DEGREE-WEIGHTED ROBUSTNESS

Let B have entries in [0,1], and set

 W=sum_x rho_x sum_a |F(a,x)-B(a,x)|,
 H=1+K^2 Lambda gamma_2(B)^2,     Q=2N_3+4N_2.

Here W measures approximation error weighted by ACTUAL row degrees.
In particular errors on empty rows have zero cost. The bound is

             (m^2/p-W)_+^2/(d p^2) <= Hm+Q+2W.            (11)

Proof. Since both masks lie in [0,1],
|F(a,x)F(a,z)-B(a,x)B(a,z)|<=|F(a,x)-B(a,x)|+|F(a,z)-B(a,z)|.
In the quadratic form on f, the first term has at most rho_x choices of
y for each a,x. Swapping the two states using the involution handles the
second term. Consequently |<f,(T_F-T_B)f>|<=2W.
Also sum_a <f,B_a>=sum_x rho_x sum_a B(a,x)
 >=sum_x rho_x^2-W>=m^2/p-W. Nonnegative B permits replacing this lower
bound by its positive part. Cauchy-Schwarz over colors and (4),(5) prove
(11). The argument does not assume B is a graph, binary, or low rank.

When every four-cycle is rainbow, Q=0. Since W<=d p^2<=p^3, (11) gives

 m<=2p^(5/3)H^(1/3)+2p^(5/4)W^(1/4).                      (12)

If m^2<=2pW, the second term already suffices, using W<=p^3. Otherwise
(11) yields m^4<=4p^5 Hm+8p^5 W; one summand is at least half of m^4,
which proves (12). For fixed nonlinear polynomial beta this implies

 m<=C_D p^(47/24)max{1,gamma_2(B)}^(2/3)
                           +2p^(5/4)W^(1/4).              (13)

The max is needed for fractional B with gamma_2(B)<1. For ordinary
entrywise error R=sum|F-B|, W<=pR recovers a p^(3/2)R^(1/4) error term.

5. CONSEQUENCES FOR ACTUAL RAINBOW-C4 SUPPORTS

Assume henceforth every four-cycle is rainbow and beta has fixed nonlinear
polynomial degree D. A nonzero binary matrix has gamma_2(F)>=1, so (7) gives

             m<=C_D p^(47/24)gamma_2(F)^(2/3).              (14)

(a) Hereditary discrepancy. Define
 h(F)=max_{J subset F_p} min_{epsilon in {-1,1}^J}
                                  max_a |sum_{x in J}F(a,x)epsilon_x|.
Matousek--Nikolov--Talwar prove gamma_2(F)<=C log(2r) h(F), where r is real
rank (their Theorem 3.1, together with the determinant discrepancy bound).
Thus a convenient weaker form is

        m<=C_D p^(47/24)[log(2p)h(F)]^(2/3).               (15)

For fixed positive density this forces h(F)>=c_{D,delta}p^(1/16)/log(2p).
Their theorem also gives gamma_2(F)<=C log(2r)detlb(F), where detlb is the
largest k-th root of an absolute k-by-k minor determinant. The same density
obstruction therefore holds with detlb instead of h. These norm comparisons
are published facts, not results of this project.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/1408.1376, Section 3.1, Theorem 3.1.

(b) Normalized trace norm. Let t=||F||_tr/sqrt(dp), with ||.||_tr the sum
of real singular values. Then

           m<=C_D p^(63/40)d^(2/5)t^(2/5)
             <=C_D p^(79/40)t^(2/5).                     (16)

Proof. Put eta=m/(dp), epsilon=eta/4. The SVD factorization F=UV can be
chosen with sum_a||u_a||^2=sum_x||v_x||^2=||F||_tr. Remove rows whose
squared factor norm exceeds ||F||_tr/(epsilon d), and columns whose
squared factor norm exceeds ||F||_tr/(epsilon p). Fewer than epsilon d
rows and epsilon p columns are removed. At least m/2 one-entries remain,
and the surviving submatrix has gamma_2<=t/epsilon=4tdp/m. Extend its
missing columns by zeros and discard the removed colors: it is an actual
subgraph, and its gamma_2 is unchanged. Applying (14) gives
m/2<=C_D p^(47/24)(4tdp/m)^(2/3). Raising to the third power and solving
for m proves (16), since 47/8+2=63/8.

The trimming lemma is standard and appears explicitly as Lemma 5.1 of
Balla--Hambardzumyan--Tomon, Factorization norms and an inverse theorem
for MaxCut: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23989v1 . The application to this
archived affine operator bound is the deduction here.

Thus t=o(p^(1/16)) forces m=o(p^2), even when F has full real rank.
Equivalently a fixed-density valid support needs
t>=c_{D,delta}p^(1/16). A nuclear norm lower bound is not an algorithm for
proving the original conjecture: generic supports have much larger norms.

(c) Relation to the previous rank theorem. The standard bound
gamma_2(B)<=sqrt(rank_R B)||B||_infinity recovers the previous low-rank
theorem. Statements (7),(11),(14)-(16) express further consequences in
different parameters; they do not claim a new operator estimate.

6. MULTIPLE ORDERINGS AND GROWING GEOMETRIC DESCRIPTIONS

There is a useful explicit factorization, requiring no relation between
the field operations and the ordering of its rows. Write L=1+ceil(log_2 p).
If each D_a is a union of at most k intervals in one common ordering, then

                        gamma_2(F)<=sqrt(2k)L.             (17)

Pad the ordered set to a power of two. Decompose each interval into maximal
dyadic blocks. At most two blocks of each size are needed per interval.
Let u_a record which blocks occur in the disjoint dyadic decomposition of
D_a, and v_x record which blocks contain x. Then <u_a,v_x>=F(a,x),
||u_a||^2<=2kL, and ||v_x||^2<=L, proving (17).

If the colors are partitioned into q classes, each with its own arbitrary
common ordering, concatenate the q coordinate systems for v_x and use
only the appropriate system in u_a. This gives

 gamma_2(F)<=sqrt(2kq)L,
 m<=C_D p^(47/24)(kq)^(1/3)L^(2/3).                        (18)

In particular kq L^2=o(p^(1/8)) suffices for m=o(p^2). This allows a
growing number of unrelated orderings. All colors within one class still
share its ordering; a separate ordering for every color gives no useful
conclusion in general.

For q=1 the archive already has the stronger interval-support estimate
O_{D,k}(p^(35/18)) for fixed k. Optimizing its displayed block inequality
also handles k=o(p^(1/8)). Thus (17) is NOT promoted as a better interval
theorem. It is a convenient factorization tool for (18).

Similarly if every support is a box described by s arbitrary shared real
coordinate maps phi_j:F_p->R, with color-dependent interval endpoints,
then gamma_2(F)<=(sqrt(2)L)^s. Indeed it is an entrywise product of s
interval-incidence matrices; tensoring their factor vectors multiplies
their gamma_2 bounds. Consequently

         m<=C_D p^(47/24)(sqrt(2)L)^(2s/3).                (19)

The sufficient growing-dimension condition is
limsup s log log p/log p <1/16. Coordinate maps may depend on p and need
not be field-linear. Ties can be broken once in a common order without
changing an interval support. The earlier Fourier-majorant argument had
an analogous rate for shared field frequencies; (19) permits arbitrary
shared coordinate maps. The tensor factorization itself is standard.

7. LIMITS AND EVIDENCE

The substantive input is the previously archived weighted affine estimate.
All added deductions are conditional on its correctness and on the stated
simple, proper affine representation with one color per nonzero slope.
No extension to arbitrary permutations, sign rank, or finite-field rank is
proved. The robust bound requires B in [0,1]. No formal Lean verification
or independent external review is claimed.

The accompanying audit checked (5), (8), the exact quadratic-form
inequalities in (11), and dyadic decompositions with integer/rational
arithmetic: 360 graph cases, 8,190 binary rows and 4,096 small binary
matrices passed. See identity-audit.json for the seed and script hash. Finite checks support these algebraic steps; they cannot prove
the asymptotic operator or literature inputs. Historical novelty remains
unverified even if all local checks pass.
