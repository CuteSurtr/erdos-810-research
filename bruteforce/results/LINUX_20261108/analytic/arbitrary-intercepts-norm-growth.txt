GROWING SUPPORT COMPLEXITY WITH ARBITRARY AFFINE INTERCEPTS
8 November 2026. Corollaries of existing common-support and matrix theorems.

Status: written deduction; external review and historical novelty checks
remain outstanding. No unrestricted Erdos #810 conclusion is asserted.
The intercept beta below is arbitrary, not necessarily polynomial.

1. STATEMENT

Let p be an odd prime, A subset F_p*, d=|A|>0, and beta:A->F_p arbitrary.
Select precisely the color-a edges (x,ax+beta(a)) with F(a,x)=1, where
F is a binary d-by-p matrix. Suppose the colored graph is simple, proper,
and every four-cycle is rainbow. Let m be its edge count. Define

 g=gamma_2(F),   t=||F||_tr/sqrt(dp),   r=rank_R(F),
                    L_p=(log p)^(1/9).

Here gamma_2 is the real Euclidean factorization norm, and ||.||_tr is
the sum of real singular values. For every fixed delta>0, there are
positive c_delta and p_delta such that m>=delta p^2, p>=p_delta imply

             g>=c_delta (log p)^(1/27),
             t>=c_delta (log p)^(1/27).                    (1)

There are also absolute C,c>0 such that, for every such graph,

             m<=C 2^r p^2 exp(-c L_p).                    (2)

The constants in (1) need not be the same on its two lines. In particular
each of the following conditions separately forces m=o(p^2):

 g=o((log p)^(1/27)),  or t=o((log p)^(1/27)),
                         or r=o((log p)^(1/9)).           (3)

The rank condition from (2) is stronger than substituting g<=sqrt(r)
into (1). It uses Boolean row types rather than a norm comparison.
These rates are conservative consequences of specified published inputs;
they are not claims to optimize known Roth or matrix-theorem exponents.

2. THE ARCHIVED COMMON-SUPPORT INPUT

For every palette A' subset F_p* and row set X subset F_p, an actual
simple proper rainbow-C4 coloring containing ALL edges

                   (x,ax+beta(a)), (a,x) in A' x X,

has at most

                  C_0 p^2 exp(-c_0 L_p)                  (4)

edges, uniformly in beta, A', X. This is Section 9, equation (Q), of
research/local-progress/overnight-2026-10-15/
pass4-arbitrary-intercept-common-support-theorem.txt.

Its earlier, more explicit form is
 |A'||X|/p^2 << p^(-1/11)+(r_3([p-1])/p)^(1/6).
The archived proof uses affine energy and crossed-operator estimates,
and an integer three-term-progression bound. The last input can be the
published Bloom--Sisask exposition of Kelley--Meka, Theorem 1:
https://msp.org/ent/2023/2-1/ent-v2-n1-p02-p.pdf .
For the stronger exponent used here, substitute Bloom--Sisask, An
improvement to the Kelley--Meka bounds on three-term arithmetic
progressions, Theorem 1: r_3([N])/N<=exp(-c(log N)^(1/9)).
https://arxiv.org/html/2309.02353v1 . Taking a sixth root only changes c;
the p^(-1/11) term is smaller and can be absorbed. This is an update of
the published Roth input, not a new Roth bound.
This note assumes the archived common-support deduction. It does not
transfer the polynomial weighted estimate to arbitrary intercepts.

3. THE PUBLISHED MATRIX INPUTS AND THE COLOR OF THE RECTANGLE

We use Balla--Hambardzumyan--Tomon, Factorization norms and an inverse
theorem for MaxCut, Math. Ann. 394 (2026), article 52:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-026-03355-2
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12916507/
The preprint with the same lemma numbers is
https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23989v1 .

Their Lemma 4.4 says that a Boolean matrix of density at most 1-epsilon
and gamma_2 at most H has a submatrix with each row and column density
at most xi, retaining at least a fraction
2^(-C(H/epsilon)log(1/xi)) of each dimension.
Their Theorem 5.2 says that a square Boolean matrix of density at most
1/2 and gamma_2 at most H has an all-zero square submatrix retaining a
fraction 2^(-C H^3) of its side length. Constants are absolute. The published Lemma 4.4
improves the preprint's epsilon^2 denominator to epsilon; we use the
published version here.
Their Lemma 5.1 is the trace-to-factorization trimming statement used
in Section 5 below. These three matrix statements are published inputs.

The all-zeros OR all-ones conclusion of their headline theorem alone
would be insufficient here: a large all-zero rectangle contains no
actual graph edges. We force the desired all-one rectangle as follows.

Suppose m>=delta p^2. Then d>=delta p, and the support density
eta=m/(dp) is at least delta. Set M=J-F. Its density is at most
1-delta and gamma_2(M)<=g+1=:H, by the triangle inequality.
Apply Lemma 4.4 to M with epsilon=delta and xi=1/10. It supplies a
submatrix with dimensions d',p' satisfying

        d'>=alpha d, p'>=alpha p,
        alpha=2^(-C H/delta),                            (5)

and total one-density at most 1/10. The harmless integer roundings can
be absorbed in C; in the nontrivial asymptotic range the retained
dimensions tend to infinity. If a claimed lower dimension is below
one, the desired eventual lower bound on H already follows, since
that case requires H/delta of order log p.

Let s=min(d',p'). Retain s rows and s columns with no increase in
one-density, by averaging subsets on the larger side. This is a square
submatrix of M with gamma_2 at most H and density at most 1/10.
Theorem 5.2 therefore gives an all-zero square of side at least

                 delta p 2^(-C(H^3+H/delta)).            (6)

In F this is an all-one rectangle. Each of its entries is an actual
colored edge, so it gives a genuine common-support subgraph with at
least

           delta^2 p^2 2^(-2C(H^3+H/delta))              (7)

edges. Properness, simplicity and the rainbow-C4 property are inherited.
There are still distinct nonzero slopes. Thus (4) applies to this
subgraph, with the ORIGINAL field size p. It is not necessary to pretend
the extracted palette or row set is itself a field.

Comparing (7) with (4) and taking logarithms gives

 c_0 L_p <= 2C log(2)(H^3+H/delta)+log(C_0/delta^2).

For fixed delta, H>=1 allows the linear H term to be absorbed into
C_delta H^3. For sufficiently large p this proves the first line of
(1), since H=g+1. This proves a quantitative density obstruction; the
matrix extraction theorem itself is not a new theorem of this project.

4. REAL RANK: A SIMPLER AND STRONGER DEDUCTION

A binary matrix of real rank r has at most 2^r distinct rows. Choose r
coordinate columns on which restriction of its row space is injective;
the binary restrictions determine each row and have at most 2^r values.
Partition A by its identical support rows. Each nonempty class is a
common-support subgraph to which (4) applies. Sum over the at most 2^r
classes to prove (2). This proof requires no deep matrix theorem.

More generally if b all-one rectangles cover the support's one-entries,
then m<=b C_0 p^2 exp(-c_0 L_p). Overlap does not cause a problem, since
we only bound a union by the sum of its sizes. Thus log b=o(L_p) suffices
for vanishing density. The same conclusion follows if the nonnegative
rank is b: each nonnegative rank-one summand has rectangular support
contained in the one-support of F, and these rectangles cover F.
These are straightforward consequences of (4), not new matrix facts.

5. TRACE NORM AND PRESERVING DENSITY WHILE TRIMMING

For any real d-by-p matrix with normalized trace norm t and any
0<epsilon<=1/2, one can delete fewer than epsilon d rows and epsilon p
columns so the remaining matrix has gamma_2<=t/epsilon.
One proof factors the SVD as UV with squared Frobenius norms both equal
to the trace norm, then removes factor rows/columns above their Markov
thresholds. This is precisely the cited Lemma 5.1.

Under m>=delta p^2 take epsilon=delta/4. Applied to binary F, at most
2epsilon dp<=delta p^2/2 actual edges are deleted. At least delta p^2/2
edges remain, and the surviving support has gamma_2<=4t/delta. Pad its
removed row-vertex columns with zeros; discard the removed colors. This
is a valid affine subgraph on the original field, and zero padding does
not change gamma_2. Applying the already proved first line of (1) with
density delta/2 proves its second line.

This step cannot be replaced by the inequality t<=gamma_2(F): that
inequality alone has the wrong direction for deducing a lower bound
on t from a lower bound on gamma_2(F).

6. COMPARISON AND LIMITS

The archive already proves the qualitative statement m=o(p^2) for
bounded-VC support families and arbitrary beta. Bounded gamma_2 implies
bounded VC dimension: a shattered k-column set contains all sign patterns
after subtracting complementary support rows, whence sqrt(k)<=2gamma_2(F).
So the fixed-gamma_2 qualitative conclusion is not an advance over that
archive. The additional content here is the explicit GROWING thresholds
in (3), and the normalized trace-norm formulation with density-preserving
trimming. Formula (2) is an elementary further corollary of the already
available common-support rate.

For a nonlinear polynomial of fixed degree, the companion note gives
much stronger polynomial thresholds g,t=o(p^(1/16)) and real rank
o(p^(1/8)). The slower logarithmic thresholds here compensate for the
absence of a degree restriction on beta.

The norm hypotheses concern the ACTUAL binary supports. No extension to
arbitrary [0,1] approximations with small entrywise error is claimed in
this note: the extraction input is Boolean. Supports of a hypothetical
dense counterexample could have large values of every complexity
parameter used here. Neither (1) nor (2) settles that case.

7. AN EXPLICIT UNIFORM TRACE-NORM BOUND

The density dependence above also yields a uniform bound, useful when
one wants more than an asymptotic threshold. For absolute C,c>0,

 m/p^2 <= C[t L_p^(-1/3)+sqrt(t)L_p^(-1/2)+exp(-c L_p)].     (8)

Thus bounded normalized trace norm gives the explicit rate
m=O(p^2/(log p)^(1/27)) for arbitrary intercepts.

Proof. Put delta=m/p^2>0. Testing F against the all-ones matrix in
trace/operator-norm duality gives t>=m/(dp)>=delta. Trim with
 epsilon=delta/4 as before. The retained graph has density at least
 delta/2 relative to p^2, and H'=1+gamma_2(F')<=1+4t/delta<=5t/delta.
Apply (5)-(7) to this graph, and compare with (4). After enlarging
absolute constants this gives

 L_p <= C[t^3/delta^3+t/delta^2+log(C/delta)].               (9)

At least one summand on the right is at least L_p/3. The three
alternatives give respectively delta<=C t L_p^(-1/3),
delta<=C sqrt(t)L_p^(-1/2), or delta<=C exp(-c L_p). Their sum proves
(8). Cases where integer rounding makes the extracted rectangle smaller
than one already satisfy a stronger alternative, since then the same
exponent is at least a constant multiple of log p, hence of L_p.
For small p all bounds follow by increasing C. The empty graph is
immediate and requires no division by delta.

Similarly, writing H=g+1, the untrimmed comparison shows that if
H^3<=c L_p for a sufficiently small absolute c, then
m/p^2<=C H/L_p+C exp(-c' L_p). No quantitative approximation statement
for non-Boolean support masks is implied.
