# How many edges does proper recoloring cost on small graphs?

Ten is the smallest order at which two deletions can be required to obtain a proper rainbow-four-cycle coloring with $n$ colors. Up to isomorphism, exactly **398** ten-vertex graphs require two. Every admissible graph on at most nine vertices permits a proper recoloring after at most one edge deletion. The smallest ten-vertex examples with two required deletions have 22 edges, and there are four such supports. [All 398 supports and their colorings](data/multiple-repairs-10.tsv.gz)

Keeping the original labels gives a different count. Write $D$ for the least inherited deletion cost, minimized over every admissible starting coloring, and $R$ for the least cost when a new coloring is allowed. The maximum $D$ is five at order nine and four at order ten. The 398 supports with $R=2$ have $D=2,3,4$ in 258, 124 and 16 cases, respectively. The ten-vertex row uses a ten-color budget throughout, including after removing edges. ## Completeness and independent checks

The input consists of every ordinary unlabeled simple graph generated by nauty's unrestricted `geng`, including disconnected graphs and isolated vertices. The primary solver tests the exact four-cycle conflicts, then adds incident-edge conflicts for the proper-coloring decision. On each admissible, nonproper support, it tries every one-edge puncture until a proper coloring exists or all punctures fail. A separate run extracts all remaining graphs. The 128 shards at order ten cover every generator residue once. [Every shard count](data/deletion-spectrum-shards.tsv) · [Native solver](native/deletion_spectrum.cpp)

For $D$, the SAT formula colors the **full** support with its original four-cycle conflicts. If two incident edges may share a color, a clause permits equality only if at least one is marked for deletion. A sequential counter finds the smallest feasible number of marks. The program checks every returned coloring and deletion set. The 398 remaining graphs were fed through the same inherited-cost calculation separately.

A second C++ program reconstructs all original cycles from four-vertex subsets. For each of the 398 graphs it finds an unrestricted coloring, rules out a proper coloring of the full graph and every one-edge puncture, then finds a proper two-edge puncture. The archive lists the graph6 support, its edge count, both costs, an optimal starting coloring, the corresponding inherited deletions, and a proper recoloring with two edges marked by hyphens. All label strings use lexicographic edge order with zero-based edge indices. A separate four-subset checker verified all archived positive colorings and their surviving labels. [Independent exact recoloring solver](native/repair_distance.cpp) · [Certificates](data/multiple-repairs-10.tsv.gz)

```sh
sh native/build.sh "$NAUTY" "$SCRATCH" "$CADICAL"
"$NAUTY/geng" -q 9 | "$SCRATCH/deletion-spectrum"
"$NAUTY/geng" -q 10 0/128 | "$SCRATCH/deletion-spectrum"
gzip -dc data/multiple-repairs-10.tsv.gz | tail -n +2 | cut -f1 | "$SCRATCH/repair-distance"
```

Apply the third command to residues 0 through 127 to reproduce the full ten-vertex run. Running the same generator into `deletion-spectrum --multiple` outputs precisely the supports without a one-edge proper repair. For those inputs, `deletion-spectrum --costs` computes the inherited deletion optimum.
