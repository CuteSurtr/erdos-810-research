PARTIAL COLOR MATCHINGS WITH SMALL PERMUTATION DIFFERENCE SET
2026-10-15. Complete conditional vanishing-density deduction. Uses established
theorems of Tao, Breuillard--Green--Tao, density Hales--Jewett, and large
abelian subgroups of finite groups. Not Lean verified. Historical novelty
of the assembled corollary has not been established.

This extends the perfect-matching arithmetic-progression argument to partial
color matchings WITHOUT assuming that their permutation completions remain
B-colored. The proof does not require the completed palette to be AP-free.

MAIN THEOREM. Fix C,D,K. For each N, let a bipartite simple graph have N
vertices in total, a proper coloring with at most DN colors, and every
four-cycle rainbow. Suppose
the two sides separately inject into a finite set Omega with |Omega|<=CN.
For every color c choose a permutation sigma_c of Omega extending that
color's partial matching: y=sigma_c x for every edge xy of color c.
Let S={sigma_c:c is a used color}. If

                             |S S^{-1}| <= K N,

then the graph has o(N^2) edges, uniformly as N tends to infinity.

The representing permutations may initially coincide for different colors.
The theorem has no solvability assumption, no bound on the order of the
generated group, and no regularity or perfect-matching assumption.

Consequently, a hypothetical positive-density counterexample family with
O(N) colors must, for EVERY permutation representation on O(N) points,
have |S S^{-1}|/N tend to infinity along that family. This is a structural
obstruction to such a family, not a proof that no such family exists.

Definitions. A properly edge-colored graph with every four-cycle rainbow is
called B-colored. The permutation representation itself forces properness,
because each color is a subset of a permutation matching.
A finite set A in a group is a K_0-approximate group if it contains the
identity, is symmetric, and A^2 can be covered by K_0 left translates of A.
In particular |A^j|<=K_0^(j-1)|A| for every positive integer j.

1. ESTABLISHED INPUTS

(i) Tao's small-product covering theorem: if |U U^{-1}|<=Q|U|, then U
is covered by L_Q left translates of a K_Q-approximate group A with
|A|<=B_Q|U|, where L_Q,K_Q,B_Q depend only on Q.
T. Tao, Product set estimates for non-commutative groups, Theorem 4.6,
PDF pp.11--12, applied to the pair (U,U^{-1}).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0601431

(ii) Breuillard--Green--Tao's structure theorem: a K_0-approximate group A
contains in A^4 a coset nilprogression P of size at least c(K_0)|A|,
with bounded rank and bounded normal-form constant depending only on K_0.
E. Breuillard, B. Green, T. Tao, The structure of approximate groups,
Theorem 2.10, and Definitions 2.6 and 2.8 (PDF pp.12--13).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5008

(iii) The largest abelian subgroup of a finite group has order tending to
infinity uniformly with the group order. Erdős--Straus, Theorem 1.1,
printed p.308 / PDF p.2, already suffices.
https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-16.pdf

(iv) The density Hales--Jewett theorem for an alphabet of four letters.
Write DHJ(4,delta) for a dimension that forces a combinatorial line in every
word subset of density at least delta.
D. H. J. Polymath, Annals of Mathematics 175 (2012), 1283--1327.
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2012/175-3/p06

No quantitative assertion from an informal remark or conjecture is used.
The recent Schildkraut theorem is not needed for this qualitative result.

2. FIXED-DIMENSION COMMUTING CUBES IN APPROXIMATE GROUPS

Lemma 1. Fix K_0 and a positive integer r. Every sufficiently large finite
K_0-approximate group A contains elements w_1,...,w_r with these properties:
  (a) the w_i commute pairwise;
  (b) every nonempty subset product is different from the identity;
  (c) every subset product, including the empty product, belongs to A^4.
The ambient group can be arbitrary.

Proof. Apply input (ii). In its global-group notation P=pi^{-1}(Q), where
pi:G_0 -> G_0/H is a quotient by a finite normal subgroup H, and
Q=P(u_1,...,u_l;L_1,...,L_l) is a nilprogression in C_0-normal form.
Here l,C_0 are bounded in terms of K_0, |P|>=c(K_0)|A|, and P subset A^4.
Normal form supplies the two specific properties needed below:

  |Q| <= C_0 product_{i=1}^l (2 floor(L_i)+1),
  u_1^{a_1}...u_l^{a_l} are distinct for |a_i|<=L_i/C_0.

If H is sufficiently large in terms of r, input (iii) gives an abelian
subgroup J<=H with |J|>2^(r-1). Choose w_i greedily in J. At step i avoid
the inverses of all subset products of the previous i-1 choices, including
the empty product. There are at most 2^(i-1) excluded elements. No nonempty
subset product is then the identity. All products stay in J<=H<=P<=A^4.

Otherwise |H| is bounded in terms of r. As |A| tends to infinity, |P|
tends to infinity and |Q|=|P|/|H| tends to infinity. The volume bound and
bounded rank force some length L_i to grow without bound. For sufficiently
large A choose such an i with L_i/C_0 >=2^r. Lift u_i to an element g of
G_0, and set w_j=g^(2^(j-1)) for j=1,...,r.

These elements commute. Every nonempty subset product is g^a with
1<=a<=2^r-1. Local properness in the quotient gives u_i^a!=1, so g^a!=1.
Moreover u_i^a belongs to Q whenever 0<=a<=2^r-1<=L_i. Thus every subset
product g^a belongs to pi^{-1}(Q)=P<=A^4. This proves all three claims.

The dichotomy is uniform: if H stays below the finite threshold for the
first case and all L_i stay below the threshold for the second, the volume
bound gives an upper bound on |P|, hence on |A|, depending only on K_0,r.
QED.

Remark. No injectivity of the whole subset-product map is required in the
finite-subgroup case. Only the absence of a nonempty identity product is
needed. This explicitly handles large elementary abelian 2-groups.

3. A LOCAL GROUP-TABLE SQUARE THEOREM

Lemma 2. Fix K_0,delta>0 and positive integers a,b. If A is a sufficiently
large K_0-approximate group and F subset A^a x A^b has size at least
delta |A|^2, then F contains

                       (x,y),(xt,y),(x,yt),(xt,yt)

with t!=1. In particular, coloring F by color(x,y)=y x^{-1} cannot make
every four-cycle rainbow.

Proof. Put delta_0=delta/K_0^(a+b+6), decreasing it to 1 if necessary, and
r=DHJ(4,delta_0). Choose weights w_1,...,w_r from Lemma 1. For a word
((c_i,d_i)) in {(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)}^r, put

 h_c=product_i w_i^{c_i},  h_d=product_i w_i^{d_i}.

Both products lie in A^4. Use basepoints p in A^(a+4), q in A^(b+4), and
map the word to (p h_c,q h_d). For each fixed word, every (x,y) in F has
exactly one basepoint pair mapping to it, namely

                         p=x h_c^{-1}, q=y h_d^{-1}.

These basepoints belong to the specified sets because A is symmetric and
h_c,h_d are in A^4. Thus the average, over basepoint pairs, of the fraction
of good words is exactly |F|/(|A^(a+4)| |A^(b+4)|), at least delta_0.
Some basepoint pair therefore gives a word set to which DHJ applies.

In the resulting combinatorial line, let I be the nonempty variable set.
Because the weights commute, its four images are exactly the four corners
displayed in the lemma, with t=product_{i in I} w_i. This t is nonidentity
by Lemma 1. The two rows and two columns are consequently distinct, and

                     (yt)(xt)^{-1}=y x^{-1}.

The diagonal colors coincide. QED.

The ambient group order may be much larger than |A|. This is the difference
from applying the ordinary finite-group-table theorem directly. No closure
of A under multiplication is presumed; all translations are averaged over
the explicitly larger sets A^(a+4) and A^(b+4).

4. DUPLICATE REPRESENTING PERMUTATIONS CAN BE REMOVED AT CONSTANT COST

Assume m>=epsilon N^2, with epsilon>0 fixed, towards a contradiction.
Discard colors with fewer than eta N edges, where eta=epsilon/(2D).
This removes at most DN*eta N=epsilon N^2/2 edges. For each permutation
sigma, the original colors represented by sigma occupy disjoint subsets
of its matching on Omega, which has at most CN positions. Hence at most
C/eta retained colors share sigma. Keep one largest color class for each
sigma. The surviving graph has at least

                (eta/C)*(epsilon N^2/2)=epsilon_1 N^2

edges, where epsilon_1=epsilon^2/(4CD)>0. Its representing permutations
are distinct. Their set S_1 is a subset of S, so
|S_1 S_1^{-1}|<=KN. We may now work only with this subgraph.

Its color count d=|S_1| satisfies

                epsilon_1 N/C <= d <= DN,

since each color contains at most CN edges. It follows that

             |S_1 S_1^{-1}| <= (KC/epsilon_1) d.

5. WEIGHTED APPROXIMATE-GROUP EXTRACTION

Apply input (i) with Q=KC/epsilon_1, a fixed constant. Obtain an approximate
group A and a cover S_1 subset X A, where |X|<=L, |A|<=B d, and A is
K_0-approximate; the constants L,B,K_0 depend only on C,D,K,epsilon.
Assign colors to covering translates if the cover overlaps. Some translate
gA carries at least epsilon_1 N^2/L edges. Retain its colors and relabel
the right-side vertex labels by y -> g^{-1}y. This preserves simplicity,
distinct vertex labels, all original colors, and the B property.

The new representing permutations are u=g^{-1}sigma, forming a set U
contained in A. Write m' for the retained edge count. Then

 m'>=epsilon_1 N^2/L,
 |U|>=m'/(CN)>=epsilon_1 N/(LC),
 |A|<=B d<=BDN.

In particular, both |U| and |A| tend to infinity with N and are O(N).
Selection was by actual edge weight, so no perfectness of the color
classes or uniformity of their supports has been assumed.

6. A BASEPOINT LIFT THAT PRESERVES THE B PROPERTY

For every z in Omega, form a bipartite graph F_z on two disjoint copies of
the permutation group. For each u,v in U, put the edge

                             (u^{-1},v u^{-1})

in F_z if the retained original graph has its color-v edge at row label
u^{-1}z. (That original edge necessarily ends at v u^{-1}z.) The row and
column labels lie in A and A^2, respectively. The group-table color is v.

For fixed z, distinct pairs (u,v) give distinct lifted edges: the row
determines u, and then the column determines v. Each original color label
corresponds to a unique v, by Section 4. For every fixed u, the map
z -> u^{-1}z is a bijection of Omega. Consequently

                          sum_{z in Omega} |F_z| = |U| m'.

Some z therefore satisfies

 |F_z| >= |U|m'/|Omega|
       >= epsilon_1^2 N^2/(L^2 C^2)
       >= delta_1 |A|^2,

where delta_1=epsilon_1^2/(L^2 C^2 B^2 D^2)>0 is fixed.

Claim: every F_z is B-colored. Suppose a nonrainbow four-cycle had distinct
rows r_1,r_2, distinct columns s_1,s_2, and equal diagonal color v. The
group-table coloring is proper, so a repeat must indeed be on a diagonal.
The equal-color equations give s_i=v r_i. Project group elements by
pi_z(h)=h z. Set x_i=r_i z, y_i=s_i z=v x_i.

If x_1!=x_2, then y_1!=y_2 because v is a permutation. All four original
edges exist by the definition of F_z, and form a genuine four-cycle with
equal diagonal color v, contradicting the original B property.

If x_1=x_2, then y_1=y_2. All four lifted edges project to the same original
simple edge. Its original color is unique, hence its representing
permutation is v. The lifted cross edge (r_1,s_2) must therefore also have
group-table color v. Thus s_2=v r_1=v r_2 and r_1=r_2, a contradiction.

This proves the claim even if the map pi_z is far from injective.

7. CONCLUSION

For the basepoint selected above, F_z subset A x A^2 is B-colored and has
at least delta_1 |A|^2 edges. Lemma 2 with (a,b)=(1,2) contradicts this as
|A| tends to infinity. The hypothesized m>=epsilon N^2 is impossible for
every fixed epsilon>0. This proves the main theorem.

8. INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS

* Unlike the perfect-color argument, the proof never infers AP-freeness of
  the chosen permutation completions. Such an inference would be false in
  general: the required four original edges may be absent.
* The parameter |SS^{-1}| is the number of distinct relative permutations,
  not the order of the generated group. It can remain O(N) while that group
  is much larger, so the theorem strengthens the small-ambient-group result.
* The hypothesis is not automatic for B colorings. There can be up to
  |S|^2=O(N^2) relative permutations, and no argument here bounds them by
  O(N). Establishing an appropriate structural reduction remains open.
* The result is qualitative and depends on deep established structure and
  recurrence theorems. No practical numerical decay rate is obtained.
* The ordinary finite-group-table case is already Solymosi's Theorem 5
  (arXiv:1309.0133). The precise local-approximate-group and partial-action
  formulation here requires a literature audit before any priority claim.

9. STRONGER SCALE-FREE FORMULATION

THEOREM. Fix K. Let a B-colored bipartite graph be represented, with separate
injections of its two sides, on a set Omega of size n. Let q be its number
of original used colors, and S the set of their representing permutations;
the representing permutations need not be distinct. If q tends to infinity
and |SS^{-1}|<=Kq, then

                                m=o(qn).

This is uniform in n and in the representation, and does not presume that
q and n have comparable sizes. In particular it rules out bounded doubling
for palettes whose color classes have a positive average density on Omega.

Proof. Suppose m>=epsilon qn. Discard color classes smaller than epsilon n/2;
at least epsilon qn/2 edges remain. At most 2/epsilon surviving original
colors can share one representing permutation, since their positions are
disjoint subsets of its matching of size n. Keeping one largest class for
each permutation leaves at least gamma qn edges, where gamma=epsilon^2/4.
The distinct surviving permutation set S_1 has size d with gamma q<=d<=q.
Consequently |S_1 S_1^{-1}|<=Kq<=(K/gamma)d.

Tao's covering theorem gives a K_0-approximate group A of size at most Bd
and a cover of S_1 by L left translates of A; K_0,B,L depend only on
K,epsilon. Choose a translate carrying at least gamma qn/L edges and
normalize its colors into A as in Section 5. Its palette U satisfies
|U|>=gamma q/L because a color class has at most n edges. For the basepoint
lift of Section 6, averaging over Omega now gives

             |F_z| >= |U|m'/n >= gamma^2 q^2/L^2
                   >= gamma^2 |A|^2/(L^2 B^2)

for some z. These F_z are B-colored subsets of A x A^2 by the same exact
projection argument. Since |A|>=|U|>=gamma q/L tends to infinity, Lemma 2
gives a contradiction. QED.

The number q counts ORIGINAL colors, not only distinct permutation labels;
the duplicate-label step is therefore part of the argument rather than an
unstated injectivity assumption. If the original palette is already
injectively represented, that step may be omitted.
