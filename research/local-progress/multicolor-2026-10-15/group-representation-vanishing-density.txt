GROUP REPRESENTATIONS FORCE VANISHING EDGE DENSITY
Research note, 2026-10-15. Status: complete elementary reductions using two
established theorems; not a proof of Erdős problem #810 and not Lean checked.
Theorem A is already a theorem of Solymosi (2013 preprint, 2015 publication).
The DHJ argument below is an alternative derivation, and Theorem B is a
representation/lifting corollary. Neither is asserted historically new.

Conventions. A B-colored graph is properly edge-colored and has every
four-cycle rainbow. The permutation representations below automatically
enforce properness. A partial
group-table graph for a finite group G has two disjoint copies of G as its
bipartition classes, any subset of the possible edges, and color

                         color(x,y) = y x^{-1}.

This coloring is automatically proper. The result below is uniform over all
finite groups, including groups of even order and nonabelian groups.

THEOREM A (Solymosi, in group-table language). For every epsilon>0 there is
M(epsilon) such that if |G| is at
least M(epsilon), every B-colored partial group-table graph has fewer than
epsilon |G|^2 edges.

THEOREM B. Fix K. Consider any sequence of bipartite B-colored simple graphs
with N vertices in total, with N tending to infinity. Suppose each graph has
a representation of the following form:
  * a finite group G with |G| <= K N acts on a set Omega;
  * each bipartition class is separately injected into Omega (their images
    need not be disjoint);
  * colors are injectively labeled by elements sigma_c of G;
  * every edge xy of color c satisfies label(y)=sigma_c label(x).
Then the number of edges is o(N^2). The action need not be transitive or
faithful. The representation may depend on N.

Thus a positive-density counterexample family to #810 cannot have such
representations with bounded |G|/N. General partial matching colorings always
have permutation representations after extending their matchings, but the
generated group can be enormously larger than N. No small-group completion
or representation theorem is supplied here.

1. PRIOR THEOREM AND EXTERNAL INPUTS

Direct prior theorem: J. Solymosi, The (7,4)-conjecture in finite groups,
arXiv:1309.0133, Theorem 5 on PDF page 4. It states that every positive-density
subset of G x G, for G sufficiently large, contains a rectangle whose two
opposite entries have equal products. Applying its coordinate map to
(x,y) -> (y,x^{-1}) gives Theorem A exactly. The original proof uses
hypergraph removal inside coset rectangles of a large abelian subgroup.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133

Our alternative proof uses the following two inputs instead of hypergraph
removal. The prior theorem establishes that Theorem A is known, not merely
that similar techniques have appeared before.

(DHJ) For every delta>0 there is an integer r=DHJ(4,delta) such that every
subset of a four-letter alphabet to the rth power with density at least
delta contains a combinatorial line. A line has a nonempty set I of variable
coordinates, a fixed letter at every other coordinate, and all four words
obtained by putting the same variable letter in every position of I.

Reference: D. H. J. Polymath, A new proof of the density Hales-Jewett theorem,
Annals of Mathematics 175 (2012), 1283-1327.
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2012/175-3/p06
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v175-n3-p06-p.pdf

(Large abelian subgroup) The minimum, over groups of order at least m, of
the largest abelian subgroup order tends to infinity as m tends to infinity.
For example, Erdős and Straus prove that a group of order m contains an
abelian p-subgroup of size greater than log m-o(log m).

Reference: P. Erdős and E. G. Straus, How Abelian is a Finite Group?, Linear
and Multilinear Algebra 3 (1976), 307-312, Theorem 1.1 on printed page 308
(PDF page 2). The PDF itself identifies 1976 as publication year.
https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-16.pdf

Only qualitative divergence is needed. Pyber's stronger bound is unnecessary.
All remaining arguments are given here.

2. UNIFORM SQUARE LEMMA FOR ALL FINITE ABELIAN GROUPS

Lemma. Let delta>0 and r=DHJ(4,delta). If H is a finite abelian group with
|H|>2^(r-1), every A subset of H x H of size at least delta |H|^2 contains

                   (x,y), (x+t,y), (x,y+t), (x+t,y+t)

for some t!=0. These are four distinct points, including in characteristic 2.

Proof. Choose w_1,...,w_r in H with no nonempty subset summing to zero.
Inductively, when choosing w_i, exclude the negatives of all subset sums of
the previous i-1 weights, including the empty sum. At most 2^(i-1) elements
are excluded, strictly fewer than |H|. This ensures the asserted property.

Use the four-letter alphabet E={(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)}. For u,v in H map a
word ((a_i,b_i)) in E^r to

                 (u + sum_i a_i w_i, v + sum_i b_i w_i).

The map need not be injective. If u,v are uniform and independent, every
fixed word maps uniformly into H x H. The average density of words mapping
into A is |A|/|H|^2 >= delta. Consequently some u,v give a word subset of
density at least delta. DHJ supplies a combinatorial line in that subset.

The four line words map to the four corners displayed in the lemma, with
t=sum_{i in I} w_i, where I is the nonempty variable-coordinate set. The
choice of the weights gives t!=0. Adding a nonzero element changes a group
coordinate, so these four corners are pairwise distinct. QED.

The argument is uniform in the exponent, rank, and prime divisors of H.
It does not require an odd-order hypothesis, a cyclic subgroup, an injective
cube embedding, or a separate multidimensional Szemerédi theorem.

3. PROOF OF THEOREM A

Fix epsilon>0. Let H be an abelian subgroup of G with size greater than
2^(DHJ(4,epsilon)-1); such an H exists when |G| is sufficiently large.
Suppose A subset G x G has density at least epsilon. Partition G x G into
rectangles gH x kH using left cosets in both coordinates. At least one such
rectangle has relative A-density at least epsilon.

Write its coordinates as x=g h_1 and y=k h_2. The square lemma in H x H,
in multiplicative notation, supplies four edges at

  (g h_1,   k h_2),       (g h_1 t, k h_2),
  (g h_1,   k h_2 t),     (g h_1 t, k h_2 t),

with t!=e. These are the edges of a genuine four-cycle: the two row labels
are distinct and the two column labels are distinct. The diagonal colors
are equal, since

 (k h_2)(g h_1)^{-1} = k h_2 h_1^{-1} g^{-1},
 (k h_2 t)(g h_1 t)^{-1} = k h_2 t t^{-1} h_1^{-1} g^{-1}
                        = k h_2 h_1^{-1} g^{-1}.

The four-cycle is not rainbow, proving the theorem. Notice that the final
identity itself needs no commutativity; commutativity was used to produce
the square in the abelian subgroup. The same argument works for any group
family whose largest abelian subgroup order tends to infinity; the external
subgroup theorem supplies that property uniformly over all finite groups.

4. LIFTING A TRANSITIVE ACTION TO THE GROUP TABLE

Let G act transitively on a finite orbit Omega. Fix a basepoint o, set
pi(g)=g o, and h=|Stab_G(o)|, so every pi-fiber has size h and |G|=h|Omega|.
Suppose a bipartite B graph is represented in this action as in Theorem B.
Use the injective vertex labels to identify its sides with subsets X,Y of
Omega. For each original edge xy of color c, and every g with pi(g)=x,
insert the lifted edge

                            (g, sigma_c g)

in the two copies of G. The right projection is sigma_c x=y. Each original
edge has exactly h lifts. Distinct original edges have distinct lifts;
injectivity of c -> sigma_c ensures that the lifted graph is simple. Its
group-table color is (sigma_c g)g^{-1}=sigma_c.

Claim: the lifted graph is B-colored. Since group-table colorings are
proper, a nonrainbow four-cycle would have equal diagonal colors. Write its
distinct rows g_1,g_2, distinct columns k_1,k_2, and equal diagonal color
sigma_a, so k_i=sigma_a g_i. Project to x_i=pi(g_i), y_i=pi(k_i).

If x_1!=x_2, the permutation sigma_a gives y_1!=y_2. The four lifted edges
then project to a four-cycle in the original graph with equal diagonal
color a, a contradiction.

If x_1=x_2, then y_1=y_2. Every one of the four edges projects to the same
original simple edge. Its unique color is a, so, in particular, the lifted
cross edge (g_1,k_2) also has group-table color sigma_a. Hence
k_2=sigma_a g_1=sigma_a g_2 and g_1=g_2, again a contradiction.

Thus an original graph with m edges lifts to a B-colored partial group-table
graph with exactly h m edges. Theorem A applies without any perfect-matching
or regularity assumption on the original graph.

5. PROOF OF THEOREM B, INCLUDING NONTRANSITIVE ACTIONS

Suppose for contradiction that along a subsequence m>=epsilon N^2, for a
fixed epsilon>0. Split the original graph according to the G-orbits of the
vertex labels. Every edge remains within one orbit because its endpoint
labels are related by an element of G. Let n_i be the total number of original
vertices on the two sides in orbit i, and m_i its edge count. Then

  sum_i n_i=N,  sum_i m_i=m,  and m_i<=|X_i||Y_i|<=n_i^2/4.

Some orbit has m_i/n_i>=m/N>=epsilon N. For that orbit,

                    n_i>=4 epsilon N,
                    m_i>=4 epsilon^2 N^2.

Its orbit Omega_i has size at least max(|X_i|,|Y_i|)>=n_i/2. In particular
|G|>=|Omega_i|>=2 epsilon N tends to infinity. Lifting this orbit as in
Section 4 produces a B-colored partial group-table graph with h_i m_i edges.
Its relative density in G x G is at least

        h_i m_i/|G|^2 >= m_i/(K^2 N^2) >=4 epsilon^2/K^2.

This contradicts Theorem A as |G| tends to infinity. QED.

One can additionally note h_i=|G|/|Omega_i|<=K/(2 epsilon), but that upper
bound is not needed for the displayed density contradiction. For a
transitive action using all N original vertices, h<=2K follows immediately
from the separate injections into Omega.

6. SCOPE AND LIMITS

* This is a genuine vanishing-density result for a structurally restricted
  class, rather than an improvement of a quadratic coefficient.
* It covers partial group tables, arbitrary deletions of their entries,
  and partial permutation colorings represented by groups of O(N) elements.
* It does not cover all proper B colorings. Extending color matchings to
  permutations only gives a subgroup of a symmetric group, with no useful
  O(N) bound on its order.
* Group-table multiplication conventions matter. The chosen y x^{-1}
  convention is compatible with the left action and the lift (g,sigma_c g).
* The proof uses no finite graph search and gives no practical numerical
  bound: its threshold inherits the size of a density-Hales-Jewett number.
* Theorem A is exactly Solymosi's established theorem after a change of
  coordinates. The square lemma is a direct DHJ consequence, and the action
  result is a lifting corollary. No historical originality is claimed for
  the alternative proof or corollary.
