CROSSED-PAIR OPERATORS FOR PERFECT B-COLORINGS
Research note, 2026-10-15. Hand proofs; not Lean checked. No historical
novelty claim, and no unconditional improvement toward o(N^2) is claimed.

The useful output is an exact conditional criterion for vanishing density,
plus a precise limitation of the first multicolor compression. The criterion
is a Hoffman-type independent-set calculation in a derived conflict graph.

1. SETUP

A perfect B-coloring is a d-regular bipartite simple graph with n vertices
on each side, whose d colors are perfect matchings and whose every C4 is
rainbow. Write its n by n biadjacency matrix as

             C = P_1 + ... + P_d,

where the P_c are permutation matrices with pairwise disjoint supports.
Use <X,Y>=tr(X^T Y) on real matrices. Let J be the all-one matrix and

             E=C-(d/n)J,     W={X: X1=X^T1=0}.

Thus E belongs to W and ||E||_F^2=nd-d^2. The total number of graph
vertices is N=2n and the number of edges is nd. Assume d>0. The trivial
case n=d=1 can be separated throughout. A nontrivial perfect B-coloring
has d<n: if d=n>=2 then C=J, and any two edges of any one color complete
to a forbidden nonrainbow C4.

Define a linear operator on the n^2-dimensional matrix space by

             T(X)=(1/d) sum_c P_c X^T P_c.

Each summand T_c(X)=P_c X^T P_c is an orthogonal involution, since
T_c^2(X)=X. Thus T is self-adjoint, has norm at most 1, and has real
eigenvalues in [-1,1]. It preserves W and satisfies T(J)=J.

If P_c(i,p_c(i))=1, then

             (T_c X)_{ij}=X_{p_c^{-1}(j),p_c(i)}.

This coordinate formula is useful both for the forbidden-cycle argument
and for interpreting the spectrum.

2. EXACT FORBIDDEN-CYCLE IDENTITIES

For every color c,

             <C,T_c(C)>=tr((C P_c^T)^2)=n.                (2.1)

Indeed the left side counts original edge positions (i,j) for which
(p_c^{-1}(j),p_c(i)) is also an original edge. If (i,j) is a c-edge,
the two positions agree. Otherwise, the two c-edges joining the opposite
corners, together with these two edges, give a genuine C4 with c repeated
on opposite edges. This is forbidden. Exactly the n c-edges survive.

Subtracting the constant part C=(d/n)J+E gives

             <E,T(E)>=n-d^2.                             (2.2)

There is a stronger identity retaining individual colors:

             <P_a,T(P_b)>=(n/d) delta_ab.                (2.3)

To prove it, expand the inner product as

       (1/d) sum_c tr(P_a^T P_c P_b^T P_c).

Every term is a nonnegative fixed-point count. The case a=b=c contributes
n. If c equals exactly one of a,b, the trace reduces to <P_a,P_b>=0.
If c differs from a,b, a fixed point gives a four-cycle with the two
opposite c-edges; the other two edges have colors a,b, which may coincide.
Properness makes all vertices of this cycle distinct. Hence the B rule
excludes every other contribution.

An independent audit by the finite_audit agent verified (2.3), including
its degeneracies. Equations (2.1) and (2.2) also follow by summing (2.3).

3. A CONDITIONAL VANISHING-DENSITY THEOREM

Let theta=max(0,-lambda_min(T restricted to W)). Since E is in W, (2.2)
implies

           d^2-n <= theta (nd-d^2),
           (1+theta)d^2-theta nd-n <= 0.                 (3.1)

Consequently

  d/n <= [theta+sqrt(theta^2+4(1+theta)/n)]/[2(1+theta)]
      <= theta/(1+theta)+1/sqrt((1+theta)n).             (3.2)

In particular theta=o(1) implies d=o(n), and therefore nd=o(N^2).
Conversely, if d/n tends to delta>0, then

           theta >= delta/(1-delta)+o(1).              (3.3)

The uniform error in (3.2) is O(n^(-1/2)), not O(n^(-1)). An O(n^(-1))
expansion requires theta to stay bounded away from zero.

This is a condition on negative pair-space spectrum. Requiring the full
norm ||T|W|| to tend to zero would be unnecessarily strong: even abelian
translation examples have many positive eigenvalues equal to 1.

4. THE HOFFMAN INTERPRETATION AND ITS LIMIT

View the n^2 matrix coordinates as vertices. Each T_c pairs the two
possible cross-edges of a rectangle whose other diagonal consists of two
c-edges. It fixes exactly the n positions of the matching P_c. Different
colors send any given position to different positions, by disjointness
of the permutation supports. Thus dT is the adjacency matrix of a simple
d-regular graph with loops: there is one loop precisely at each original
edge position, and no loop at a nonedge position.

The B condition says that the nd original edge positions form an
independent set after those loops are removed. Centering their indicator
gives E. The estimate in Section 3 is the corresponding Hoffman argument,
with a loop correction and restriction to W using the balanced row and
column sums. It is not a new general spectral principle.

For the classical ratio-bound context, see W. H. Haemers, "Hoffman's ratio
bound," Linear Algebra and its Applications 617 (2021), arXiv:2102.05529:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05529

No theorem here proves theta=o(1) for arbitrary perfect B-colorings.
Establishing that condition, or extracting useful structure when it fails,
is precisely the remaining difficulty. No reduction from arbitrary B
graphs to this perfect setting is asserted in this note.

5. THE ENTIRE FIRST COLOR COMPRESSION HAS ONLY ONE NEGATIVE MODE

Set V_a=P_a-J/n. All V_a belong to W. Their Gram matrix and their T-form
are exactly

       <V_a,V_b>       = n delta_ab-1,
       <V_a,T(V_b)>    = (n/d) delta_ab-1.               (5.1)

The Gram matrix is nI_d-J_d, positive definite when d<n. Therefore the
orthogonal compression of T to span{V_1,...,V_d} has generalized
eigenvalues

       (n-d^2)/(d(n-d))     in the aggregate direction sum_a V_a=E,
       1/d                 with multiplicity d-1.       (5.2)

For example, if the coefficients t_a sum to zero, the two quadratic
forms in (5.1) are n sum t_a^2 and (n/d) sum t_a^2, respectively.
In the all-one coefficient direction they are d(n-d) and n-d^2.

This is a compression statement. The span of the V_a need not be
T-invariant, and E need not be an eigenmatrix of the full operator.

Thus keeping all pairwise matching orthogonality at this first level
forces only one negative aggregate direction. It does not force a growing
number of negative directions, a rank explosion, or a density decrement.
Higher-order information must involve how T sends this span into its
orthogonal complement, or constraints not captured by (5.1).

There is also a useful elementary positivity restriction: for every real
rank-one matrix X=f g^T,

       <X,T(X)>=(1/d) sum_c (f^T P_c g)^2 >= 0.          (5.3)

A negative witness therefore cannot have rank one. No bound on the rank
or useful factorization of a negative witness is proved here.

6. ORDINARY SINGULAR VALUES DESCRIBE ONLY THE STRIPE SECTORS

The matrix space splits orthogonally into constants, row/column stripes,
and W. On mean-zero stripes u1^T and 1v^T, T exchanges them through C/d
and C^T/d. Consequently the stripe eigenvalues are

                   +/- sigma_i(C)/d,    i=2,...,n,

where the Perron singular value d has been removed. These ordinary
singular-value modes do not determine the restriction of T to W.

A concrete obstruction to a purely singular-value inference is available
without the B assumption. Let G=S_3 x Z_t and take all n=6t regular
permutation matrices P_g. They are disjoint and sum to C=J, so the
nonprincipal singular values of C are all zero. Nevertheless T|W has
an eigenvalue -1/2.

Here is the algebra. The real standard two-dimensional representation rho
of S_3 occurs twice in its regular representation. On the corresponding
four-dimensional subspace U, the regular matrices have the form
rho(g) tensor I_2. This subspace is orthogonal to the constant vector;
use the constant mode on the Z_t factor. Let X act on U as

              I_2 tensor [[0,1],[-1,0]],

and vanish on its orthogonal complement. Then X^T=-X, X belongs to W,
and X commutes with every regular matrix on U. In S_3, the identity and
the three reflections have square I_2. The squares of the two rotations
sum to -I_2. Hence

              average_g rho(g)^2=(1/2)I_2,
              T(X)=-(1/2)X.

The occurrence of rho twice can alternatively be checked using its four
matrix coefficient functions on S_3: they are orthogonal, have mean zero,
and split into two copies under left translation.

These complete bipartite colorings ARE NOT B-colorings. Therefore this
example does not disprove a theorem using B plus ordinary quasirandomness.
It proves that ordinary singular-value information by itself cannot bound
the negative spectrum needed in (3.1).

7. ABELIAN TRANSLATIONS RECOVER THE FAMILIAR FOURIER OBSTRUCTION

For an abelian group G of size n and a translation set S of size d, take
P_s(i,j)=1 exactly when j=i+s. Let mu be uniform on S. On character
matrices f_{alpha,beta}(i,j)=chi_alpha(i)chi_beta(j),

       T(f_{alpha,beta})=mu_hat(beta-alpha) f_{beta,alpha}.

Equal indices give eigenvalue +1. Unequal indices give a two-dimensional
block with eigenvalues +/-|mu_hat(beta-alpha)|. The subspace W retains
exactly the character matrices with both indices nontrivial. If n>=3,
each nonzero character difference can be realized with both indices
nontrivial. Therefore

       theta=max_{gamma != 0}|mu_hat(gamma)|=sigma_2(C)/d.

In this special setting (3.3) is a large Fourier-coefficient obstruction,
consistent with standard density-increment reasoning for progression-free
sets. This is not claimed as a new additive-combinatorial theorem. The
step missing for general permutation colorings is a useful structure
theorem for their negative pair-space modes.

8. EXACT HIGHER-WORD FORMULAS AND WHY AN ODD MOMENT DOES NOT CLOSE THE GAP

Use Tr for the trace of a superoperator on the n^2-dimensional matrix
space, and tr for the ordinary n-dimensional matrix trace. Then

                 Tr(T)=n,        Tr(T^2)=n^2/d.          (8.1)

The first formula counts n fixed coordinates for every T_c. For the
second, T_a T_b(X)=P_a P_b^T X P_b^T P_a. The trace of this operator is
tr(P_a P_b^T)tr(P_b^T P_a), which is n^2 for a=b and zero otherwise.

After removing the constant and stripe sectors,

       Tr(T|W)=n-1,
       Tr((T|W)^2)=(n^2-2n)/d+1.                       (8.2)

Indeed the stripe square trace is
2 sum_{i>=2} sigma_i(C)^2/d^2=2(n/d-1).

For three colors, direct composition gives

  Tr(T_a T_b T_c)
       =tr(P_a P_b^T P_c P_a^T P_b P_c^T).              (8.3)

This is a nonnegative fixed-point count. Whenever at least two indices
coincide, the trace equals n. Thus

                 Tr(T^3)>=n(3/d-2/d^2).                (8.4)

The all-distinct terms encode six-step color words, including colored
hexagons allowed by the B condition. Their values are not supplied by
the four-cycle exclusion. For instance, commuting translations make every
word in (8.3) the identity, so Tr(T^3)=n. This remains compatible with B
when the translation set is suitable.

Therefore the third moment introduces genuinely further multicolor data,
but neither its nonnegativity nor the contribution from repeated indices
gives a proof that the negative eigenvalue in (3.3) vanishes. Higher words
are a possible place for additional structure, not an established new
asymptotic bound.

9. WHAT IS AND IS NOT ESTABLISHED

Established by the displayed hand proofs: the exact operator identities,
the quantitative conditional criterion (3.2), the compression (5.2), the
singular-value separation example, and the moment identities (8.1)-(8.4).
The first compression was independently audited. No finite graph search
was used, and none of these assertions is labeled Lean certified.

Not established: a universal estimate theta=o(1), a general density
increment from a negative pair eigenmatrix, a general passage to a small
group representation, or a solution of problem #810. The operator is a
concrete way to locate the missing multicolor information, not evidence
that that information has already been controlled.
