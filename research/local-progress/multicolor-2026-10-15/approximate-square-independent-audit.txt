INDEPENDENT AUDIT: A SQUARE IN A DENSE APPROXIMATE-GROUP REGION
2026-10-15. Complete hand reduction using stated established theorems.
Not Lean checked; no originality claim.

The qualitative proof below does not require a proof sketch from a recent
preprint, a quantitative approximate-group theorem, or a circular choice
of the DHJ dimension.

1. AN ENTIRE FIXED-DIMENSION COMMUTING CUBE INSIDE A^4

Claim. Fix K and r. For every sufficiently large finite centered
K-approximate group A, in any ambient group, there are pairwise commuting
w_1,...,w_r such that every subset product belongs to A^4 and no nonempty
subset product is the identity.

Use Breuillard-Green-Tao, The Structure of Approximate Groups, Theorem 2.10,
and Definitions 2.6 and 2.8:
https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~emmanuel.breuillard/structure.pdf
The source was checked directly, printed pages 12-13.

It gives P subset A^4 with |P|>=c_K|A|, where P is the full preimage of a
nilprogression Q in a quotient G_0/H, with H finite. The rank R and the
normal-form constant C are bounded in terms of K. The defining normal form
provides both a volume bound and local distinctness of ordered powers.

Choose a threshold H_0(r) so that every finite group of order at least
H_0(r) has an abelian subgroup of order greater than 2^(r-1). Such a
threshold follows from the standard large-abelian-subgroup theorem used
in group-representation-vanishing-density.txt.

If |H|>=H_0(r), choose r weights inside such an abelian subgroup of H,
greedily excluding the inverses of the preceding subset products at each
step. At step i there are at most 2^(i-1) exclusions. The weights commute,
no nonempty subset product is the identity, and the entire cube stays in
H subset P subset A^4.

Otherwise |H|<H_0(r). Write the side lengths of Q as L_1,...,L_R. The
volume bound says

                 |Q| <= C product_i(2 floor(L_i)+1).

If all L_i/C<2^r, the right side is bounded by a constant depending only
on K,r. Then |P|=|H||Q| is also so bounded, contrary to |P|>=c_K|A| for
large enough |A|. Therefore some L_i/C>=2^r.

Choose any lift g of that generator in G_0. Local properness says the
cosets g^j H for 0<=j<=2^r-1 are pairwise distinct. In particular none
of g^j, 1<=j<=2^r-1, is the identity. All these powers belong to P, since
their images belong to Q and P is the FULL preimage of Q. Take

                    w_i=g^(2^(i-1)),    1<=i<=r.

Their subset products are precisely powers g^j with 0<=j<=2^r-1. This
gives the required cube. The two cases give a uniform threshold in K,r;
the argument is not merely a subsequence dichotomy. QED.

2. DENSE LOCAL SQUARE LEMMA

Fix K,s and epsilon>0, where s is a positive integer. If A is a sufficiently
large centered K-approximate group and

           F subset A^s x A^s,       |F|>=epsilon |A|^2,

then F contains

                 (x,y), (xt,y), (x,yt), (xt,yt)

for some t!=1. The statement is uniform over the ambient group.

Proof. Choose

          delta=epsilon/K^(2(s+4)),
          r=DHJ(4,delta),

reducing epsilon if necessary to ensure delta<=1. The elementary power
bound |A^j|<=K^(j-1)|A| makes this a conservative density choice.

Choose the commuting r-cube from Section 1. For a word in
{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)}^r, write its letters (a_i,b_i) and define

       u(word)=product_i w_i^(a_i),
       v(word)=product_i w_i^(b_i).

Both products belong to A^4. Average over p,q in A^(s+4), mapping the word
to (p u(word), q v(word)). For each fixed word, every target (x,y) in F
has exactly one origin

                 p=x u(word)^(-1), q=y v(word)^(-1),

and these origins lie in A^(s+4), because A is symmetric. Thus the average
proportion of words mapped into F is exactly

                  |F|/|A^(s+4)|^2 >= delta.

Some p,q attain at least this density. DHJ gives a line with a nonempty
variable set I. The commuting weights let us factor its four images as

                   (x t^a, y t^b),   (a,b) in {0,1}^2,

where t=product_{i in I}w_i is nonidentity. Hence all four corners are
distinct. QED.

There is no quantifier cycle: K,s,epsilon determine delta and r first;
Section 1 then supplies the cube for sufficiently large |A|. Increasing
r does not enlarge A^4, so the averaging density does not deteriorate
with r. This is the essential point of placing the ENTIRE cube in A^4.

3. APPLICATION TO PARTIAL GROUP TABLES

Under the convention color(x,y)=y x^(-1), opposite corners have equal
color:

                 (yt)(xt)^(-1)=y x^(-1).

Therefore a partial group-table graph with every C4 rainbow cannot have
positive normalized density inside A^s x A^s for a bounded-K approximate
group A of unbounded size. Equivalently, for fixed K,s, its number of
entries there is o(|A|^2), uniformly in the ambient group.

This is a local qualitative statement. It gives no explicit decay rate
and does not assert that arbitrary B graphs lie in such a region.

4. OPTIONAL SIMPLE LIFT AFTER A PALETTE COSET HAS BEEN CHOSEN

The following extraction verifies the action degeneracies without using
entire stabilizer fibers. Suppose an original bipartite B graph has N
vertices, at least epsilon N^2 edges, and injectively labeled permutation
colors all lying in a centered approximate group A with |A|=O(N). Assume
each edge x->y of color b satisfies y=b(x), after any fixed relabeling of
the right side.

Let the bipartition be X,Y. Averaging

           sum_{x in X} sum_{y in N(x)} deg(y)
                    = sum_{y in Y} deg(y)^2

shows that some x_0 has a neighborhood Y_0 carrying Omega(N^2) incident
edges. Indeed the average is at least m^2/(|X||Y|)>=4epsilon^2 N^2.
For each y in Y_0 choose the color a_y in A of the edge x_0->y.

Every left vertex x with an edge x->y in this retained graph can be written
as x=b^(-1)a_y(x_0), hence belongs to A^2(x_0). Choose ONE representative
g_x in A^2 with g_x(x_0)=x, for every such left vertex. Lift each retained
edge of color b to

                         (g_x,b g_x).

The lift lies in A^2 x A^3, has the same number of edges, and has group-table
color b. The row representatives are distinct and their projection to
original left vertices is injective. If a lifted C4 has equal diagonal
color b, its distinct rows project to distinct x_1,x_2, while its column
projections are b(x_1),b(x_2), also distinct. It therefore projects to a
genuine forbidden C4 in the original graph. So the lift remains B.

If |A| tends to infinity, Section 3 contradicts its Omega(N^2) edges and
|A|=O(N). If |A| stays bounded, its containment in A^3 x A^3 already
contradicts that many edges. Thus the extraction is sufficient once a
bounded-complexity palette cover by an approximate group has been supplied.
That cover is an additional step, not an assumption proved by density.

5. AUDIT OF THE COMPLETED PARTIAL-MATCHING THEOREM

Independently read partial-small-difference-theorem.txt, including its
stronger scale-free Section 9. The completed proof passes. In particular:

* Removing small original colors before merging duplicate permutation
  labels ensures that the loss is a fixed fraction. In the scale-free
  statement, m>=epsilon qn leaves at least gamma qn edges with distinct
  labels, gamma=epsilon^2/4, and at least gamma q distinct permutations.
* Tao's covering parameter is then fixed. Selecting a translate by its
  edge weight, rather than its color count, does preserve enough edges.
* The note's basepoint lift is an alternative to Section 4 above and uses
  fewer powers of A. For each z, it takes edges (u^(-1),v u^(-1)), with
  u,v in the selected palette U. Distinct pairs (u,v) give distinct
  lifted edges, and sum_z |F_z|=|U|m' exactly.
* The projection of a forbidden lifted C4 either has two different row
  labels, yielding a genuine original C4, or collapses all four edges to
  the same original simple edge. In the second case the unique color of
  that original edge forces the lifted rows to coincide, a contradiction.
  Thus no freeness or injectivity of the group action has been smuggled in.
* In the scale-free version, |U|>=gamma q/L, |A|<=Bq and
  |F_z|>=gamma^2 q^2/L^2 give both |A|->infinity and fixed normalized
  density. No comparison between q and n is needed.

Hence the scale-free conclusion is valid: for fixed K, if q->infinity
and the completion palette has |SS^(-1)|<=Kq, then m=o(qn), where n is
the action-set size. This remains a conditional result because bounded
global palette doubling has not been established for arbitrary B graphs.

6. AUDIT OF THE MULTIPLICATIVE-ENERGY STRENGTHENING

Independently read multiplicative-energy-obstruction.txt and checked
Tao's Theorem 5.4(i) -> (iv) directly in the primary PDF, printed pp17-18:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0601431

The application to (S,S^(-1)) is exact: its product energy is the number
of quadruples ab^(-1)=ce^(-1). Positive normalized energy supplies a
bounded-complexity approximate group A of size at most Bd and one left
translate meeting S in at least alpha d elements. There is no assumption
that the two subsets in a two-set BSG conclusion coincide.

When each original color has at least eta n edges, the selected palette
U still has m'>=eta n|U| edges. The basepoint average gives
|F_z|>=eta|U|^2>=(eta alpha^2/B^2)|A|^2 for some z. This contradicts the
local square lemma as d->infinity. Thus E(S)=o(d^3), uniformly for fixed
eta, is proved by the stated dependencies.

The collision identity has the correct multiplication order:

  (ab^(-1))(x)=(ce^(-1))(x)
       iff (e c^(-1) a b^(-1))(x)=x.

Consequently sum_x E_x=sum_{a,b,c,e}|Fix(e c^(-1) a b^(-1))|. Cauchy-
Schwarz gives E_x>=d^4/n at every x, whereas the globally identical
products contribute nE(S). If d=Theta(n), this is o(d^4). The required
remaining collision mass therefore comes from nonidentical products
that agree at points. This last observation identifies a gap in a
potential contradiction; it does not fill that gap.

All these checks are mathematical audits, not kernel verification. The
structural hypotheses, dependence on deep established theorems, and lack
of a universal o(N^2) edge bound must remain explicit in any summary.
