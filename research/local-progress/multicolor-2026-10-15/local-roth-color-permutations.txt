INPUTS AND QUANTIFIERS

Theorem 1.10: in a solvable group, a finite set A such that
  x,xy,xy² in A ==> y=1
satisfies |AA^-1|/|A| >= exp(c(log|A|)^(1/1305)), with absolute c>0.
The convention includes xy²=x. See also its footnote 5.

For arbitrary ambient groups, Remark 12.3 (printed p54) supplies the fixed-K
abelian-structure input through BGT, with an ineffective threshold F(K).
Here is the precise qualitative reduction. Set K=|AA^-1|/|A|.

Use the original Tao Proposition 4.5, Product set estimates for
non-commutative groups, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0601431, pp10–11:
there is a symmetric identity-containing B with |B|>=|A|/(2K) and
  |AB^m A^-1| <= 2^m K^(2m+1)|A|.                         (1)
The cardinality on the right is |A| in Tao's original statement. His
popular-difference construction also gives B subset A^-1 A and |B|<=2K|A|.
Thus |B^3|<=16K^8|B|. For fixed K and |A|->infinity, Remark 12.3 supplies
a commuting set T subset B^4 whose size tends to infinity. Let H=<T> and
enlarge T to B^4 intersect H, which is still commuting.

Write Q=TT^-1. Count pairs (a,q) by g=qa^-1:
  sum_{g in QA^-1} |gA intersect Q| = |A| |Q|.
Since |QA^-1|=|AQ|<=|AB^8|<=2^8 K^17 |A|, there is g such that
  A0=gA intersect Q subset H,
  |A0| >= 2^-8 K^-17 |Q| >= 2^-8 K^-17 |T|.              (2)

To bound its difference set without suppressing an intersection estimate,
Ruzsa covering gives B^16 subset ZB^2 with
  |Z| <= |B^17|/|B| <= 2^18 K^36,
using (1) and |A|<=2K|B|. Any nonempty zB^2 intersect H has at most |T|
elements: fix h0 there and map h to h0^-1 h in B^4 intersect H=T. Hence
  |A0 A0^-1| <= |B^16 intersect H|
             <= 2^18 K^36 |T| <= 2^26 K^53 |A0|.        (3)

Strong progression-freeness survives left translation and taking subsets.
Abelian local Roth (Schildkraut Lemma 9.2) applied to A0 contradicts (2),
its unbounded size, and (3). Therefore, for each K there is a uniform bound
on the size of strong progression-free A with |AA^-1|<=K|A|, regardless of
ambient group. Equivalently |AA^-1|/|A|->infinity uniformly. This uses
Remark 12.3's proof sketch as an input; no explicit universal rate follows.

SHARPENING THE SOLVABLE SPECIALIZATION OF THE DISPLAYED PROOF

For solvable G, Theorem 1.2 applied to B gives directly
  log|T| >= c (log|B|)^(1/6) / log(2K),
after absorbing the constant tripling exponent into c. This is stronger
than the common exponent used in the statement of Lemma 9.1. Set L=log(2K).
Equations (2)–(3), followed by abelian local Roth, give
  c(log|B|)^(1/6)/L - C L <= log|A0| <= C L^144.
Consequently log|B| <= C L^870 and log|A|<=log|B|+L<=C' L^870.
After adjusting absolute constants for small sets, this yields
  |AA^-1| >= |A| exp(c'(log|A|)^(1/870))
asymptotically in solvable groups. The exponent 1/1305 is what Theorem 1.10
explicitly states; 1/870 is an immediate specialization of its proof using
Theorem 1.2, not a separately claimed literature theorem or new method.
In abelian groups Lemma 9.2 itself gives exponent 1/144 directly.

APPLICATION: THE MATCHING PERMUTATIONS ARE STRONGLY PROGRESSION-FREE

Let G be a bipartite graph with n vertices in each part. Every one of its d
color classes is a perfect matching. Every C4 is rainbow. Identify both parts
with [n], and let S={p_c} subset Sym(n) be their n-to-n matching bijections.

Suppose a,b,c in S satisfy a b^-1 a=c with a!=b. For any x, let
  x'=b^-1 a(x), y=a(x), y'=a(x').
Properness gives x'!=x; bijectivity of a gives y'!=y. The four edges are
  (x,y):a, (x',y):b, (x',y'):a, (x,y'):c.
They form a nonrainbow C4, a contradiction. Therefore a b^-1 a=c implies
a=b=c. This is exactly strong translational progression-freeness, since
  (x,xt,xt²) = (b,a,a b^-1 a), with t=b^-1 a.
If c=b, the same contradiction is a bichromatic C4; this case must not be
excluded from the definition. Relabeling either bipartition transforms S
to uSv, preserving both the equation and |SS^-1|.

Consequently, for any sequence of these colorings with d->infinity,
  D/d -> infinity, where D=|SS^-1|.

CONDITIONAL VANISHING DENSITY

Assume D<=K0 n for a fixed K0. If d>=epsilon n on an infinite subsequence,
then D/d<=K0/epsilon while d->infinity, contradicting the previous result.
Thus d=o(n), so |E(G)|=nd=o(n²). Bounded d also gives this conclusion.

For the coordinate-invariant quantitative hypothesis, let
  Gamma0=<SS^-1>.
If Gamma0 is solvable, fix s0 in S and use A=S s0^-1 subset Gamma0.
Right translation preserves strong progression-freeness and AA^-1=SS^-1.
The proof specialization therefore gives
  D >= d exp(c(log d)^(1/870)).
When d>=sqrt(n), log d >= (log n)/2, and hence
  d <= K0 n exp(-c 2^(-1/870) (log n)^(1/870)).
When d<sqrt(n), this estimate holds after adjusting constants for large n.
Thus, with some absolute c'>0,
  d = O_K0(n exp(-c' (log n)^(1/870))),
  |E(G)| = O_K0(n² exp(-c' (log n)^(1/870))).
If Gamma0 is abelian, replace 1/870 by 1/144. Under relabeling S->uSv,
Gamma0 is merely conjugated by u, so these hypotheses do not depend on
the arbitrary identification of the bipartition classes.

LIMITATION

UPDATE: The qualitative perfect-color conclusion below is now subsumed by
the stronger partial-small-difference-theorem.txt. That proof uses the full
BGT Theorem 2.10 directly, so it does not depend on Remark 12.3's sketch.
The route above is retained for its explicit solvable/abelian estimates
and for an accurate record of what follows from the local Roth argument.

The hypothesis D=O(n) is additional. The trivial bound is D<=d². The B
condition alone has not been shown to give a useful upper bound on D.
Solvability is needed for the stated explicit rate, not for the attributed
qualitative fixed-K consequence. Arbitrary nonperfect color classes do not
define these total permutations without a separate completion argument.
The result is a corollary of existing local Roth/approximate-group work,
not a proof of Erdős #810 and not a claim of a new published bound.
