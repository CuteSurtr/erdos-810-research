1. COMMON-MASK SPECTRAL INTERMEDIATE: PASS

Read and independently checked:
  pass3-common-mask-spectral-intermediate.txt

For sigma_a(x)=ax+a^2 and A subset F_p* of size d, the quotient
sigma_a sigma_b^-1 has slope rho=a/b and intercept
rho(rho-1)b^2. Every nonidentity quotient has at most two representing
pairs, while the identity has d. Thus E(S)<=3d^2-2d.

The zero-sum permutation character of Aff(F_p) is p-1 at identity,
-1 at a nonidentity translation, and zero elsewhere. In the fourth
moment tr((TT*)^2), all group-word coefficients are nonnegative.
Discarding the negative translation terms therefore proves

 ||sum_{a in A} P_sigma_a - (d/p)J||_op
       <=[(p-1)(3d^2-2d)]^(1/4).

The operator convention may reverse the order of compositions; this
does not change the identity-word count E(S) or the estimate.

For a common row mask X, properness is exactly
X intersect -(A restricted-sum A)=empty. On those rows the operator
matrix is the actual binary graph adjacency G. Its cut discrepancy,
and the corresponding bound on every color cross matrix
K_a(x,z)=G(x,sigma_a(z)), follow by row restriction and column
permutation. These claims count actual selected edges.

2. WHY THIS DOES NOT YET CONFLICT WITH B

B imposes K_a(x,z)K_a(z,x)=0 for x!=z, while K_a(x,x)=1.
These conditions are compatible with an individual matrix being
spectrally close to a positive-density constant matrix.

The independent oriented-edge random model in the companion note
proves that assertion. For fixed real unit vectors u,v, the centered
bilinear form is a sum over independent unordered pairs. The sum
of squared ranges is bounded by

 sum_{i<j} (|u_i v_j|+|u_j v_i|)^2
       <=2 sum_{i!=j} u_i^2 v_j^2 <=2.

Hoeffding's inequality and a constant-resolution net of both unit
spheres then give an O(sqrt(k)) operator norm bound for some k-by-k
outcomes. This is even smaller than the O(p^(3/4)) allowance in the
dense common-mask spectral estimate.

This is only a counterexample to an inference from the INDIVIDUAL
cross-matrix conditions. The matrices from a graph must all arise
from the same G and the prescribed sigma_a. No simultaneous graph
realization, or dense B counterexample, is constructed by the model.

The companion note's pair-action zero-frequency expression also
checks: writing r=z-x and averaging over the common translation
coordinate gives

 (1/p) sum_{a in A,r!=0,x,y} G(x,y)G(x+r,y-ar).

The line-occupancy Cauchy--Schwarz lower bound is valid. However the
crossed test is a swapped tensor of G, and its nuclear norm may be
as large as ||G||_*^2. The remaining Fourier modes are not controlled
at the p^3 counting scale by the stated operator estimate alone.

3. GENERIC LOW-COMPLEXITY SUPPORT CRITERIA

These elementary criteria hold for every properly B-colored
bipartite graph, and are recorded as boundaries rather than new
quadratic-palette progress.

If a color matching has k edges, restrict the adjacency G to its
matched rows and columns in matching order. The resulting matrix M
has diagonal ones and M_ij M_ji=0 for i!=j. Hence

                 M Hadamard-product M^T = I_k.

The Hadamard-product rank bound gives
k<=rank(M)^2<=rank(G)^2. With q colors and m edges,

                         m<=q*rank(G)^2.

Thus q=O(p) and rank(G)=o(sqrt(p)) force m=o(p^2). This is the
standard fooling-set rank mechanism, not a special property of
affine colors. Low rank of the separate color-by-row mask matrix
does not imply low rank of G: even one full permutation color has
mask rank one but adjacency rank p.

There is a similar biclique-cover criterion. Every complete
bipartite subgraph has at most one edge of each color: two edges
of a color are disjoint by properness, and their crossed edges
would create a repeated-color C4. Thus each biclique contains at
most q edges. A cover of G by t bicliques gives m<=qt. The same
applies to a nonnegative rank-t factorization of its binary
adjacency, because each factor's positive support is a biclique.

None of these complexity bounds has been derived for a general
quadratic common-mask graph in this pass.

4. PRECISE UNPROVED TARGET

For fixed delta>0, take A subset F_p*, X subset F_p with
|A|,|X|>=delta*p and X avoiding -(A restricted-sum A). Must there
exist a,b,c in A and distinct x,z in X such that

       b^2+bx=a^2+az,     c^2+cz=a^2+ax ?

An affirmative answer would rule out dense B graphs even when the
color-by-row mask has rank one, and would consequently handle a
bounded exact union of rectangular masks by restricting to one
dense rectangle. It is not proved here.

The new near-complete-color theorem derived from the cited prime
incidence input concerns average defect o(p). It does not cover a
common mask of size delta*p for a fixed delta<1. The two statements
must not be conflated.
