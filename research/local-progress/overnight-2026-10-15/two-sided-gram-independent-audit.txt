INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE TWO-SIDED INTRINSIC GRAM RELAXATION
15 October 2026. Hand proof; no graph search or Lean certificate.

Scope: Section 6A of formal-audit-energy-and-partial-kernel.txt.
Verdict: PASS. The dense abstract model survives both PSD matricizations
and their exact reshuffling identity. This is a relaxation obstruction,
not a graph construction or a counterexample to Erdos #810.

1. TRACE IDENTITY, WITH ALL INDEX ORDERS CHECKED

For real X-by-Y color matching matrices B_a, put

  Q^Y_ab = B_a^T B_b,       Q^X_ac = B_a B_c^T.

The Frobenius Gram entry on Y is

  G^Y_(ab),(ce) = tr(B_b^T B_a B_c^T B_e).

Transpose the product, then cyclically rotate the factors:

  tr(B_e^T B_c B_a^T B_b)
    = tr(B_c B_a^T B_b B_e^T)
    = G^X_(ac),(be).

The dimensions agree also when |X| differs from |Y|. Neither the B
condition nor perfect matchings are needed for this identity. Each
matricization is PSD because it is separately an actual Frobenius Gram
matrix.

2. THE EXPLICIT TENSOR OF THE ABSTRACT MODEL

Let q be a positive multiple of 4, n=4q-3, k=q/4. For distinct colors
consider a uniformly chosen oriented k-matching M on the q-color set.
The off-diagonal-pair Gram model is

  G_* = n(n-1) E[v_M v_M^T].

For a specified ordered arc, its probability of membership is
k/[q(q-1)]. For two specified vertex-disjoint ordered arcs, their joint
probability is k(k-1)/[q(q-1)(q-2)(q-3)]. Consequently the complete
four-index tensor, after adjoining the diagonal-pair nJ_q block, is

  T_abce = n,       if (a=b and c=e) or (a=c and b=e);
           gamma,  if a,b,c,e are all distinct;
           0,      otherwise,

where

  gamma = n(n-1)k(k-1)/[q(q-1)(q-2)(q-3)]
        = n(q-4)/[4(q-2)(q-3)].

The all-equal case belongs to both n-cases but is assigned n once.
The other pairing, a=e and b=c with a!=b, has value zero. Every overlap
pattern not explicitly assigned n also has value zero.

Exchanging b and c interchanges the two n-cases. It preserves the
all-distinct case and the union of all remaining cases. Therefore

                   T_abce = T_acbe

entry by entry. The reshuffled matrix is literally the same matrix,
not merely another matrix with the same spectrum. Its PSD property
follows from the block decomposition nJ_q direct-sum G_*.

The rank bound is at most 1+q(q-1), which is below n^2. Simultaneously
reversing both ordered pairs also preserves these cases, as required
by Q_ab^T=Q_ba. This checks all claims made in Section 6A.

3. A FURTHER FIRST-MOMENT CHECK: THE ALL-ONES MATRIX

Perfect permutation matrices satisfy <Q_ab,J>=n as well as
<Q_ab,I>=0 for a!=b. It is natural to ask whether adjoining the fixed
all-ones matrix J to the abstract Gram model immediately excludes it.
It does not.

Let L=q(q-1), let G denote its off-diagonal-pair block, and let
W=J-I. The forced data are <Q_ab,W>=n and ||W||^2=n(n-1).
Adjoining W to G is PSD exactly when

                   G - [n/(n-1)] J_L >= 0.

Every row sum of G is nk: indeed every v_M has exactly k nonzero
entries, and G has diagonal n. Also L/(n-1)=k. Thus the rank-one
subtraction kills precisely the constant-vector eigenvalue nk and
leaves all orthogonal eigenvalues unchanged. They are nonnegative
because G is PSD. The augmented Gram is therefore PSD. The diagonal
pair vector I is orthogonal to this off-diagonal block and to W, and
has norm squared n, so it introduces no further problem.

This additional observation concerns only scalar Gram constraints. It
does not supply row-by-row permutation supports, common domains, or the
matrix identities Q_ab Q_bc=Q_ac. Those realization and multiplication
constraints remain essential and unresolved.
