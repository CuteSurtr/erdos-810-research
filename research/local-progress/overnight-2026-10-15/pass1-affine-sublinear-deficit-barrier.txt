SUBLINEAR AFFINE DEFICITS DO NOT FORCE A BOUNDED PENCIL COVER
Overnight pass 1, 15 October 2026.
Root-agent construction, independently audited by the finite-audit agent.
Exact proof, no finite search. This graph is NOT B. No novelty claim.

SUMMARY

There are properly colored complete bipartite graphs with affine color
completions on n points such that:

 * there are n-1 colors;
 * every color omits only sqrt(n)+1 points of its completed matching;
 * global multiplicative energy is o(n^3);
 * any cover of the palette by parallel or concurrent line pencils
   needs at least about sqrt(n) pencils.

Consequently the bounded-pencil structural lemma cannot be extended,
using properness alone, from a fixed deficit to every o(n) deficit.
The example also strengthens the warning that small global energy by
itself says little about the actual repeated-color rectangles.

1. CONSTRUCTION AND PROPERNESS

Let s be a prime power, let n=s^2, and let F=F_{s^2} with its subfield
F_s. For every a in F^* use the affine permutation

                        sigma_a(x)=a x+a^s.

Define

             H={-t^(s-1):t in F^*},       D=F\H.

The power map t->t^(s-1) on the cyclic group F^* has image size
(s^2-1)/(s-1)=s+1. Thus |H|=s+1 and |D|=n-s-1.

For fixed x, the map

                         L_x(a)=x a+a^s

is F_s-linear on the two-dimensional vector space F. It has a nonzero
kernel element iff x=-a^(s-1), equivalently x in H. Therefore for x in D
it is invertible and sends F^* bijectively onto F^*.

For each color a retain precisely the edges (x,sigma_a(x)) with x in D.
At every x in D, the n-1 colors produce exactly the n-1 distinct nonzero
right coordinates. The resulting graph is exactly

                         K_{n-s-1,n-1},

with its rows labeled by D and columns by F^*. It is properly colored:
at a row all outputs are distinct, and each color is an affine matching.
Every class has n-s-1 edges, omitting only s+1=o(n) of its n possible
matching edges. The edge count is (n-s-1)(n-1)=(1-o(1))n^2.

2. EXACT GLOBAL ENERGY

Write r=a/b for an ordered pair of nonzero parameters. Direct composition
gives

    sigma_a sigma_b^(-1)(x)=r x+(r^s-r)b^s.            (1)

If r in F_s^*, then r^s-r=0. For each of these s-1 slopes there is one
relative map, represented by all n-1 choices of b.

If r in F^*\F_s^*, then r^s-r!=0. There are n-s such slopes. For each,
the n-1 possible nonzero b give n-1 distinct intercepts, since the
Frobenius map b->b^s is a bijection of F^*.

Different relative slopes cannot give equal affine maps. Hence exactly

    |S S^(-1)|=(s-1)+(n-s)(n-1),
    E(S)=(s-1)(n-1)^2+(n-s)(n-1).                    (2)

Since n=s^2, E(S)=O(n^(5/2))=o(n^3), although the underlying graph has
(1-o(1))n^2 edges and its color supports are nearly complete.
This family does have global permutation progressions; it does not
replace the separate strong-progression-free quadratic example.

3. A LOWER BOUND ON THE NUMBER OF PENCILS

Slopes are distinct, so a parallel pencil contains at most one palette
line. A concurrent pencil through (x,y) contains exactly the nonzero
solutions a of L_x(a)=y.

If x outside H, L_x is invertible, so there is at most one solution.
If x in H, the F_s-linear map L_x is nonzero and singular; it has rank
one and kernel size s. To see that it is not identically zero, note that
the Frobenius map a->a^s is not scalar multiplication on F_{s^2}: at
a=1 the alleged scalar would be 1, while elements outside F_s are not
fixed by Frobenius. Thus each nonempty fiber has exactly s elements,
and restricting to nonzero a can only decrease the count.

Every concurrent pencil therefore contains at most s palette lines.
Any cover by these two kinds of pencils has at least

                         ceil((n-1)/s)

members. This tends to infinity.

4. WHY THIS IS NOT A B COLORING

For sufficiently large s, every color has at least two edges. Choose
two distinct retained edges of one color. Their rows differ and their
columns differ because that color is a matching. Both cross edges are
present because the graph is complete bipartite on D by F^*. Thus these
four positions form a C4 with a repeated color on its diagonal.

This explicit failure is the point of the example. It leaves open a
possible theorem that uses the B condition itself to handle o(n)
deficits or fixed-density affine supports. What it rules out is deriving
a bounded pencil cover from properness and sublinear deficits alone,
or deriving a density contradiction from small global energy alone.
