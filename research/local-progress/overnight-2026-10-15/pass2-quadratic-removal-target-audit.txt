AN EXACT FOUR-SLOT REMOVAL TARGET FOR QUADRATIC AFFINE SUPPORTS
15 October 2026. Independent audit: the reduction below is correct.
The proposed removal lemma is UNPROVED. It is a stronger sufficient
target, not a theorem established by this research or a solution of #810.

1. SUPPORT COORDINATES AND THE SYSTEM

Let p be an odd prime. Let R subset F_p* x F_p encode the actual
quadratic-affine edges as follows:

    (a,b) represents color a, row -(a+b), column -ab.

Indeed -ab=a[-(a+b)]+a^2. The first coordinate is required to be
nonzero, so every color is a partial affine permutation. Assume the
support is properly oriented: the map from selected pairs to graph
edges assigns a unique color to each edge. In particular, distinct
ordered pairs (a,b) and (b,a) cannot both be selected when both would
be valid color pairs.

Consider the four slots

                 (a,b), (a,c), (d,e), (f,g) in R

and the exact equations

                 a+b=d+e,       ac=de,
                 a+c=f+g,       ab=fg.                  (1.1)

They specify two diagonal edges of color a, and the two crossed
edges of colors d,f. Their rows are -(a+b), -(a+c), and their columns
are -ab,-ac. Therefore a solution with b!=c gives a genuine C4 with
a repeated color, contrary to B.

Conversely, every nonrainbow C4 in the proper quadratic coloring has
a repeated color on opposite edges and gives a solution after the
appropriate ordering. The system retains all actual support tests.

2. DEGENERACIES ARE EXACTLY b=c

The two rows coincide if and only if b=c. Because a!=0, the two
columns coincide if and only if b=c as well. Thus there is no other
vertex-degeneracy case to remove from (1.1).

When b=c, the crossed pair (d,e) satisfies

                     d+e=a+b,      de=ab.

Its entries are roots of (t-a)(t-b)=0, so it has at most two possible
orders. The same holds independently for (f,g). There are at most
p(p-1) choices of (a,b), hence at most 4p^2 degenerate solutions.

For a properly oriented common support R, the sharper count is
exactly |R|: the reversed ordered pair, if different and valid, would
assign another color to the same graph edge and cannot also lie in R.
Thus only the all-equal-slot solution remains for each pair in R.
The weaker 4p^2 upper bound is already sufficient below.

Consequently, if the actual graph is B, the TOTAL number of solutions
of (1.1), including degeneracies, is O(p^2)=o(p^3).

3. THE PRECISE UNPROVED REMOVAL STATEMENT

The following uniform four-partite statement would suffice:

  For every epsilon>0 there exist eta>0 and p_0 such that, for every
  prime p>=p_0 and every four sets

                 R_1,R_2,R_3,R_4 subset F_p* x F_p,

  if (1.1) has at most eta*p^3 solutions with its four ordered pairs
  in the four respective slots, one can delete at most epsilon*p^2
  pairs from EACH R_i so that no solutions of (1.1) remain.

All solutions, including the degenerate ones, must be counted and
removed in this statement. The p^3 scale is appropriate to this
parameterization: after a,b,c are fixed, each crossed pair has at
most two possible orders, so the unrestricted count is O(p^3).

This statement is not being invoked as a known polynomial-removal
theorem. Its uniformity and its applicability to this coupled system
are precisely the missing assertion.

4. WHY THAT STATEMENT WOULD FORCE QUADRATIC B SPARSITY

Suppose |R|>=delta*p^2 for a fixed delta>0 and the graph is B. Apply
the hypothetical lemma to four identical copies R_i=R, choosing
epsilon<delta/4. Section 2 gives at most 4p^2 solutions, which is
below eta*p^3 for every sufficiently large p.

Let D_i be the deleted pairs in slot i. For EVERY (a,b) in R there
is the diagonal solution

                      c=b,  d=f=a,  e=g=b.              (4.1)

If that pair survived in all four slots, this solution would remain.
Since all solutions were removed, every pair of R belongs to at
least one D_i. Thus

                   |R|<=sum_i |D_i|<=4epsilon*p^2,

contradicting |R|>=delta*p^2. No additional argument for discarding or
repairing degeneracies is needed; the diagonal solutions provide the
contradiction themselves.

5. SCOPE

The full four-partite removal statement is stronger than needed.
It would be enough to prove the corresponding deletion assertion
for four identical properly oriented supports R in the small-solution
regime just used. A direct supersaturation theorem for this special
support class could also bypass removal entirely.

The reduction is exact and elementary. The missing removal or
supersaturation theorem would be substantive new mathematical work.
Nothing here establishes it, and ordinary graph removal at the p^4
rectangle scale cannot be substituted for the stated p^3-scale input.

6. AN EXACT THREE-SLOT TRIANGLE PARAMETERIZATION, AND ITS MISSING TEST

Set U=a, V=a+b, W=ac. Since U!=0, these are valid coordinates:

  slot 1 = (U,V-U),
  slot 2 = (U,W/U),
  slot 3 = an ordered root pair with sum V and product W.

The first membership test depends on (U,V), the second on (U,W),
and the third on (V,W). Thus the first THREE tests can be represented
by the edges of an ordinary tripartite triangle. Proper orientation
ensures at most one selected ordered root pair for the third test.

However the fourth selected root pair must have

  sum U+W/U,        product U(V-U).

That test depends simultaneously on all three triangle parameters.
Counting triangles does not count the required four-support solutions,
and B allows such triangles when this final crossed position is absent.
This identifies the precise extra test an attempted triangle-removal
representation must implement; it is not removed by the change of
coordinates. No claim is made that another representation is impossible.
