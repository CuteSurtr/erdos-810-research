# Pass 6: oscillatory affine operators and common-potential masks

Primary-source check, 15 October 2026. This note separates published inputs from the operator estimate being proved and audited in this workspace. It does not certify priority.

## Exact target

For an odd prime \(p\), a nonzero \(s\in\mathbb F_p\), and arbitrary coefficients \(|w(a)|\leq1\), the target is

\[
C_{s,w}f(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
w(a)e_p\!\left(s(a^2x+a^3)\right)f(ax+a^2).
\]

The palette and multiplicative twists are covered simultaneously by
\(w(a)=1_A(a)\chi(a)\). The proposed estimate is
\(\|C_{s,w}\|_{2\to2}\ll p^{7/8}\), uniformly in \(w\) and \(s\neq0\).
The team has reduced the corresponding two-dimensional crossed operator to these twisted one-dimensional blocks. The independent algebraic audits are maintained separately; the present note audits literature and inputs.

The distinction \(s\neq0\) is essential: with \(s=0\) and \(w=1_A\), the constant function has eigenvalue \(|A|\).

## Verified classical input

Iwaniec and Kowalski, *Analytic Number Theory*, Proposition 11.5, printed p.275 (PDF page index 8), proves the square-root magnitude of a nontrivial Gauss sum. See the [author-hosted chapter](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/ik-ant-exp-sums.pdf). Completing the square gives

\[
\left|\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}e_p(\alpha a^2+\beta a)\right|
=\sqrt p\qquad(\alpha\neq0).
\]

When \(\alpha=0\) and \(\beta\neq0\), additive-character orthogonality gives zero. This is classical, elementary input, not a new exponential-sum theorem.

The exact consequence used in the current proof is worth recording independently. For \(B,D\neq0\), define

\[
Q_{B,D}H(v)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}e_p(Ba)H(v+Da^2).
\]

Its Fourier multiplier at frequency \(\xi\) is
\(\sum_a e_p(Ba+\xi Da^2)\). It is zero at \(\xi=0\) and has magnitude \(\sqrt p\) otherwise. Therefore
\(\|Q_{B,D}\|_{2\to2}=\sqrt p\). If the sum excludes \(a=0\), the safe bound is \(\sqrt p+1\). No regularity or boundedness assumption on \(H\) is needed.

## Closest inspected published frameworks

**Dong's finite-field Hörmander theorem.** Dong Dong, “A Hörmander type theorem in finite fields,” *Finite Fields and Their Applications* 59 (2019), 22–31, [publisher full text and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ffa.2019.05.002), Theorem 1.1. For a kernel bounded by \(Cp^{-a}\), its type-I condition requires off-diagonal Gram sums bounded by \(C_1p^{-b}\), apart from a bounded number of exceptional partners per column. It yields operator decay with exponent \(\min(a,b/2)-1/2\).

This is relevant methodology, but it does not verify those cancellation hypotheses for the present arbitrary palette. Our normalized kernel is

\[
K(x,y)=p^{-1}\!\!\sum_{\substack{a\neq0\\y=ax+a^2}}
w(a)e_p(say).
\]

The size bound \(|K|\leq2/p\) is immediate. Cancellation with unrestricted \(w\) is not immediate from this size bound or from complete Weil sums. The present Cauchy–Schwarz/Gauss calculation supplies additional work. Accordingly, the current estimate is not being attributed as a direct corollary of Dong's theorem.

**Polynomial Roth averages.** Dong–Li–Sawin's [original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00080) studies averages of the form
\(\mathbb E_y f_1(x+P_1(y))f_2(x+P_2(y))\); Dong's 2019 paper, Theorem 3.1, recalls the \(p^{-1/8}\) estimate for \(P_1(y)=y,P_2(y)=y^2\).
The shared exponent \(1/8\) does not establish an equivalence of operators. The current forms \((a,x,ax+a^2)\), with the extra phase \(e_p(sa(ax+a^2))\), have not been reduced to that published additive-translation average.

**Recent paraboloid major-arc lemma.** Jianghao Zhang, [“Oscillatory Integral Operators Along the Paraboloid and Applications,” arXiv:2610.14841v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14841v1), Lemma 7, treats
\[
f\longmapsto\mathbb E_{y_1,y_2}
f\bigl(x-(y_1,y_2,y_1^2+y_2^2)\bigr)
e_p\bigl(\tau_x(y_1^3+y_2^3)\bigr),
\quad \tau_x\neq0.
\]
Its norm has a power saving. Theorem 5 concerns the corresponding three-slot pattern in \(\mathbb F_p^4\). The paper uses Fourier transform, \(TT^*\), exceptional sets, and polynomial exponential sums. This is a recent preprint, and I checked the stated lemma and its proof mechanism, not every result in the paper. Its complete two-parameter additive averaging does not match arbitrary weighted affine dilations here. No valid reduction was found.

**Wave-packet terminology.** A. Ghaani Farashahi, [“Wave Packet Transforms over Finite Fields,” *Electronic Journal of Linear Algebra* 30 (2015), 507–529](https://emis.de/ft/34296), develops the representation generated by dilation, translation, and modulation. Our summands belong to that familiar operator family. Its frame and reconstruction results do not, by themselves, give cancellation for this particular polynomially indexed sum with arbitrary coefficients.

## Why polynomial Szemerédi is not an automatic substitute

Peluse's [“On the polynomial Szemerédi theorem in finite fields”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02200), *Duke Mathematical Journal* 168 (2019), 749–774, applies to shared-base progressions
\(x,x+P_1(y),\ldots,x+P_m(y)\), where the fixed integer polynomials are linearly independent and have zero constant terms, in sufficiently large characteristic. Those precise hypotheses are not a verified encoding of the support condition

\[
D_a=\{x:a^2x+a^3\in H\}.
\]

For example, writing a repeated-color rectangle with other colors \(b=ra,c=ta\), and rows \(x=aX,z=aZ\), gives
\[
rX+r^2=Z+1,\qquad tZ+t^2=X+1.
\]
For \(rt\neq1\),
\[
X=\frac{1+t-t^2-tr^2}{rt-1},
\qquad Z=rX+r^2-1.
\]
Its support tests are membership in \(H\) of
\[
a^3(X+1),\quad a^3(Z+1),\quad
ra^3(Z+1),\quad ta^3(X+1),
\]
alongside \(a,ra,ta\in A\). These are correlated rational/multiplicative tests, not a demonstrated shared-base polynomial progression. This calculation identifies a missing reduction; it does not prove that no such reduction can exist.

There is also a simple warning against forcing a collision from properness alone. For \(p\equiv1\pmod3\), take \(A\) to be the cube subgroup and \(H\) disjoint from \(-A\), with positive density. The intersection of colors \(a,b\in A\) is \((x,y)=(-a-b,-ab)\), whose potential values are \(-a^2b,-ab^2\in-A\); those incidences are omitted. Thus these common-potential supports can form a proper graph of positive density. This example is not asserted to have all \(C_4\)'s rainbow.

## Scoped novelty verdict

The Gauss estimate, Cauchy–Schwarz method, Fourier block decomposition, and wave-packet setting are established tools. None of the inspected primary statements explicitly supplies the uniform \(C_{s,w}\) estimate above, or the resulting rainbow-\(C_4\) common-potential support application. That is a bounded literature finding, not proof of originality.

The appropriate description is: an elementary estimate derived here from classical Gauss sums, with independent local audits; its exact prior appearance and the priority of any support consequence remain unestablished. A full theorem about arbitrary \(H\) must also control the Fourier summation over support masks. A bound for each nonzero frequency alone does not automatically control that sum without a justified norm or orthogonality estimate.
