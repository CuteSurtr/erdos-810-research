BOUNDED VC SUPPORTS: INDEPENDENT LIMIT-AND-BRACKETING AUDIT
15 October 2026, pass 5. Audit verdict: PASS.

The following qualitative deduction is sound. Let p range over odd primes,
A subset F_p*, beta:A->F_p arbitrary, and D_a subset F_p be the actual
color-a row domains for a simple proper B coloring by x->ax+beta(a).
If the set family {D_a:a in A} has VC dimension at most a FIXED v, then

                                 m=o(p^2).

There is still one color per slope. This is a restricted-class theorem,
not a solution of arbitrary affine supports or unrestricted Erdos #810.
No explicit rate, Lean certificate or historical-priority claim is made.
The previous failure of a naive finite metric cover does not apply to
this proof: finite bracketing is used only in a fixed limit measure space,
before extracting one positive rectangle.

1. REDUCTION TO A POSITIVE-DENSITY SUPPORT LIMIT

If m>=delta p^2 along a counterexample sequence, then d=|A|>=delta p.
Form the bipartite support graph whose parts are the d colors and p rows,
with incidence a~x iff x in D_a. Its density m/(dp) is at least delta.
After a subsequence and the standard bipartite graphon compactness and
alignment procedure, its kernels W_n converge in cut norm to a kernel W
on probability spaces. The edge density of W is positive.

The induced shattering bigraph with v+1 column vertices and 2^(v+1) row
vertices realizing all binary patterns has induced density zero in every
finite support graph, and hence in W. Noninjective induced samples do
not cause a problem: different required patterns force the relevant
vertices to be distinct, and ordinary collision errors vanish in any
case because both finite parts tend to infinity.

2. RANDOM-FREENESS CAN BE CHECKED DIRECTLY

Suppose W is fractional on a positive-measure set. Some region
epsilon<W<1-epsilon then has positive measure. Its indicator has positive
density of the finite complete bipartite graph with the shattering graph's
part sizes (by iterated Jensen/Hölder, or the usual positive-density
complete-bipartite count). On those samples every prescribed edge and
nonedge factor of the induced shattering graph is at least epsilon.
That would give positive induced shattering density, a contradiction.
Thus W is {0,1}-valued almost everywhere.

This direct argument agrees with the thin-bigraphon theorem of Lovasz
and Szegedy. Their Theorem 3.1 provides pure representatives; Theorem 4.1
makes a thin pure bigraphon binary with compact row metric; Lemma 4.7
gives finite essential dual VC dimension. These exact statements were
independently checked at https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.4377 . The direct
row-law argument below avoids needing pointwise choices in a pure version.

3. A COUNTABLE ROW FAMILY HAS ORDINARY FINITE VC AFTER NULL CLEANUP

View the rows W(x,.) as random elements of the separable L1 column space.
The row map is measurable; this follows, for example, by approximating
the jointly measurable kernel by finite rectangle functions. Choose a
countable family C of actual binary row functions that is dense in the
support of this row law. Such a family may be obtained by an iid sample
and restricting to an event of probability one. Choose representatives.

For every finite subfamily of C, remove all its Boolean atoms that have
column measure zero. There are only countably many such atoms in total,
so the remaining column space is conull. Every nonempty Boolean atom of
a finite row subfamily now has positive measure.

The family C has VC dimension at most v on this conull space. Otherwise
choose v+1 shattered column points and the 2^(v+1) witnessing row sets.
The required column atoms for this finite row tuple are nonempty and
hence positive-measure. Its conditional induced shattering density is
therefore positive.

That conditional density is continuous in the row functions under L1:
each Boolean-atom integral is Lipschitz, by telescoping products of factors
in [0,1], and the conditional density is a finite product of such integrals.
Every chosen row function lies in the support of the row law. Hence small
product neighborhoods of the witnessing tuple have positive probability
and still positive conditional density. The unconditional induced density
would then be positive, contradiction. This justifies the step from
essential patterns to an ordinary countable VC class without assuming
that arbitrary null point modifications preserve VC dimension.

4. FINITE BRACKETS YIELD AN ACTUAL POSITIVE RECTANGLE IN THE LIMIT

Adams--Nobel Corollary 1 states that a separable measurable VC class has
finite epsilon-bracketing numbers for every epsilon>0. A countable class
is separable in their stated sense. The brackets can be taken to have
indicator lower and upper endpoints. The exact statement and definitions
were checked directly on PDF pages 4--5 of
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4515 .
Published source: Uniform approximation of Vapnik--Chervonenkis classes,
Bernoulli 18(4) (2012), 1310--1319, DOI 10.3150/11-BEJ379.

Apply it to C with bracket width epsilon less than one-quarter of the
positive edge density of W. Modulo null sets, each bracket [L_i,U_i] is
closed in L1: the two inequalities L_i<=f<=U_i are preserved by L1
convergence. A finite union of these closed brackets therefore covers the
entire row-law support, since it contains the dense subfamily C.

At least one bracket has both a positive-measure lower set V=L_i and a
positive-measure class U of rows lying in it. If no such bracket existed,
almost every row would lie in a bracket with null lower endpoint and so
have integral at most epsilon, contradicting positive mean edge density.
The row class is measurable: membership is equivalent to the vanishing
of the two nonnegative measurable integrals

          integral L_i(y)(1-W(x,y))dy,
          integral W(x,y)(1-U_i(y))dy.

Fubini now gives W=1 almost everywhere on U x V, with both U,V of positive
measure. This is one fixed rectangle; there is no dependence on an
increasing finite covering number.

5. CUT CONVERGENCE UPGRADES TO L1 AT A BINARY LIMIT

After the chosen alignment, W_n->W in cut norm and 0<=W_n<=1. Since W is
binary,

 ||W_n-W||_1=integral W_n+integral W-2 integral W_n W.

Approximate W in L1 by a finite rectangle simple function. The integrals
against that function converge by cut convergence, and the L1 approximation
error is bounded uniformly because 0<=W_n<=1. Thus integral W_n W tends
to integral W, proving L1 convergence. In particular,

                       integral_(U x V) (1-W_n)=o(1).        (1)

6. DETERMINISTIC FINITE VERTEX EXTRACTION

The alignment may split finite vertex cells; it need not be a mere
vertex permutation. Let alpha=measure(U)>0 and beta_0=measure(V)>0.
For each color cell C_a of measure 1/d, put u_a=d*measure(C_a intersect U).
For each row cell C_x of measure 1/p, put v_x=p*measure(C_x intersect V).
These numbers lie in [0,1], with averages alpha and beta_0. Equation (1)
means

        sum_(a,x) (1-1_Da(x)) u_a v_x=o(dp).

Select A'={a:u_a>=alpha/2}, X'={x:v_x>=beta_0/2}. Each selected part
has positive proportional size: for example |A'|/d>=alpha/(2-alpha).
Every selected pair has u_a v_x>=alpha beta_0/4. Consequently the number
of missing incidences in A' x X' is o(dp)=o(p^2).

Since d>=delta p, both |A'| and |X'| are Omega(p), so their actual support
rectangle contains Omega(p^2) incidences. Restricting the original colored
graph to these colors and rows preserves simplicity, properness and B.
Its domains are subsets of common X', with total omissions o(p^2).
The arbitrary-intercept near-common theorem from pass 4 therefore forces
o(p^2) edges, the desired contradiction.

7. AUDIT CONCLUSION AND SCOPE

The main delicate steps all pass: the forbidden induced pattern survives
limits; fractional limits are impossible; null atom cleanup gives an
ordinary countable VC class; finite brackets are L1-closed and have a
measurable positive row cluster; and fractional vertex-cell intersections
are converted to actual linear-size vertex subsets by thresholding.

No uniform finite bracketing bound over changing finite probability spaces
is assumed. No rate of convergence is extracted. The argument proves a
qualitative fixed-VC support theorem using the independently audited
near-common affine theorem, and does not address unbounded-VC supports.
