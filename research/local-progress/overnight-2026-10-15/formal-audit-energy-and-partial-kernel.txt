GLOBAL ENERGY CAN BE SMALL FOR DENSE NON-B GRAPHS;
AN INTRINSIC PARTIAL-COMPOSITION REPLACEMENT
15 October 2026. Adversarial research note, hand proofs, no finite graph
enumeration and no Lean certificate. No historical novelty claim.

The earlier energy theorem remains valid: in a B-colored graph, many
large color classes force E(S)=o(|S|^3) for EVERY choice of distinct
permutation completions. This note audits how much information that
conclusion contains. It proves that a naive local-to-global energy bridge
is false even for complete bipartite graphs with perfect color matchings,
and records an intrinsic kernel which retains the actual B constraint.

1. AN INTRINSIC TENSOR COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE NAIVE ENERGY BRIDGE

Write T_s(x)=x+s on Z/6Z, and let tau exchange 0 and 3 and fix the other
four elements. Define the six permutation colors

              S_0={tau,T_3 tau,T_1,T_2,T_4,T_5}.

Their graphs partition K_{6,6}. At inputs other than 0 and 3 these are
the six ordinary translations. At inputs 0 and 3, the outputs belonging
to T_0 and T_3 have merely been exchanged. Thus every input-output pair
belongs to exactly one of the six matchings.

Let D_0=S_0 S_0^(-1). Differences of the four unchanged translations
already give all six translations. Indeed {1,2,4,5}-{1,2,4,5}=Z/6Z:
the residues 0,1,2,3,4,5 are represented by 1-1,2-1,1-5,4-1,5-1,1-2.
Also tau T_1^(-1) belongs to D_0 and is not a translation. Otherwise tau
would be a translation, impossible since it is nonidentity and has fixed
points. Therefore |D_0|>=7.

For a finite set S of d group elements, put

             r(g)=#{(a,b) in S^2:ab^(-1)=g}.

Then 0<=r(g)<=d, sum_g r(g)=d^2, and

             E(S)=sum_g r(g)^2 <= d^3.                  (1.1)

If equality holds, every positive r(g) equals d, so |SS^(-1)|=d.
For completeness this is precisely the coset case: r(g)=d implies
gS=S; the difference set is then the setwise left stabilizer of S, is
a subgroup of size d, and S is one of its right cosets. Only the easy
implication equality => |SS^(-1)|=d is needed here.

Since |D_0|>6, (1.1) is strict. Its integer value therefore gives

                         E(S_0)<=215<216.              (1.2)

No enumeration or numerical energy calculation is used.

Take r coordinatewise products of this coloring. The vertex sets are
(Z/6Z)^r, of size n=6^r; the palette S=S_0^r has d=6^r=n colors.
For every row and column there is exactly one color tuple, so this is
a proper perfect-matching coloring of the COMPLETE graph K_{n,n}.
Group multiplication and equality hold coordinatewise, hence

                         E(S)=E(S_0)^r<=215^r,
                         E(S)/n^3<=(215/216)^r ->0.    (1.3)

There is NO permutation-completion freedom in this example: all colors
are already perfect on the n-point set.

At every point x, by contrast, the pointwise energy is exactly n^3.
For a fixed target y and each b in S, there is exactly one a in S with
a(b^(-1)(x))=y, because the palette partitions the complete bipartite
graph. Thus each target has exactly n representations by an ordered
product ab^(-1), giving

        E_x(S)=sum_y n^2=n^3       for EVERY x.          (1.4)

Equations (1.3)-(1.4) give the same asymptotic local/global separation
previously found necessary for a hypothetical dense B example, while
the underlying graph is as dense as possible.

Scope. These graphs are NOT B-colored. Already T_1 and T_4 in the base
have relative permutation T_3 of order 2, and give a bichromatic C4.
The tensor product preserves such violations. Consequently this does
not refute a bridge that uses the full B condition; it refutes any
inference from density, perfect matching colors and large pointwise
energy alone to positive normalized global energy.

It also does not satisfy the B crossed-pair identities. In this example
C=J and d=n, so <C,T(C)>=n^2 rather than the required n. The centered
matrix C-(d/n)J is zero. Therefore the earlier negative-spectrum
criterion is not being contradicted or satisfied by this family.

2. ARBITRARY COMPLETIONS CAN HIDE GLOBAL RELATIONS EVEN MORE COMPLETELY

There is a separate and stronger warning for partial matchings. Start
with ANY properly colored bipartite simple graph represented on an
n-point set, with q=O(n) colors. No B hypothesis is imposed. Extend each
color arbitrarily to a permutation p_c of these n points.

Add a disjoint block of n unused points. On it choose independent uniform
permutations R_c, and set sigma_c=p_c direct-sum R_c. With positive
probability, for sufficiently large n, the resulting palette has only
the unavoidable relations

       sigma_a sigma_b^(-1)=sigma_c sigma_e^(-1)

coming from (a=c,b=e) or from (a=b,c=e). In particular

           E(S)=2q^2-q,
           |SS^(-1)|=q(q-1)+1.                         (2.1)

Here is a direct union-bound proof. For a quadruple of labels outside
the two trivial patterns, if some label occurs exactly once, conditioning
on all other random permutations determines that remaining permutation
uniquely. Its relation has probability 1/n!.

If no label occurs exactly once, the only nontrivial pattern is
a=e, b=c, a!=b. Its relation says that the uniform permutation
R_a R_b^(-1) is an involution. If I_n is the number of involutions,

 I_n <= sum_{k=0}^{floor(n/2)} binom(n,2k)n^k
     <= 2^n n^(n/2),
 I_n/n! <= (2e/sqrt(n))^n.

The first inequality chooses the moved points and bounds the number of
pairings by n^k. Thus a union bound over at most q^4 quadruples tends to
zero. Distinctness of the R_c is forced by the absence of the same
nontrivial relations. This proves (2.1) by existence, without a search.

Padding changes the action size by only a factor of two. If the original
classes have at least eta n edges, they still have at least (eta/2)
times the new action size. Thus even the large-color-class scale of the
earlier energy theorem does not prevent this graph-independent way of
making global energy tiny.

The valid theorem quantifies over EVERY completion, so this observation
does not invalidate it. It explains its actual content: it prohibits
structured completions of B graphs. Merely finding one low-energy
completion is uninformative. Minimizing global energy over completions
is correspondingly useless for an unrestricted density argument.

3. A COMPLETION-INDEPENDENT PARTIAL-COMPOSITION KERNEL

Let the original bipartition be X,Y. For color a let

                    f_a:D_a subset X -> R_a subset Y

be its actual partial bijection. Let B_a be its X by Y matching matrix,
with B_a(x,f_a(x))=1 and all other entries zero. Define

        Q_ab=B_a^T B_b,
        tau_ab=f_a composed with f_b^(-1).              (3.1)

The domain of tau_ab is f_b(D_a intersect D_b); it maps f_b(x) to f_a(x)
for x in D_a intersect D_b. Q_ab is its Y by Y partial permutation
matrix, with rows indicating output and columns indicating input.
For a!=b it has zero diagonal, by properness of the original coloring.

Index only ordered pairs (a,b) with a!=b. Set

  G_(ab),(ce)=<Q_ab,Q_ce>_F
            =#{y in dom(tau_ab) intersect dom(tau_ce):
                                      tau_ab(y)=tau_ce(y)}.             (3.2)

This is an actual positive-semidefinite Gram matrix. Its entries count
agreements of DEFINED partial maps, not agreements of arbitrary added
edges. The associated squared distance

       ||Q_ab-Q_ce||_F^2
        =|D_a intersect D_b|+|D_c intersect D_e|-2G_(ab),(ce)             (3.3)

is a completion-independent measure of their disagreement, including
the difference between their domains.

Adding unused points only adds zero rows and columns to these matrices,
and cannot change G. Relabeling X cancels out of (3.1); relabeling Y
simultaneously conjugates the Q_ab and also leaves G unchanged.

4. EXACTLY HOW B APPEARS IN THIS KERNEL

For a B-colored graph,

          G_(ab),(ab)=|D_a intersect D_b|,              (4.1)

and, if (a,b)!=(c,e) and {a,b} intersects {c,e},

                         G_(ab),(ce)=0.               (4.2)

Proof. An agreement gives two original two-edge paths from a column y
to a column z: one through a row x with colors b,a, the other through
a row x' with colors e,c. Since a!=b, properness gives y!=z. If x=x',
properness at that row forces a=c and b=e, contrary to the pairs being
different. Thus the agreement gives a genuine C4. A shared color between
the two ordered pairs makes this C4 nonrainbow. QED.

Conversely, in a properly colored graph every nonrainbow C4 has a repeat
on its opposite edges, and yields a positive entry forbidden by (4.2).
Thus, for the ACTUAL partial maps, (4.2) is equivalent to the B property.
The zero entries from a=c or b=e already follow from properness; the
cross-role equalities a=e or b=c carry the extra B constraint.

The all-four-colors-distinct entries count permissible rainbow C4s.
They need not be small. In particular, replacing G by a global product
equality count would discard precisely these genuine local agreements.

Let B=sum_a B_a be the uncolored biadjacency matrix, let d_X(x) be the
degree of x, and let k(y,z) be the number of common neighbors of y,z in X.
The exact trace and total-mass identities are

        tr G = sum_x d_X(x)(d_X(x)-1),
        sum_(ab),(ce) G_(ab),(ce) = sum_{y!=z} k(y,z)^2.                (4.3)

For the second identity, sum_{a!=b}Q_ab(y,z)=k(y,z) when y!=z, and
the diagonal is zero. The first identity counts ordered pairs of
different incident colors at each row.

More concretely, for each ordered pair of distinct columns y,z, the
color pairs furnished by their common neighbors form a directed matching
on the palette: no color can occur twice, in either role. Therefore
k(y,z)<=floor(q/2). This is an exact local interpretation of (4.2),
but by itself yields only constant-density restrictions.

5. THE COHERENCE LOST BY A BARE GRAM RELAXATION

The matrices in (3.1) satisfy much more than positivity and the zero
pattern. Write D_b^X=B_b B_b^T, the diagonal projection onto the original
row domain of color b. Then

        Q_ab^T=Q_ba,
        Q_ab Q_bc=B_a^T D_b^X B_c.                    (5.1)

The second formula is Q_ac restricted to underlying rows belonging to
D_a intersect D_b intersect D_c. In particular, with perfect color
matchings on the same two n-element sides,

        Q_ab are permutation matrices,
        Q_ab Q_bc=Q_ac,
        Q_ab Q_ba=I.                                  (5.2)

These exact compatibility relations express that all relative maps
come from the SAME family of color matchings. They are absent from a
free positive-semidefinite matrix having the sparsity (4.2).

6. THE FIRST INTRINSIC GRAM CONSTRAINTS STILL HAVE A DENSE ABSTRACT MODEL

The following is an abstract moment model, NOT a graph construction.
It shows that the listed positivity, overlap zeros, diagonal sizes,
orientation symmetry and first regularity moments alone do not force
vanishing density. Additional coherence such as (5.1) is needed.

Take q divisible by 4, n=4q-3 and k=q/4. On a q-color set choose uniformly
an oriented matching M of size k. Let v_M be its 0/1 incidence vector on
the q(q-1) ordered pairs of distinct colors. Define

                      G_*=n(n-1) E[v_M v_M^T].        (6.1)

It is positive semidefinite. Distinct overlapping ordered pairs never
co-occur, so it has exactly the required overlap zeros. Symmetry under
reversing every arc gives the transpose symmetry required by Q_ab^T=Q_ba.
For each ordered pair,

       Pr((a,b) in M)=k/[q(q-1)],
       (G_*)_(ab),(ab)=n(n-1)k/[q(q-1)]=n.             (6.2)

Thus its diagonal matches n-edge perfect color classes. Also

       tr G_*=nq(q-1),
       sum G_*=n(n-1)k^2.                             (6.3)

A q-regular bipartite graph on n+n vertices would have average off-
diagonal column codegree q(q-1)/(n-1)=k, so (6.3) is exactly the
constant-codegree first-moment model, including equality in its Jensen
lower bound. Its index-space dimension q(q-1) is below n(n-1), so a
naive Gram-rank upper bound from the off-diagonal Y by Y matrix space
does not exclude it either.

Here q/n tends to 1/4, a fixed POSITIVE bipartite edge density. This
fraction is unrelated to the earlier global N^2/4 endpoint-method
threshold: N=2n, and this abstract model has nq approximately N^2/16.
Its relevance is nonvanishing density, not that particular number.

No actual partial permutation matrices satisfying (5.1) are supplied.
The model therefore establishes only a limitation of the specified
first Gram relaxation, and is not a counterexample to #810.

6A. ADDING THE OTHER BIPARTITION'S GRAM MATRIX STILL DOES NOT REMOVE IT

Include also the diagonal color pairs in the two kernels. On the left
side use Q^X_ac=B_a B_c^T, and on the right use Q^Y_ab=B_a^T B_b.
Cyclic invariance of trace, together with transposition, gives the exact
reshuffling identity

                   G^Y_(ab),(ce)=G^X_(ac),(be).        (6.4)

Thus both matricizations of the same four-color tensor must be PSD.
This is additional genuine information beyond one arbitrary Gram
matrix, but it does not exclude the model above. In the perfect case,
all Q_aa equal I, so their Gram block is nJ_q and their inner products
with off-diagonal Q_ab are zero. Extend (6.1) by this block.

The extended matrix is PSD. Its entries are n when a=b,c=e or when
a=c,b=e; they are zero for reversed off-diagonal pairs and all other
overlap patterns; and they have one constant value for all-four-colors-
distinct patterns, by symmetry of the random matching. These cases are
invariant under the reshuffling (ab,ce)->(ac,be). Hence the reshuffled
matrix is exactly the same PSD matrix. Its total rank is at most
q(q-1)+1<=n^2, also consistent with the ambient matrix-space bound.

Therefore adding the second-side PSD condition, perfect-class identity
block, and the reshuffling equality still leaves this positive-density
abstract model. The missing issue is realization by coherent nonnegative
partial permutation matrices, not just either scalar Gram matrix.

7. RESEARCH CONSEQUENCES

The earlier vanishing-global-energy theorem is a valid necessary
condition with a conditional group-structure application. It is not
by itself evidence that density must force a contradiction: Section 1
realizes its local/global asymptotics in dense complete graphs, even
without completion choices, and Section 2 makes minimum completion
energy almost trivial for any partial graph.

The intrinsic kernel (3.2) is a more faithful object: its overlap zeros
encode B exactly and cannot be altered by unused permutation freedom.
But its first scalar/PSD constraints also admit a positive-density
abstract model. A potentially useful next step must exploit the actual
partial-map composition and support compatibility in (5.1), or higher
simultaneous agreement information; no theorem doing so sufficiently
to obtain o(N^2) is established here.
