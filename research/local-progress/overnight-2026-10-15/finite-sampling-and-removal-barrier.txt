WHY ORDINARY FIXED-VERTEX SAMPLING AND C4 REMOVAL LOSE THE CONSTRAINT
2026-10-15. Complete elementary observations and a scope audit. No finite
graph search. Historical novelty is not claimed.

1. FIXED VERTEX SAMPLES ARE ALMOST ALWAYS FULLY RAINBOW

Proposition. Let G be any properly edge-colored simple graph on N vertices,
with any number of colors. Choose a uniformly random k-element vertex set,
where 4<=k<=N and N>=4. Then

 P(the induced sample contains two edges of the same color)
             <= (k)_4 / [8(N-3)],

where (k)_4=k(k-1)(k-2)(k-3). In particular the probability tends to zero
for fixed k as N tends to infinity. It also tends to zero for k=o(N^(1/4)).

Proof. A color class is a matching of size k_c<=N/2. Write m=|E(G)|.
The number P of unordered same-color edge pairs is

 P=sum_c binom(k_c,2) <= ((N-2)/4) sum_c k_c = (N-2)m/4.

Each such pair has four distinct endpoints by properness. The probability
all four appear in the random sample is (k)_4/(N)_4. The union bound gives

 P(sample has a color repeat)
   <= m (k)_4/[4N(N-1)(N-3)]
   <= (k)_4/[8(N-3)],

using m<=N(N-1)/2. QED.

Consequently any ordinary finite-vertex sampling limit of proper colorings
looks fully rainbow on its present edges. This applies equally to valid B
colorings and to dense Latin-square colorings that fail B badly. A method
using only fixed-order, unrescaled colored subgraph densities cannot retain
the needed equal-color information. Conditioning on a color or retaining
the natural 1/N collision scale avoids this particular obstruction; the
proposition does not rule out those stronger methods.

2. A DENSE EXAMPLE THAT IS FAR FROM B BUT HAS o(n^4) BAD C4s

Let n be odd. Take K_{n,n} with both sides labeled by Z/nZ, and color edge
(x,y) by y-x. This is a proper coloring with exactly n colors. Each color
is a perfect matching of size n. All n^2 edges are present.

A same-color pair determines a unique four-cycle in the complete bipartite
graph. No four-cycle can contain two same-color diagonal pairs: those two
equalities would imply 2(x_1-x_2)=0, which is impossible when n is odd and
x_1!=x_2. Since properness rules out adjacent repeats, it follows that the
number of nonrainbow four-cycles is exactly

                n binom(n,2) = n^2(n-1)/2 = Theta(n^3).

The total number of four-cycles is binom(n,2)^2. Thus the fraction of bad
four-cycles is exactly 2/(n-1), tending to zero.

Nevertheless, at least n^2/4 edges must be deleted to make every remaining
four-cycle rainbow while retaining the original colors. To see this, each
edge belongs to exactly 2(n-1) bad four-cycles: choose the other row, and
there is one other column for which its own diagonal repeats and one for
which the opposite diagonal repeats. Oddness makes these columns distinct.
Every deleted edge can therefore hit at most 2(n-1) original bad cycles,
so every hitting set has size at least

             [n^2(n-1)/2]/[2(n-1)] = n^2/4.

This is an elementary deletion lower bound. Solymosi's established group
table theorem in fact implies that only o(n^2) edges can survive, but that
stronger input is unnecessary for the present obstruction.

Thus there is NO uniform colored-C4 removal statement of the form

  o(n^4) nonrainbow four-cycles in a proper O(n)-coloring
       => o(n^2) deletions suffice to reach a B-coloring.

Growing palettes invalidate that ordinary dense-graph normalization. The
natural bad-configuration scale in this example is n^3, not n^4. A removal
theorem at the n^3 scale would be a genuinely stronger and presently
unprovided ingredient; it cannot be replaced by ordinary C4 removal.

3. CAUTION ABOUT B-TO-C AND INDUCED-MATCHING EXTRACTION

The 2023 paper of Gyárfás--Sárközy distinguishes:
  A: proper, with no alternating two-color four-edge path or four-cycle;
  B: proper, with every C4 rainbow;
  C: both A and B.

Its Proposition 1.6 identifies the C-variant with the open (7,4) Brown--
Erdős--Sós problem. Hence a fixed-positive-density theorem extracting a
constant fraction of a B graph into a C graph, with O(N) colors and O(N)
vertices, would reduce #810 to that open problem; it would not by itself
prove the required o(N^2) bound.

Strong colorings, whose classes are induced matchings, are different:
their O(N)-color extremal bound o(N^2) is the Ruzsa--Szemerédi (6,3) theorem.
Extracting a positive fraction of any fixed-density B graph into that
stronger class would settle #810, so such an extraction cannot be treated
as a routine application of the known removal lemma.

The earlier tensor/DHJ obstruction excludes a uniform retained fraction
for B-to-C extraction in the near-quadratic regime. Its input densities
tend to zero. It does not eliminate a retained fraction depending on a
fixed positive quadratic density.
