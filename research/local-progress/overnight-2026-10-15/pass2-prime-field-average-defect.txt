PRIME-FIELD AFFINE COLORS: A QUANTITATIVE TOTAL-DEFECT BOUND
15 October 2026. Hand proof using an existing incidence theorem and
quantitative Roth bounds. A qualitative alternative uses the previously
audited group-table bound. No finite graph search or Lean certificate.
No assertion of historical novelty or a general #810 result.

QUANTITATIVE THEOREM.
There are absolute constants C,c>0 such that every B-colored bipartite
graph on two copies of F_p, whose colors are restrictions of nonconstant
affine permutations, satisfies

  m <= C*p*(D+1)^(7/11) + 8D
                  + C*p^2*exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)],        (Q)

where D is its total number of omitted colored incidences, counted
over its original color labels. Duplicate completions are allowed.
The proof of the quantitative statement is in Section 5A, combining
the incidence dichotomy with a pruning and Roth argument. The bound
can be trivial for large D; no small-D hypothesis is required in (Q).

COROLLARY (TOTAL DEFECT, AND THE AVERAGE-DEFECT VERSION).
Let p tend to infinity through primes. Let a B-colored bipartite graph
have both vertex classes F_p. Every color is an arbitrary restriction
of a nonconstant affine permutation x -> a*x+b. Let d be the number
of color labels, m the edge count, and let

  D=sum_colors (p-size of the actual color class)=d*p-m

be the total number of omitted colored incidences. If

                         D=o(p^(11/7)),                 (T)

then m=o(p^2). The same conclusion follows from the AVERAGE-defect
condition D/d=o(p^(4/7)); the case d=0 is interpreted trivially.

In particular the conclusion holds when every color omits at most
r=o(p^(4/7)) points. This extends the defect range from the preceding
stretched-log result, over PRIME fields, and needs only average control.
It does not handle arbitrary positive support fractions or all
sublinear defects. The field restriction is used in the incidence
theorem's characteristic condition.

1. TWO INPUTS, WITH THEIR SCOPES

1a. Incidences over a field.

Stevens--de Zeeuw, "An Improved Point-Line Incidence Bound Over Arbitrary
Fields," Theorem 3, states that a point set of size v and a line set
of size w have O(v^(11/15) w^(11/15)) incidences when

  v^(7/8)<w<v^(8/7),       v^(-2) w^13 << p^15           (1.1)

in characteristic p. Constants are absolute. Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.06284 .

We also use the elementary bound

                         I(P,L)<=|P|+sqrt(|P|)*|L|.     (1.2)

To verify it, if I is the incidence count, counting pairs of lines
through points gives I^2/|P|-I<=|L|^2, and solving this quadratic
implies (1.2). These estimates will be applied to dyadic richness
classes, not outside the size range in (1.1).

1b. A single affine pencil cannot contain dense B edges.

A parallel pencil of affine permutations normalizes to the additive
group table of F_p. A concurrent pencil normalizes to the multiplicative
group table of F_p*, apart from its one possible edge at the common
fixed point. The known uniform partial-group-table theorem therefore
bounds the number of ACTUAL B edges in any one such pencil by o(p^2).
Arbitrary supports are permitted. This is the group-table input used
in pass1-orbit-and-homogeneous-independent-audit.txt, not a new theorem.

2. DENSE-CASE REDUCTION AND DUPLICATE COMPLETIONS

Suppose, for contradiction, that m>=delta*p^2 along a sequence, for
some fixed delta>0. Under (T),

  d*p=m+D<=p^2+D=p^2+o(p^(11/7)),

while m<=dp implies d>=delta*p. Thus d=Theta(p), d<2p eventually, and

                         D=o(d^(11/7))=o(p^2).          (2.1)

For the average-defect alternative, write r_bar=D/d=o(p^(4/7)). The
identity d*(p-r_bar)=m<=p^2 first gives d<2p for large p. Therefore
D=d*r_bar=o(p^(11/7)), reducing to (T). No assumed bound on the number
of colors or a per-color maximum defect is hidden in these deductions.

Initially distinct color labels could have the same affine completion.
For t colors on one completed p-point line, their actual edge sets
are disjoint, so their combined defect is at least (t-1)*p. Summing,
the number of labels in excess of the number of distinct completed
lines is at most D/p=o(d). Keep one color from each completed line.
At most D actual edges are discarded, since every discarded class
has at most p edges. The retained graph is still dense and B; its
total defect is at most D; and it has d'=d-o(d)=Theta(p) distinct
completions. Rename d' as d. We may therefore assume all completed
color lines are distinct, retaining (2.1).

Let L be the largest number of these lines in a parallel or concurrent
pencil. If L>=eta*d for any fixed eta>0 along a subsequence, that
pencil has at least

  L*p-D >= eta*delta*p^2-o(p^2)

actual edges. This contradicts the group-table input in Section 1b.
Consequently

                              L=o(d).                  (2.2)

This is where B is used. Properness and small defect alone do not
give (2.2), and cannot substitute for the group-table theorem.

3. DUALITY CONVERTS MISSING SUPPORTS INTO A TOTAL INCIDENCE BUDGET

Associate to each completed line y=a*x+b the dual point (a,b) in F_p^2.
Let P be this set of d points and let mathcal L be all dual lines
determined by at least two points of P. For ell in mathcal L put
k_ell=|ell intersect P|>=2. Then

             sum_ell binom(k_ell,2)=binom(d,2),          (3.1)

because every pair of dual points determines exactly one line.
Moreover max k_ell=L: a vertical dual line corresponds to parallel
original lines, and a nonvertical line b=-x*a+y corresponds to the
original lines concurrent at (x,y).

At an original point shared by t>=2 completed color lines, at most
one of those incidences is retained in the actual graph. Therefore
at least t-1 colored incidences there were omitted. Summing over these
points gives sum(t-1)<=D. Since t<=2(t-1), the TOTAL incidences on
nonvertical determined dual lines are at most 2D. Determined vertical
dual lines have disjoint sets of dual points, so their total incidence
count is at most d. Hence

             I_*:=sum_ell k_ell <= 2D+d=o(d^(11/7)).    (3.2)

The full incidence budget (3.2) is stronger than just bounding the
number of determined dual lines. Using only their number with the
published Beck corollary would yield the weaker defect exponent 1/7.

4. THE INCIDENCE BUDGET AND L=o(d) CANNOT ACCOUNT FOR ALL PAIRS

Fix a sufficiently large absolute constant A and set

                              K=A*d^(3/7).

Group the determined dual lines into dyadic classes: for k=2^j,
let mathcal L_k consist of the lines with k<=k_ell<2k, and write
T_k=|mathcal L_k|. There are O(log d) nonempty classes.

4a. Low richness k<K.

For these lines, binom(k_ell,2)<=K*k_ell. Their total contribution
to (3.1) is at most

                 K*I_*=A*d^(3/7)*o(d^(11/7))=o(d^2).    (4.1)

Here A is fixed before taking the limit.

For all remaining classes k>=K, equation (3.2) gives

                    T_k<=I_*/k<=I_*/K=o(d^(8/7)).       (4.2)

Thus the upper size restriction in (1.1) is available for every rich
class where we will use the nontrivial incidence bound.

4b. Large classes: T_k>d^(7/8).

By (4.2), T_k<d^(8/7) eventually. The characteristic condition is
satisfied with ample margin: since d=Theta(p),

  d^(-2)*T_k^13 <= d^(90/7)=O(p^(90/7))=o(p^15).        (4.3)

Apply (1.1) to P and mathcal L_k. As k*T_k is at most their incidence
count, it follows that

  k*T_k << d^(11/15)*T_k^(11/15),
  T_k << d^(11/4)*k^(-15/4).                            (4.4)

This class contributes at most 2*k^2*T_k to (3.1). Summing over the
dyadic k>=K bounds the total contribution of these large classes by

  C_1*d^(11/4)*K^(-7/4)=C_1*A^(-7/4)*d^2,             (4.5)

where C_1 is absolute. The geometric sum, not the number of classes,
controls this term.

4c. Intermediate classes: d^(1/2)<T_k<=d^(7/8).

Apply (1.2): k*T_k<=d+sqrt(d)*T_k<=2*sqrt(d)*T_k.
Thus k<=2*sqrt(d), and the pair contribution of each class is at most

  2*k^2*T_k<=8*d^(15/8).

After summing O(log d) classes this is o(d^2).

4d. Small classes: T_k<=d^(1/2).

Now (1.2) gives k*T_k<=2d. Their pair contribution is at most
2*k^2*T_k<=4d*k. Every such k is at most L; summing its dyadic values
is at most 2L. The total is therefore at most

                              8dL=o(d^2).              (4.6)

4e. Contradiction.

Combining (4.1), (4.5), and the other two ranges, equation (3.1) gives

                   binom(d,2)<=C_1*A^(-7/4)*d^2+o(d^2).

Choose A so large that C_1*A^(-7/4)<1/4, and then let d tend to
infinity. The left side divided by d^2 tends to 1/2, a contradiction.
This completes the proof of the theorem.

5. QUANTITATIVE GEOMETRIC ALTERNATIVE

The argument yields the following standalone incidence dichotomy.
There are absolute constants c_1,c_2>0 such that, for sufficiently
large d and any d-point set P in F_p^2 with d<=2p, either

  some line contains at least c_1*d points of P, or
  sum_{ell determined by P}|ell intersect P|>=c_2*d^(11/7).             (5.1)

To see the constants explicitly at the level needed here, assume
I_*<epsilon*d^(11/7) and L<epsilon'*d. Repeat Section 4 with
K=A*d^(3/7). The low-richness contribution is at most A*epsilon*d^2;
the large-class contribution is at most C_1*A^(-7/4)*d^2; the small-
class contribution is at most 8*epsilon'*d^2; and the intermediate
contribution is o(d^2), uniformly for d<=2p. Choose A first so that
C_1*A^(-7/4)<1/16, then epsilon<1/(16A) and epsilon'<1/128.
For all large d the sum is smaller than binom(d,2). Also
T_k<=epsilon*d^(11/7)/K<d^(8/7), and d<=2p ensures the characteristic
condition uniformly as d grows. This proves (5.1).

Thus the operative deficit obstruction is an incidence alternative,
not merely the published count of determined lines. Using only that
count would give the weaker r=o(p^(1/7)) conclusion. That initial
observation is superseded by the total-incidence argument above.

5A. QUANTITATIVE PRUNING AND ROTH PROOF OF (Q)

Delete every original color whose defect is at least p/8. There are
at most 8D/p such labels, so this deletes at most 8D actual edges.
Write d for the number of retained colors. Each retained class has
more than 7p/8 edges. Their affine completions are automatically
distinct, and d<8p/7 since the graph has at most p^2 edges.

The retained completed palette is strongly permutation-progression-
free. Indeed, if C=A B^(-1) A with A!=B, the rectangle construction
in pass1-affine-quantitative-audit.txt loses fewer than p/2 starting
rows to the four missing-edge tests and at most one more to affine
degeneracy. For p>2 a genuine nonrainbow rectangle remains. The
finitely many small cases are absorbed into the absolute constant.

Normalize a parallel or concurrent retained pencil to the additive
or multiplicative group of F_p. The quantitative Roth argument from
pass1, with its cyclic-to-odd-order embedding where needed, gives

                    L <= C*p*exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)].     (5A.1)

The primary quantitative input here is Schildkraut, Theorem 11.4,
https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15125v1#S11.SS2 ; the exponent is an existing
Roth estimate, not an improvement established by this research.

The retained palette has total defect at most D and total dual-line
incidence at most 2D+d. If the large-pencil case of (5.1) holds, then
d<=L/c_1, and (5A.1) applies. Otherwise

                         c_2*d^(11/7)<=2D+d.            (5A.2)

For all sufficiently large d, the last d is at most half the left
side, giving d<=C*D^(7/11). Bounded d is handled by replacing D with
D+1. Thus in both cases

  d<=C*(D+1)^(7/11)+C*p*exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)].           (5A.3)

The original graph has at most p*d+8D edges. Substitution proves (Q),
with absolute constants. This quantitative proof avoids any duplicate
merging and does not need a maximum-defect assumption for the original
palette. It uses actual support restrictions precisely in the pruning,
the robust forbidden-rectangle argument, and the dual incidence budget.

When D=o(p^(11/7)), dividing (Q) by p^2 proves m=o(p^2). The average-
defect reduction in Section 2 then gives the stated average-defect
corollary. The qualitative group-table proof in Sections 1--4 is
retained as an independent route with the same exponent and a weaker
literature input; it is not the strongest final bound.

6. WHAT HAS AND HAS NOT BEEN ACHIEVED

The result uses the ACTUAL support deficits to bound all incidences
of the dual palette, then uses B to rule out a large remaining pencil.
It does not rely on global palette energy, arbitrary completions
having a small difference set, or the earlier Gram relaxation.

The prime-field condition is substantial. For a general extension
field of size n, a dense palette has d=Theta(n), and (4.3) need not
hold in its much smaller characteristic. The earlier Frobenius-family
examples explain why those incidence structures require different
arguments; their homogeneous subgroup decomposition remains useful.

There is still no conclusion for all r=o(p), for arbitrary positive
support fractions in quadratic or general affine palettes, or for
unrestricted B colorings. This is a concrete conditional asymptotic
advance obtained by combining existing inputs with the deficit
count, not a new incidence theorem or a claimed solution of #810.
