COMMON ROW MASKS: A SPECTRAL INTERMEDIATE AND THE REMAINING GAP
15 October 2026, overnight pass 3. Analytic proof only.

1. THE PRECISE COMMON-MASK TARGET

Let p be odd, A subset F_p* of size d, and X subset F_p of size k.
For every a in A and x in X select the edge

  x -> sigma_a(x)=ax+a^2

with color a. There are m=dk selected colored incidences. They form
a proper simple graph exactly when

  X intersect -(A restricted-sum A) is empty,         (1)

where the restricted sum uses two distinct elements. This follows
from sigma_a(x)-sigma_b(x)=(a-b)(x+a+b).

The actual color-by-row support matrix is the rank-one matrix
1_A(a)1_X(x). Thus any failure of a simple spectral transfer already
occurs at the lowest possible nonzero rank for this support matrix.

The open target in this note is: if the graph is proper and every
C4 is rainbow, must d*k=o(p^2)? No affirmative conclusion is proved.

2. A UNIFORM COMPLETED-PALETTE OPERATOR ESTIMATE FOR EVERY A

Let P_g be the permutation operator of an affine bijection g on F_p,
and let

  C_A=sum_{a in A} P_{sigma_a}.

Let J be the p-by-p all-ones matrix. Then

  ||C_A-(d/p)J||_op
    <= [(p-1)(3d^2-2d)]^(1/4)
    = O(p^(1/4)*sqrt(d)).                            (2)

This applies to an arbitrary palette subset A, without assuming a
small multiplicative Fourier norm or approximate subgroup structure.

Proof. First bound the multiplicative energy of S={sigma_a:a in A}.
The quotient sigma_a sigma_b^-1 has slope rho=a/b and intercept

  a^2-ab = rho(rho-1)b^2.

The identity quotient has multiplicity d. If rho!=1, a fixed quotient
has at most two representing pairs (a,b), since its intercept fixes
b^2. Therefore

  E(S)=sum_g #{(a,b):sigma_a sigma_b^-1=g}^2
       <=d^2+2(d^2-d)=3d^2-2d.                      (3)

Each affine permutation preserves the constants and their orthogonal
complement W. On W its character (trace) is the number of its fixed
points minus 1. Hence that trace equals

  p-1 at the identity;
  -1 at a nonidentity translation;
   0 at an affine map whose slope differs from 1.

Let T=C_A restricted to W. Expand tr((TT*)^2) as a sum over four
palette elements. Every group-word coefficient is a nonnegative
integer. The identity words number E(S), nonidentity translations
contribute negatively, and all other terms vanish. Consequently

  ||T||_op^4 <= tr((TT*)^2) <= (p-1)E(S).

The constant vector has singular value d and is invariant on both
sides, so removing (d/p)J leaves exactly T. Equations (2)--(3) follow.

3. THE ACTUAL COMMON-MASK GRAPH IS CUT-QUASIRANDOM ON ITS ROW STRIP

Choose the matrix convention C_A(x,y)=#{a in A:sigma_a(x)=y}.
Under properness (1), its restriction G to the rows X has entries
0 or 1 and is exactly the selected graph's adjacency matrix.

For every U subset X and V subset F_p, (2) gives

  | e_G(U,V) - d|U||V|/p |
    <= [(p-1)(3d^2-2d)]^(1/4) sqrt(|U||V|).          (4)

In particular, if d,|U|,|V| are each at least a fixed positive
multiple of p, the error is O(p^(7/4))=o(p^2). The estimate is
uniform over all choices of A,X,U,V satisfying these hypotheses.

This is a genuine intermediate restriction on a putative dense
common-mask example. It counts ordinary edges, rather than the
much rarer repeated-color squares relevant to the problem.

4. THE EXACT FIXED-COLOR CROSS MATRICES

For each a in A, define a k-by-k binary matrix indexed by X:

  K_a(x,z)=G(x,sigma_a(z)).                           (5)

Every diagonal entry is 1, since every color a is present at every
row in X. A repeated-color C4 with a on its opposite edges exists
precisely when, for some x!=z,

  K_a(x,z)=K_a(z,x)=1.

Consequently B is exactly the simultaneous condition

  K_a(x,z)K_a(z,x)=0 for all a in A and x!=z.         (6)

Column permutation and row/column restriction of (2) imply

  ||K_a-(d/p)J_X||_op <= [(p-1)(3d^2-2d)]^(1/4).   (7)

Thus every color-local matrix is close to a dense constant matrix
in normalized operator norm if d and k have positive density.
Nevertheless (6) and this type of spectral closeness are compatible.

For example, for any fixed 0<theta<=1/2 and each unordered pair of
k vertices, independently choose one forward arc with probability
theta, the reverse arc with probability theta, and no arc otherwise.
Put ones on the diagonal. The resulting binary matrix K has no
reciprocal off-diagonal pair and mean

  E K=theta J+(1-theta)I.

There exist outcomes with ||K-E K||_op=O(sqrt(k)). One direct proof
uses a fixed 1/4-net of the two real unit spheres: for fixed unit
vectors u,v, the bilinear form u^T(K-EK)v is a sum of independent
bounded pair contributions whose squared ranges sum to O(1).
Hoeffding gives a tail exp(-c t^2); a union bound over at most
9^(2k) net pairs, with t=C sqrt(k), and the standard net inequality
give the asserted operator bound. Thus even a stronger spectral
approximation than (7) does not contradict (6) for one color.

This random-matrix observation is an analytic existence argument,
not a claim that these matrices can be realized simultaneously by
the quadratic palette. Their simultaneous realization, including
the shared G and the permutations in (5), is the remaining issue.

5. WHY THE SIMPLE PAIR-ACTION TRACE TRANSFER LOSES TOO MUCH

On ordered distinct vertex pairs, write a pair as

  r=z-x !=0,   t=x/r.

The affine map sigma_a sends it to

  r'=ar,      t'=t+a/r=t+r'/r^2.                    (8)

For the FULL palette, Fourier transformation in t makes each
nonzero-frequency block a submatrix of the additive Fourier matrix,
with at most two repeated columns (r and -r). Its norm is at most
sqrt(2p). The zero-frequency block is the all-ones matrix. Equivalently,
this is ordinary point-line incidence: (r,t) encodes the line
y=r^-2 x+t, and (r',t') encodes a point. It is a completed-palette
incidence estimate, not an actual-support theorem.

For the actual common-mask count, let

  H((x,z),(y,w))=G(x,w)G(z,y),

on ordered distinct pairs. The number of ordered repeated-color
squares is the pairing of H with the A-restricted pair-action sum.
Although H is independent of a, it is NOT a product of a single
input-pair function and a single output-pair function.

The zero-additive-frequency part of this pairing is exactly

  (1/p) sum_{a in A} sum_{r!=0} sum_{x,y}
                   G(x,y)G(x+r,y-ar).               (9)

For each a, partition F_p^2 into its p lines of direction (1,-a).
Cauchy--Schwarz on their G-edge occupancies gives

  sum_{r!=0,x,y} G(x,y)G(x+r,y-ar) >= m^2/p-m.

Thus (9) is at least d(m^2/p^2-m/p), of order p^3 at fixed density.

The remaining modes cannot simply be discarded. The crossed test is
a restriction of a swapped tensor G tensor G. Its nuclear norm is
at most ||G||_*^2, which can be large. A direct operator-norm times
nuclear-norm estimate therefore fails at the required p^3 scale.
Low rank of the COLOR-BY-ROW support matrix does not control the
rank or nuclear norm of G or of this crossed tensor.

Even a resulting bound of order rank(G)>=p^(1/4) would be weaker
than the generic fooling-set observation: a color matching of size
k gives a submatrix B with diagonal ones and B_ij B_ji=0, hence

  I_k=B circ B^T,  k<=rank(B)^2<=rank(G)^2.

So a common-mask B graph already satisfies rank(G)>=sqrt(k),
without using any quadratic-palette structure.

6. CHECKPOINT

The common-mask class is genuinely narrower than arbitrary supports
and would be a useful positive target. This pass proves (2)--(4)
and isolates the precise matrix conditions (5)--(7). It does not
turn those individual spectral conditions into a contradiction.

A successful continuation must exploit joint compatibility of the
K_a, or count directly the five one-dimensional tests in

  b^2+bx=a^2+az,    c^2+cz=a^2+ax,
  a,b,c in A,      x,z in X,       x!=z.

Replacing their actual membership tests by completed-palette mixing
or by ordinary graph quasirandomness would leave the central gap.
