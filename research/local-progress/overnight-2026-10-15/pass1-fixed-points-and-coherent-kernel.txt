FEW FIXED POINTS FORCE FEW REPEATED RECTANGLES;
THE FIRST TWO-SIDED GRAM RELAXATION STILL HAS AN INTEGRAL DENSE MODEL
15 October 2026. Hand proofs, no finite search or Lean certificate.
No historical novelty claim. This is an obstruction and a conditional
corollary, not a vanishing-density theorem for arbitrary B colorings.

The new input used below is the previously audited global-energy theorem
in ../multicolor-2026-10-15/multiplicative-energy-obstruction.txt:

  For every fixed eta>0, if d distinct permutation completions represent
  B color classes having at least eta*n actual edges each, then
  E(S)=#{(a,b,c,e):a b^(-1)=c e^(-1)}=o(d^3), uniformly as d tends to
  infinity. The action size n may vary independently of d.

All remaining arguments in this note are elementary. The fixed-point
assumption below applies to the whole acting group (or, sufficiently,
to the particular four-letter words), not just color differences.

1. ACTUAL RECTANGLE PATTERNS AND THE CORRECT WORD

Let X,Y be separately embedded in an n-point set Omega. Write
f_a:D_a subset X -> Y for the actual partial bijection of color a and
sigma_a for its permutation completion on Omega. Let B_a be its X-by-Y
matching matrix, padded with zeros if necessary, and put

  Q_ab=B_a^T B_b,
  t_abce=<Q_ab,Q_ce>_F.

Here all color pairs, including a=b, are permitted. A contribution to
t_abce is an agreement of the DEFINED partial maps

  f_a f_b^(-1) and f_c f_e^(-1).

Equivalently, it is a rectangle whose first row has colors b,a and whose
second row has colors e,c, with the ordered columns being the input and
output of the two relative maps. We allow degenerate rectangles. Thus

  R=sum_(a,b,c,e) t_abce^2                              (1.1)

counts pairs of identically labeled ordered rectangles, including
degeneracies. Any nondegenerate octahedron convention is bounded above
by R, up to at most a fixed ordering factor.

Every point counted by t_abce is fixed by

  w_abce=sigma_e sigma_c^(-1) sigma_a sigma_b^(-1).       (1.2)

The original supports are still required; a fixed point of (1.2) need
not give an actual rectangle. The inequality

  t_abce <= |Fix(w_abce)|                               (1.3)

is always valid. Also w_abce=1 if and only if

  sigma_a sigma_b^(-1)=sigma_c sigma_e^(-1),

so the number of identity-word quadruples is exactly E(S), when the
completion permutations are distinct.

There is a simple bound using properness alone:

  sum t_abce <= n*d^3.                                 (1.4)

Indeed choose the first row x and the three colors a,b,e. The first two
edges determine its two columns, color e determines the second row if
its required edge exists, and then the last edge has at most one color
c because the graph is simple. This also handles degeneracies. It is
essential here to count only actual edges, not arbitrary completions.

2. A CONDITIONAL OCTAHEDRON-COUNT THEOREM

Assume every color has at least eta*n actual edges, the d completions
are distinct, d tends to infinity, and every nonidentity word (1.2)
has at most f points fixed. Then

  R <= f*n*d^3 + n^2 E(S).                              (2.1)

For a nonidentity word, (1.3) gives t^2<=f*t. For an identity word use
t^2<=n^2, and sum using (1.4). This proves (2.1), without replacing any
partial map by a completed edge in the rectangle count.

Consequently, if f=o(n), the earlier energy theorem implies

  R=o(n^2*d^3).                                        (2.2)

In the dense palette regime d=Theta(n), this becomes

  R=o(n^5).                                           (2.3)

For partial affine maps of a finite field, every nonidentity affine
permutation fixes at most one point: (u-1)x=-v has at most one solution
unless u=1,v=0. Hence (2.3) holds for heavy B color classes with distinct
affine completions and d=Theta(n).

Duplicate completions do not create a hidden assumption in passing
from a dense graph to this situation. If each of several colors has
at least eta*n edges and all have the same completion, their actual
edge sets are disjoint subsets of its n-edge permutation graph. There
are therefore at most 1/eta of them. Keeping one representative per
distinct completion retains a fixed positive fraction of the colors
and edges, with all retained colors still heavy. A graph with at most
C*n colors and at least delta*n^2 edges first retains a positive
fraction of its edges by deleting classes smaller than
eta*n, for example eta=delta/(2C). The retained palette has Theta(n)
colors; representative selection preserves this scale.

Scope of the consequence. Gowers--Long's inverse theorem for partial
Latin squares assumes Omega(n^5) pairs of identically labeled rectangles.
Equation (2.3) says that the heavy, distinct-completion subgraph of a
putative dense B example in a few-fixed-points action lies strictly
below that input threshold. Thus a proof that B itself forces the
Gowers--Long threshold would immediately rule out this representation
class. No such concentration theorem is established here. The result
does not rule out partial affine dense B graphs by itself, and does not
obstruct a different theorem that works below the n^5 scale.

For the exact prior-source scope, see literature-bridges.md, section
"What Gowers--Long actually provides". Its primary reference is
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-020-00553-1 . The earlier energy theorem is
a proved local research result, not asserted here to be published.

3. WHY THE FIRST-POINT ENERGY BRIDGE DOES NOT SUFFICE

For complete permutations put

  E_x(S)=#{(a,b,c,e): sigma_a sigma_b^(-1)(x)
                        =sigma_c sigma_e^(-1)(x)}.

There are d^2 outputs distributed among n points, hence

  sum_x E_x(S)>=d^4.

The fixed-point bound gives, in the opposite direction,

  sum_x E_x(S)<=n E(S)+f(d^4-E(S)).                     (3.1)

When f=1 the two leading terms are both d^4. Therefore (3.1) does not
force E(S)=Omega(d^3). The fact that original supports might avoid the
single fixed point of each nonidentity word is exactly the correlation
problem; completing the maps does not remove it.

The completion-independent count R in (1.1) is a more faithful statistic.
Nevertheless a generic dense-graph calculation gives only Omega(n^4),
one factor n short of the concentration needed above. The following
integral Gram model demonstrates that several natural extra constraints
do not supply that factor.

4. AN INTEGRAL TWO-SIDED GRAM MODEL AT POSITIVE DENSITY

Fix any integer q>=4 and set n=5q. Index a symmetric matrix G by the
q(q-1) ordered pairs (a,b), a!=b, of colors. Define

  G_(ab),(ce) = n, if (a,b)=(c,e);
               1, if a,b,c,e are all distinct;
               0, otherwise.                           (4.1)

Every entry is a nonnegative INTEGER. Distinct overlapping ordered
color pairs have zero inner product, exactly the B overlap zeros. The
four-distinct entries satisfy the strongest possible integer bound
0<=G<=1 for distinct relative maps in a one-fixed-point action.

4a. Positive semidefiniteness, proved without a spectral citation.

Write A for the ordered-disjoint adjacency matrix, so G=nI+A. Split
functions on ordered pairs into reversal-symmetric and reversal-
antisymmetric parts. A vanishes on the latter. On the symmetric part
it acts as twice the disjointness adjacency K on unordered 2-subsets.

Let U be the vertex-versus-2-subset incidence matrix. Then

  K=J+I-U^T U,     U U^T=(q-2)I+J.

The eigenvalues of K are binom(q-2,2) on constants, -(q-3) on centered
vertex-incidence functions, and 1 on ker U. Thus the least eigenvalue
of A is -2(q-3), and G is PSD whenever n>=2q-6, in particular at n=5q.

4b. The second side, identity block, and exact reshuffling.

Adjoin the diagonal color pairs (a,a), with block nJ_q, and set their
cross terms with off-diagonal color pairs to zero. The full four-index
tensor is n on the two pairing patterns a=b,c=e and a=c,b=e; it is 1
on all-four-distinct patterns and zero otherwise. This is invariant
under the reshuffling

  (ab,ce) -> (ac,be).

Consequently both bipartition-side Gram matricizations are PSD and
obey the exact identity

  <B_a^T B_b,B_c^T B_e> = <B_a B_c^T,B_b B_e^T>.

This is a check on the abstract tensor, NOT a construction of the B_a.
The full tensor rank is at most q(q-1)+1<n^2, consistent with the
ambient matrix-space rank bound.

4c. First moments, the all-ones vector, and codegree upper bounds.

Put L=q(q-1). Each row of the off-diagonal block has sum

  lambda_0=n+(q-2)(q-3)=q^2+6.

Its trace is nL, exactly the value required for q perfect matchings.
Its total mass L*lambda_0 is at least the mandatory codegree Jensen
bound n L^2/(n-1), since

  lambda_0 - nL/(n-1)
    = (4q^2+30q-6)/(5q-1)>0.                            (4.2)

Equality in Jensen is not required of an actual graph. The opposite
bound coming from codegree<=q/2 is also satisfied:

  L*lambda_0 <= (q/2)*nL,

because q^2+6 <= (5/2)q^2 for q>=4.

For a permutation Q_ab with zero diagonal, its inner product with
W=J-I is n and ||W||_F^2=n(n-1). Adjoining this W requires the Schur
complement G-[n/(n-1)]J_L to be PSD. The only affected eigenvalue is
the constant one, so (4.2) proves this condition too. The diagonal
identity block is orthogonal to W.

4d. The repeated-pattern count remains on the n^4 scale.

The sum of squares of the full tensor entries is exactly

  n^2(2q^2-q) + q(q-1)(q-2)(q-3) = Theta(n^4).          (4.3)

The first term is precisely the contribution of the unavoidable
identity-product pairings. Formula (4.3) is compatible with (2.3) and
is one factor n below the Gowers--Long scale. The associated abstract
regular graph density would be q/n=1/5. With total graph vertex count
N=2n this would correspond to N^2/20 edges, not the unrelated earlier
N^2/4 endpoint-method threshold.

5. PRECISE GAP AND NEXT TARGET

This model survives integrality of the four-index counts, overlap
zeros, the one-fixed-point upper bound, both PSD matricizations and
their reshuffling, ambient rank, first degree moments, the Jensen
lower bound, the local codegree upper bound, and adjoining J-I.
It still need not realize actual nonnegative permutation matrices.
In particular it does not supply the common-factor/product coherence

  Q_ab Q_bc = B_a^T D_b^X B_c,

or Q_ab Q_bc=Q_ac in the perfect case. It also is not a construction
of an affine palette, a Frobenius action, or a counterexample to #810.
Perfect colorings in a Frobenius action are already covered by the
earlier conditional theorem; the unresolved case here is partial.

A genuinely new route would have to use the simultaneous actual
supports to show concentration of relative-map agreements (or a
different structure theorem). Neither a first-point collision count
nor the audited two-sided four-index Gram constraints force that
concentration. This pass proves a quantitative obstruction to those
specific mechanisms and does not claim a new general density bound.
