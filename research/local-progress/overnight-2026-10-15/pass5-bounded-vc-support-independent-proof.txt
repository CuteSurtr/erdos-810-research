BOUNDED VC SUPPORTS: AN INDEPENDENT GRAPHON/BRACKETING EXTRACTION PROOF
2026-10-15, finite_audit agent. The measure-theoretic argument below passed
formal_audit's independent check. It verifies and simplifies the transfer
proposed by the literature agent; it is not a priority claim.

APPLICATION THEOREM

Let p range over primes, A_p subset F_p^*, and beta_p:A_p->F_p arbitrary.
For each color a choose an arbitrary row domain D_a subset F_p and select
the edges (x,a*x+beta_p(a)) for x in D_a. Suppose the actual coloring is
proper and every C4 is rainbow. If the set family {D_a:a in A_p} has VC
dimension bounded by one fixed integer D, then m=o(p^2).

The proof uses the already audited near-common-domain theorem for arbitrary
beta. It does not use any algebraic structure of the domains, and proves
no effective rate. One color per nonzero slope remains part of this
application. The extraction lemma itself is an abstract binary-matrix fact.

1. THE PRECISE EXTRACTION LEMMA AND QUANTIFIER ORDER

Suppose binary matrices M_n have row and column counts q_n,s_n tending to
infinity, density at least delta>0, and row-neighborhood VC dimension <=D.
Then a SUBSEQUENCE has row and column subsets I_n,J_n satisfying

 |I_n|>=c*q_n, |J_n|>=c*s_n,
 #{(i,j) in I_n x J_n:M_n(i,j)=0}=o(q_n*s_n),             (1)

for one fixed c>0 along that subsequence. The value c can depend on the
limiting object selected from the sequence. We do not claim a bound for c
solely in terms of D,delta, or a uniform finite rate.

This quantifier order is sufficient for the application: a hypothetical
dense counterexample sequence supplies a subsequence to which (1) applies.

2. THE LIMIT AND ITS FORBIDDEN PATTERN

Use standard compactness for dense bipartite graphons to pass, after
separate measure-preserving relabelings of the two sides, to a cut-norm
limit W:[0,1]^2->[0,1]. The finite matrices are represented by rectangular
step functions U_n. They have integral at least delta, so int W>=delta.

Put k=D+1 and h=2^k. Let F_D be the bipartite binary pattern with k column
vertices and one row for every subset of these k columns. Its row family
shatters all k columns. Every finite M_n has induced density zero for this
pattern. Repeated finite vertex choices cannot create an exception: the
rows of F_D are all different, as are its columns, so any such repeated
choice forces a conflicting 0/1 requirement. By the counting lemma,

                         t_ind(F_D,W)=0.                (2)

3. THE LIMIT IS BINARY, WITHOUT A PURE-REPRESENTATIVE ASSUMPTION

If W were not 0/1 almost everywhere, some measurable set
Z={epsilon<=W<=1-epsilon} would have positive measure. A positive-density
bipartite measurable set has positive K_(h,k) density, by repeated Jensen
(equivalently the elementary complete-bipartite counting inequality).
On every such grid inside Z, each factor W or 1-W required by F_D is at
least epsilon. This would give t_ind(F_D,W)>0, contradicting (2).

Hence W is binary almost everywhere. We modify it on a null set so that
its sections define measurable binary functions for almost all rows.

4. A COUNTABLE VC FAMILY FROM THE ESSENTIAL ROW DISTRIBUTION

Regard the row section x -> W(x,.) as a random element of the separable
space L1([0,1]). Its pushforward probability measure is denoted nu, and
let K be its topological support. The row map is measurable (for example
by approximation by simple functions), and nu(K)=1. The binary functions
form a closed subset of L1, so every point of K has a binary representative.

Choose a countable L1-dense subset {f_i} of K, with measurable indicator
representatives f_i=1_(C_i). Remove from the column space the union of all
zero-measure Boolean atoms formed by finite subfamilies of the C_i. There
are countably many such atoms, so the removed set N is null. On the
remaining column space every nonempty finite Boolean atom has positive
measure.

CLAIM: {C_i minus N} has ordinary VC dimension at most D.

Otherwise choose k shattered column points and h witnessing row sets.
For each of the k columns, its incidence vector across those h sets
defines a nonempty Boolean atom. All these atoms have positive measure
after the deletion of N. Therefore, for this fixed row tuple, the
conditional column-integral of the forbidden induced pattern is positive:
it is the product of the measures of those k prescribed atoms.

This conditional integral is continuous in the h row functions under
their L1 distances. More explicitly, changing any selected row function
by L1 distance epsilon changes each Boolean-atom measure by at most
epsilon; the finite product is consequently continuous. Thus the integral
stays positive on a product of L1 neighborhoods of the witnessing rows.
Every neighborhood has positive nu-measure, since f_i belongs to K.
Integrating over these row neighborhoods makes t_ind(F_D,W)>0, contrary
to (2). This proves the claim.

The null deletion is essential: pointwise shattering by exceptional
zero-measure column atoms alone would not contradict an induced density.
Using the row-law support is equally essential: arbitrary exceptional
row sections need not have neighborhoods of positive sampling measure.

5. FINITE BRACKETING YIELDS A POSITIVE COMPLETE RECTANGLE

Use the Adams--Nobel finite-bracketing theorem for the countable VC family
from Section 4, with column Lebesgue measure and bracket width epsilon.
Countability ensures the separability hypothesis. We obtain finitely
many measurable set brackets [A_j,B_j], with

 A_j subset B_j, measure(B_j minus A_j)<=epsilon,

covering the family. After restoring the null set, regard each bracket as
a subset of L1 modulo equality almost everywhere. Such a bracket is
closed in L1: along an L1-convergent sequence one can take an almost-
everywhere convergent subsequence, preserving the two pointwise bounds.
The finite union of these closed brackets contains the L1 closure K.

Let epsilon=delta/4. Since the mean row measure is at least delta, a
positive nu-measure set of rows has measure at least delta/2. One of the
finitely many brackets contains a positive nu-measure set of these rows.
For that bracket its lower set A has measure at least delta/4. Let P be
the original row parameters whose section lies in this bracket and has
measure at least delta/2. Then measure(P)>0, and

                         W=1 a.e. on P x A.             (3)

The row-class P is measurable: bracket membership is specified by
int_A(1-W(x,y))dy=0 and int_(B^c)W(x,y)dy=0, two measurable nonnegative
integrals, together with a measurable lower bound on row measure.

The exact published input is Adams--Nobel, "Uniform approximation of
Vapnik--Chervonenkis classes", Bernoulli 18(4) (2012), 1310--1319,
Corollary 1 (preprint PDF page 5): every separable VC class has finitely
many epsilon-brackets for every epsilon>0.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4515
https://doi.org/10.3150/11-BEJ379

6. BINARY LIMITS UPGRADE ALIGNED CUT CONVERGENCE TO L1

For 0<=U_n<=1 and binary W,

 ||U_n-W||_1 = int U_n + int W - 2 int U_n W.

The first integral tends to int W by cut convergence. Also int U_n W
tends to int W^2=int W: approximate the fixed W in L1 by a finite linear
combination of rectangle indicators, use cut convergence against that
finite sum, and bound the two approximation errors by their L1 norms.
Hence

                         ||U_n-W||_1 -> 0.              (4)

In particular, (3)-(4) give missing mass o(1) on the fixed rectangle P x A.

7. PULLBACK TO ACTUAL FINITE VERTEX SETS

The relabelings need not align P or A with finite vertex cells. Let
alpha=measure(P)>0, beta=measure(A)>0. For each finite row cell i let u_i
be the fraction of its relabeled cell lying in P, and for column cell j
let v_j be the fraction lying in A. Then

 sum_i u_i=alpha*q_n, sum_j v_j=beta*s_n,
 sum_(i,j) u_i v_j (1-M_n(i,j))=o(q_n*s_n).               (5)

Retain I_n={i:u_i>=alpha/2}, J_n={j:v_j>=beta/2}.
The low-weight vertices account for at most half the total weight, so
|I_n|>=alpha*q_n/2 and |J_n|>=beta*s_n/2. Each retained pair has
u_i v_j>=alpha*beta/4, whence its total unweighted missing count is at
most 4/(alpha*beta) times the last sum in (5). This proves (1).

No step assumes that a measurable limiting rectangle is literally a union
of finite vertices. Thresholding fractional pullback weights supplies the
needed actual vertex subsets.

8. APPLICATION TO AFFINE B COLORINGS

Suppose m>=delta*p^2 along a sequence of the stated affine colorings.
Take the color-by-row support matrix M(a,x)=1_(x in D_a). Its number of
rows q=|A_p| lies between delta*p and p, and its density is at least delta.
Apply the extraction lemma to obtain along a subsequence palettes I_p
and row sets J_p, each of size Omega(p), for which only o(p^2) colored
incidences are missing from I_p x J_p.

Restrict the original graph to those colors and rows. It is still proper
and B, contains Omega(p^2) edges, and has an arbitrary affine palette with
distinct nonzero slopes. It is a near-common-domain instance with
R=o(p^2). This contradicts the theorem proved and independently audited
in pass4-arbitrary-affine-common-support-independent-audit.txt.

Therefore m=o(p^2). All small-o conclusions are obtained by contradiction
along subsequences; no uniform finite bracketing size or quantitative
graphon rate was assumed.

SOURCES AND SCOPE

Standard dense bigraphon compactness and counting continuity are used in
Section 2. Lovasz--Szegedy's treatment of excluded induced bigraphs and
thin graphons gives a stronger structural framework:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.4377 .
The direct row-law argument here avoids needing a pointwise VC assertion
for a particular pure representative, or uniform control of bracketing
numbers over all probability measures.

This proof is an application of established graph-limit and VC tools plus
the previously audited affine near-common theorem. It does not imply
bounded VC dimension for arbitrary color supports, and hence does not
resolve the unrestricted affine-support problem or Erdős #810.
