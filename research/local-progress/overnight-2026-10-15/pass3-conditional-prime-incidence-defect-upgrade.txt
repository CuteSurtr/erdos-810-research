1. THE PRECISE EXTERNAL INPUT

Assume that every finite set P of v points and every set L of w
distinct affine lines over F_p satisfy

       I(P,L) <= 3(vw)^(2/3)+v+w+vw/p.                 (ST_p)

The official abstract of Mark Lewko, "A Szemeredi--Trotter Theorem
in Arbitrary Fields", arXiv:2609.27023v3 (7 October 2026), states
this prime-field inequality. Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27023v3

The abstract was checked directly. The separate source audit also
read the proof and formalization and reported no gap; see the status
above for what was and was not verified. We use (ST_p) as the cited
external mathematical input. None of its strength is attributed to
our work.

2. QUANTITATIVE CONCLUSION USING (ST_p)

Consider a proper B-colored simple bipartite graph with both sides
F_p, where every original color is a restriction of a nonconstant
affine permutation. B means every C4 has four distinct colors.
Let d_0 be the number of original color labels, m the number of
actual edges, and

                   D=d_0*p-m

the total number of omitted colored incidences. Distinct original
labels may initially have identical affine completions.

Using (ST_p), absolute C,c>0 give

 m <= C*p*sqrt(D+1)+8D
        +C*p^2*exp[-c*(log p)^(1/12)].                 (1)

In particular, D=o(p^2) implies m=o(p^2). The same follows if the
average defect D/d_0=o(p), with the empty graph treated separately.

3. PRUNING AND THE PENCIL BOUND

Discard every color omitting at least p/8 incidences. There are at
most 8D/p such labels, so at most 8D actual edges are discarded.
Each retained class has more than 7p/8 edges. Thus their affine
completions are distinct: two equal completions would have overlapping
supports and assign different colors to the same graph edge.

Write d for the retained number of colors and D' for their total
defect. Since D'<=D and the actual graph has at most p^2 edges,

                  d < 8p/7.                            (2)

The retained affine palette is strongly progression-free: if
nontrivially A B^{-1} A=C, choose a starting row x and put
z=B^{-1}A(x). The potential four edges have colors A,B,A,C on
rows x,z and columns A(x),A(z). Each of the four support failures
excludes fewer than p/8 starting rows. Because B^{-1}A is a
nonidentity affine map, x=z excludes at most one further row.
For sufficiently large p, some x remains, producing a forbidden C4.

Let L be the largest number of retained completed lines in any
parallel or concurrent pencil. Every such pencil is a left coset
of an abelian affine subgroup of order p or p-1. Normalization
preserves A B^{-1} A=C. The previously audited quantitative Roth
input therefore gives

                  L <= C*p*exp[-c*(log p)^(1/12)].      (3)

This is the same pencil estimate as in
pass1-affine-quantitative-audit.txt and
pass2-prime-field-average-defect.txt. It is an existing Roth input,
not a new exponential-sum or progression estimate. For the even
cyclic group of order p-1, its exponent representatives embed into
the odd cyclic group of order 2(p-1)+1 without creating integer
three-term progressions; the constants remain absolute.

4. TOTAL DUAL INCIDENCE COST

Dualize each distinct completed line y=a*x+b to the point (a,b).
Every determined nonvertical dual line corresponds to a finite
intersection point of completed original lines. If its richness
is t>=2, at least t-1 colored incidences at that original point
are omitted, because at most one actual edge/color occupies it.
Summing over these points gives total nonvertical incidence count
at most 2D'. Determined vertical dual lines partition their points
by slope, so their total incidence count is at most d.

If I_* is the sum of all richnesses over all determined dual lines,

                         I_* <= 2D+d.                  (4)

The largest dual richness is precisely L from Section 3.

5. A BECK-TYPE DICHOTOMY FROM (ST_p)

For any d-point dual set with d<8p/7, let T_k denote the number of
determined lines whose richness lies in [k,2k), for dyadic k.
Applying (ST_p) to that class gives

 k*T_k <= 3(d*T_k)^(2/3)+d+(1+d/p)T_k.

Since 1+d/p<3, for k>=8 we have

 (k/2)T_k <= 3d^(2/3)T_k^(2/3)+d.

Splitting according to which summand on the right is at least
(k/4)T_k yields the convenient bound

                 T_k <= 1728*d^2/k^3+4*d/k.            (5)

Every pair of distinct dual points lies on exactly one determined
line. Fix a large dyadic K>=8. Lines with richness below K account
for at most (K/2)I_* pairs. For higher-richness lines, (5) gives

 sum_{k>=K} sum_{lines in class k} binom(richness,2)
   <= sum_{k>=K} [3456*d^2/k+8*d*k]
   <= 6912*d^2/K+16*d*L.                               (6)

The sums are dyadic and terminate at k<=L; this is why the second
term is O(dL), with no logarithmic loss.

For example choose the dyadic constant K=131072. Then
6912/K<1/16. If L<=d/256, the last term in (6) is at most d^2/16.
For d>=2, binom(d,2)>=d^2/4, so pair counting gives

 d^2/4 <= (K/2)I_*+d^2/8,
 hence I_* >= d^2/(4K).

We have proved an absolute-constant alternative:

       either L>d/256, or I_*>=d^2/(4K).               (7)

The d=0,1 cases are harmless and absorbed into constants below.

6. COMPLETING THE QUANTITATIVE BOUND

In the first case of (7), (3) bounds d by a constant multiple of
p*exp[-c*(log p)^(1/12)]. In the second case, (4) gives

                   d^2/(4K)<=2D+d.

Absorbing the linear term, or handling bounded d separately, gives
d<=C*sqrt(D+1). In either case,

 d <= C*sqrt(D+1)+C*p*exp[-c*(log p)^(1/12)].

The retained graph has at most p*d edges. Adding back at most 8D
discarded edges proves (1).

If D=o(p^2), every term on its right is o(p^2). If instead
D/d_0=o(p), then

        m=d_0*(p-D/d_0)<=p^2

implies d_0<=p/(1-o(1)), and consequently D=o(p^2). Thus the
average-defect formulation follows as claimed.

7. LIMITS

This remains a near-complete-color-class condition: it allows an
average o(p) missing incidences per color, but not an arbitrary fixed
positive missing fraction. It does not settle common masks of size
delta*p for fixed delta<1, arbitrary supports, extension fields, or
Erdos #810 in general.
