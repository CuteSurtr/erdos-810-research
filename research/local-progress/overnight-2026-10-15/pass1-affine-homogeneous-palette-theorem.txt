VANISHING DENSITY FOR HOMOGENEOUS AFFINE PALETTES
Overnight pass 1, 15 October 2026.
Complete hand proof; independently checked by root and formal-audit agents.
No Lean certificate. This is a conditional deduction from Solymosi's
known theorem; historical priority of this affine formulation is unverified.

THEOREM

Let F be a finite field of size n, let H be a subgroup of F^* of size h,
and suppose beta:F^*->F satisfies

                     beta(ua)=u beta(a)
                     for every u in H and a in F^*.                 (1)

Consider a properly colored bipartite simple graph whose two sides are
labeled by F, with at most one color a for each a in F^*. The actual
color-a edges may be an ARBITRARY subset of the matching

                     sigma_a(x)=a x+beta(a).

If every C4 is rainbow, then, uniformly as h tends to infinity,

                              m=o(n^2).                            (2)

There is no minimum support-size condition and no bounded-deficit
condition. The number of colors is automatically at most n-1. Unused
slopes are allowed. No assumption that the affine group has size O(n)
or that the palette has a small global difference set is imposed.

More exactly, put L=(n-1)/h and let R_B(H) be the maximum number of
positions in a B-colored partial group table on H, with its prescribed
coloring (x,y)->y x^(-1). Then

                         m <= L^2 R_B(H)+L.                        (3)

The proof works for the cyclic subgroup H without depending on the
extension degree or characteristic of F.

ESTABLISHED INPUT

Solymosi, The (7,4)-conjecture in finite groups, Theorem 5,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133 (PDF page 4), implies uniformly over
finite groups H that

                         R_B(H)/|H|^2 -> 0
                         as |H| -> infinity.                       (4)

The theorem there uses a rectangle satisfying alpha beta=delta gamma.
Under the coordinate change (x,y)->(y,x^(-1)), this is precisely a
rectangle with equal diagonal colors y x^(-1). The detailed translation
is in ../multicolor-2026-10-15/group-representation-vanishing-density.txt.
The group-table statement is known; it is not a new theorem of this
research pass. The prior small-difference theorem also supplies (4),
but the established direct input suffices.

PROOF OF THE EXACT BLOCK BOUND

Partition F^* into its L multiplicative cosets gH. Partition the color
palette accordingly. Work with one family whose slopes have the form
a=gu, u in H. By (1), its completions satisfy

              sigma_(gu)(x)=gu x+u beta(g)
                            =u[gx+beta(g)].                         (5)

Within this color family, relabel the left vertices by the bijection

                         x'=gx+beta(g).

The right labels are unchanged. Its color-gu edges are now of the form
y=u x'. This operation preserves the graph and the equality pattern
of colors, so the retained subgraph is still proper and every C4 is
rainbow.

If x'=0, then y=0. Since the graph is simple and each edge has just one
color, at most one actual edge in this family occurs at (0,0). Delete
that edge if present. Every remaining edge has x',y in F^* and satisfies
y/x'=u in H. Hence its endpoints lie in the SAME multiplicative coset
vH of H in F^*.

This splits the family into L blocks indexed by vH. On one such block
write x'=v x_0 and y=v y_0, with x_0,y_0 in H. Equation (5) becomes

                           y_0=u x_0.

Thus the block is exactly a partial group table on H. The original
color gu corresponds bijectively to u, so equal and unequal colors
are preserved; no merging of distinct original colors occurs. The
rainbow-C4 condition is consequently inherited by this group table.
Its edge count is at most R_B(H).

There are L color families and L blocks in each, plus at most one
exceptional zero edge per family. Families partition the original
colors, so their edge counts add even though they use different row
relabelings. This proves m<=L^2 R_B(H)+L.

Divide by n^2. Since Lh=n-1,

       m/n^2 <= [(n-1)^2/n^2] [R_B(H)/h^2] +(n-1)/(h n^2).

The first term tends to zero by (4) when h->infinity; the second is at
most 1/(hn). This proves the theorem.

EXAMPLES AND EXTENSIONS

1. FROBENIUS PALETTES, WITH ARBITRARY SUPPORTS.

   Let F=F_(s^k), where k may vary, and use beta(a)=a^s. For
   H=F_s^*, beta(ua)=u beta(a), so h=s-1. Consequently every B-colored
   subgraph of this affine palette has o(n^2) edges as s->infinity,
   without any restriction on the color supports or on k.

   More generally, the same conclusion holds for beta given by any
   F_s-linear map F->F, including linearized polynomials
   beta(a)=sum_j c_j a^(s^j). Only its homogeneity over F_s is used;
   additivity is not needed in the theorem itself.

2. THE PREVIOUS DEFICIT BARRIER IS A SOLVED PALETTE CLASS.

   In pass1-affine-sublinear-deficit-barrier.txt, n=s^2 and beta(a)=a^s
   give a nearly complete properly colored graph, with deficit s+1 per
   class, low global energy, and an unbounded pencil-cover requirement.
   The present theorem shows that every inherited B-colored subgraph
   of that example retains only o(n^2) edges. Thus the obstruction to
   a bounded pencil cover does not prevent a different proof for its
   particular palette: the relevant group-table blocks have size h,
   and their total possible edge capacity remains O(n^2).

3. FIXED TRANSLATIONS AND BOUNDED UNIONS.

   The conclusion is unchanged if beta(a)-b_0, rather than beta(a),
   satisfies (1), for one fixed b_0: first replace y by y-b_0.

   It also holds for a bounded union of such palettes, with potentially
   different subgroups H_i and functions beta_i, provided min_i |H_i|
   tends to infinity. Partition colors among their assigned palettes,
   apply the theorem to each inherited B-colored subgraph, and add the
   bounded number of o(n^2) estimates. Each palette must still have at
   most one color for each slope; a fixed multiplicity can be handled
   by making that many separate copies of a palette in the partition.

4. SCALAR-MONOMIAL EXAMPLES.

   For beta(a)=a^k, any subgroup satisfying u^(k-1)=1 for every u in H
   gives (1). The theorem applies whenever the size of such a subgroup
   grows along the chosen fields. In contrast, beta(a)=a^2 has only
   the trivial such subgroup. Therefore this theorem does not dispose
   of the quadratic affine family from the separate barrier note.

SCOPE AND REMAINING GAP

The proof handles an unbounded number of abelian color families because
their orbit blocks shrink in precisely the compensating proportion.
It is also a concrete application of the earlier uniform scale-free
small-difference theorem, after a piecewise palette argument; it does
not transcend that theorem plus partitioning. Indeed the palette is
covered by L cosets of subgroups of order h, with total cover volume
Lh=n-1. For a fixed delta>0, pieces containing fewer than delta h
actual colors together contribute at most delta n^2 edges. On each
remaining piece the relative difference set has size at most h, hence
at most 1/delta times its palette size. The prior uniform m=o(q n)
theorem applies, since these palette sizes tend to infinity with h.
Summing its uniform estimates and then sending delta to zero proves
the same conclusion. This argument needs no special semiregularity of
the action. The block proof above is an independent, direct route using
only the known Solymosi input, and gives the explicit bound (3).

It is not an inference that every arbitrary affine intercept function
has this homogeneity. For an unrestricted beta, H may be trivial and
(3) yields no asymptotic improvement. The general affine problem with
fixed positive color-support densities remains open in this pass, as
does the unrestricted Erdos #810 problem.
