SUPPORT-WEIGHTED CROSSED REFLECTIONS WITH A COMMON FOURIER MAJORANT
15 October 2026, continuation 5. Complete analytic proof.

Status: restricted-class result with independent hand audits. No
historical-priority claim, Lean certificate, or unrestricted #810
conclusion is asserted. This argument keeps the actual color-dependent
supports; it does not assume the masked operator is an expander.

1. SETUP AND STATEMENT

Let p be an odd prime, A subset F_p*, d=|A|, and beta:A->F_p.
For each a choose D_a subset F_p and put in precisely the edges

                  (x, ax+beta(a)), x in D_a,

with color a. Assume the selected graph is simple and proper and
every C4 is rainbow (B). Let m=sum_a |D_a|.

Use normalized Fourier coefficients

 hat D_a(k)=(1/p) sum_x 1_Da(x)e_p(-kx).

The JOINT Fourier majorant is

             W=sum_(k in F_p) sup_(a in A) |hat D_a(k)|.

For the empty palette or empty graph all conclusions are immediate.
For a nonempty graph, Fourier inversion implies W>=1.

THEOREM A. If beta is a polynomial of fixed degree 2<=D<p, then

                   m <= C_D p^(47/24) W^(2/3).               (1)

Thus W=o(p^(1/16)) implies m=o(p^2). Domains need not coincide,
be nested, or be intervals in any common ordering.

More generally (1) holds with a constant depending on C_0 if the
coefficient points {(a,beta(a)):a in A} have at most C_0 sqrt(p)
points on any affine line. This uses the published PRRW energy bound.

The joint quantity W must not be replaced by
sup_a sum_k |hat D_a(k)|: interchanging the supremum and sum
would invalidate the operator error bound below.

2. WEIGHTED COMPLETED OPERATORS

Write sigma_a(x)=ax+beta(a) and

 tau_a(x,y)=((y-beta(a))/a, ax+beta(a)).

For arbitrary COMPLEX weights w(a), set S_w=sum_a w(a)U_(tau_a),
where U_tau f=f composed with tau. Each permutation preserves
constants and mean-zero functions. Let P be orthogonal projection
onto constants in counting-measure l2(F_p^2):

                 Pf=(1/p^2)(sum_(x,y) f(x,y)) 1.

Let E_aff be the quotient energy of the affine palette, L its
maximum coefficient-plane line occupancy, and E_x(A) its
multiplicative slope energy. Put

 Lambda^4=max{(p-1)E_aff+d^3,
                         p(L+2)d^2+2sqrt(p)E_x(A)}.          (2)

Then

       ||S_w-(sum_a w(a))P|| <= Lambda ||w||_infinity.       (3)

PROOF. The invariant Fourier product blocks are the same as in
Pass 4. For t!=0 the block matrix is

 M_w(r,s)=w(rs/t)1_A(rs/t)
                e_p((r-s)beta(rs/t)/(rs/t)), r,s!=0.

It need not be Hermitian. Use its fourth SINGULAR moment
tr(M_w M_w* M_w M_w*) instead. With a=rs/t, q=u/r, h=v/s,
the weights are

 w(a) conjugate(w(qa)) w(qha) conjugate(w(ha))

and the phase remains exactly alpha*r+gamma/r from the Pass 4
polynomial-intercept proof. The weights do not depend on r.
At most (L+2)d^2 supported triples have alpha=gamma=0;
there are E_x(A)<=d^3 supported triples altogether. The classical
Kloosterman bound, including the elementary one-zero cases, gives

 tr(M_w M_w* M_w M_w*)
   <=||w||_infinity^4 [p(L+2)d^2+2sqrt(p)E_x(A)].

On the coordinate-axis block, apply the affine permutation
character calculation to C_w C_w* C_w C_w*, where
C_w=sum_a w(a)P_(sigma_a) on mean-zero functions on F_p.
Identity words are bounded in absolute value by
(p-1)E_aff ||w||_infinity^4. Nontrivial translations have
character -1, but their coefficients can be complex: they CANNOT
simply be dropped as in the unweighted proof. Bound them in
absolute value. All slope-one four-words number at most d^3,
since three palette slopes determine the fourth. Other affine
maps have zero mean-zero character. This gives the first term
in (2). The two off-diagonal axis blocks have the same norm,
by complex conjugation and adjoint.

The singular fourth moments dominate the fourth powers of the
respective operator norms. Together the blocks prove (3). QED.

For fixed-degree nonlinear beta, E_aff=O_D(d^2), L<=D,
E_x(A)<=d^3 and d<=p give Lambda=O_D(p^(7/8)).
Alternatively PRRW, Theorem 2.1, gives
E_aff<<d^(5/2)+Ld^2 with one color per slope; hence L<=C_0 sqrt(p)
also gives Lambda=O_(C_0)(p^(7/8)).

3. THE MASKED OPERATOR AND ITS APPROXIMATION

Let M_g multiply a function on F_p^2 by g(x). Define

 T_D=sum_a M_(1_Da) U_(tau_a) M_(1_Da).

This is selfadjoint. It includes a crossed transition only when
both closing color-a incidences are actual. Consequently

                       <1_E,T_D 1_E>=m.                    (4)

Indeed a non-fixed internal transition would be an actual
nonrainbow C4. A fixed transition with nonzero mask is exactly
the actual color-a edge; simplicity makes the total count m.

For k in F_p put M_k=M_(e_p(kx)). Fourier expansion is finite
and exact:

       T_D=sum_(k,l) M_k S_(w_kl) M_l,
       w_kl(a)=hat D_a(k)hat D_a(l).

Using (3), replace each S_(w_kl) by (sum_a w_kl(a))P.
The resulting operator is

       T_0=(1/p^2) sum_a |v_a><v_a|,
       v_a(x,y)=1_Da(x).

This follows directly by Fourier inversion on the two row
coordinates; D_a is real so the resulting rank-one operators
are positive. Every M_k is unitary, and therefore

                     ||T_D-T_0|| <= Lambda W^2.             (5)

This step uses sum_(k,l) sup_a |hat D_a(k)hat D_a(l)|<=W^2.
No color-independent support function or random mask is assumed.

4. POSITIVE MAIN TERM AND THE EDGE BOUND

Let r_x be the actual degree of row x. Properness and simplicity
give r_x=sum_a 1_Da(x), and sum_x r_x=m. Hence

 <1_E,T_0 1_E>
   =(1/p^2)sum_a (sum_x 1_Da(x)r_x)^2
   >=(1/(d p^2))(sum_x r_x^2)^2
   >=m^4/(d p^4).                                        (6)

The first inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz over colors, the second
over the p rows. Since ||1_E||^2=m, (4)--(6) imply the general
master inequality

                      m^3 <= d p^4(1+Lambda W^2).            (7)

For Lambda<=C_D p^(7/8) and d<=p, taking cube roots gives

        m<=p^(5/3)+C_D p^(47/24)W^(2/3).

Since W>=1 for a nonempty graph, this implies (1). The ratio of
its right side to p^2 is C_D (W/p^(1/16))^(2/3), proving the
vanishing-density criterion.

5. CONCRETE COLOR-DEPENDENT BOHR-BOX SUPPORTS

Choose nonzero frequencies k_1,...,k_r in F_p, shared by the
colors but otherwise arbitrary and allowed to depend on p.
For each a and j choose any cyclic interval I_(a,j) in F_p,
of arbitrary position and length. Suppose

             D_a={x:k_j x in I_(a,j) for every j}.

The supports may have many components in the usual row ordering.
The normalized Fourier coefficients of ANY cyclic interval
satisfy a common bound

 b(0)=1,  b(l)<=min(1,C/min(l,p-l)) for 1<=l<=p-1,
                        sum_l b(l)<=C log(2p).

This is the finite geometric-series formula; the interval's
position changes only a phase. Multiplication by k_j permutes
the frequency indices. The Fourier transform of a product is
convolution, so convolution of these r common nonnegative
majorants bounds |hat D_a| for every a. Consequently

                       W <= (C log(2p))^r.                  (8)

For fixed r and fixed nonlinear polynomial degree D, (1) gives

             m <<_D p^(47/24)(C log(2p))^(2r/3).

The exact criterion (8)=o(p^(1/16)) even permits growing r.
For example,

 limsup_(p->infinity) r(p) log log p / log p < 1/16

suffices. Thus this is not merely a theorem for a single fixed
VC-dimension class. It still requires a shared list of frequencies
and does not cover arbitrary color-dependent frequency systems.

6. OPTIONAL QUALITATIVE EXTENSION FOR ARBITRARY INTERCEPTS

If W is uniformly bounded, arbitrary beta also gives m=o(p^2).
Under a hypothetical m>=epsilon p^2, discard colors with fewer
than epsilon p/2 actual edges. At least epsilon p^2/2 edges
remain, d'=Theta(p), and the joint W cannot increase.

A coefficient line carrying Omega(p) retained colors would give
Omega(p^2) actual edges in a partial multiplicative-group table,
contradicting Solymosi's Theorem 5 after deleting the single
exceptional vertex-pair edge. Thus L=o(p). PRRW then gives
E_aff=o(p^3). Formula (2) implies Lambda=o(p), and (7) contradicts
the retained positive density when W=O(1).

This is an additional hypothesis, not a deduction that arbitrary
heavy B supports have bounded W. No growing-W rate for arbitrary
intercepts is asserted in this argument.
