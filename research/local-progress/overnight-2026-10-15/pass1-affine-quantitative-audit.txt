QUANTITATIVE VANISHING DENSITY FOR AFFINE COLORS WITH SMALL DEFECT
15 October 2026. Independent hand audit and strengthening of the parent
agent's deficit-count argument. No Lean certificate, no finite search,
and no assertion of historical novelty or a general solution of #810.

THEOREM.
There are absolute constants C,c>0 such that the following holds.
Let F be a finite field of size n, and let a B-colored bipartite graph
on two copies of F have every color represented by a nonconstant affine
permutation, with at most r of its n points omitted. If n>4r+1, then

  number of colors <= C(r+1)n exp[-c(log n)^(1/12)],
  number of edges  <= C(r+1)n^2 exp[-c(log n)^(1/12)].    (T)

In particular, the edge count is o(n^2) if

  log(r+1)=o((log n)^(1/12)).                            (V)

The proof combines an elementary incidence inequality, the actual B
support condition, and an existing quantitative Roth theorem. The
representation and near-perfect-support assumptions remain essential
to this proof. No claim is made for arbitrary fixed positive fractions
of the affine color lines.

1. AN EXACT LINEAR DEFECT INEQUALITY

The n>4r+1 assumption implies n>2r, so different colors cannot have
identical affine completions: two disjoint actual color classes on
the same n-point line cannot each have n-r>n/2 points. Let d be the
number of distinct color lines. If d=0 there is nothing to prove.
Let L be the largest number of these lines in a parallel pencil or
a pencil concurrent at a finite point.

For each finite point p incident to m_p>=2 color lines, at most one
of these lines retains p in its actual color class. Thus at least
m_p-1 incidences at p have been omitted. Summing over such points,

  sum_p (m_p-1) <= r*d.                                (1.1)

Every nonparallel pair of color lines meets at one finite point, and
m_p<=L. Therefore their number is

  sum_p binom(m_p,2) <= (L/2) sum_p(m_p-1) <= L*r*d/2.  (1.2)

If the sizes of the parallel classes are h_i, every h_i<=L, so the
number of parallel pairs is

  sum_i binom(h_i,2) <= (L-1)*d/2.                      (1.3)

Adding (1.2) and (1.3) yields

  d(d-1)/2 <= (L-1)*d/2 + L*r*d/2,

and hence the EXACT inequality

                       d <= (r+1)*L.                  (1.4)

This does not require B; proper assignment of one color to each
actual point is enough. In particular it is stronger, for bounding
d, than covering the palette by (r+1)^2 pencils and pigeonholing.

2. B AND SMALL DEFECT FORBID ALL STRONG PERMUTATION PROGRESSIONS

Use composition of affine maps as group multiplication. Suppose three
color completions A,B,C satisfy

                       C=A B^(-1) A.                  (2.1)

If A=B then C=A. Suppose instead A!=B. For any first row x put

  y_1=A(x),     y_2=B(x),     z=A^(-1)B(x).

Then C(z)=A(x) and A(z)=B(x), so the completed four-edge rectangle
has color matrix

      A  B
      C  A.

The conditions that its four edges are actual fail for at most 4r
values of x: the missing sets for A at x, B at x, C at z, and A at z
each have at most r preimages, since x -> z is a permutation. A and
B are distinct affine maps, so A(x)=B(x) holds at at most one point.
Moreover z=x if and only if A(x)=B(x). Removing this one possible
degeneracy, at least n-4r-1>0 choices of x remain.

Such a choice gives a genuine nonrainbow C4, contradicting B. Thus
(2.1) has only the solution A=B=C within the palette. The case C=B
with A!=B is also forbidden; this is the strong progression convention
which includes repeated endpoints in groups with 2-torsion.

3. NORMALIZING EACH PENCIL TO AN ABELIAN GROUP

A parallel pencil lies in a coset of the additive translation group
of F. A pencil through (s,t) consists of T_t M_a T_(-s) and is the
left coset T_(t-s) H_s, where H_s={T_s M_a T_(-s):a in F*} is a
conjugate of the multiplicative dilation group.

Left translation of a group set preserves strong progression-freeness:
if A=g*a, B=g*b, C=g*c, then C=A B^(-1) A is exactly
c=a b^(-1) a. Consequently every pencil piece normalizes to a
strongly three-term-progression-free subset of either (F,+) or the
cyclic group F* of order n-1.

4. PRIMARY QUANTITATIVE INPUT AND THE EXPONENT 1/12

Schildkraut, "Abelian structure in approximate groups and Alon's
conjecture on Ramsey Cayley graphs," arXiv:2512.15125v1, Theorem 11.4,
states the following quantitative Roth bound: a progression-free
subset of a finite abelian group of odd order N has size at most

                     N exp[-c_0(log N)^(1/12)]          (4.1)

for an absolute c_0>0. The paper attributes this to the Bloom--Sisask
version of the Kelley--Meka improvement. Source checked directly:
https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15125v1#S11.SS2 .

The slower exponent 1/144 of its local Roth Lemma 9.2 is unnecessary
for these two specific ambient abelian groups. Here are all torsion
and size cases needed to use (4.1).

4a. Additive pencils.

If F has odd characteristic, (F,+) has odd order n and (4.1) applies
directly. In characteristic 2, a strongly progression-free subset of
(F,+) has size at most one: any two distinct elements x,x+h give the
forbidden strong progression x,x+h,x+2h=x. This is stronger than the
claimed bound after adjusting the absolute constant for small n.

4b. Multiplicative pencils, including even cyclic order.

Write M=n-1 and identify F* with Z/MZ using a generator. Represent the
normalized pencil subset by exponents A subset {0,...,M-1}. It contains
no nonconstant integer three-term progression, because any such
progression reduces to a nontrivial progression modulo M.

Now embed these same integer representatives into the ODD cyclic group
Z/(2M+1)Z. If representatives u,v,w in A satisfy u+w=2v modulo 2M+1,
then

                 |u+w-2v| <= 2(M-1) < 2M+1,

so the equation holds as an integer equation. It must therefore be
trivial. This embedding is progression-free, so (4.1), with N=2M+1,
implies

  |A| <= (2n-1) exp[-c_0(log(2n-1))^(1/12)]
       <= 2n exp[-c_0(log n)^(1/12)].                    (4.2)

The case M=1 is immediate. No assumption that n-1 is odd is needed.

Combining both types of pencil and adjusting absolute constants gives

                         L <= C n exp[-c(log n)^(1/12)]. (4.3)

5. FINISH AND QUANTIFIER CHECK

Substitute (4.3) in (1.4), then multiply by n because each color has
at most n edges. This proves (T). All constants are absolute and
independent of the field, characteristic, r, and palette.

If (V) holds, r=n^o(1), so n>4r+1 holds automatically for sufficiently
large n. The factor r+1 is absorbed by the stretched-log exponential
decay, proving the stated o(n^2) conclusion. More explicitly, if
r+1<=exp[(c/2)(log n)^(1/12)], (T) is at most
C n^2 exp[-(c/2)(log n)^(1/12)].

This is a rigorously checked extension beyond fixed defect, obtained
from existing quantitative Roth bounds and an elementary structural
reduction. It does not supply affine representations for general
B colorings, or a bound when colors omit a positive fraction of n.
