PRIMARY SOURCE CHECKED

Stevens--de Zeeuw, An improved point-line incidence bound over arbitrary
fields, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.06284 .
Corollary 14, printed page 16, states: for d points over characteristic
p with d<<p^(7/6), either Omega(d) points lie on a line or the point set
determines Omega(d^(8/7)) lines. Its proof, and Theorem 3, provide the
dyadic estimates used below. The theorem and the displayed exponents
were independently read in the primary PDF.

SETUP AND DUAL INCIDENCE BUDGET

Let a B-colored graph on two copies of F_p have d distinct affine color
completions, each missing at most r of its p edges. Distinctness follows
from properness when p>2r. Dualize y=ax+b to the point (a,b).

A nonvertical line determined by the dual points has equation
b=-x a+y and corresponds to one original intersection point (x,y).
If t>=2 original color lines pass through that point, properness
forces at least t-1 missing incidences there. Therefore the total
number of nonvertical determined lines is at most rd, and their total
incidence count with the d dual points is at most 2rd, using t<=2(t-1).

Vertical determined lines correspond to parallel classes. They number
at most d/2 and have total incidence count at most d, since their point
sets are disjoint. Thus the determined dual lines have incidence budget

                         I <= d(2r+1).                (1)

Their maximum richness L is exactly the maximum number of original
palette lines in any parallel or concurrent pencil.

The B condition and p>4r+1 imply strong progression-freeness of the
completion palette, as audited in pass1-affine-quantitative-independent-
audit.txt. The quantitative Roth bound then gives

                     L <= C p exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)].  (2)

FIRST CONSEQUENCE: THE DIRECT BECK DICHOTOMY

If r<p/4, properness and d(p-r)<=p^2 give d<2p, so the characteristic
condition d<<p^(7/6) holds for large p. Corollary 14 and the determined-
line budget imply either L>=c_0 d, or d=O((r+1)^7).
Together with (2) and m<=dp, this gives

       m <= C p(r+1)^7 + C p^2 exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)].

In particular r=o(p^(1/7)) suffices for m=o(p^2).

STRONGER CONSEQUENCE: r=o(p^(4/7)) SUFFICES

The formal-audit agent's dyadic refinement also passes independent
audit. Suppose for contradiction that r=o(p^(4/7)) but m>=epsilon p^2
along a sequence. Then epsilon p<=d<2p, so d=Theta(p), r=o(d^(4/7)),
and (2) gives L=o(d). By (1), I=o(d^(11/7)).

Fix a sufficiently large absolute constant C_0 and put K=C_0 d^(3/7).
The pairs of dual points on determined lines of richness below K total
at most K I/2=o(d^2).

For each remaining dyadic richness range [k,2k), let T be its number of
lines. Since k>=K, the incidence budget gives

                      T <= I/k = o(d^(8/7)).

Split these ranges into three cases.

1. T>d^(7/8).

   The size comparison needed for Theorem 3 holds because
   d^(7/8)<T<d^(8/7) eventually. Its characteristic condition holds:

                  d^(-2)T^13 <= O(d^(90/7))=o(p^15).

   Therefore kT<=O(d^(11/15)T^(11/15)), which rearranges to

                      T<=O(d^(11/4)k^(-15/4)).

   This range contributes O(k^2 T)<=O(d^(11/4)k^(-7/4)) pairs.
   Summing geometrically over k>=K gives

                      O(C_0^(-7/4)d^2).              (3)

2. d^(1/2)<=T<=d^(7/8).

   The elementary incidence inequality I_range<=sqrt(d)T+d gives
   k=O(sqrt(d)). Hence the pair count per range is O(dT), at most
   O(d^(15/8)). There are O(log d) ranges, so the total is o(d^2).

3. T<d^(1/2).

   The same elementary inequality gives kT=O(d), so the pair count
   per range is O(dk). The geometric sum of the dyadic k values is
   O(L), yielding O(dL)=o(d^2).

Every pair of distinct dual points determines exactly one line. Thus
their total pair count must be binomial(d,2). After C_0 is fixed large
enough, (3), the low-richness contribution, and Cases 2--3 sum to less
than binomial(d,2) for all sufficiently large d. This contradiction
proves the claimed o(p^2) bound.

TOTAL-DEFICIT PRUNING AND THE QUANTITATIVE FINAL FORM

The stronger final formulation requested by the root and formal-audit
agents also passes. For the ORIGINAL coloring let

                         D=sum_c (p-|E_c|).

No common upper bound on individual deficits is assumed here. Discard
every color with deficit at least p/8. There are at most 8D/p such
colors, and each has at most p edges, so the edge loss is at most 8D.

Each remaining class has more than 7p/8 edges. Consequently:

 * its completion is distinct from every other retained completion,
   since two disjoint actual domains of that size cannot lie on the
   same affine line;
 * the number d of retained colors is less than 8p/7;
 * a putative nontrivial affine progression C=A B^(-1) A loses fewer
   than p/2 choices of initial row to its four support omissions, and
   at most one further row to degeneracy. For p>2 at least one valid
   rectangle remains. Thus the retained palette is strongly
   progression-free, including its pencils.

The retained dual incidence budget is

                          I<=2D+d.                   (4)

The dyadic proof above can be read quantitatively: there are absolute
constants c_1,c_2>0 such that, for sufficiently large d<8p/7, either

                      I>=c_1 d^(11/7)
             or       L>=c_2 d.                      (5)

To verify the quantifiers, first choose the constant C_0 in K large
enough that the high-richness tail is a small fraction of d^2. Next
choose c_1 small enough that the low-richness term K I is a small
fraction, and c_2 small enough for the O(dL) term. The intermediate
dyadic contribution is o(d^2) with no additional parameter. These
choices make the no-alternative hypothesis contradict binomial(d,2).
The characteristic condition holds uniformly for d<8p/7 once d,p
are large; bounded d is handled separately by the final constants.

In the first alternative, (4) gives

                  c_1 d^(11/7)<=2D+d.

For d above a fixed constant, the final d is absorbed into the left
side, yielding d<=C(D+1)^(7/11). In the second alternative, the robust
progression exclusion and the previously audited quantitative Roth
bound give d<=C p exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)]. Restoring discarded colors
and using at most p edges per retained color proves

  m <= C p(D+1)^(7/11) + 8D
                         + C p^2 exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)].              (6)

In particular D=o(p^(11/7)) implies m=o(p^2). Formula (6) permits
arbitrary individual deficits; its conclusion is useful when their
total is small. This verifies the final pruning argument and each
exponent in the quantitative bound independently.

VERDICT AND SCOPE

The characteristic restriction, comparability range, and every exponent
in the 4/7 refinement and total-deficit formulation check. This is a
conditional prime-field theorem, including the per-color regime
r=o(p^(4/7)) and the total-deficit regime D=o(p^(11/7)). It neither
settles arbitrary positive-density supports of the quadratic palette
nor the unrestricted Erdos problem. The proof combines established
incidence/Roth inputs with the support-induced incidence budget.
