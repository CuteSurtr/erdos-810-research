A COMMUTING LINEARIZATION THAT LOSES BOTH DENSITY AND VERTEX INCIDENCES
Overnight pass 2, 15 October 2026.
Root-agent construction with an independent projection audit.
Exact algebra, no computation. No historical novelty claim.

1. THE NEW COMMUTING ACTION

Let p be an odd prime and put Omega=F_p x F_p^*. For each a in F_p^*
define a permutation

                  M_a(x,t)=(a x+a^2 t, a t).           (1)

Using the coordinates (u,t)=(x/t,t), this becomes

                         (u,t)->(u+a,at).             (2)

These permutations are translations in the abelian group

                         G=(F_p,+) x (F_p^*,times),

acting regularly on itself. The palette corresponds to

                         S={(a,a):a in F_p^*}.

This is the classical Ruzsa Sidon construction in product-group
coordinates, not a new Sidon set. If a=theta^j for a primitive root,
the coordinates become (theta^j,j) in F_p x Z/(p-1), equivalent by
the Chinese remainder theorem to the construction described in
Martin--O'Bryant, Constructions of Generalized Sidon Sets, Section
2.2.1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0408081 . That primary research
paper also proves its generalized version. The new use here is only
an audit of why this encoding cannot transfer the original problem.

2. ITS COMPLETE COLORED BIPARTITE GRAPH IS C4-FREE

An equality of products of two palette elements is equivalent to

                         a+c=b+d,       ac=bd.

The sum and product determine the unordered pair as the two roots of
a quadratic. Thus {a,c}={b,d}. Equivalently every NONIDENTITY ordered
quotient of distinct palette elements is unique: its two coordinates
are (a-b,a/b), which determine b=(a-b)/(a/b-1), then a.

For a bipartite group-table graph generated by S, a genuine C4 would
give two different representations of one nonidentity ordered quotient.
The uniqueness above excludes this. Hence the full graph consisting
of ALL M_a matching edges is C4-free. Every subgraph, regardless of
the supports used to select its edges, is therefore B-colored.

Its two sides each have v=p(p-1) vertices, its degree is p-1, and it
has p(p-1)^2 edges. Its density relative to v^2 is 1/p.

3. EDGEWISE REPLICATION OF THE ORIGINAL QUADRATIC SUPPORTS

Suppose original color a has actual domain D_a in the matching
sigma_a(u)=au+a^2. Encode each original colored edge by the p-1 edges
of (1) whose inputs are

                         (ut,t),       t in F_p^*.

Their outputs are

                      (t sigma_a(u), at).             (3)

This selection makes exactly p-1 new edges per original colored edge.
If the original has m edges, the new graph has m'=(p-1)m edges, on
two sides each of size v=p(p-1). Therefore

                         m'/v^2 = m/[p^2(p-1)].       (4)

A positive original density m/p^2 becomes a density smaller by the
factor p-1. The dense partial-group-table theorem cannot be applied
at a fixed positive density to these new graphs.

4. PROJECTION CAVEAT: THIS IS NOT A GRAPH LIFT OF THE ORIGINAL COLORING

The operation in Section 3 is valid as an edgewise encoding, but there
are no natural fixed vertex projections recovering the original graph.
In (u,t) coordinates, the natural left projection is u. Formula (2)
then sends it to u+a, whereas the original right vertex was au+a^2.

In (3), recovering the original right vertex requires dividing the
first output coordinate by the INPUT t. The second output coordinate
is at, so recovering that input t requires the edge color a. This is
not a function of the right vertex alone.

The failure is explicit even if one tries to choose some other right
projection while keeping the natural left projection. A fixed right
vertex (v,s) in the (u,t) coordinates receives a color-a edge from
(v-a,s/a). That original edge has right endpoint

                         a(v-a)+a^2=av.

For v!=0, two different incoming colors require different original
right endpoints. In the complete new graph all p-1 colors enter the
vertex, so no fixed right projection is possible. With selected supports
the same failure occurs whenever v-a belongs to D_a for two distinct
a at some v!=0. In fact, if no such v existed, the total number of
original colored edges would be at most (p-1)+(p-1)=2p-2: at most one
incoming color for each nonzero v, and at most p-1 for v=0. Thus any
m>2p-2 already forces this projection failure.

Thus saying merely that the construction 'preserves B' is misleading.
It always produces a C4-free graph, independently of whether the
original coloring is B, because original right-vertex incidences and
cycles are not reflected. It is not a completion or a bounded-fiber
graph cover to which an argument can transfer original C4s.

CONCLUSION

This commuting representation of the polynomial expressions is a valid
algebraic construction. It does not solve the quadratic palette problem:
the edge density falls to order 1/p and the original graph's incidence
structure is lost. Both losses must be addressed by any proposed
linearization-based reduction, rather than assuming that an algebraic
commuting action gives an equivalent colored-graph representation.
