THE FOUR-SLOT QUADRATIC VARIETY AND A CUBIC MIXING OBSTRUCTION
15 October 2026, overnight pass 3. Analytic algebra only; no finite graph search.

Status: elementary identities and estimates proved below. These are barriers
to proposed counting arguments, not a proof or counterexample to Erdős #810.
No historical originality claim is made.

1. EXACT PARAMETRIZATION AND COUNT

Work over F_p, p odd. The four ordered-root slots are

  (a,b), (a,c), (d,e), (f,g),       a,d,f != 0,

with the other coordinates arbitrary, and equations

  a+b=d+e,  ac=de,  a+c=f+g,  ab=fg.                  (1)

The associated graph edge of (r,u) has row -(r+u), column -ru,
and color r. Its repeated-color rectangle is nondegenerate iff b!=c.
This is exactly the convention of pass2-quadratic-removal-target-audit.txt.

Put Delta=df-a^2. If Delta!=0, there is exactly one solution for each
choice of nonzero a,d,f:

  P=d^2-ad+af-a^2,             Q=f^2-af+ad-a^2,
  b=fP/Delta,                 c=dQ/Delta,
  e=aQ/Delta,                 g=aP/Delta.              (2)

One way to derive (2) is to solve the two linear equations

  db-ac=d(d-a),               ab-fc=f(a-f),

and then set e=a+b-d and g=a+c-f. Direct subtraction gives

  b-c=(d-f)(d-a)(f-a)/Delta.                          (3)

Thus the generic parametrization is nondegenerate exactly when a,d,f
are pairwise distinct.

If Delta=0, write d=ar and f=a/r. Consistency of the two linear
equations is equivalent to

  (r-1)^2(r+1)=0.

There are exactly two exceptional families:

  d=f=a:     b=c,        e=g=b;
  d=f=-a:    b+c=-2a,    e=-c, g=-b.                  (4)

The first is degenerate. The second is nondegenerate except when b=-a.
It is the antipodal two-color rectangle family.

For fixed a, there are (p-1)(p-2) choices of (d,f) with Delta!=0.
The two exceptional families each contribute p choices. Consequently
the exact total number X_p of solutions of (1) is

  |X_p|=(p-1)^2(p-2)+2p(p-1)
       =(p-1)(p^2-p+2).                              (5)

For generic nondegenerate solutions, start with ordered pairwise
distinct nonzero a,d,f, then exclude Delta=0. For each a the latter
exclusions are r!=1,-1, of which there are p-3. Hence the generic
nondegenerate count is (p-1)(p-3)^2. Adding the nondegenerate second
family in (4) gives

  |X_p^nondeg|=(p-1)(p-3)^2+(p-1)^2
             =(p-1)(p^2-5p+8).                      (6)

As a separate check, the degenerate count is (p-1)(4p-6): for b!=0,a
there are four choices of the two crossed root orders, while b=0
and b=a each give one. This is exactly the difference of (5) and (6).

2. AN EXACT CUBIC PHASE IDENTITY

On every solution of (1), including exceptional and degenerate ones,

  a^2 b-a^2 c + de(d+e)-fg(f+g)=0.                   (7)

Indeed de(d+e)=ac(a+b)=a^2c+abc, whereas
fg(f+g)=ab(a+c)=a^2b+abc.

Write e_p(t)=exp(2 pi i t/p). Define four bounded functions on
Omega=F_p* x F_p:

  F_1(r,u)= e_p(r^2u),       F_2(r,u)=e_p(-r^2u),
  F_3(r,u)= e_p(ru(r+u)),    F_4(r,u)=e_p(-ru(r+u)).

Their product on the respective four slots of every configuration
is identically 1. Therefore the four-linear count is exactly |X_p|,
and its restriction to nondegenerate configurations is |X_p^nondeg|.
Both are (1+o(1))p^3. Yet F_1 and F_2 have mean exactly zero.

This already disproves a product-of-global-means mixing assertion.
The stronger point is that all four functions have vanishing L2
conditional averages on each of the following natural one-coordinate
factors: color r, row-sum r+u, and column-product ru.

3. ELEMENTARY MARGINAL ESTIMATES; NO KLOOSTERMAN INPUT NEEDED

All conditional expectations below use the uniform distribution on
Omega. Color fibers have p elements, and sum/product fibers have p-1
elements. Both the sum and product are uniform on F_p. Normalize L2
using the uniform distribution on the appropriate factor.

The estimates are uniform if every phase is multiplied by any fixed
nonzero lambda in F_p. Conjugation has no effect on these bounds.

For P(r,u)=e_p(lambda r^2u):

  COLOR: E_u P(r,u)=0 for every r!=0.

  PRODUCT t: E[P | ru=t] is 1 at t=0 and -1/(p-1) otherwise.
  Its squared L2 norm is exactly 1/(p-1).

  SUM s: M(s)=(p-1)^-1 sum_{r!=0} e_p(lambda(r^2s-r^3)).
  Orthogonality in s gives

    E_s |M(s)|^2
      =(p-1)^-2 sum_{r,v!=0:r^2=v^2} e_p(lambda(v^3-r^3))
      <=2/(p-1).                                     (8)

No cubic exponential-sum theorem is required for this averaged bound.

For Q(r,u)=e_p(lambda ru(r+u)):

  COLOR r: the sum over u is a nondegenerate quadratic Gauss sum,
  so the conditional mean has modulus p^-1/2. Squared L2 is 1/p.

  SUM s: the phase is lambda(s^2r-sr^2). At s=0 the mean is 1.
  At s!=0, completing a quadratic Gauss sum and removing r=0 gives
  modulus at most (sqrt(p)+1)/(p-1). Thus squared L2 is at most

    1/p + (sqrt(p)+1)^2/[p(p-1)] = O(1/p).            (9)

  PRODUCT t: the conditional mean is

    K(t)=(p-1)^-1 sum_{r!=0} e_p(lambda(tr+t^2/r)).

  Expand its squared L2 norm, then sum over t. Terms r=v contribute
  p; terms r!=v are quadratic sums with nonzero t^2 coefficient
  lambda(1/r-1/v), hence have modulus sqrt(p). Consequently

    E_t |K(t)|^2
      <= 1/(p-1) + (p-2)/[(p-1)sqrt(p)]
       = O(p^-1/2).                                 (10)

The standard quadratic-sum modulus used here follows directly by
expanding |sum_x e_p(Ax^2+Bx)|^2, changing variables x-y and x+y,
and using additive-character orthogonality; p odd and A!=0 suffice.

In particular, every displayed natural marginal has L2 norm o(1),
while the normalized four-slot correlation tends to 1. A counting
estimate that treats small color/sum/product marginals as sufficient
uniformity is therefore false.

This does not address conditioning on a much richer joint factor, a
higher-order uniformity norm, or the fully selected common support.

4. FOUR DENSE NONNEGATIVE SUPPORTS WITH THE SAME MARGINAL BARRIER

The phase example can be made into an exact zero-configuration example
for four DIFFERENT sets, without relying on cancellation.

Let J_p={j in {0,...,p-1}: p/16<j<3p/16}, and alpha_p=|J_p|/p.
For each slot i, let R_i contain precisely those (r,u) whose respective
signed phase polynomial

  H_1=r^2u, H_2=-r^2u, H_3=ru(r+u), H_4=-ru(r+u)

has its standard residue in J_p. Then R_1,...,R_4 contain NO solution
of (1): all four signed residues would have sum strictly between p/4
and 3p/4, whereas (7) says that sum is divisible by p.

Nevertheless each R_i has density alpha_p+o(1)->1/8 in Omega, and
each of its color, sum, and product marginal densities differs from
alpha_p by o(1) in normalized L2.

Proof of the latter statement: expand 1_{J_p} in the additive Fourier
basis on F_p. Its zero coefficient is alpha_p, while the sum of the
absolute values of its other normalized Fourier coefficients is
O(log p), by the finite geometric-series formula and the bound
|hat{1_J}(k)|<=min(|J|/p, O(1/min(k,p-k))). Apply the uniform-in-lambda
estimates (8)--(10) and the L2 triangle inequality. The worst resulting
bound is O(p^-1/4 log p)=o(1).

Therefore even positive-density four-partite supersaturation under
these natural marginal conditions is false. The raw phase-defined
sets need not be properly oriented, but the strengthening below
removes this issue.

4A. ALL FOUR SUPPORTS CAN SHARE ONE PROPER ORIENTATION

There is one properly oriented set O subset Omega such that the four
sets R'_i=R_i intersect O still contain no four-slot solution, each
has density 1/16+o(1), and each has color/sum/product marginal density
1/16+o(1) in normalized L2. In particular, their union is also
properly oriented: the same graph edge never receives two colors
even across different supports.

Choose O randomly as follows, independently for each unordered root
pair. For distinct nonzero r,u, put exactly one of (r,u),(u,r) into O,
with equal probabilities. For the singleton diagonal pair (r,r), and
for (r,0) whose reverse has invalid first coordinate, use an independent
fair retention coin. Thus every point of Omega is in O with probability
exactly 1/2, and O is properly oriented in every outcome.

Fix i and one color, sum, or product fiber L. The count |R_i intersect
O intersect L| is a sum of independent random variables, indexed by
the unordered pairs meeting the fiber. Each contribution lies in
[0,1]: if both orientations of a nonzero pair lie in R_i intersect L,
their contribution is exactly 1, whereas if only one does, its
contribution is a fair Bernoulli variable. A color fiber has no two
distinct points with the same unordered pair. Sum/product fibers can
have a reversed pair, which is exactly the case just handled.

There are at most p independent contributions, and the expected count
is |R_i intersect L|/2. The bounded-variable Hoeffding estimate gives

  P( | |R_i intersect O intersect L|-|R_i intersect L|/2 | > t )
      <=2 exp(-2t^2/p).

Take t=C sqrt(p log p) with a fixed sufficiently large C. There are
fewer than 12p fibers to check over all four supports and all three
types. A union bound shows that with probability tending to 1 all
fiber counts differ from half their original counts by at most t.
Dividing by p or p-1, all conditional densities simultaneously differ
from half their original values by O(sqrt(log p/p)). Combining this
with Section 4 proves the asserted L2 marginals and densities.

No configurations can have been added by taking subsets. This proves
the claim by the probabilistic method, without constructing any
finite graph or invoking a computational search.

Both versions DO NOT disprove the four-partite removal target from pass 2:
there are already no solutions, so removal has nothing to do. Nor
does it disprove supersaturation/removal for FOUR IDENTICAL properly
oriented supports. In that case the diagonal configurations force a
very different obstruction, and remain the substantive open target.

Indeed the first two phase-defined supports here are disjoint, as are
the last two: J_p and -J_p are disjoint. Common proper orientation
must not be confused with using one common support in all four slots.

5. INTERPRETATION AND FOLLOW-UP

The natural triangle coordinates retain color/sum/product tests, but
there is also the exact cubic compatibility (7). A proof based on
ordinary one-variable marginal pseudorandomness would lose this data.

The companion pass3-polynomial-phase-classification.txt classifies
formal polynomial slotwise phase identities in characteristic zero
(and bounded degree in positive characteristic). Within that class,
the cubic term is the only extra term beyond separate color, sum,
and product potentials. This is a statement about polynomial phase
identities, not a classification of all analytic obstructions.
