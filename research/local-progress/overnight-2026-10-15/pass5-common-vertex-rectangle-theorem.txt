COMMON ROW AND COLUMN MASKS: A BROADER ACTUAL-SUPPORT THEOREM
15 October 2026, pass 5. Complete proof, independently audited.

Let p be an odd prime, A subset F_p*, and sigma_a(x)=ax+beta(a), with
one color per slope. Fix ANY row set X and column set Y. Define the
eligible color-a domain

                         H_a=X intersect sigma_a^-1(Y).

Select actual domains D_a subset H_a. Assume the selected graph is
simple, properly colored and B-colored (every C4 rainbow). Put

                     m=sum_a |D_a|, R=sum_a |H_a minus D_a|.

THEOREMS

(1) If beta is a polynomial of fixed degree 2<=D<p, then

                      m<=C_D p^(19/10)+sqrt(2)p sqrt(R).       (1)

(2) For arbitrary beta:A->F_p, R=o(p^2) implies m=o(p^2), uniformly.

In particular, the exact support D_a=X intersect sigma_a^-1(Y) forces
vanishing density. These domains can vary substantially with the color.
No interval, order or VC hypothesis is imposed on X or Y.

The theorem concerns the common rectangle of eligible VERTEX pairs X x Y.
It is stronger than requiring all colors to share one row domain. It is
still a structured support class, not a general arbitrary-domain theorem.
The explicit stretched-logarithmic estimate previously proved for exactly
common ROW support is not asserted here: its pencil-Roth proof does not
automatically survive a varying domain induced by Y.

1. WHY THE SAME CROSSED OPERATOR COUNTS ACTUAL RECTANGLES

Define tau_a(x,y)=(sigma_a^-1(y),sigma_a(x)) and S_A=sum_a P_tau_a as
in pass 4. If two actual edge positions are crossed partners, write them

                      (x,sigma_a(z)), (z,sigma_a(x)).

Both belong to X x Y. It follows that x,z lie in X and both sigma_a(x)
and sigma_a(z) lie in Y. Hence BOTH closing color-a incidences are eligible:
x,z belong to H_a. This uses the rectangle geometry exactly; it does not
require D_a to equal a common row set.

If both closing incidences are actual and x!=z, the four edges are a
genuine C4 with repeated opposite color a, contrary to B. If x=z and the
color-a incidence is actual, it is one of the m allowed actual loops.
Every remaining internal transition has a missing eligible incidence.
This includes any fixed point whose completed color a differs from its
actual edge color.

A fixed missing (a,x) can be the first missing diagonal in at most
deg_E(x)<=d transitions, and the second in at most that many. Thus

                       <1_E,S_A1_E><=m+2dR.                  (2)

For R=0 equality holds. This is the same rigorous charge as in pass 4,
with the eligibility implication above replacing common row support.

2. POLYNOMIAL INTERCEPTS

The completed-map Fourier bound is independent of the selected supports.
For beta of fixed degree D>=2, the pass-4 theorem gives a mean-zero norm

          lambda<=C_D[p^(1/4)d^(1/2)+p^(1/8)d^(3/4)].

Centering 1_E and applying (2) yields

                    (d+lambda)m^2/p^2<=(1+lambda)m+2dR.

For d>0 this implies m<=p^2(1+lambda)/d+sqrt(2)p sqrt(R). Combining it
with m<=dp and splitting at d=p^(9/10) proves (1), exactly as before.
For d=0 there are no edges. All constants remain uniform in X and Y.

3. ARBITRARY INTERCEPTS: HEAVY PRUNING AND PUBLISHED ENERGY INPUTS

Suppose toward a contradiction that m>=epsilon p^2 along a sequence,
with epsilon>0 fixed and R=o(p^2). Discard colors carrying fewer than
epsilon p/2 actual edges. Since there are at most p colors, fewer than
epsilon p^2/2 edges are lost. The retained graph has m'>=epsilon p^2/2,
and its d' colors each contain at least epsilon p/2 actual edges.
In particular epsilon p/2<=d'<=p.

Pruning whole colors preserves the eligible-domain description for every
retained color, and its total missing budget R' is at most R. In the exact
case R=0 it preserves completeness inside X x Y as well.

Let L be the largest occupancy of a line in the retained coefficient
palette {(a,beta(a))}. We have L=o(p). Otherwise a nonvertical line
beta(a)=u a+v contains at least delta p colors along a subsequence.
(Vertical lines contain at most one color.) Its actual subgraph has at
least delta epsilon p^2/2 edges by the heavy-class condition. Relabel
rows and columns by x'=x+u and y'=y-v. These colors now obey y'=a x'.
Delete the at most one actual edge at (0,0); all remaining entries form
a positive-density partial table of the multiplicative group F_p*.
Solymosi's finite-group-table theorem supplies an actual rectangle with
equal opposite color, a contradiction. Hence L=o(p).

PRRW Theorem 2.1 now gives affine quotient energy

                         E_aff << d'^(5/2)+L d'^2=o(p^3).

Its hypotheses hold because there is one coefficient point per vertical
line and d'<=p. The general crossed-moment inequality, already established
for arbitrary intercepts in pass 4, gives

 lambda^4<=max{(p-1)E_aff,
                       p(L+2)d'^2+2sqrt(p)E_x(A')}=o(p^4).

Thus lambda=o(p). Applying (2) to the retained actual graph gives

 m'<=p^2(1+lambda)/d'+sqrt(2)p sqrt(R')=o(p^2),

contrary to its positive density. This proves assertion (2) uniformly.

4. ELIGIBILITY-BOX COVERS

More generally suppose the actual colored incidences are covered by K
fully present eligibility boxes (A_i,X_i,Y_i): for every a in A_i and
x in X_i with sigma_a(x) in Y_i, that color-a incidence is actual, and
every actual incidence belongs to at least one box. Restricting to each
box preserves properness and B. Consequently the polynomial case gives

                              m<=C_D K p^(19/10).

A bounded number of boxes gives o(p^2) also for arbitrary intercepts.
The boxes may overlap. This is not a biclique cover of the ACTUAL edge
graph: a vertex rectangle can contain many nonedges because no selected
palette line passes through them. Nor is it merely a color-row rectangle
cover, since the common column condition is retained.
