AFFINE COLOR CLASSES WITH A BOUNDED NUMBER OF MISSING POINTS
Overnight pass 1, 15 October 2026.
Complete hand proof using an established group-table theorem.
No claim of historical novelty; not Lean checked.

THEOREM

Fix a nonnegative integer r. Let F range over finite fields of order n.
Consider a bipartite simple graph with its two sides labeled by F, with
a proper edge coloring for which every C4 is rainbow. Suppose each
nonempty color c is represented by an affine permutation

                  sigma_c(x)=a_c x+b_c,   a_c!=0,

and its actual color class contains at least n-r edges of that
permutation matching. Then the number d of colors is o(n), and the
number m of edges is o(n^2), uniformly as n tends to infinity.

There is no assumption that the full affine group has order O(n), that
the color permutations commute, or that their relative difference set
has size O(n). The conclusion concerns the bounded-deficit subclass;
it does not establish the assertion for a fixed support fraction eta<1.

The combinatorial steps below use only properness. They give both a
bounded cover by parallel or concurrent pencils of affine lines and a
sharper bound d<=(r+1)L, where L is the largest number of palette lines
in any one such pencil. The rainbow-C4 condition is used afterwards
through Solymosi's known theorem.

1. PENCIL-COVER LEMMA

Let d distinct affine lines of nonzero slope in F^2 have selected subsets
of at least n-r points each. Suppose no point is selected on two lines.
If d>=r+1, the lines can be covered by at most

               L(r)=(r+1)+r(r+1)=(r+1)^2.               (1)

parallel or concurrent pencils. A concurrent pencil means all lines
through one specified point; a parallel pencil means all lines of one
specified slope. A cover, rather than a disjoint partition, is enough.

Proof. Choose k=r+1 of the lines as base lines. List these pencils:

 (i) the k pencils parallel to a base line;
 (ii) the pencils through every unselected point of a base line,
      numbering at most kr.

Repeated pencils in this list can be ignored. Every base line is already
covered by (i). Suppose another line L is not covered. It is parallel to
none of the base lines, so it meets each one. Each intersection point is
selected on its base line: otherwise L belongs to (ii). The k points
are distinct, because a coincident pair would be selected on two base
lines, contrary to properness. Since selected sets are disjoint, L must
omit all k of these distinct points. This contradicts its deficit at
most r. QED.

The sharper count (r+1)^2 uses properness at base/base intersections;
separately listing their concurrent pencils would overcount them.

For affine color classes, the hypotheses apply to their graph lines.
If two colors had the same affine completion, their selected domains
would be disjoint by properness; for n>2r, two sets of size at least n-r
cannot be disjoint. Thus the representing lines are automatically
distinct once n>2r.

1A. A SHARPER DEFICIT-VERSUS-PENCIL INEQUALITY

Let L be the maximum number of palette lines in any parallel or
concurrent pencil. Then

                         d <= (r+1)L.                (1a)

This refinement was found independently in the root and finite-audit
passes; the proof does not require a cover or a choice of base lines.

For each slope, let d_a be the number of lines with that slope, so
d_a<=L and sum_a d_a=d. The number of parallel pairs is at most

              sum_a binomial(d_a,2) <= d(L-1)/2.

For every finite point P incident with at least two palette lines, let
t_P be that multiplicity. Properness forces at least t_P-1 of these
line-point incidences to be omitted. Thus, writing R for the total
number of omitted incidences, we have

                 sum_P(t_P-1) <= R <= rd.

Every pair of nonparallel lines meets in exactly one finite point.
Since t_P<=L, the number of nonparallel pairs is at most

       sum_P binomial(t_P,2) <= (L/2)sum_P(t_P-1) <= Lrd/2.

These two categories partition all unordered line pairs. Therefore
d(d-1)<=d(L-1)+Lrd, which gives (1a) when d>0. More generally, without
a common deficit bound the same argument gives

                       d(d-L) <= LR.                 (1b)

Hence (1a) needs only an average deficit bound R/d<=r; the later
group-table edge estimate will still use a lower bound on each class.

2. EACH PENCIL IS A PARTIAL GROUP TABLE

Input: Solymosi, The (7,4)-conjecture in finite groups, Theorem 5,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133 (PDF page 4), implies that a partial group
table whose entries are colored by y x^(-1), and whose every C4 is
rainbow, has o(|G|^2) entries, uniformly over finite groups G.
The change of coordinates from Solymosi's product formulation is given
in ../multicolor-2026-10-15/group-representation-vanishing-density.txt.
Only additive and multiplicative groups of finite fields are used here.

For a parallel pencil of slope a_0, replace the right coordinate y by
a_0^(-1)y. Its color-b edges now have

                         y=x+b/a_0.

Different lines have different translations. The resulting colored
subgraph is a partial table of the additive group (F,+). Its equality
pattern of colors is preserved exactly, so it still has all C4s rainbow.
It therefore has o(n^2) edges.

For a concurrent pencil through (x_0,y_0), replace x by x-x_0 and y by
y-y_0. A line of slope a now has equation y=a x. Apart from the point
(0,0), its points have both coordinates nonzero. Delete that one graph
position if it is selected. The remaining graph is a partial table of
the multiplicative group F^*, colored by y/x=a. Different lines have
different slopes. Again Solymosi gives o((n-1)^2)=o(n^2) edges, and the
single deleted position does not affect the conclusion.

The uniformity in the input theorem permits the field and pencils to
vary with n. It is not an assumption about a fixed ambient group.

3. CONCLUSION

If d<r+1 then m<=rn=o(n^2). Otherwise apply the pencil-cover lemma.
Partition the palette among its at most L(r) pencils in any manner
consistent with the cover, and retain the associated colored subgraphs.
Each has o(n^2) edges by Section 2. Since L(r) is fixed, m=o(n^2).
Finally d(n-r)<=m, so d=o(n).

The proof in fact gives a useful quantified reduction. Let h(n) denote
the maximum fraction of entries in a B-colored partial table of either
(F,+) or F^*, with the latter normalized by n^2, maximized over fields
of order n. Then h(n)->0. Whenever n>2r, a pencil with L colors has at
least L(n-r) edges and, by Section 2, at most h(n)n^2+1. Combining this
with (1a) yields the stronger estimate

            d <= (r+1)[h(n)n^2+1]/(n-r),
            m <= (r+1)n[h(n)n^2+1]/(n-r).             (2)

For a growing deficit r(n), (2) proves m=o(n^2) whenever r(n)=o(n) and

                    (r(n)+1)[h(n)+n^(-2)]->0.

No explicit growth rate for r(n) is extracted here, and (2) does not
justify replacing the bounded deficit by an arbitrary o(n) deficit.
The completed quantitative refinement in
pass1-affine-quantitative-audit.txt proves

        m <= C(r+1)n^2 exp[-c(log n)^(1/12)]

when n>4r+1. It uses a robust strong-progression-free palette argument
and a quantitative abelian Roth bound, separately from this group-table
proof. Its independent check is recorded in
pass1-affine-quantitative-independent-audit.txt.

4. WHY THE SAME PENCIL ARGUMENT STOPS AT FIXED-DENSITY SUPPORTS

If each class has only eta n edges, its deficit can be (1-eta)n. The
cover above then has order n^2 pencils. A separate o(n^2) estimate on
each pencil does not imply an o(n^2) estimate for their union. The
quadratic example in pass1-affine-global-relations-barrier.txt has
Theta(n) colors, constant-density supports, and no three concurrent
lines; consequently any such pencil cover has Theta(n) members.
This is a concrete obstruction to extending this particular bounded-
cover proof by simply dropping the bounded-deficit hypothesis. The
additional Frobenius family in pass1-affine-sublinear-deficit-barrier.txt
has deficits only sqrt(n)+1 yet still needs an unbounded pencil cover.
It is not B, and therefore obstructs only the properness-based structural
lemma, not a possible stronger theorem using the full B condition.
