2. PRUNING AND GEOMETRIC INCIDENCE COST

Write d_0 for the original label count, m for edges, and D=d_0*p-m.
Deleting labels with defect at least p/8 removes at most 8D edges.
Each surviving class has >7p/8 edges. Two surviving labels cannot
have the same affine completion, as their supports would overlap
and assign two colors to one simple-graph edge. Also d<8p/7.

A nontrivial completion relation C=A B^-1 A produces a forbidden
four-cycle after excluding fewer than 4p/8 support failures and at
most one fixed point of B^-1 A. This validates the previously used
strong progression-free pencil bound

  L <= C p exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)].

The dual incidence cost is exact: a finite intersection with t>=2
completed color lines forces at least t-1 missing colored incidences.
Each missing incidence belongs to only one such geometric point.
Since t<=2(t-1), determined nonvertical dual lines contribute at
most 2D incidences. Vertical determined lines partition points by
slope and contribute at most d. Hence I_*<=2D+d.

3. BECK DICHOTOMY AND CONSTANTS

For the dyadic class of T_k lines of richness in [k,2k), the input
with d<8p/7 gives, for k>=8,

  (k/2)T_k <=3 d^(2/3) T_k^(2/3)+d.

One right-hand term is at least kT_k/4. If it is the first, cubing
gives T_k<=1728d^2/k^3; if the second, T_k<=4d/k. Their sum is a
valid bound without choosing a case.

Each line in the class contributes fewer than 2k^2 pairs. Summing
dyadically over k>=K and k<=L therefore gives

  high-richness pairs <=6912d^2/K+16dL.

There is no logarithmic loss: sum 1/k<=2/K and sum k<=2L along
the dyadic sequence. Low-richness lines contribute at most K I_*/2.

With K=131072, the first coefficient is less than 1/16. If L<=d/256,
the second term is at most d^2/16. For d>=2,

  d^2/4 <=binom(d,2)
           <=K I_*/2+d^2/8,

so I_*>=d^2/(4K). Thus either L>d/256 or this incidence lower bound
holds. Combining with I_*<=2D+d gives d=O(sqrt(D+1)) in the second
case. The first case is controlled by the Roth pencil bound.

4. CONCLUSION AND SCOPE

Multiplying the retained color bound by p and restoring at most 8D
edges gives exactly

  m <= C p sqrt(D+1)+8D
       +C p^2 exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)].

Thus D=o(p^2) implies m=o(p^2). If the average defect D/d_0=o(p)
and d_0>0, the identity m=d_0(p-D/d_0)<=p^2 first gives
d_0<=p/(1-o(1)), and then D=o(p^2). This implication does not assume
d_0=O(p) without justification.

The result is limited to prime fields and affine completions with
sublinear average defect. It does not handle a common row mask
of fixed density below 1, nor arbitrary quadratic-palette supports.
The improvement here is a deduction using the explicitly attributed
recent incidence theorem; it is not a replacement proof of that input.
