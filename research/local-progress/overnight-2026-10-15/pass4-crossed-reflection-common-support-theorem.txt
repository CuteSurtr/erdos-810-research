QUADRATIC AFFINE COLORS WITH COMMON OR NEARLY COMMON ROW SUPPORT
15 October 2026, scheduled continuation 4.

Status: analytic proof independently audited by two agents. The only
exponential-sum input is the classical Weil bound for Kloosterman sums.
This is a conditional class theorem, not a solution of unrestricted
Erdos #810. Historical originality of the assembled result is unverified.
No Lean certificate or finite graph enumeration is claimed.

1. STATEMENTS

Let p be an odd prime, A subset F_p* with d=|A|, and X subset F_p.
For each a in A choose D_a subset X and retain precisely the edges

                     x -> sigma_a(x)=a*x+a^2, x in D_a,

with color a. Assume these edges form a simple properly colored
bipartite graph and every C4 is rainbow (condition B). Put

       m=sum_a |D_a|,       R=sum_a (|X|-|D_a|).

Thus R counts omissions from A x X, not omissions from A x F_p.

THEOREM. There is an absolute constant C such that

                    m <= C p^(19/10) + sqrt(2) p sqrt(R).       (1)

In particular:

(a) COMMON SUPPORT: if every D_a=X, then m=O(p^(19/10)).
    This permits any A and any X, including fixed positive support
    fractions. No additive or multiplicative structure of A is assumed.

(b) NEARLY COMMON SUPPORT: R=o(p^2) implies m=o(p^2), even if X has
    a fixed density strictly below one.

(c) MANY EXACT RECTANGLES: if the actual color-by-row support is
    covered by K all-present rectangles A_i x X_i, then

                         m <= C K p^(19/10).

    This follows by restricting the graph to each rectangle and summing.
    Thus K=o(p^(1/10)) suffices for m=o(p^2). Rectangles may overlap.

The palette is specifically quadratic affine. This does not prove
the same statements for arbitrary affine intercepts or permutations.

2. CROSSED REFLECTIONS AND THEIR FOURIER BLOCKS

On the p^2-element space V=F_p^2, define

       tau_a(x,y)=(y/a-a, a*x+a^2),
       (S_A f)(x,y)=sum_{a in A} f(tau_a(x,y)).

Each tau_a is an involution. Hence S_A is a real symmetric operator
with constant row and column sum d. It is the sum of the reflections
fixing the affine lines y=a*x+a^2; those reflections preserve the
split quadratic form on differences, Q(delta_x,delta_y)=delta_x*delta_y.

Use counting-measure L2 norms. For e_p(t)=exp(2 pi i t/p), set
chi_(xi,eta)(x,y)=e_p(xi*x+eta*y). Then

 S_A chi_(xi,eta)
   =sum_{a in A} e_p(-a*xi+a^2*eta) chi_(a*eta,xi/a).    (2)

Thus the product xi*eta is invariant. The zero frequency has
eigenvalue d. All other frequencies split into:

  - one block consisting of the two nonzero coordinate axes;
  - a block of size p-1 for each t=xi*eta != 0.

For t!=0, index frequency vectors as (s,t/s). The output first
frequency r equals ta/s, so a=rs/t. The block matrix is

       M_t(r,s)=1_A(rs/t) e_p(rs(r-s)/t), r,s != 0.     (3)

It is Hermitian: its weight is symmetric and its phase changes sign
when r and s are interchanged.

3. THE FOURTH MOMENT ON A NONZERO-PRODUCT BLOCK

Expand tr(M_t^4) over r,s,u,v != 0. Its four weights are

        1_A(rs/t) 1_A(su/t) 1_A(uv/t) 1_A(vr/t).

The phase numerator factors exactly:

 rs(r-s)+su(s-u)+uv(u-v)+vr(v-r)
       =(r-u)(s-v)(r+u-s-v).                         (4)

Make the bijective substitution

       a=rs/t,  q=u/r,  h=v/s,
       s=ta/r,  u=qr,  v=h*ta/r.

For each a,q,h != 0 the four membership tests become

                    a, qa, qha, ha in A,             (5)

and the remaining sum over r is

 sum_{r!=0} e_p(alpha*r+beta/r),                     (6)
 alpha=a(1-q)(1-h)(1+q),
 beta=-t*a^2(1-q)(1-h)(1+h).

If alpha and beta are both nonzero, the classical Kloosterman bound
gives absolute value at most 2 sqrt(p). If exactly one is zero,
the sum is -1, by additive-character orthogonality.

Both are zero precisely in these cases:

                q=1, or h=1, or q=h=-1.             (7)

Here p odd is used. The number of triples in (5) with q=1 or h=1
is 2d^2-d. The third case adds |A intersect (-A)|, at most d.
Consequently there are at most 2d^2 exceptional triples. Their
individual sums are p-1.

The total number of triples satisfying (5) is the multiplicative
energy

 E_x(A)=#{(a,b,c,e) in A^4 : a*c=b*e} <= d^3,

since a,qa,ha determine the fourth entry. Taking absolute values
of the nonexceptional sums in the fourth-moment expansion yields

       0 <= tr(M_t^4)
          <= 2p*d^2 + 2 sqrt(p) E_x(A)
          <= 2p*d^2 + 2 sqrt(p) d^3.                 (8)

Because M_t is Hermitian, its operator norm to the fourth power
is at most its fourth trace. No claim of independence of the
four palette membership tests is used.

4. THE AXIS BLOCK

The axis block has off-diagonal form [0 N; N* 0], where

                N(r,s)=1_A(r/s)e_p(r^2/s).

This is the zero-sum Fourier block of the one-point operator
C_A=sum_{a in A} P_(sigma_a) on F_p. The following elementary
fourth-moment bound was independently proved in pass 3:

                ||N||^4 <= (p-1)(3d^2-2d).           (9)

For completeness, sigma_a sigma_b^-1 has slope rho=a/b and
intercept rho(rho-1)b^2. The identity quotient has multiplicity d;
each other quotient has multiplicity at most two. Hence the
noncommutative quotient energy is at most 3d^2-2d.

On the zero-sum permutation representation of Aff(F_p), the trace
of a group element is p-1 for the identity, -1 for a nontrivial
translation, and 0 for an affine map of slope different from one.
Expand tr((C_A C_A*)^2) on that representation. Identity words
contribute at most (p-1)(3d^2-2d), translations have nonpositive
contributions, and other words contribute zero. This proves (9).

Together, (8)--(9) show that on the mean-zero space of V,

 ||S_A|| <= lambda,
 lambda := [3p*d^2+2 sqrt(p) E_x(A)]^(1/4)
         <= C[p^(1/4)d^(1/2)+p^(1/8)d^(3/4)].         (10)

The bracket is only an upper bound on the nontrivial operator
norm. The argument does not assume that it is smaller than d
in all ranges.

5. THE ACTUAL EDGE SET AND THE B CONDITION

Regard the actual bipartite edge set as E subset V. Its size is m.
A term of <1_E,S_A 1_E> is an ordered triple (a,x,z) for which

                  (x,sigma_a(z)), (z,sigma_a(x)) in E.          (11)

If x!=z and both a-colored incidences at x,z are retained,
the two edges in (11) complete a nondegenerate C4 whose opposite
edges both have color a, violating B. The four vertices are
distinct within their respective parts since sigma_a is bijective.

If x=z and the a-colored incidence is retained, (11) is a loop.
Properness gives exactly one such retained loop for each actual
edge, hence exactly m of these terms in total.

Every other term in (11) has at least one missing incidence
(a,x) or (a,z) from A x X. This includes a virtual loop that
coincides with an actual edge of a different color.

Fix a missing incidence (a,x). At most deg_E(x)<=d choices of z
can satisfy (11) with x in the first position: distinct z give
distinct neighbors sigma_a(z) of row x. The same bound holds
for the second position, either directly or by the involution.
A union bound therefore proves

 <1_E,S_A 1_E>
     <= m+2 sum_{missing (a,x)} deg_E(x)
     <= m+2dR.                                      (12)

This is an actual-support estimate. It is the point missing from
the earlier completed-map mixing arguments.

6. SPECTRAL LOWER BOUND AND OPTIMIZATION

Center 1_E on the p^2-element space. Since S_A is symmetric,
has constant degree d, and satisfies (10),

 <1_E,S_A 1_E>
    >= d*m^2/p^2 - lambda*(m-m^2/p^2).               (13)

Combining (12)--(13) gives

          (d+lambda)m^2/p^2 <= (1+lambda)m+2dR.       (14)

For R=0 and d>0, this gives the particularly useful bound

                    m <= p^2(1+lambda)/(d+lambda).  (15)

For general R, discard the extra lambda*m^2 term and put
B=p^2(1+lambda)/d. The resulting m^2<=B*m+2p^2 R implies

                       m <= B+sqrt(2)p sqrt(R).     (16)

If d=0 the theorem is immediate. For d>0, (10) gives

 B <= p^2/d + C p^(9/4)d^(-1/2)
                 + C p^(17/8)d^(-1/4).              (17)

If d<=p^(9/10), use simply m<=dp<=p^(19/10).
If d>p^(9/10), the three exponents in (17) are at most
11/10, 9/5, and 19/10 respectively. Equation (16) proves (1).

If d>=delta*p for a fixed delta>0 and R=0, (17) also gives

                       m=O_delta(p^(15/8)).          (18)

This already contradicts m=d|X| when both d and |X| are fixed
positive fractions of p.

7. INPUT, INTERPRETATION, AND LIMITS

Kloosterman input:
Iwaniec--Kowalski, Analytic Number Theory, Theorem 11.11 and
equation (11.15), printed p.280, author-hosted extract:
https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/ik-ant-exp-sums.pdf
The one-zero and both-zero parameter cases in (6) are handled
explicitly above. Only the two-nonzero case uses Weil's estimate.

The source of the power saving is the exact action of the crossed
involutions and a fourth-moment estimate that retains all four
palette membership tests. It is not an inference from small
one-coordinate marginals, a claim of generic nonlinear mixing,
or an application of ordinary dense colored removal.

The common row support turns actual B into an independent-set
condition for these involutions. For nearly common supports,
(12) measures the exact failure of that condition. Arbitrary
partial supports need not have R=o(p^2) for any common container.

For K groups of colors with respective containers X_i and total
omission R_total, summing (1) gives

 m <= C K p^(19/10) + sqrt(2) p sqrt(K R_total).

This requires K=o(p^(1/10)) and K R_total=o(p^2) to force
vanishing density. A bounded VC-dimension assertion alone does
not imply these conditions by the naive covering argument:
its covering number can grow too rapidly as the error shrinks.
No such VC corollary is asserted.

The theorem's historical priority has not been established.
The universal 0.298444... bound and its novelty status are unchanged.
