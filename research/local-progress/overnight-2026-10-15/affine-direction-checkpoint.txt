AFFINE COMPLETIONS: A PRECISE UNRESOLVED NEXT STEP
15 October 2026. Research checkpoint, not a theorem or novelty claim.

This direction was identified but not developed into a proof during the
current pass. No finite graph searches were run.

SETTING

Let F be a finite field of order n. Suppose a properly colored bipartite
graph on two copies of F has color c given on an actual domain D_c by

           sigma_c(x)=a_c x+b_c,       a_c in F nonzero.

There may be several colors of the same slope; affine transformations
are assumed distinct after any duplicate-color reduction. Let q be the
number of colors. A possible next target is:

  q=O(n), every color support at least eta n, and all C4s rainbow
  imply q=o(n), or equivalently exclude m=Omega(n^2) in this class.

This assertion is UNPROVED here. It is a conditional representation
class, not a reduction of general Erdos #810 to affine permutations.

WHY THE PREVIOUS GROUP ARGUMENT DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY APPLY

The full affine group has n(n-1) elements and acts on n points with
point stabilizers of size n-1. Thus the ambient-group-size hypothesis
O(n) from the earlier group-representation theorem is absent. A palette
of O(n) affine maps can also have a difference set of order n^2, so the
small-difference theorem cannot simply be invoked. An argument here
would have to exploit the specific action or its incidence geometry.

EXACT LOCAL EQUATION

For clarity consider the subcase with at most one color per nonzero
slope, writing sigma_a(x)=a x+beta_a. A forbidden rectangle with
diagonal color a and cross colors b,c has rows x,z satisfying

  b x+beta_b = a z+beta_a,
  c z+beta_c = a x+beta_a,

as well as the support constraints

  x,z in D_a,       x in D_b,       z in D_c,       x!=z.

Eliminating z gives the exact equation

  (bc-a^2)x = (a+c)beta_a-c beta_b-a beta_c.          (1)

If bc!=a^2, each ordered triple of slopes a,b,c supplies at most one
potential row pair, before testing the support constraints and x!=z.
If bc=a^2, either the intercept relation is inconsistent (no solution)
or it is an identity (every x solves the affine equations). The identity
case is precisely the global permutation relation

                 sigma_c = sigma_a sigma_b^(-1) sigma_a.

The many nonidentity triples in (1) are local coincidences at one point.
They need not imply repeated products or high global multiplicative
energy, which is the central obstruction already exposed by the tensor
example in formal-audit-energy-and-partial-kernel.txt.

A rigorous continuation could ask for a density theorem forcing one of
these locally determined row pairs to satisfy all four support tests,
while accounting for properness (selected affine lines do not share a
selected point). Counting potential solutions of (1) alone is not such
a theorem: arbitrary domains D_a can correlate with all the solutions.
No incidence estimate or removal lemma establishing the required
uniform statement has yet been found or verified.

For reference, the family sigma_a(x)=a x+a^2 has the simple collision
identity sigma_a(x)=sigma_b(x) iff x=-(a+b), for a!=b. It is merely an
algebraic test family for future proofs; no B construction or extremal
claim is asserted for it.
