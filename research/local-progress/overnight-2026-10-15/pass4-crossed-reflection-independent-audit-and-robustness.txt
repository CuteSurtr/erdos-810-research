1. SETUP AND THE POSITIVE CONCLUSIONS

Let p be odd, A subset F_p* have size d, and sigma_a(x)=ax+a^2.
For each a in A select a row support D_a subset X subset F_p.
The selected colored incidences form a proper simple bipartite graph
G on two F_p sides. Assume every C4 is rainbow (property B).
Let E subset F_p^2 be its actual edge set and let m=|E|.

If all D_a=X, then

                         m=O(p^(19/10)).               (1)

More generally, put R=sum_{a in A}(|X|-|D_a|). Then

                  m<=C*p^(19/10)+sqrt(2)*p*sqrt(R)      (2)

for an absolute C. In particular R=o(p^2) implies m=o(p^2),
without assuming d or |X| has positive density.

These conclusions concern this quadratic completion palette. They
do not assert the same for arbitrary affine palettes or arbitrary
edge colorings. Original labels correspond to distinct a values.

2. THE CROSSED INVOLUTIONS AND THE ACTUAL GRAPH

Define a permutation of the point space F_p^2 by

 tau_a(x,y)=(sigma_a^-1(y),sigma_a(x))
           =((y-a^2)/a, ax+a^2).

It is an involution. Let

                (S_A f)(u)=sum_{a in A} f(tau_a(u)).

The operator is real symmetric and has every row and column sum d.

First suppose D_a=X for all a. A selected point u=(x,sigma_b(x))
is fixed by tau_b. No other tau_a can fix it, since properness
would assign two colors to the same actual edge.

If a!=b and tau_a(u)=(z,sigma_a(x)) were another selected point,
then x,z are both in X. Thus the two a-colored incidences
(x,sigma_a(x)) and (z,sigma_a(z)) are selected, and
sigma_a(z)=sigma_b(x). If z=x, properness fails; otherwise these
four selected incidences make a C4 with color a repeated on its
diagonal. This contradicts B. Consequently

                      <1_E,S_A 1_E>=m.                 (3)

This exact internal-adjacency identity is the step that was missing
from ordinary one- or two-point mixing: it retains the actual crossed
selected-edge test, rather than replacing it by an unrelated set.

3. FOURIER BLOCKS

Use the orthogonal Fourier basis
chi_(xi,eta)(x,y)=e_p(xi*x+eta*y), where e_p(t)=exp(2*pi*i*t/p).
Then

 P_tau_a chi_(xi,eta)
   =e_p(eta*a^2-xi*a) chi_(a*eta,xi/a).

The frequency product xi*eta is invariant. The zero frequency is
the constant eigenvector of eigenvalue d.

For t=xi*eta!=0, index its block by frequencies (r,t/r), r!=0.
With row r and column s, its matrix is

 M_t(r,s)=1_A(rs/t)*e_p(rs(r-s)/t).                     (4)

It is Hermitian: swapping r,s conjugates the phase and keeps the
indicator unchanged. Thus its fourth power has nonnegative trace
and ||M_t||^4<=tr(M_t^4).

The two nonzero coordinate axes form a block [[0,B],[B*,0]]. Its
off-diagonal matrix, up to transpose conventions, is

          B(r,s)=1_A(r/s)*e_p(r^2/s).

This is exactly the nonconstant Fourier matrix of the earlier
one-point operator sum_{a in A} P_sigma_a. The independently proved
affine-character fourth-moment estimate gives

           ||B||^4 <=(p-1)(3d^2-2d).                   (5)

For completeness, every nonidentity quotient sigma_a sigma_b^-1
has at most two representations: its slope rho=a/b and intercept
rho(rho-1)b^2 determine b up to sign. The identity multiplicity is
d, so the affine quotient energy is at most 3d^2-2d. On the
one-point zero-sum representation the character is p-1 at identity,
-1 at nonidentity translations, and zero otherwise. Expanding
tr((BB*)^2) and dropping negative terms proves (5).

4. THE NONZERO-PRODUCT FOURTH MOMENT

Expanding the trace in (4), the phase numerator is

 rs(r-s)+su(s-u)+uv(u-v)+vr(v-r)
       =(r-u)(s-v)(r+u-s-v).                          (6)

Make the bijective substitution

       a=rs/t,  q=u/r,  h=v/s,
       s=ta/r,  u=qr,  v=hta/r,

with all a,q,h,r nonzero. The four indicator weights become

                   1_A(a)1_A(qa)1_A(qha)1_A(ha).       (7)

The phase becomes alpha*r+beta/r, where

 alpha=a(1-q)(1-h)(1+q),
 beta=-t*a^2*(1-q)(1-h)(1+h).                          (8)

The required standard estimate is

 |sum_{r!=0} e_p(alpha*r+beta/r)|<=2sqrt(p)

when alpha,beta are both nonzero. If exactly one is nonzero the
sum is -1, and if both are zero it is p-1. A primary reference is
Iwaniec--Kowalski, Analytic Number Theory, Theorem 11.11 and (11.15),
printed page 280, author-hosted chapter:
https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/ik-ant-exp-sums.pdf

Because a,t!=0 and p is odd, both coefficients in (8) vanish
precisely if q=1, h=1, or q=h=-1. With weights (7), the first two
sets have d^2 elements each and overlap in d elements. The third
has |A intersect (-A)| elements and is disjoint from the first two.
Thus the exact exceptional count is

              2d^2-d+|A intersect (-A)| <=2d^2.         (9)

The total number of supported (a,q,h) is the multiplicative energy
E_x(A)=#{(a,b,c,e) in A^4 : ae=bc}, hence at most d^3.
Bounding the nonexceptional sums in absolute value proves

 tr(M_t^4) <=2p*d^2+2sqrt(p)*E_x(A)
            <=2p*d^2+2sqrt(p)*d^3.                    (10)

All algebraic degeneracies have been included in (9); no generic
nonvanishing assumption was used to apply the exponential-sum bound.

5. THE MEAN-ZERO NORM AND THE COMMON-MASK EDGE BOUND

Equations (5) and (10) cover every nonconstant Fourier block. Thus
on mean-zero functions on F_p^2,

 ||S_A|| <=lambda,
 lambda <=C[p^(1/4)d^(1/2)+p^(1/8)d^(3/4)].            (11)

The sharper energy-dependent version replaces d^(3/4) by E_x(A)^(1/4).

Using (3), center 1_E in the p^2-point space. The spectral lower
bound gives

 m>=d*m^2/p^2-lambda*(m-m^2/p^2).

Therefore, for m>0,

 m<=p^2*(1+lambda)/(d+lambda)
   <=p^2/d+C*p^(9/4)d^(-1/2)+C*p^(17/8)d^(-1/4).     (12)

There is also the trivial m<=dp. If d<=p^(9/10), this already
gives (1). Otherwise the three terms in (12) have respective
upper bounds p^(11/10), C*p^(9/5), C*p^(19/10).
This proves (1) uniformly in d. If d is a fixed positive fraction
of p, (12) instead gives the sharper m=O(p^(15/8)).

6. WHY THIS IS STRONGER THAN THE PREVIOUS MIXING INTERMEDIATES

The operator acts on potential graph edges, and an internal
transition is exactly the crossed pair needed to complete a
repeated-color square. Common support ensures both diagonal
a-colored edges are actual edges. This converts B into (3), an
independent-set-type condition for a single symmetric operator.

The earlier pair-action operator acted simultaneously on two row
points; its crossed-edge test remained a coupled tensor. The new
operator removes that specific transfer gap. No inference from
individual tournament-like color matrices is required.

7. ROBUSTNESS, INCLUDING VIRTUAL FIXED-POINT LOOPS

Return to D_a subset X and let R be as in Section 1. An ordered
internal transition consists of (a,u) with u in E and tau_a(u) in E.
Write u=(x,y), tau_a(u)=(z,w). If both (a,x) and (a,z) are selected
colored incidences, then B forbids x!=z. If x=z, this is an actual
a-colored loop. Properness gives exactly m such good loops in total.

Every other internal transition has at least one missing diagonal
incidence (a,x) or (a,z). This also includes a virtual loop fixed by
tau_a when that a-incidence is missing and the point is selected in
some other color; such loops must not be silently counted as actual
color-a loops.

For a fixed missing (a,x), there are at most deg_G(x)<=d internal
transitions with x as their first row. Since tau_a is an involution,
there are at most deg_G(x)<=d with x as their second row. A union
bound, allowing double charging, gives the exact useful estimate

 <1_E,S_A 1_E>
   <=m+2 sum_{missing (a,x)} deg_G(x)
   <=m+2dR.                                          (13)

The same spectral lower bound now gives

       (d+lambda)m^2/p^2 <=(1+lambda)m+2dR.            (14)

Dropping lambda from the left denominator implies

              m^2 <=[p^2(1+lambda)/d]m+2p^2R.

For nonnegative m,B,C, the inequality m^2<=Bm+C implies
m<=B+sqrt(C). Hence

             m<=p^2(1+lambda)/d+sqrt(2)*p*sqrt(R).     (15)

Combining this with m<=dp at the same threshold d=p^(9/10)
proves (2). The d=0 case is the empty graph and needs no division.

8. A LOW-COMPLEXITY ACTUAL-SUPPORT CONSEQUENCE

Suppose the color-by-row support set is covered by L complete
rectangles A_i x X_i, each contained in that support. Restricting
the actual graph to any rectangle preserves properness and B, so
(1) and a union bound give

                         m<=C*L*p^(19/10).

Thus L=o(p^(1/10)) forces m=o(p^2). In particular this applies to
a bounded number of exact shared masks. It concerns rectangles in
the COLOR-BY-ROW support matrix, not bicliques in the edge adjacency
matrix; these are different complexity notions.

For a partition of the palette into L clusters whose supports are
subsets of common masks X_i, let R_i be their total missing entries.
Summing (2) and using Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 m<=C*L*p^(19/10)+sqrt(2)*p*sqrt(L*sum_i R_i).          (16)

Consequently L=o(p^(1/10)) and L*sum_i R_i=o(p^2)
are sufficient conditions for vanishing density.

This does NOT by itself settle arbitrary bounded-VC support families.
If an epsilon-cover uses L(epsilon) clusters and has total error
O(epsilon*p^2), (16) still requires epsilon*L(epsilon) to be small.
A polynomial covering bound alone need not supply that. No bounded-VC
conclusion is asserted without an additional argument.

9. SCOPE AND CERTIFICATION

The theorem rules out positive-density proper B graphs in the
quadratic common-mask subclass, and gives the stated robust and
rectangle-cover extensions. It does not solve arbitrary-support
quadratic colorings, arbitrary affine palettes, or Erdős #810.
The algebra and combinatorial charging were independently checked;
the Kloosterman estimate is standard cited input. This document is
a hand proof and carries no Lean or historical-novelty certification.
