INDEPENDENT AUDIT: QUADRATIC COMMON-DOMAIN COLORINGS
2026-10-15, finite_audit agent; root's crossed-involution argument.

Status: PASS. This is an analytic proof using the classical Weil bound for
Kloosterman sums. No finite graph enumeration or Lean verification is claimed.
Priority has not been established. The common-domain hypothesis is essential
to the transfer step below; this does not settle Erdős problem #810.

THEOREM

Let p be an odd prime, A a subset of F_p^*, and X a subset of F_p. On the two
copies of F_p, put in every edge (x,a*x+a^2) for a in A and x in X, with color a.
Suppose this is a proper coloring of a simple graph and every C4 is rainbow.
Then, with an absolute implied constant,

                  m = |A| |X| = O(p^(19/10)).

More precisely, put d=|A|. For d>0 set

 lambda = max( [(p-1)(3d^2-2d)]^(1/4),
               [2p*d^2+2sqrt(p)*d^3]^(1/4) ).

Then

             m <= min(dp, p^2(1+lambda)/(d+lambda)).       (1)

In particular d>=delta*p gives m=O_delta(p^(15/8)).

1. THE OPERATOR AND ITS EXACT SUPPORT INTERPRETATION

On Omega=F_p^2 define the affine involution

       tau_a(x,y)=(y/a-a, a*x+a^2).

Let U_a f=f composed with tau_a and S=sum_(a in A) U_a. These are unitary
involutions on counting-measure l2(Omega), so S is selfadjoint, and S*1=d*1.

Let E be the set of actual edge points. If e=(x,y) and tau_a(e)=(z,w) both
belong to E, then x,z belong to X. Since all colors of A have this SAME domain,
the two additional edges (x,w) and (z,y), both of color a, are also in E.
If x!=z, invertibility of a gives y!=w. The four positions are consequently a
genuine C4 with repeated color a, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence every
internal transition of S on E is a fixed point.

The equality tau_a(x,y)=(x,y) is equivalent to y=a*x+a^2. Every actual edge has
exactly one such a in A, since the coloring is simple and proper. Therefore

                    <1_E,S 1_E> = m.                    (2)

This is stronger than merely bounding the number of internal transitions.
It uses the actual support and is precisely where arbitrary separate color
domains would invalidate the proof.

2. FOURIER DECOMPOSITION

Write e_p(z)=exp(2*pi*i*z/p). In the normalized character basis,

 e_(xi,eta) composed with tau_a
    = e_p(-a*xi+a^2*eta) e_(a*eta,xi/a).

Thus the product xi*eta is invariant. The zero frequency is the constant
eigenvector. The two nonzero coordinate axes form one invariant subspace.
For each t!=0, the frequencies (r,t/r), r!=0, form another invariant subspace.
Their dimensions add to 1+2(p-1)+(p-1)^2=p^2, so no subspace is omitted.

With row index r and column index s, the t!=0 block is the Hermitian matrix

 M_t(r,s)=1_A(r*s/t) e_p(r*s*(r-s)/t),       r,s!=0.       (3)

Indeed the unique possible color is a=r*s/t. Reversing r and s conjugates the
phase, and the color indicator is unchanged.

3. THE FOURTH MOMENT, INCLUDING EVERY EXCEPTIONAL CASE

Expanding the trace gives

 tr(M_t^4) = sum_(r,s,u,v !=0)
   1_A(rs/t) 1_A(su/t) 1_A(uv/t) 1_A(vr/t)
   e_p((r-u)(s-v)(r+u-s-v)/t).                            (4)

The phase factorization follows by expanding
rs(r-s)+su(s-u)+uv(u-v)+vr(v-r).

Make the bijective substitution

 a=rs/t, q=u/r, h=v/s;
 s=t*a/r, u=q*r, v=h*t*a/r.

The color product becomes W(a,q,h)=1_A(a)1_A(qa)1_A(qha)1_A(ha), independent
of r. The remaining complete sum in r is

 sum_(r!=0) e_p(alpha*r+beta/r),
 alpha=a(1-q)(1-h)(1+q),
 beta=-t*a^2(1-q)(1-h)(1+h).                             (5)

Because p is odd and a,t are nonzero, both coefficients vanish exactly when
q=1, or h=1, or q=h=-1. The W-weighted number of such triples is

        d^2+d^2-d+|A intersect (-A)| <= 2d^2.             (6)

For example q=1 leaves W=1_A(a)1_A(ha), giving d^2; the q=h=1 overlap has d
triples. The q=h=-1 case contributes |A intersect (-A)| and is disjoint from
the first two cases in odd characteristic.

The total W-weighted number of triples is at most d^3: specifying a,qa,ha in A
determines q,h and leaves only the additional restriction qha in A.

If exactly one of alpha,beta is zero, the complete sum in (5) is -1. If both
are nonzero, its absolute value is at most 2sqrt(p), by the classical Weil
Kloosterman bound. If both are zero, its value is p-1. Consequently

       tr(M_t^4) <= 2p*d^2+2sqrt(p)*d^3.                 (7)

Since M_t is Hermitian, every eigenvalue to the fourth power is nonnegative,
and ||M_t||^4 <= tr(M_t^4). No positivity of individual summands in (4) is
being assumed.

External input, verified at its exact statement: Gergely Harcos, "Weil's
bound for Kloosterman sums", Theorem 1, page 1, gives
|sum_(r in F_p^*) e_p(alpha*r+beta/r)|<=2sqrt(p) for odd prime p and nonzero
alpha,beta. This is a classical theorem due to Weil, not a new estimate here.
https://users.renyi.hu/~gharcos/weil.pdf

4. THE AXIS BLOCK

The nonzero x-axis and y-axis are swapped by each U_a. In Fourier coordinates
the block has the form [0 B; B* 0], where B is (up to swapping the two axes)
the nonconstant Fourier matrix of the one-point operator

                 C_A = sum_(a in A) P_(x -> a*x+a^2).

For completeness, its bound does not require a new exponential-sum estimate.
If sigma_a(x)=a*x+a^2, the quotient sigma_a sigma_b^(-1) has slope rho=a/b and
intercept rho(rho-1)b^2. The identity has multiplicity d. Every nonidentity
quotient has multiplicity at most two: rho!=1 and the intercept fixes b^2.
Thus the multiplicative energy of the permutation set satisfies

                         E <= 3d^2-2d.

On the zero-sum representation of the affine permutation group, the character
is p-1 at the identity, -1 at a nonidentity translation, and 0 at every map
with slope different from 1. The group-algebra coefficients in (C_A C_A*)^2
are nonnegative counts. Its trace on that representation is therefore at
most (p-1)E, whence

                  ||B|| <= [(p-1)(3d^2-2d)]^(1/4).        (8)

This is also proved in pass3-common-mask-spectral-intermediate.txt. Either
convention for pullback permutation matrices gives the same quotient energy.

Equations (7) and (8) establish the displayed lambda bound for S on 1^perp.

5. HOFFMAN-TYPE INEQUALITY AND THE EXPONENT

Let N=p^2 and write 1_E=(m/N)1+v, with v perpendicular to 1. Then
||v||^2=m-m^2/N. Selfadjointness, (2), and the lower spectral bound give

 m = d*m^2/N + <v,Sv>
   >= d*m^2/N - lambda*(m-m^2/N).

For m>0 this rearranges to m<=N(1+lambda)/(d+lambda), proving (1). The empty
case is immediate. In particular

 m <= C [p^2/d + p^(9/4)d^(-1/2) + p^(17/8)d^(-1/4)].   (9)

If d<=p^(9/10), use m<=dp. Otherwise the three terms in (9) are at most
p^(11/10), p^(9/5), p^(19/10), respectively. This proves the uniform power
saving m=O(p^(19/10)). If d>=delta*p, (9) instead gives O_delta(p^(15/8)).

SCOPE AND REMAINING ISSUE

The theorem allows every palette A and every common row set X; neither must
be an interval, a subgroup, or near-full. It does not apply to arbitrary
color-dependent supports, because the two closing a-edges in Section 1 may
then be absent. A bounded union of color-row rectangles can be handled by
applying the theorem to the corresponding inherited B subgraphs and summing.
Any such extension must count the actual rectangles and their edge overlaps.

The proof is independent of the recent prime-field Szemeredi-Trotter input,
and uses only the explicitly stated classical Kloosterman estimate beyond
the elementary linear algebra and Fourier calculations above.
