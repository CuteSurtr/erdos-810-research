EXACT TWO-POINT MIXING FOR THE COMPLETED QUADRATIC AFFINE PALETTE
15 October 2026. Parent's spectral lemma independently audited.
Elementary finite Fourier proof; no graph search or novelty claim.

1. STATEMENT

Let p be an odd prime and sigma_a(x)=a*x+a^2 for a in F_p*.
On the ordered distinct-pair space

              Omega_2={(x,z) in F_p^2 : x!=z},

define the unnormalized operator

       (C_2 f)(x,z)=sum_{a!=0} f(sigma_a(x),sigma_a(z)).  (1)

Then for every f with sum_{Omega_2} f=0,

                       ||C_2 f||_2 <= sqrt(2p)||f||_2.  (2)

Either counting measure or uniform measure can be used for these
norms, provided the same normalization is used on both sides.

Consequently, for arbitrary fixed A,B subset Omega_2,

 | sum_{a!=0} |{u in A : sigma_a^{(2)}u in B}|
       - |A||B|/p |
 <= sqrt(2p) sqrt(|A||B|).                              (3)

Here sigma_a^{(2)} acts on both coordinates. The stronger centered
bound replaces the last square root by

 sqrt[|A|(1-|A|/[p(p-1)]) |B|(1-|B|/[p(p-1)])].         (4)

In particular, fixed positive-density pair sets have the expected
transition count, with relative error O(p^(-1/2)). This statement
concerns the completed maps and fixed pair sets. It does not count
configurations whose selected pair support varies with the color a.

2. FOURIER BLOCKS ON ALL OF F_p^2

First define C_2 on the whole square F_p^2 by the same formula.
Write e_p(t)=exp(2*pi*i*t/p) and use the orthogonal Fourier basis

             chi_(u,v)(x,z)=e_p(u*x+v*z).

The zero frequency is an eigenvector of eigenvalue p-1. Nonzero
frequencies split into projective scalar orbits. Fix a nonzero
representative v=(v_1,v_2), write frequencies as s*v with s!=0,
and put c=v_1+v_2. Then

 C_2 chi_(s*v)
   =sum_{a!=0} e_p(c*s*a^2) chi_(a*s*v)
   =sum_{r!=0} e_p(c*r^2/s) chi_(r*v).

Thus the matrix in this invariant Fourier block is

                  M_(r,s)=e_p(c*r^2/s), r,s!=0.         (5)

3. NONEXCEPTIONAL BLOCKS

If c!=0, additive-character orthogonality and the permutation
s->1/s of F_p* give

 (M M*)_(r,t)=sum_{s!=0} e_p(c*(r^2-t^2)/s)
             = p-1, if r^2=t^2;
             = -1,  otherwise.

Since p is odd and r,t are nonzero, r^2=t^2 precisely when t=r or
t=-r. Let K be the permutation matrix r->-r and let J be the
all-ones matrix of size p-1. Then

                         M M*=p(I+K)-J.                (6)

On functions odd under r->-r, this is zero. On the constant vector
it has eigenvalue 2p-(p-1)=p+1. On the even mean-zero subspace it
has eigenvalue 2p. Consequently ||M||<=sqrt(2p).

For p>=5 the even mean-zero sector is nonempty. For p=3 it is
empty, and the block norm is sqrt(p+1); the stated bound still holds.

4. THE EXCEPTIONAL PROJECTIVE ORBIT

Exactly one projective orbit has c=0, represented by (1,-1).
Its matrix M is J. The large singular direction is

 sum_{s!=0} chi_(s,-s)(x,z)=p*1_{x=z}-1,

with eigenvalue p-1; the other p-2 directions are in the kernel.
Together with the zero Fourier frequency, the only uncontrolled
directions are therefore the span of

                           1, 1_{x=z}.                 (7)

Every other block has the bound from Section 3. Both this span and
its orthogonal complement are reducing subspaces: each completed
map, and its inverse, preserves the diagonal and the whole square.

5. RESTRICTION TO DISTINCT PAIRS AND MIXING

Extend a mean-zero f on Omega_2 by zero on the diagonal. It is
orthogonal to 1 because its sum is zero, and orthogonal to 1_{x=z}
because it vanishes there. The estimate on the complement of (7)
therefore applies. The operator preserves the off-diagonal space,
proving (2).

Let N=|Omega_2|=p(p-1), and center the two indicators:

       f_A=1_A-|A|/N,       f_B=1_B-|B|/N.

Each sigma_a^{(2)} is a permutation, so both row and column sums of
C_2 are p-1. Hence, using counting-measure inner products,

 <1_A,C_2 1_B> = (p-1)|A||B|/N + <f_A,C_2 f_B>.

The first term is |A||B|/p. Cauchy--Schwarz and (2) give (3), with
the refinement (4) from ||f_A||_2^2=|A|(1-|A|/N).

6. GROUP INTERPRETATION AND THE ACTUAL-SUPPORT GAP

The full affine group of F_p acts sharply transitively on ordered
distinct pairs: a unique affine map sends any such pair to any
other. Thus C_2 can also be regarded as a Cayley convolution operator
on Aff(F_p) for the palette {(a,a^2):a!=0}. The proof above already
establishes the bound directly, without needing group expansion
theorems or an assertion that this estimate is historically new.

For an actual partial color a with row support D_a, its relevant
two-point action is multiplied by

                         1_{D_a}(x)1_{D_a}(z).

This weight depends on a as well as the input pair. It cannot in
general be moved outside the sum in (1), and it need not define a
fixed set A to which (3) applies. Crossed selected-edge conditions
introduce further dependence on input and output coordinates.

Accordingly, strong two-point mixing of the completions does not
prove the required four-slot counting/removal assertion. The cubic
phase note supplies a separate exact obstruction to a simple
four-partite counting assertion even when natural marginals are
uniform. Neither fact alone resolves arbitrary-support B-sparsity.
