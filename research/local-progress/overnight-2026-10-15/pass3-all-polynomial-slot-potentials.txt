ALL LOW-DEGREE POLYNOMIAL ADDITIVE IDENTITIES ON THE FOUR-SLOT VARIETY
15 October 2026. Parent's strengthened statement independently audited.
Hand proof; no claim of historical novelty, an inverse theorem, or a
new density bound. This strengthens the symmetric-ansatz audit in
pass3-polynomial-potential-classification-audit.txt.

1. PRECISE ALGEBRAIC STATEMENT

Let k be a field of characteristic zero, or of characteristic p>D,
where D bounds the total degrees of four polynomials

                         P_1,P_2,P_3,P_4 in k[x,y].

Suppose

       P_1(a,b)+P_2(a,c)+P_3(d,e)+P_4(f,g)=0              (1)

for ALL solutions over an algebraic closure of k of

       a+b=d+e,  ac=de,  a+c=f+g,  ab=fg,               (2)

with a,d,f nonzero. This is an identity on the algebraic solution
set, not merely a relation tested at the F_p-rational points of one
field, and not an identity restricted to selected root orientations.

Then there are univariate polynomials g_3,g_4,h_3,h_4,q over k and
a scalar kappa in k such that

 P_3(d,e) = g_3(d+e)+h_3(de)+kappa*de*(d+e),
 P_4(f,g) = g_4(f+g)+h_4(fg)-kappa*fg*(f+g),
 P_1(a,b) = -g_3(a+b)-h_4(ab)+kappa*a^2*b+q(a),
 P_2(a,c) = -g_4(a+c)-h_3(ac)-kappa*a^2*c-q(a).          (3)

Conversely, every choice in (3) satisfies (1) on (2).
The univariate decomposition is not claimed to be uniquely normalized;
constants can be moved between its terms.

2. THE CROSSED POLYNOMIALS MUST BE SYMMETRIC

On the open set where e!=0, replacing (d,e) by (e,d) preserves (2)
and the admissibility of the first coordinates. Subtract (1) for
these two solutions. This gives P_3(d,e)=P_3(e,d).

This equality holds on a dense set of (d,e). Explicitly, for any
nonzero d,e over the algebraic closure, choose a!=0,d+e and put

              b=d+e-a,             c=de/a.

Take f,g to be roots with sum a+c and product ab. Since ab!=0,
both roots are nonzero, and this extends (d,e) to an admissible
solution of (2). Thus P_3(d,e)=P_3(e,d) on all nonzero d,e, hence
as a formal polynomial identity. The same argument, interchanging
the two crossed positions, proves P_4 symmetric.

The fundamental representation of symmetric polynomials gives

          P_3(d,e)=G_3(d+e,de),
          P_4(f,g)=G_4(f+g,fg)                          (4)

with G_3,G_4 over k. They can be chosen with weighted degree <=D,
where sum has weight one and product has weight two. In particular
their usual total degrees are <=D<p in positive characteristic.

3. REDUCTION TO A FORMAL THREE-VARIABLE IDENTITY

Substituting (4) and (2) into (1) gives

 P_1(a,b)+P_2(a,c)+G_3(a+b,ac)+G_4(a+c,ab)=0.            (5)

For generic a,b,c, all a,b,c are nonzero and both crossed root
pairs exist over the algebraic closure with nonzero entries.
Therefore (5) holds on a dense open set of (a,b,c), and consequently
is a formal polynomial identity.

Mixed differentiation in b and c, followed by cancellation of a,
gives

          (G_3)_st(a+b,ac)+(G_4)_st(a+c,ab)=0.           (6)

Set b=c. The substitution (s,t)=(a+b,ab) is injective on polynomial
rings: s,t are algebraically independent elementary symmetric
polynomials in a,b. Equivalently, every (s,t) over the algebraic
closure has roots a,b. Hence

                       (G_4)_st=-(G_3)_st.             (7)

4. THE MIXED DERIVATIVE IS CONSTANT

Let F=(G_3)_st. Equations (6)-(7) imply

                        F(a+b,ac)=F(a+c,ab).

Set c=0 and f(s)=F(s,0). Then

                            F(a,ab)=f(a+b).             (8)

If f=0, coefficient comparison in b immediately gives F=0.
Otherwise put L=deg f and write F(s,t)=sum_j F_j(s)t^j.
Coefficients of b^j with j>L first give F_j=0. The coefficient of
b^L gives

                     a^L F_L(a)=leading_coefficient(f).

For L>0 the left side is divisible by a while the right side is a
nonzero constant, a contradiction. Thus L=0 and F is constant,
say kappa. By (7), (G_4)_st=-kappa.

5. INTEGRATION AND RECOVERY OF THE FIRST TWO POTENTIALS

In characteristic zero, or with deg G_i<=D<p, differentiation is
injective on each nonconstant monomial exponent that occurs.
The mixed-derivative identities therefore imply

           G_3(s,t)=g_3(s)+h_3(t)+kappa*st,
           G_4(s,t)=g_4(s)+h_4(t)-kappa*st.              (9)

After substituting (9) into (5), the cubic contribution is

           kappa[(a+b)ac-(a+c)ab]=kappa*a^2*(c-b).

Consequently

 [P_1(a,b)+g_3(a+b)+h_4(ab)-kappa*a^2*b]
 +[P_2(a,c)+g_4(a+c)+h_3(ac)+kappa*a^2*c]=0.

The first bracket depends only on a,b and the second only on a,c.
Setting c=0 shows the first is a polynomial q(a); the second is
then -q(a). These are exactly the remaining two formulas in (3).
Direct substitution proves the converse.

6. MEANING AND LIMITS

All polynomial additive identities within these hypotheses are
generated by the evident row, column, and repeated-color potentials,
together with the cubic identity

               a^2*b-a^2*c+de(d+e)-fg(f+g)=0.

Thus after factoring out the evident one-variable coordinate
potentials, there is one scalar cubic contribution in this class.
This is an algebraic classification only. It does not establish that
every analytic correlation comes from such a polynomial phase.

In particular, it supplies no inverse theorem for arbitrary bounded
functions, no treatment of polynomial identities holding only on a
chosen oriented branch of the root map, and no uniform four-partite
removal theorem. The general quadratic-affine arbitrary-support
density problem remains open in this research.
