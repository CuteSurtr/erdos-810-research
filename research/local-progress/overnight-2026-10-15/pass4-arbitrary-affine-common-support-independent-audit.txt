ARBITRARY AFFINE PALETTES WITH NEAR-COMMON DOMAINS
2026-10-15, finite_audit agent. Independent audit: PASS.

This is a qualitative extension of the crossed-reflection argument. It
uses the earlier independently audited global color-energy theorem and
small-difference theorem, which in turn use substantial standard inputs
including BGT structure, noncommutative BSG, and density Hales-Jewett.
No priority claim, Lean verification, or solution of the unrestricted
Erdos problem is asserted.

UPDATE: Section 7 gives a second proof using the published PRRW affine
energy theorem and Solymosi's group-table theorem directly. This second
route avoids the earlier BGT-based input and also avoids heavy-color
pruning. Its two exact source statements have been independently checked.

THEOREM

For each odd prime p let A subset F_p^*, let beta:A->F_p be arbitrary, and
let X subset F_p. For each a choose D_a subset X, and select precisely

                 (x,a*x+beta(a)), x in D_a,

with color a. Suppose the selected graph is simple, properly colored, and
every C4 is rainbow. Define the number of missing parameter incidences
relative to the common domain by

                   R=sum_(a in A) |X minus D_a|.

If R=o(p^2), then m=o(p^2). In particular, this holds with no regularity
assumption on beta when every D_a is the SAME set X.

One color per nonzero slope is part of the statement. The asymptotic is
uniform in all the data subject to the stated missing-incidence condition.

1. TWO PREVIOUSLY AUDITED INPUTS

(E) If d distinct permutations of an n-point set extend d original color
matchings, each of size at least eta*n, of a proper B graph, then uniformly
as d->infinity their global multiplicative energy is o(d^3).

(D) For a proper B graph represented by q distinct color permutations on
an n-point set, |SS^(-1)|<=Kq with fixed K and q->infinity implies
m=o(qn), uniformly over the actual partial supports.

The exact statements and complete proofs are saved in
../multicolor-2026-10-15/multiplicative-energy-obstruction.txt and
../multicolor-2026-10-15/partial-small-difference-theorem.txt.

These results apply to ORIGINAL partial matchings; they do not assume the
completed permutation graphs are B. This distinction is essential here.

2. GEOMETRIC FOURTH-MOMENT BOUND FOR EVERY BETA

Let d=|A|, S=sum_a U_(tau_a), with
tau_a(x,y)=((y-beta(a))/a, a*x+beta(a)). Define

 L=max{|{a in A:(a,beta(a)) lies on ell}|: ell an affine line},
 E_aff=E({sigma_a:a in A}), sigma_a(x)=a*x+beta(a).

The coordinate-axis block has norm to the fourth power at most (p-1)E_aff,
by the affine permutation character calculation. The nonzero xi*eta blocks
satisfy

             tr(M_t^4) <= p(L+2)d^2 + 2sqrt(p)d^3.       (1)

Here is the point needing no polynomial assumption. In the fourth-moment
coordinates a=rs/t, q=u/r, h=v/s, the weighted exceptional phases outside
q=1 or h=1 are precisely the collinear quadruples with slopes
a,qa,qha,ha. For each ordered pair a!=qa the first two points determine a
line, and ha has at most L choices on it. The fourth slope is then fixed.
Thus the exceptional count is at most L*d*(d-1)+2d^2-d. All other terms are
complete Kloosterman sums, bounded by 2sqrt(p), and the total weighted triple
count is at most d^3.

The coefficient-row proof of the equivalence with collinearity, including
repeated slopes, is in pass4-polynomial-palette-independent-audit.txt.
Only the upper bound L is substituted for its polynomial degree D.

Consequently the nonconstant operator norm obeys

 lambda^4 <= max((p-1)E_aff, p(L+2)d^2+2sqrt(p)d^3).       (2)

3. DENSE ACTUAL COLOR CLASSES FORCE L=o(p) AND E_aff=o(p^3)

Suppose d=Omega(p) and every color has at least eta*p actual edges, with eta
fixed. The completions sigma_a are distinct, because their slopes differ.
Input (E) therefore gives E_aff=o(d^3)=o(p^3).

Vertical lines contain at most one palette point. A line containing L>=2
palette points consequently has equation beta(a)=u*a+v. For those colors

 sigma_a(x)=a*(x+u)+v,
 sigma_a sigma_b^(-1)(y)=(a/b)*(y-v)+v.

Their quotient set lies in a group of at most p-1 dilations about v.
Writing T for the L colors on this line, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

                E_aff >= E(T) >= L^4/(p-1).

Since E_aff=o(p^3), this implies L=o(p). Thus input (E) alone suffices for
this step. Alternatively a hypothetical L>=epsilon*p would contradict
input (D) directly, because |TT^(-1)|<=p-1<=|T|/epsilon and all of its
original color matchings are heavy.

Equation (2) now gives lambda=o(p). No effective uniform rate is claimed,
since inputs (E) and (D) are qualitative.

4. THE MISSING-INCIDENCE CHARGE, INCLUDING EXTRA FIXED LOOPS

Let E be the actual edge-point set. Consider an internal transition
e=(x,y) -> tau_a(e)=(z,w) with both points in E. If the two closing color-a
incidences (a,x) and (a,z) are present, B forces x=z, and the transition is
the actual color-a loop. Summing these allowed loops gives exactly m.

Every other internal transition has at least one of (a,x),(a,z) missing.
For a fixed missing (a,x), at most deg_E(x)<=d choices of the first edge
point e can use it. The same bound applies with it as the second row,
by the involution symmetry. Thus

                     <1_E,S1_E> <= m+2dR.               (3)

This also charges an extra fixed loop when the point is an actual edge of
another color but the corresponding color-a incidence is missing. There
is no assumption that the completed common-domain coloring is proper.

The constant/nonconstant decomposition gives the lower bound

 <1_E,S1_E> >= d*m^2/p^2 - lambda*(m-m^2/p^2).            (4)

5. HEAVY-COLOR PRUNING AND CONTRADICTION

Suppose, contrary to the theorem, that m>=delta*p^2 on a sequence, for fixed
delta>0, while R=o(p^2). Since m<=d|X| and d,|X|<=p, both d and |X| are at
least delta*p.

Discard every color missing more than |X|/2 incidences. Their number is at
most 2R/|X|=o(p), and they carry at most 2pR/|X|=o(p^2) actual edges. Thus
the retained graph has m'=Omega(p^2), d'=Omega(p), and every retained class
has at least |X|/2>=delta*p/2 edges. Its missing count R' is at most R.

Section 3 applies to the retained palette, giving lambda=o(p). The right
side of (4) is then Omega(p^3), whereas (3) is at most
p^2+2pR'=o(p^3). This contradiction proves the theorem.

6. COROLLARY: ARBITRARY AFFINE PALETTES WITH NESTED DOMAINS

If the D_a are ordered by inclusion, then m=o(p^2), for arbitrary beta.
Indeed fix epsilon>0, order the colors by their domains, and split them
into consecutive full blocks of w=ceil(epsilon*p) colors. In each full
block retain only its minimum domain; discard the last short block. The
telescoping argument in pass4-nested-support-theorem.txt loses at most
2wp<=2epsilon*p^2+O(p) edges. There are at most 1/epsilon full blocks.
For each fixed epsilon, the common-domain theorem bounds the total number
of retained edges by o(p^2). Thus limsup m/p^2<=2epsilon. Letting epsilon
tend to zero proves the assertion. The quantifier order is fixed epsilon,
then p->infinity, then epsilon->0; no effective rate is inferred.

For a fixed nonlinear polynomial beta the separate explicit spectral bounds
give the stronger quantitative nested-domain bound O_D(p^(35/18)).

DEPENDENCY/SCOPE CHECK

The new argument retains the actual supports throughout. It combines the
already proved global-energy obstruction with a new Fourier-to-support
transfer valid in the common/near-common setting. Arbitrary unrelated
domains need not have R=o(p^2) or an inclusion-chain decomposition, so the
unrestricted affine-support problem and Erdős #810 remain unresolved.

7. SECOND PROOF USING ONLY DIRECT PUBLISHED INPUTS

Theorem 2.1 of Petridis--Roche-Newton--Rudnev--Warren, "An Energy Bound in
the Affine Group", bounds the energy of a finite affine palette S by

 E(S) << v^(1/2)|S|^(5/2) + L|S|^2,

where v is its maximum vertical-line occupancy, L its maximum line
occupancy, and v|S|<=p^2 in characteristic p. Here v=1 and d<=p-1, so

                    E_aff << d^(5/2)+L*d^2.              (5)

The source's energy convention counts g^(-1)h=u^(-1)v. This equals our
right-quotient energy: a*b^(-1)=c*d^(-1) if and only if
c^(-1)*a=d^(-1)*b, a bijective rearrangement of the four palette entries.
The source was checked at Theorem 2.1, printed page 3:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03401 .

Assume m>=delta*p^2 and R=o(p^2). Then |X|>=delta*p and d>=delta*p. If some
palette line has q>=epsilon*p points, its colors have at least

                     q|X|-R = Omega(p^2)

actual edges. Write its equation beta(a)=u*a+v and relabel rows and columns
by r=x+u and s=y-v. Its edges now satisfy s=a*r and carry color a=s/r.
At r=0, all such edges would have s=0; simplicity permits at most one
actual edge there. Remove it. All remaining rows and columns belong to
G=F_p^*, and the remaining point set still has positive density in G x G.

Solymosi's Theorem 5 says every positive-density subset of G x G, as the
finite group order grows, contains a rectangle whose opposite products
coincide. Applied after the coordinate bijection (r,s)->(s,r^(-1)), this
gives an actual rectangle with equal opposite ratios s/r, hence equal
opposite colors. This contradicts B. Therefore L=o(p).
The exact source statement was checked at Theorem 5, printed page 4:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133 .

Equation (5) now gives E_aff=o(p^3), and (2) gives lambda=o(p), directly
for the original palette. The contradiction between (3) and (4) completes
the proof, with no pruning and no use of inputs (E) or (D).

This is a dependency simplification, not a claim to have proved either
published theorem. The novel-status question concerns the combined
crossed-operator/support argument and remains unverified.

8. QUANTITATIVE COMMON-DOMAIN REFINEMENT VIA INTEGER ROTH NUMBERS

Independent audit of root's quantitative refinement: PASS. This section
assumes the domain is exactly common, R=0. It does not assert this rate
for an arbitrary sequence R=o(p^2).

Let r_3(n) be the largest size of a subset of {0,...,n-1} containing no
nonconstant integer three-term arithmetic progression. Define

             F(p)=r_3(p-1)/p.

Then every common-domain proper B coloring with arbitrary beta satisfies

        m/p^2 << p^(-1/11) + F(p)^(1/6).                 (6)

8A. The exact pencil bound

For a palette line beta(a)=u*a+v containing L colors, normalize rows and
columns to r=x+u and s=y-v. Remove the possible row r=0. Choose a generator
of the cyclic group F_p^* of order n=p-1. Write C for the logarithms of the
L colors, and B for the logarithms of (X+u) minus {0}. For each t in Z/nZ put

                         H_t=C intersect (B-t).

Suppose h-r,h,h+r lie in H_t, with r!=0 in Z/nZ. The selected multiplicative
table then has the following rectangle in logarithmic coordinates:

 rows:       t+h-r,       t+h;
 columns:    t+2h-r,      t+2h;
 colors:         h        h+r
                 h-r      h.

Both rows belong to B, and all three displayed color values belong to C.
Thus all four edges are ACTUALLY selected under the common-domain
hypothesis. Since r!=0, the rows and columns are each distinct. This is a
nonrainbow C4. If 2r=0, the two cross colors coincide, but the same genuine
four-cycle contradiction remains valid.

Hence H_t contains no such cyclic progression. Its representatives in
{0,...,n-1} are in particular free of nonconstant INTEGER progressions,
and |H_t|<=r_3(n). Averaging intersections gives

        L |B| = sum_t |H_t| <= n*r_3(n).

As |B|>=|X|-1, this proves the exact bound

             L(|X|-1) <= (p-1)*r_3(p-1).                (7)

Vertical palette lines contain at most one point and cause no difficulty:
the maximum L is attained on a nonvertical line whenever d>=2; for d=1
any nonvertical line through the sole point may be used.

8B. Normalization and exponent arithmetic

The cases d=0 or |X|<=1 have m<=p and are harmless. Otherwise set

       kappa=d/p, theta=|X|/p, rho=m/p^2=kappa*theta.

Thus 0<rho<=kappa<=1. From (7), because |X|-1>=|X|/2,

                        L/p <= 2F(p)*kappa/rho.          (8)

PRRW gives E_aff << d^(5/2)+Ld^2. Combining this with the nonzero-block
moment bound and using L>=1, d<=p gives

 lambda/p << p^(-1/8) kappa^(5/8)
                         + (L/p)^(1/4) kappa^(1/2).     (9)

Here the term 2sqrt(p)d^3 contributes p^(-1/8)kappa^(3/4), which is at most
the first term in (9); the additive 2 in L+2 is absorbed using L>=1.

The exact internal-count identity and spectral decomposition imply

 rho <= (1+lambda)/d.

Substitution of (8)-(9), followed by kappa>=rho, gives

 rho << p^(-1)rho^(-1)
          + p^(-1/8)rho^(-3/8)
          + F(p)^(1/4)rho^(-1/2).                       (10)

At least one summand on the right is a fixed positive fraction of rho.
The three respective possibilities give

 rho << p^(-1/2),   rho << p^(-1/11),   rho << F(p)^(1/6).

Absorbing the first into the second proves (6). All implied constants are
absolute and uniform over beta, A, and X.

8C. Published progression input and resulting rate

The Kelley--Meka bound, in Bloom--Sisask's published exposition, states
r_3(n)<=n*exp[-c(log n)^(1/12)] for an absolute c>0. Theorem 1, page 1,
was checked directly in its version-3 source:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.07211v3 .
Published version: Essential Number Theory 2 (2023), 15--44,
https://doi.org/10.2140/ent.2023.2.15 .

Consequently (6) gives absolute C,c'>0 such that

            m <= C*p^2*exp[-c'(log p)^(1/12)].           (11)

This uses a verified published progression bound, and makes no claim that
1/12 is the best available exponent. Formula (6) also transparently
transfers any stronger independently verified bound for r_3.

9. QUANTITATIVE NESTED AND INTERVAL SUPPORTS

Suppose a uniform common-domain upper bound is m<=G(p)*p^2, with G(p)->0.
We may assume G(p)>=1/p, because a single-color perfect matching is one
of the common-domain B graphs. Then support chains and bounded-interval
domains admit the upper bound

                       m << p^2*sqrt(G(p)).              (12)

For a chain, choose an integer K comparable to G(p)^(-1/2) and let
w=ceil(p/K). Divide the colors, ordered by inclusion of domains, into
consecutive full blocks of w colors. Restrict each to its minimum domain,
and discard the final short block. At most 2wp edges are lost by the
previous telescoping argument, and at most K common-domain blocks remain.
Thus

 m <= K*G(p)*p^2 + 2p^2/K + 2p = O(p^2*sqrt(G(p))).

For domains which are intervals in ONE common ordering of the p rows,
partition those rows into full consecutive blocks of w rows. For each
color retain only blocks wholly contained in its domain. Its two partial
boundary blocks and the final short block account for at most 3w deleted
edges. Over all colors the loss is at most 3wp. In each retained row block,
all its retained colors share that block as a common domain. There are at
most K such blocks, so the same bound proves (12). A union of at most r
intervals changes the boundary loss to at most (2r+1)wp, yielding (12) with
a constant depending on fixed r.

In particular, substituting G(p)=C*exp[-c(log p)^(1/12)] from (11) gives
the same stretched-logarithmic form, with a different positive constant
in the exponent, for arbitrary beta and nested domains or a fixed bounded
number of intervals in any common row ordering. No arithmetic structure
of this ordering is used.
