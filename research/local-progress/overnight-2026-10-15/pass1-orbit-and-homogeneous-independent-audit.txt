ARBITRARY-SUPPORT HOMOGENEOUS AFFINE COLORS AND AN ORBIT-SUM CRITERION
15 October 2026. Independent audit of finite_audit's homogeneous-affine
theorem and the parent agent's subgroup-orbit extension. Both pass
with the explicit distinct-completion condition stated below.
Hand proofs only, no finite search or Lean certificate. These are
conditional consequences of the previously audited group-table bound;
no general #810 solution or historical novelty claim is asserted.

Scope clarification added during audit: the strongest abstract cover
criterion below is an elementary weighted corollary of the earlier
UNIFORM m=o(q*n) small-difference theorem. It needs no free-action or
exceptional-set hypothesis. The explicit orbit decomposition remains
a transparent specialization and verification of the affine example;
it is not a stronger abstract structural theorem.

0. UNIFORM GROUP-TABLE INPUT

There is a function epsilon(h)->0 with the following property. For any
finite group H of order h, a partial bipartite group table, with rows
t in H, columns u in H, and color u*t^(-1), has at most

                       epsilon(h)*h^2                  (0.1)

actual edges if every actual C4 is rainbow. The bound is uniform in H.

One may use the earlier audited partial-small-difference theorem:
the at most h color permutations are left multiplications on H;
their difference set has size at most h, so its N-scale form applies
on the two h-element sides. This proves precisely the needed uniform
version of (0.1), including arbitrary actual supports and missing
colors. This note does not need an explicit rate for epsilon.

0A. STRONGER WEIGHTED COVER COROLLARY: NO ACTION-FREENESS NEEDED

Suppose distinct permutation completions of a B-colored graph on an
n-point action domain are covered by cosets g_i H_i, with h_i=|H_i|.
Assume

  sum_i h_i=O(n),
  for every fixed T, sum_{i:h_i<=T}h_i=o(n).             (0A.1)

Then m=o(n^2), regardless of stabilizers or orbit sizes. In particular,
min_i h_i->infinity is a sufficient replacement for the second condition.

Proof. Assign every color to one containing coset and write q_i for
the number assigned there. Distinctness gives q_i<=h_i. Fix delta>0.
Families with q_i<delta*h_i have altogether at most

  n*sum q_i <= delta*n*sum h_i=O(delta*n^2)              (0A.2)

edges. For the other families, their assigned palette S_i satisfies

  |S_i S_i^(-1)|<=h_i<=q_i/delta.                      (0A.3)

The earlier uniform small-difference theorem therefore bounds their
actual edge counts by epsilon_delta(q_i)*q_i*n, where
epsilon_delta(q)->0 as q->infinity, independently of the action size.
For h_i>T, q_i>=delta*T. Summing their bounds gives at most

  n*(sum q_i)*sup_{q>=delta*T}epsilon_delta(q)=o_T(1)*n^2,

because sum q_i<=sum h_i=O(n). The families with h_i<=T contribute
at most n*sum_{h_i<=T}h_i=o(n^2), by (0A.1). First send n to infinity,
then T to infinity, and finally delta to zero. This proves the claim.

The duplicate-completion reduction in Section 1(c) and the allowance
of o(n^2) uncovered actual edges apply here as well. This argument is
just a weighted use of the already proved uniform theorem; it does
not constitute a new inverse theorem producing a useful cover.

1. AN EXPLICIT SUBGROUP-ORBIT SPECIALIZATION

Let B be a properly colored B graph whose two sides are separately
embedded in an n-point set Omega. Suppose its actual colors have
DISTINCT permutation completions sigma_c. Cover these permutations by
left cosets g_i H_i of finite subgroups of Sym(Omega). Write h_i=|H_i|.
Assign each color to one containing coset, so an edge is counted in
only one family, even if the cosets overlap.

For each i assume that H_i has an invariant exceptional set Z_i and
acts freely on Omega\Z_i. Assume also

  sum_i h_i <= C*n,                                    (1.1)
  min_i h_i -> infinity,                               (1.2)
  sum_i h_i*|Z_i| = o(n^2),                            (1.3)

where C is fixed. Then the actual graph has o(n^2) edges.

Proof. Fix one family and write g=g_i, H=H_i, h=h_i, Z=Z_i. Because
the completions in this family are distinct, there are at most h
colors, each of the form g*s with s in H. On an H-orbit O outside Z,
choose a base point x_0. The free action gives a bijection

                 t in H -> t*x_0 in O.

The corresponding column set is g*O, labeled by u in H via g*u*x_0.
An edge of color g*s from row t*x_0 has column g*s*t*x_0. In these
coordinates it is exactly the group-table edge with column u=s*t and
color s=u*t^(-1). The original actual supports are retained unchanged.

Restricting colors and vertices and relabeling the row and column
sets preserve B. Thus each free orbit contributes at most
epsilon(h)*h^2 edges by (0.1). There are at most n/h such orbits, so
their total is at most n*epsilon(h)*h.

Rows in Z contribute at most h*|Z| edges, because there are at most
h colors and every color is a matching. Invariance ensures that these
edges go to g*Z and that the orbit decomposition did not miss other
rows. Summing all the families gives the exact upper bound

  m <= n*sum_i epsilon(h_i)*h_i + sum_i h_i*|Z_i|.       (1.4)

The first term is at most C*n^2*sup_{h>=min h_i}epsilon(h), which is
o(n^2); the second is o(n^2) by assumption. This proves the criterion.

Useful precise variants.

(a) Condition (1.2) can be replaced by the weaker weighted condition

  for every fixed T,  sum_{i:h_i<=T} h_i=o(n).

Split (1.4) at T, bound epsilon<=1 on the small orders, and choose T
large enough that sup_{h>T}epsilon(h) is as small as desired.

(b) Leaving o(n^2) actual edges uncovered does not affect the result.

(c) Distinctness of completions cannot simply be dropped inside a
block. Different original colors may have identical completions;
merging their symbols can create a nonrainbow C4 which the original
coloring did not have. For a graph with q<=C_0*n colors, however, the
dense-case reduction handles duplicates correctly. If m>=delta*n^2,
discard classes smaller than eta*n with eta=delta/(2C_0), retaining
at least delta*n^2/2 edges. At most 1/eta of these heavy classes can
share a completion, since their actual edges are disjoint subsets of
the same n-edge matching. Selecting one representative per completion
retains a fixed positive proportion of the remaining edges. It still
satisfies the same coset cover and budgets, so the distinct-completion
criterion gives a contradiction. This proves a q=O(n) extension under
the same cover assumptions without incorrectly merging colors.

2. HOMOGENEOUS AFFINE PALETTES: NO DEFECT ASSUMPTION

Let F be a finite field of size n and H<=F* a multiplicative subgroup
of size h. Assume h tends to infinity. Let beta:F*->F obey

                   beta(u*a)=u*beta(a)  (u in H).        (2.1)

There is at most one color for each nonzero slope a; its completion is

                   sigma_a(x)=a*x+beta(a).              (2.2)

The original color class may be ANY restriction of this permutation.
If the actual coloring is B, its total edge count is o(n^2), uniformly
over F, H, beta, and the actual supports.

Here is a direct block proof, giving all counts explicitly. Let
L=(n-1)/h, and partition slopes into the L cosets gH. In one such
family write a=g*u, u in H. Relabel its rows by the bijection

                       x'=g*x+beta(g).

Using (2.1), the color map becomes

  sigma_(gu)(x)=gu*x+u*beta(g)=u*x'.                     (2.3)

At x'=0 every color in the family maps to column 0. Because the
original graph is simple and its edges have unique colors, at most
ONE actual edge of this family occurs at (0,0). Discard this edge.

For x'!=0, equation (2.3) puts x' and its output in the same H-coset
vH of F*. There are L such cosets. In a block vH x vH, label rows
and columns by dividing by v. The color u is now the multiplication
table symbol y/x'=u on H. Missing slopes or missing edges simply give
a partial table. Therefore each block has at most epsilon(h)*h^2
actual edges. Different row relabelings for different color families
cause no problem: B is being used separately in each restricted
family, and the original edge sets of the families are disjoint.

There are L families and L blocks per family, so

  m <= L^2*epsilon(h)*h^2 + L
     = (n-1)^2*epsilon(h) + (n-1)/h
     = o(n^2).                                        (2.4)

This proof permits arbitrary supports. It is strictly a statement
about the specified representation class, not arbitrary affine
intercept functions.

The same result also follows from Section 1. For each family,
sigma_(gu)=M_u R_g, where R_g(x)=g*x+beta(g). Rewrite this right coset
as the left coset R_g*(R_g^(-1) H R_g). The conjugated dilation group
has order h and acts freely except at the one fixed point R_g^(-1)(0).
Thus sum_i h_i=Lh=n-1 and sum_i h_i|Z_i|=n-1. The direct argument
improves the exceptional term from n-1 to L but either is o(n^2).

3. EXAMPLES AND BOUNDED UNIONS

For F=F_(s^2) and beta(a)=a^s, take H=F_s*. Then beta(ua)=u*beta(a)
and h=s-1 tends to infinity. Hence EVERY B subgraph formed by arbitrary
restrictions of the complete palette x->a*x+a^s has o(n^2) edges.
This does not assert that the unreduced dense proper coloring from
the Frobenius construction is B; it proves that it cannot retain
positive density after imposing B.

More generally, any map beta linear over a subfield F_s satisfies
(2.1) for H=F_s*, and the conclusion holds as s tends to infinity,
with no restriction on the extension degree. Additivity of beta is
unnecessary: equivariance (2.1) alone is the exact hypothesis.

The palette may also be the union of a fixed number K of such families
beta_j, with at most one color per slope in each family. Restrict to
each family and apply (2.4); summing a bounded number of o(n^2) terms
still gives o(n^2). In the subgroup formulation it is enough that the
total group-order budget remain O(n) and the weighted small-order
and exceptional conditions hold. Identical-completion multiplicities
must be handled as in Section 1(c), not by merging symbols.

4. STATUS AND THE REMAINING STRUCTURAL PROBLEM

The weighted extension permits the number of structured palette
pieces to grow: it is their total group-order budget that controls
the proof. A bounded number of cosets is not required, and no global
small-difference bound for the entire palette is assumed. Section 0A
shows that this is already a consequence of the earlier uniform
theorem, without free-orbit assumptions. Sections 1--3 give an explicit
orbit-based verification and a concrete homogeneous-affine application.

However, no theorem here shows that arbitrary dense B colorings admit
such a cover. That representation theorem is the missing general
step. The argument above gives a complete arbitrary-support conditional
class and an exact structural target, rather than a universal density
bound or a solution of Erdős #810.

5. AN EXACT SIDON OBSTRUCTION TO FORCING SUCH A COVER

Call a finite permutation palette S multiplicatively Sidon if all its
nonidentity ordered quotients a*b^(-1), a!=b, are distinct. For every
nonempty subset U of size q this means exactly

                       |U U^(-1)|=q(q-1)+1.            (5.1)

If an assigned palette piece U_i of size q_i lies in g_i H_i, its
quotients lie in the conjugate g_i H_i g_i^(-1), so

  q_i(q_i-1)+1<=h_i,     q_i<=2*sqrt(h_i).              (5.2)

Consequently a Sidon palette covered under sum h_i<=C*n and
min h_i->infinity must have

  |S|=sum q_i<=2*sum sqrt(h_i)
                <=2*C*n/sqrt(min h_i)=o(n).            (5.3)

The weighted escape version (0A.1) gives the same result. For fixed T,
small groups contribute at most sum_{h_i<=T}h_i=o(n) colors. Large
groups contribute at most 2*sum h_i/sqrt(T)<=2*C*n/sqrt(T). Letting
T tend to infinity proves |S|=o(n).

In particular, a Theta(n)-sized Sidon palette cannot satisfy this
weighted subgroup-cover criterion. More generally, a Sidon subset
with |U U^(-1)|<=K|U| has |U|<=K+1 by (5.1). Hence a method seeking
large fixed-doubling subsets also cannot extract them from such a
palette without additional information or a changed representation.

There are two explicit reasons this limitation matters here:

  - The parabolic affine palette sigma_a(x)=a*x+a^2 on an antipodal
    half-set of F_p* is Sidon, has Theta(p) colors, and admits proper
    classes of size (3/4+o(1))*p. It is NOT B, as shown by an explicit
    forced rectangle in pass1-affine-independent-audit.txt. Thus
    properness, affine representation, and large supports do not
    force the cover criterion.

  - The earlier padding construction gives arbitrary proper partial
    colorings Sidon permutation completions after doubling the action
    size. Therefore choosing arbitrary completions and expecting this
    criterion to hold is not justified. The existing theorem supplies
    an implication IF a useful cover exists; it does not construct one.

These observations do not refute a theorem constructing favorable
completions or structured actual restrictions using the full B
condition. They identify exactly where such a new theorem is required
and prevent the weighted corollary from being mistaken for a general
inverse result.
