CLASSIFICATION OF FORMAL POLYNOMIAL PHASE IDENTITIES FOR THE FOUR SLOTS
15 October 2026, overnight pass 3.

Status: complete elementary algebraic proof. Independent checks by the
root and formal-audit agents. This is not a solution of the selected-
support counting problem, and no historical originality is asserted.

This is an independent proof/audit of the SAME classification saved in
pass3-all-polynomial-slot-potentials.txt, which may be used as the
canonical consolidated theorem statement. They are not separate results.

1. PRECISE DOMAIN OF THE STATEMENT

Let k be a field of characteristic zero. Alternatively let k have
positive characteristic p and suppose every polynomial P_i below
has total degree strictly less than p.

Work over an algebraic closure of k, and assume that the formal
polynomial identity

  P_1(a,b)+P_2(a,c)+P_3(d,e)+P_4(f,g)=0              (1)

holds on the full algebraic solution variety

  a+b=d+e, ac=de, a+c=f+g, ab=fg,   a,d,f!=0.      (2)

Here P_i is a polynomial in its displayed ordered pair. “Formal”
means an identity on this algebraic variety, not merely an equality
of functions on the F_p-rational points for one fixed small p.
No differentiation of an arbitrary finite-field function is used.

Then there are univariate polynomials U_3,V_3,U_4,V_4,A and a
constant kappa such that

 P_1(a,b)= kappa a^2b - U_3(a+b) - V_4(ab) + A(a),
 P_2(a,c)=-kappa a^2c - U_4(a+c) - V_3(ac) - A(a),
 P_3(d,e)= kappa de(d+e) + U_3(d+e) + V_3(de),
 P_4(f,g)=-kappa fg(f+g) + U_4(f+g) + V_4(fg).     (3)

Conversely every choice in (3) gives an identity (1) on (2).

Thus the separate shared color, sum and product potentials, together
with one cubic compatibility term, generate ALL polynomial slotwise
additive identities in this precise formal/bounded-degree sense.

2. THE CROSSED POLYNOMIALS ARE SYMMETRIC

Swapping d and e preserves the equations (2); on the dense open set
where both are nonzero it preserves the color-coordinate restrictions
as well. Subtracting the identities before and after the swap gives

  P_3(d,e)=P_3(e,d).

The projection of that open set onto (d,e) is dominant: for generic
d,e choose a!=0, set b=d+e-a and c=de/a, then take f,g to be roots of
the polynomial with sum a+c and product ab. Generically both roots
are nonzero. Hence the displayed symmetry is a polynomial identity
in d,e. The same argument applies independently to P_4(f,g).

By the elementary symmetric-polynomial theorem, write

  P_3(d,e)=H_3(d+e,de),
  P_4(f,g)=H_4(f+g,fg).

The identity now becomes

  P_1(a,b)+P_2(a,c)
    +H_3(a+b,ac)+H_4(a+c,ab)=0                     (4)

as a polynomial identity in a,b,c. Indeed all generic a,b,c lift
to (2) over the algebraic closure. Polynomial identities extend
from this dense open set.

3. THE MIXED DERIVATIVES ARE OPPOSITE CONSTANTS

Differentiate (4) once in b and once in c. For a!=0 this yields

  (H_3)_{st}(a+b,ac)+(H_4)_{st}(a+c,ab)=0.         (5)

Set c=b. The map (a,b)->(a+b,ab) is dominant, so (5) implies

  (H_4)_{st}=-(H_3)_{st}.

Putting H=(H_3)_{st}, equation (5) becomes

  H(a+b,ac)=H(a+c,ab).                              (6)

Here is an elementary invariant-polynomial lemma. Set c=0 and put
f(s)=H(s,0). Then (6) says

  H(a,ab)=f(a+b).                                    (7)

If f has degree m>0 and nonzero leading coefficient, compare the
coefficient of b^m. On the left it is divisible by a^m in k[a]; on
the right it is the nonzero constant leading coefficient of f.
This is impossible. Thus f is constant, and (7) makes H constant
because the substitution (a,b)->(a,ab) is dominant.

Equivalently, in the Laurent ring k[s,s^-1,t], (7) says
H(s,t)=f(s+t/s); a nonconstant f would give a highest t coefficient
with a negative power of s, impossible for a polynomial H.

We have proved

  (H_3)_{st}=kappa,       (H_4)_{st}=-kappa.

In characteristic zero, integration gives

  H_3(s,t)=kappa st+U_3(s)+V_3(t),
  H_4(s,t)=-kappa st+U_4(s)+V_4(t).                 (8)

The same conclusion holds in positive characteristic under the
degree hypothesis. The symmetric-polynomial representations H_i
have weighted degree at most deg(P_i), with s of weight 1 and t
of weight 2, so each variable's degree is less than p. A monomial
s^i t^j with i,j>0 therefore has nonzero mixed derivative unless
it is absent; Frobenius derivative-zero terms cannot occur.

4. RECOVER THE TWO DIAGONAL POLYNOMIALS

Substitute (8) into (4), using

  (a+b)ac-(a+c)ab=a^2(c-b).

The result separates into a polynomial in (a,b) plus a polynomial
in (a,c). Each must depend only on a, with opposite signs. Denote
the first by A(a). Rearranging gives precisely the first two
formulas in (3). Direct substitution verifies the converse.

5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY

The kappa=0 terms are the obvious cancellations along variables
shared by pairs of slots: a, a+b, a+c, ab, ac. The kappa term is
the additional cubic identity recorded and quantitatively tested in
pass3-quadratic-variety-and-cubic-phase-obstruction.txt.

This is a classification of formal polynomial ADDITIVE potentials.
It does not classify rational or analytic potentials, multiplicative
character obstructions, functions of growing degree, nilsequence
factors, or all possible mechanisms obstructing a counting lemma.

Most importantly, the proof uses both choices of each crossed root
pair. A single properly oriented support may keep only one choice,
so symmetry of arbitrary selected-support test functions must NOT
be inferred from Section 2. The formal classification does not
justify throwing away the fourth membership test, nor does it prove
supersaturation for one common properly oriented support.
