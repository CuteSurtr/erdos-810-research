1. COMPLEX WEIGHTS AND FOURTH SINGULAR MOMENTS

For complex palette weights w(a), the crossed operator S_w need not be
selfadjoint. The canonical proof correctly uses tr(M_w M_w* M_w M_w*)
instead of tr(M_w^4). Its cyclic entry product is

 M(r,s) conjugate(M(u,s)) M(u,v) conjugate(M(r,v)).

With a=rs/t, q=u/r, h=v/s, the four palette slopes are a,qa,qha,ha and
the coefficient product is w(a)conj(w(qa))w(qha)conj(w(ha)). It is
independent of the free complete-sum variable r. The phase is exactly the
earlier alpha*r+gamma/r. Thus the same Kloosterman bound applies after
bounding coefficient magnitudes by ||w||_infinity^4. The exceptional
collinearity count (L+2)d^2 and total slope count E_times(A)<=d^3 remain
valid.

The coordinate-axis correction is necessary and is now included correctly.
Affine-character coefficients in a weighted fourth moment can be complex,
so nonidentity translations with character -1 cannot simply be discarded.
Their absolute contribution is at most d^3||w||_infinity^4, since the
slope-one condition determines the fourth slope from the first three,
and the palette has one map per slope. Identity words cost at most
(p-1)E_aff||w||_infinity^4. Other affine maps have zero mean-zero character.

The two off-diagonal axis blocks have equal norms: in original point
coordinates the permutation matrices are real, so conjugating weights
conjugates the matrix, and the other block is an adjoint of this matrix.
This verifies the stated uniform bound

 ||S_w-(sum_a w(a))P|| <= Lambda||w||_infinity.

2. THE EXACT MASKED EXPANSION AND ITS POSITIVE MAIN TERM

For the real binary row masks D_a,

 T_D=sum_a M_Da U_(tau_a) M_Da
    =sum_(k,l) M_k S_(hat D_a(k)hat D_a(l)) M_l.

There is no conjugate in this Fourier product, since it expands two
multiplication operators directly. After replacing each middle operator
by its constant projection, Fourier inversion recombines the terms into

 T_0=sum_a M_Da P M_Da=(1/p^2)sum_a |v_a><v_a|,
 v_a(x,y)=1_Da(x).

This is positive because the masks are real. Unit norm of the Fourier
multipliers and the triangle inequality give

 ||T_D-T_0|| <= Lambda sum_(k,l) sup_a|hat D_a(k)hat D_a(l)|
             <= Lambda [sum_k sup_a|hat D_a(k)|]^2
              = Lambda W^2.

Interchanging the supremum and sum would not be valid. The canonical
definition of W has them in the required order.

3. ACTUAL-EDGE TRANSFER AND EXPONENTS

The two masks in T_D require both closing color-a incidences to be actual.
Thus every internal non-fixed transition would give a genuine nonrainbow
C4. Fixed masked transitions are precisely the actual color-a edges;
properness gives one per actual edge. Hence <1_E,T_D1_E>=m exactly.

With r_x=sum_a 1_Da(x) the actual row degree,

 <1_E,T_0 1_E>
  =p^(-2)sum_a [sum_x 1_Da(x)r_x]^2
  >=(dp^2)^(-1)[sum_x r_x^2]^2
  >=m^4/(dp^4).

The first inequality is color Cauchy-Schwarz, the second is row
Cauchy-Schwarz. Since ||1_E||^2=m, the canonical master inequality

                       m^3<=dp^4(1+Lambda W^2)

follows. For fixed nonlinear polynomial degree D, Lambda=O_D(p^(7/8))
and d<=p, giving m<=C_D p^(47/24)W^(2/3). The extra p^(5/3) term is
absorbed because a nonempty mask has Fourier l1 norm at least one by
Fourier inversion, and hence W>=1.

The same Lambda estimate holds when the palette has at most C_0 sqrt(p)
points on any coefficient-plane line, using PRRW with vertical occupancy
one. In both cases the exact vanishing-density threshold is
W=o(p^(1/16)), because 2-47/24=1/24 and (1/24)/(2/3)=1/16.

4. COMMON ENVELOPE FOR ARBITRARY CYCLIC INTERVALS

Let I be any cyclic interval in F_p of any length and position. For l!=0
write j=min(l,p-l). The normalized geometric-series formula gives

 |hat 1_I(l)|<=min(|I|/p, 1/[p |sin(pi*l/p)|])
             <=min(1,1/(2j)),

using sin(pi*j/p)>=2j/p for 1<=j<=p/2. At l=0 use the bound 1.
The same nonnegative envelope b therefore works for EVERY interval, and

                         sum_l b(l)<=C log(2p).

If the mask is 1_I(k*x) for k!=0, its Fourier coefficient at frequency l
is hat 1_I(l/k); thus its common envelope is a permutation of b. For

 D_a={x:k_j*x belongs to I_(a,j) for all 1<=j<=r},

the indicator is the product of r such masks. Under normalized Fourier
coefficients, the transform of a product is the ordinary convolution of
the transforms. Convolving the r shared nonnegative envelopes bounds
every color's transform simultaneously. The total mass of a convolution
is the product of the masses, so

                         W<=(C log(2p))^r.

Repeated frequencies, empty or full intervals, varying interval lengths,
and arbitrary cyclic shifts do not invalidate this bound. What matters
is that the LIST OF FREQUENCIES is shared across the colors; otherwise
the same small collection of common envelopes is unavailable.

5. GROWING COMPLEXITY AND CONSTANTS

If

 limsup_(p->infinity) r(p)*log log p/log p < 1/16,

choose a fixed positive gap from 1/16. Since
log(C log(2p))=log log p+O(1), and the limsup hypothesis implies
r(p)=O(log p/log log p), the additional O(r(p)) in the logarithm of the
majorant is o(log p). Therefore for some fixed epsilon>0, eventually

 (C log(2p))^r <= p^(1/16-epsilon)=o(p^(1/16)).

This verifies the claimed growing-r consequence without dropping the
C^r factor. The implicit polynomial-palette constant depends on its
fixed degree D, not on r or on the shared frequencies. The result is
therefore stronger in scope than merely fixing one VC dimension.

6. OPTIONAL ARBITRARY-BETA STATEMENT

The W=O(1), arbitrary-intercept extension also passes. Heavy-color pruning
loses at most half of a hypothetical positive edge density and cannot
increase W. Solymosi rules out a palette line of linear size among the
remaining heavy colors. PRRW then gives E_aff=o(p^3), and the corrected
weighted Lambda formula gives Lambda=o(p), including its d^3 term.
The master inequality contradicts m=Omega(p^2). This proof gives no
growing-W rate for arbitrary beta and makes no such claim.

SCOPE

The negative-spectrum obstruction for arbitrary masks is compatible with
this theorem: it has no proved small JOINT Fourier majorant. The proof
approximates T_D by a positive support-dependent main term and controls
the error under an explicit hypothesis. It does not assume T_D itself is
a uniform expander or has o(p) negative spectrum without that hypothesis.
