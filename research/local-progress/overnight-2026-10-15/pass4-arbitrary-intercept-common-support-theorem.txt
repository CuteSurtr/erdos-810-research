ARBITRARY AFFINE INTERCEPTS WITH COMMON OR NEARLY COMMON SUPPORT
15 October 2026. Complete analytic deduction, hand audited.

Status: subquadratic theorem for a support subclass; Section 9 also gives
an explicit common-support rate from integer Roth bounds. No
polynomial hypothesis is imposed on the intercept function. This does
not solve arbitrary-support affine colorings or unrestricted Erdos #810.
Historical priority is unverified. No Lean certification is asserted.

1. STATEMENT

For each odd prime p let A subset F_p* and let beta:A->F_p be arbitrary.
For a in A select exactly the color-a edges

                    x -> ax+beta(a), x in D_a,

where D_a subset X subset F_p. Suppose the resulting colored graph is
simple and proper, and every C4 is rainbow (property B). Write

           d=|A|, m=sum_a |D_a|, R=sum_a (|X|-|D_a|).

THEOREM. Uniformly over all these choices, R=o(p^2) implies m=o(p^2).
In particular, common row support D_a=X always implies m=o(p^2).
There is one original color per slope, so the representing affine maps
are distinct and d<=p-1. No degree restriction or structured formula for
beta is used.

For EXACT common support, the additional argument in Section 9 proves

 m/p^2 << p^(-1/11)+(r_3([p-1])/p)^(1/6)
        << exp(-c(log p)^(1/12)).                            (Q)

Here r_3([n]) denotes the maximum size of an integer subset of
{0,...,n-1} containing no nontrivial three-term arithmetic progression.
The last inequality uses a published Kelley--Meka-type Roth bound.

The proof below uses three established inputs: Weil's Kloosterman bound,
the affine-energy bound of Petridis--Roche-Newton--Rudnev--Warren, and
Solymosi's finite-group-table theorem. An alternative route through the
earlier general heavy-color energy theorem is recorded in Section 7,
but is not needed for the main proof.

2. A MASTER SPECTRAL INEQUALITY FOR ANY INTERCEPT FUNCTION

Let P={(a,beta(a)):a in A} in the coefficient plane, and let L be the
largest number of its points on an affine line (take L=0 if A is empty).
Let E_aff be the quotient energy of S={sigma_a:a in A}:

 E_aff=#{(g,h,u,v) in S^4:gh^-1=uv^-1}.

Let E_x(A) be the multiplicative energy of the slope set, at most d^3.
Define crossed involutions on F_p^2 by

       tau_a(x,y)=((y-beta(a))/a, ax+beta(a)),
       S_A f=sum_{a in A} f composed with tau_a.

On mean-zero functions,

 ||S_A|| <=lambda,
 lambda^4 <= max{(p-1)E_aff,
                       p(L+2)d^2+2sqrt(p)E_x(A)}.              (1)

Here lambda may be chosen as the fourth root of the right-hand side.
It need not be less than d in sparse ranges.

Proof of (1). The Fourier product blocks and phase coefficients are
exactly those in pass4-polynomial-intercept-common-support-theorem.txt,
Sections 3--4. The proof there did not use polynomiality until bounding
line intersections. For q,h!=1, the two coefficient equations vanish
exactly when the values at slopes a,qa,qha,ha are affine in slope; the
rank-two argument includes repeated slopes. Choose ordered a,b=qa with
a!=b, then choose c=ha on their coefficient-plane line. There are at
most L choices for c, and the fourth slope bc/a is determined. The
q=1 or h=1 cases contribute 2d^2-d. Thus at most (L+2)d^2 exceptional
triples occur. All other r-sums are bounded by 2sqrt(p), proving the
second term in (1). The axis-block character argument gives the first
term (p-1)E_aff without any special formula for beta. QED.

For the actual edge set E, the missing-incidence charging argument also
uses no polynomial hypothesis:

               <1_E,S_A1_E> <= m+2dR.

In particular, it charges virtual fixed-point loops correctly. Centering
1_E gives the exact useful inequality

              (d+lambda)m^2/p^2 <=(1+lambda)m+2dR.           (2)

For d>0 this implies

              m<=p^2(1+lambda)/d+sqrt(2)p sqrt(R).          (3)

3. THE PUBLISHED AFFINE-ENERGY INPUT AND ITS HYPOTHESES

Petridis, Roche-Newton, Rudnev and Warren, An Energy Bound in the Affine
Group, Theorem 2.1, gives for an affine-map set S of size d with maximum
line occupancy L and vertical-line occupancy v,

                        E_aff << v^(1/2)d^(5/2)+L d^2,

provided v d<=p^2 in characteristic p. For our P there is at most one
point on any vertical line, hence v=1 and d<=p-1 verifies the hypothesis.
Thus

                         E_aff <<d^(5/2)+L d^2.             (4)

Their displayed energy is #{g^-1 h=u^-1 v}, which has the same value
as our right-quotient energy: gh^-1=uv^-1 iff u^-1 g=v^-1 h. This is a
bijection of ordered quadruples, not an abelian-group assumption.

Primary source, Theorem 2.1 on PDF page 3:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03401
Published: IMRN 2022, issue 2, pages 1154--1172.
https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/2022/2/1154/5850679

The theorem statement and the vertical occupancy / characteristic
conditions were checked directly in the primary PDF by two agents.

4. A LARGE COLLINEAR SUBPALETTE WOULD CONTRADICT SOLYMOSI'S THEOREM

Assume for contradiction along a sequence p->infinity that

                         m>=epsilon p^2, R=o(p^2),           (5)

with fixed epsilon>0. Since m<=d|X| and d,|X|<=p, both d and |X| are
at least epsilon p. We show L=o(p) along this sequence.

If not, some coefficient-plane line contains at least delta p points
for fixed delta>0 along a subsequence. Vertical lines contain at most
one point, so the line has equation beta(a)=u a+v. The corresponding
color subgraph has at least

                         L|X|-R >=delta epsilon p^2-o(p^2)

actual edges. Make row and column changes x'=x+u, y'=y-v. These maps
are bijections and the retained lines become y'=a x'. The only possible
edge with x'=0 or y'=0 is the point (0,0), and simplicity/properness
allows at most one actual edge there. Delete it.

The remaining graph is a partial multiplication-table graph on the two
sides F_p*, with edge color a=y'/x'. Invert the row labels if desired
to write the color as the product of row and column. Solymosi's Theorem
5 says that a positive-density subset of a sufficiently large finite
group table contains a nondegenerate rectangle with equal diagonal
products. This gives a C4 with repeated opposite color, contrary to B.
Consequently

                                L=o(p).                     (6)

Source: J. Solymosi, The (7,4)-conjecture in finite groups, Theorem 5,
PDF page 4:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133

The partial supports need not coincide within this pencil. The estimate
L|X|-R uses only the total missing-incidence budget, so the cited group
theorem is applied to the actual retained edges, not to completed maps.

5. FINISHING THE QUALITATIVE THEOREM

Under (5) we have epsilon p<=d<=p. Equations (4) and (6) give
E_aff=o(p^3). Equation (1) then gives

 lambda^4 <=max{o(p^4), o(p^4)+O(p^(7/2))}=o(p^4),

so lambda=o(p). Equation (3), together with d>=epsilon p and R=o(p^2),
forces m=o(p^2), contradicting (5). This proves the theorem uniformly.

The error rates in this qualitative deduction include the finite-group
table theorem. Section 9 gives an explicit stretched-logarithmic rate
for exact common support. No polynomial saving is asserted for arbitrary
intercepts. Fixed-degree nonlinear polynomial intercepts admit the
separate O_D(p^(19/10)) bound proved in the companion note.

6. STANDARD EXPONENTIAL-SUM INPUT

The nonexceptional block estimate uses only the prime-field Kloosterman
bound, with one-zero coefficients handled by character orthogonality:
Iwaniec--Kowalski, Analytic Number Theory, Theorem 11.11 and (11.15),
printed page 280:
https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/ik-ant-exp-sums.pdf

The Fourier reduction and the treatment of zero coefficients are fully
written in pass4-polynomial-intercept-common-support-theorem.txt. There
is no assumed randomness of the four palette membership tests.

7. ALTERNATIVE DEPENDENCY ROUTE, NOT NEEDED ABOVE

The earlier note multicolor-2026-10-15/multiplicative-energy-obstruction.txt
proves that d->infinity distinct completions of proper B color classes,
each of size at least eta p, have E_aff=o(d^3). That theorem uses a local
approximate-group square argument, BGT, noncommutative BSG, and DHJ.

It also yields the result here. Under (5), discard colors missing at
least epsilon p/2 entries from X. There are at most 2R/(epsilon p)=o(p)
such colors, so o(p^2) edges are lost. Every retained class has at least
epsilon p/2 edges, and there remain Theta(p) distinct affine maps.
The heavy-color theorem gives E_aff=o(p^3).

For any nonvertical coefficient-plane line with L' retained points, its
affine maps have a relative quotient set of size at most p-1: after
normalization they form a subset of the multiplicative group. Hence
Cauchy--Schwarz gives E_aff>=L'^4/(p-1), so L'=o(p). The master estimate
and (3) again contradict density. This alternative is logically sound,
but the published affine-energy bound plus Solymosi provides a simpler
and more directly sourced dependency chain for this affine subclass.

8. LIMITS

The conclusion requires a common container with o(p^2) omissions, or a
separate support decomposition that reduces to that situation. General
partial affine colorings can have Theta(p^2) omissions in every such
container; this proof supplies no universal way to repair them. It also
assumes one color for each slope and prime fields. The theorem is an
audited mathematical deduction, not a claim to solve general #810 or a
certification of historical originality.

9. AN EXPLICIT COMMON-SUPPORT RATE VIA INTEGER ROTH

Suppose R=0, put theta=|X|/p, kappa=d/p, and rho=m/p^2=kappa theta.
If |X|<=1, the edge bound m<=p is already stronger than (Q), so assume
|X|>=2. Consider any nonvertical coefficient-plane line containing L_0
palette points; write it beta(a)=u a+v and let T be its slope set.
Discard the possible exceptional row x=-u, and put Y=(X+u)\{0}.
Thus |Y|>=|X|-1. After shifting columns by -v, the pencil edges are
precisely (x',a x') for a in T and x' in Y.

Choose a generator of F_p*, put n=p-1, and write A_0=log T and
Y_0=log Y as subsets of Z_n. For every t in Z_n, define

                         H_t=A_0 intersect (Y_0-t).

The set H_t is strongly three-term-AP-free. Indeed, suppose
h-r,h,h+r all belong to H_t with r!=0 in Z_n. Take two row logarithms
t+h-r and t+h and diagonal color h. The column logarithms are
t+2h-r and t+2h. The cross-colors are h+r and h-r, respectively.
All four edges are selected by common support, all rows and columns
are distinct because r!=0, and the two diagonal colors coincide.
This violates B.

This argument also handles an element r of order two: h-r=h+r can
then hold, but the rectangle still has distinct rows and columns and
repeated diagonal color. No odd-order group theorem is being assumed.
Integer representatives of H_t in {0,...,n-1} are consequently ordinary
integer 3AP-free, so |H_t|<=r_3([n]). Averaging over t gives

               L_0 |Y|=sum_t |H_t|<=n r_3([n]).             (20)

Vertical coefficient-plane lines contain at most one palette point.
Let F=r_3([p-1])/p. Since |X|-1>=|X|/2, (20) implies for the maximum
line occupancy L

                             L/p<=2F/theta,                 (21)

with a harmless absolute enlargement for the vertical-line case.

Using the affine-energy estimate (4) in the master bound (1), followed
by taking fourth roots and using E_x(A)<=d^3, gives

 lambda/d <<p^(-1/8)kappa^(-3/8)
             +(L/p)^(1/4)kappa^(-1/2)
             +p^(-1/8)kappa^(-1/4).                        (22)

The exact common-support identity and the spectral lower bound yield
rho<=(1+lambda)/(d+lambda)<=(1+lambda)/d. Substitute (21) and note
kappa>=rho and theta=rho/kappa. Since 0<rho<=1,

 rho <<p^(-1)rho^(-1)+p^(-1/8)rho^(-3/8)
                                   +F^(1/4)rho^(-1/2).     (23)

At least one of the three terms is a fixed positive fraction of rho.
The resulting alternatives give respectively rho<<p^(-1/2),
rho<<p^(-1/11), or rho<<F^(1/6). This proves the first estimate in (Q).

The published Bloom--Sisask exposition of the Kelley--Meka theorem
gives r_3([n])/n<=C exp(-c(log n)^(1/12)). Taking its sixth root changes
only the constant c, proving the final estimate in (Q). Using a stronger
integer Roth estimate in the first part of (Q) is also legitimate; the
displayed exponent is a conservative published input, not a claim of
the currently best numerical Roth exponent.
