INTERVAL ROW SUPPORTS: A POWER SAVING WITHOUT NESTEDNESS
15 October 2026. Complete deduction and independent audit.

Fix any linear ordering of the p elements of F_p. It need not be the
usual integer ordering and need not have any relation to field operations.
Let beta be a polynomial of fixed degree 2<=D<p. For each a in A subset
F_p*, choose a support D_a that is a union of at most r intervals in
this common ordering, with r fixed. Select edges

                        x -> ax+beta(a), x in D_a.

If this forms a simple proper B coloring (every C4 rainbow), then

                           m=O_{D,r}(p^(35/18)).             (1)

Thus arbitrary intervals, rather than just a nested family of supports,
still force vanishing density. This is a restricted support-complexity
theorem, not a general bounded-VC-dimension theorem. No Lean certificate
or historical-priority claim is made.

PROOF

Choose an integer w between 1 and p. Partition the ordered rows into
consecutive full blocks of size w and one final block of size less than
w. Discard every selected edge in the final block. In each full block
and each color, retain that color only if the entire block lies in its
support.

Each interval meets at most two full blocks partially. Thus for each
color this deletes at most (2r+1)w incidences, including the final block.
There are d<=p colors, so the total deletion is at most (2r+1)wp.

In a full row block B_j let d_j be the number of colors whose whole
support contains B_j. The retained subgraph has precisely d_j*w edges
and common row support B_j. For d_j>0 the polynomial common-support
spectral theorem gives

 d_j*w <=C_D[p^2/d_j+p^(9/4)d_j^(-1/2)
                               +p^(17/8)d_j^(-1/4)].        (2)

At least one of these three summands is at least a third of the left
side after incorporating C_D. Solving the resulting three inequalities
and summing their upper bounds gives

 d_j<=C_D[p w^(-1/2)+p^(3/2)w^(-2/3)
                                +p^(17/10)w^(-4/5)].        (3)

Blocks with d_j=0 need no estimate. There are at most p/w full blocks.
Multiplying (3) by w and summing proves

 m <=(2r+1)wp+C_D[p^2 w^(-1/2)+p^(5/2)w^(-2/3)
                                      +p^(27/10)w^(-4/5)]. (4)

Take w=ceil(p^(17/18)). This is at most p. The exponents of the three
bracketed terms are respectively 55/36, 101/54, and 35/18. The deletion
term also has exponent 35/18, proving (1), including rounding.

ARBITRARY INTERCEPTS: QUALITATIVE VARIANT

If beta:A->F_p is arbitrary, retain the same interval-support assumption
with fixed r. Then m=o(p^2) still follows from the arbitrary-intercept
common-support theorem. For any fixed epsilon>0 take w=ceil(epsilon p).
The deletion term is at most (2r+1)(epsilon+o(1))p^2. There are at most
1/epsilon full blocks, each a common-support B graph and hence containing
o(p^2) edges uniformly over the choices of its palette and intercepts.
Taking p->infinity first and then epsilon->0 proves m=o(p^2).

EXACT SCOPE AND CHECKS

The subgraphs after deletion are genuine subgraphs; properness and B
are inherited. Different color intervals may cross or overlap freely.
The proof does not approximate support sets by an arbitrary metric cover:
the interval boundary count directly controls the number of discarded
incidences, without a circular dependence on a covering number.

The common-support input is proved in
pass4-polynomial-intercept-common-support-theorem.txt, and the qualitative
arbitrary-intercept version in
pass4-arbitrary-intercept-common-support-theorem.txt.

The block construction, solving (2), all exponents, and the extension to
r intervals were independently checked by the finite-audit agent.

SEPARATE AUDIT: NESTED SUPPORTS

The proof in pass4-nested-support-theorem.txt also passes independently.
There, ordering colors by inclusion and replacing each block of w colors
by its minimum domain loses at most wp by telescoping, plus wp for the
last short color block. The bound

 2wp+C_D[p^3 w^(-2)+p^(13/4)w^(-3/2)+p^(25/8)w^(-5/4)]

at w=ceil(p^(17/18)) has exponents 35/18,10/9,11/6,35/18, as stated.
Neither proof asserts that arbitrary bounded-VC support families admit
the needed telescoping or interval-boundary decomposition.
