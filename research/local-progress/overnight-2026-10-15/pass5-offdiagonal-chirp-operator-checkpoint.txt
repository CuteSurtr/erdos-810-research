PASS 5: OFF-DIAGONAL CHIRP OPERATOR — EXACT CHECKPOINT
Date: 2026-10-15
Status: exact identities only; the requested norm saving remains UNPROVED.

1. Setup and unresolved target

Let p be an odd prime, A a subset of F_p^*, and s in F_p^*.
Write e(t)=exp(2*pi*i*t/p), and equip functions on F_p^2 with the
counting-measure Hilbert norm. Define

  sigma_a(x)=a*x+a^2,
  u_a(x)=a^2*x+a^3=a*sigma_a(x),
  tau_a(x,y)=(y/a-a, a*x+a^2),
  U_tau f=f composed with tau,
  R_a=M_{e(s*u_a(x))} U_{tau_a},
  R_s=sum_{a in A} R_a.

Each tau_a is an involution, and each R_a is unitary. The elementary
upper bound is ||R_s||<=|A|. This probe did not prove a uniform o(p)
bound for ||R_s||, and did not find an Omega(p) lower-bound example.
In particular, none of the identities below supplies either conclusion.

2. Chirp conjugation

For Q(x,y)=x*y, direct substitution gives

  Q(tau_a(x,y))=x*y+a*y-a^2*x-a^3.

Consequently

  R_a M_{e(s*Q)}=M_{e(s*Q)} M_{e(s*a*y)} U_{tau_a}.

Thus R_s is unitarily conjugate to

  sum_{a in A} M_{e(s*a*y)} U_{tau_a}.

This is an exact alternative form, not a norm estimate.

3. Fourier action and failure of the old block decomposition

On the character e(xi*x+eta*y), R_a produces the character with
frequency

  (xi,eta) -> (a*eta+s*a^2, xi/a)

and scalar phase e(a^2*eta-a*xi+s*a^3).
The new coordinate product is xi*eta+s*a*xi. Therefore xi*eta is not
preserved when s!=0. The product-frequency blocks used for the completed
unweighted crossed operator cannot simply be reused for this operator.

4. Exact pair-product identity

Set F_ab=tau_b composed with tau_a. Then

  F_ab(x,y)=((a/b)*x+(a^2-b^2)/b,
             (b/a)*y+b*(b-a)).

Since the adjoint of R_b is U_{tau_b} M_{e(-s*u_b(x))},

  R_a R_b^* f(x,y)
    = e(s*a*(a-b)*(x+a)) f(F_ab(x,y)).                 (1)

Indeed the phase difference is

  u_a(x)-u_b(F_ab(x,y)_x)=a*(a-b)*(x+a).

This calculation fixes the pullback convention explicitly: U_tau_a
U_tau_b is pullback by tau_b composed with tau_a.

5. A one-dimensional compression

Equation (1) shows that R_s R_s^* preserves the subspace of functions
independent of y. Identify that subspace isometrically with functions
on F_p by f(x)->p^(-1/2)*f(x). Define

  C_s f(x)=sum_{a in A} e(s*a*(a*x+a^2)) f(a*x+a^2).

Writing W_a for its a-th summand, W_a W_b^* has exactly the x-pullback
and phase appearing in (1). Hence the restriction of R_s R_s^* to this
subspace is C_s C_s^*. In particular,

  ||R_s|| >= ||C_s||.                                 (2)

IMPORTANT DIRECTION: (2) is a LOWER bound on ||R_s||. An upper bound
for ||C_s|| alone would not give an upper bound for ||R_s||.

In the orthonormal character basis on F_p, the matrix of C_s is

  C_hat_s(xi,t)
    = sum_{a in A: xi=a*(t+s*a)} e(a*xi).              (3)

For xi=0, the admissible input frequencies are t=-s*a, one for each
a in A, and every corresponding entry is 1. Therefore

  ||C_s|| >= sqrt(|A|),

which only yields a square-root lower bound for R_s, not a linear one.

6. Precise stopping point

The remaining issue is an oscillatory affine-operator estimate with
color-dependent modulation. The old unweighted Kloosterman block proof
does not apply because the invariant frequency product is lost.
Even a saving for the one-dimensional operator (3) has not been
established here; furthermore, it would still leave the other blocks
of R_s R_s^* to control.

No finite graph enumeration, numerical norm search, or symbolic
calculation was used to establish the identities in this note. They
follow by the displayed substitutions. No implication for arbitrary
actual supports or for the full Erdos problem is claimed.
