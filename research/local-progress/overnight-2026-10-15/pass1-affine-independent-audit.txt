1. FIXED DEFECT PER AFFINE COLOR IMPLIES VANISHING DENSITY

Statement. Fix an integer r>=0. Let the two sides of a B-colored graph
be copies of a finite field F of size n. Every color is a restriction
of a nonconstant affine permutation x -> a*x+b, a!=0, and retains at
least n-r of its n points. Then the number of edges is o(n^2) as n
tends to infinity, uniformly over the field and the coloring.

This is a conditional representation-class theorem. It does not cover
classes retaining only eta*n points for a fixed eta<1.

1a. Distinctness and elementary size issues.

For n>2r two different colors cannot have the same affine line: their
actual edges would be disjoint subsets of an n-point line, each of
size at least n-r>n/2. Hence the affine completions are distinct for
all sufficiently large n. Also d(n-r)<=n^2, so d=O(n). If fewer than
k=r+1 color lines exist, the edge count is at most r*n=o(n^2).

1b. A bounded pencil cover.

Choose k=r+1 distinct base color lines. Cover all color lines belonging
to any of these families:

  - the k parallel pencils with directions of the base lines;
  - pencils through each omitted point of any base line, at most kr.

Repeated pencils may be counted several times; this only enlarges the
bound. The number of pencils is at most

  K_r=k+kr=(r+1)^2.                                    (1.1)

Suppose a color line L were uncovered. It is not parallel to any base
line, so it meets every base line in one point. These k intersections
are all retained by the corresponding base colors, because otherwise
L would pass through an omitted base-line point. They are therefore
distinct: a coincidence would be an edge retained by two base colors,
contrary to the unique assignment of an edge color.
As an edge cannot carry two colors, L must omit all these k points.
Distinct points on a nonvertical affine graph have distinct row
coordinates, so these are k=r+1 separate omissions. Contradiction.

There is no need to add pencils through base/base intersections:
every such point must be omitted by at least one of its base colors,
so its pencil is already among those counted. This proves the cover.
The argument uses the actual supports and
proper assignment of a unique color to each edge; no completed point
is silently treated as an actual edge.

1c. Why the earlier small-difference theorem applies.

A parallel pencil with common slope a has maps T_b M_a, where T is
translation and M is dilation. It is a coset of the translation group;
all quotients lie in a group of size n.

A pencil through (s,t) consists of maps

  T_t M_a T_(-s) = T_(t-s) (T_s M_a T_(-s)).

The parenthesized maps form a conjugate of the multiplicative dilation
group, of size n-1. Thus this pencil is a left coset of that subgroup,
and its difference set has size at most n-1. This checks the coset
orientation explicitly.

If the full graph had at least delta*n^2 edges along a subsequence,
one of the boundedly many pencils would carry at least
delta*n^2/K_r edges. Its palette size is Theta(n), its action size is
n, and its difference set has size at most n. The previously audited
partial-small-difference theorem therefore says its edge count is
o(n^2), a contradiction. Alternatively normalize a parallel pencil
to a partial additive group table, or a concurrent pencil (after
discarding the exceptional zero row/column) to a partial multiplicative
group table, and use the corresponding known group-table theorem.

Conclusion: the proposed fixed-defect affine theorem is valid. The
boundedness of r is essential to THIS proof: when r grows, the number
of pencils grows quadratically, and no uniform rate has been supplied
which would justify summing their o(n^2) bounds.

2. DENSE HEAVY AFFINE COLORS WITH MINIMUM ENERGY NEED NOT BE B

Let p>=7 be an odd prime, d=(p-1)/2, and

  A={1,2,...,d} subset F_p,
  sigma_a(x)=a*x+a^2,   a in A.                         (2.1)

The set A contains exactly one of each pair {a,-a} of nonzero elements.

2a. Strong three-term permutation relations are absent.

The equation sigma_c=sigma_a sigma_b^(-1) sigma_a requires
c=a^2/b from the slope. The intercept equation then becomes

  a*(a-b)^2*(a+b)=0.

Since a,b are nonzero and A contains no opposite pair, it follows that
a=b=c. Thus this palette satisfies the strong translational
three-term-progression avoidance condition previously found necessary
for perfect B palettes. In the partial setting this algebraic
condition is far from sufficient.

2b. Global multiplicative energy is exactly minimal.

For a!=b the quotient sigma_a sigma_b^(-1) has slope and intercept

  u=a/b !=1,       v=a^2(1-1/u).                        (2.2)

The pair (u,v) determines a^2. The only two possible nonzero roots
are a and -a, and A contains at most one, so it determines a and then
b uniquely. Every nonidentity ordered quotient is therefore distinct.
The identity has d representations, and all other d(d-1) quotients
have one representation, giving

  E(S)=d^2+d(d-1)=2d^2-d.                              (2.3)

2c. The actual supports can all be heavy.

Lines a and b intersect at

  x=-(a+b),       y=-ab.                               (2.4)

No point lies on three such lines: its incident line parameters are
roots of z^2+x*z-y=0. In particular, on each line the d-1 intersection
points with other palette lines are distinct.

Retain every point belonging to just one palette line. At each double
point choose one of its two lines as its color. Orient a balanced
tournament on the d line labels and use its edge orientation for this
choice. Such a tournament exists explicitly: for odd d use cyclic
orientation; for even d delete one vertex from a regular tournament
on d+1 vertices. Every label receives at least floor((d-1)/2) of its
double points. Consequently every color has at least

  p-(d-1)+floor((d-1)/2)=p-ceil((d-1)/2)               (2.5)

actual edges, asymptotic to 3p/4. Its color is a matching because each
line has nonzero slope. A point is assigned only once, so this gives
a proper coloring of a simple bipartite graph. The total edge count is

  m=d*p-binom(d,2)=(3/8+o(1))*p^2.                     (2.6)

2d. A nonrainbow rectangle is forced independently of the choices.

At rows -1,+1 and columns 2,6 the four edge colors are

      2  3
      1  2.

Each point belongs to exactly one palette line. The two roots for
the respective color equations are {2,-1}, {3,-2}, {1,-2}, {2,-3};
only the first listed root lies in A. Thus these four points are all
retained, regardless of how double points were assigned. The rows
and columns are distinct for p>=7, giving a genuine nonrainbow C4.

Conclusion: this is an explicit asymptotic proper dense family with
heavy partial affine classes, the smallest possible global energy,
no nontrivial strong permutation progression, and at most one fixed
point for every nonidentity affine word. It is NOT B. It disproves
any proposed implication to vanishing density which uses only those
listed weaker hypotheses. It does not disprove a route that uses the
actual B support restrictions.

3. CONNECTION WITH THE FIXED-POINT KERNEL AUDIT

See pass1-fixed-points-and-coherent-kernel.txt for the completion-
independent statistic R=sum <B_a^T B_b,B_c^T B_e>^2. In a heavy B
family represented by an action with o(n) fixed points per nonidentity
word, the earlier global-energy theorem forces R=o(n^5). The dense
non-B construction above also has only O(n^4) such repeated patterns,
by the same elementary inequality with f=1 and (2.3).

Therefore the n^5 concentration required by the current Gowers--Long
inverse theorem cannot be recovered from heaviness, affine algebra,
and global energy alone. Any useful concentration argument must use
the B constraints on the simultaneously defined partial maps.
