A DENSE PROPER AFFINE EXAMPLE WITH MINIMAL GLOBAL ENERGY
Overnight pass 1, 15 October 2026.
Exact algebraic construction; no finite search. This example is NOT B.
No claim of historical novelty; not Lean checked.

PURPOSE

The previous theorem says that a dense B coloring with heavy classes
must have very small normalized global multiplicative energy for every
choice of distinct permutation completions. Could that condition, or
absence of global three-term permutation relations, rule out a dense
affine example? The following family shows that neither condition alone
does so, even with fixed affine completions and supports about 3n/4.

1. PALETTE AND GEOMETRY

Let p>=7 be an odd prime and set

        A={1,2,...,(p-1)/2} subset F_p,      d=|A|=(p-1)/2.

Thus A contains exactly one representative from each pair {a,-a} of
nonzero field elements. Define affine permutations

                  sigma_a(x)=a x+a^2,   a in A.

Two different graph lines a,b meet at exactly

                       (x,y)=(-(a+b),-ab).             (1)

There is no triple intersection: for any point (x,y), its incident
palette parameters solve t^2+x t-y=0, which has at most two roots.
In particular the d-1 intersection points on each line are distinct.

Choose a tournament on the d colors with each outdegree at most
ceil((d-1)/2). Such a balanced tournament exists: for odd d use the cyclic
regular tournament; for even d, delete one vertex from a cyclic regular
tournament on d+1 vertices.

At each double intersection, retain the point in exactly one of its two
colors, making the loser the tail of the corresponding tournament arc.
Retain every other point of every palette line. The selected points are
then given unique colors, so this is a proper bipartite coloring. Each
color is a partial matching because its affine map is a permutation.
The size of every color class is at least

                      p-ceil((d-1)/2)=(3/4+o(1))p,

and the exact number of edges is

                      m=dp-binomial(d,2)
                       =(3/8+o(1))p^2.                (2)

No choices at different intersections conflict: all those points on a
given line are distinct, and no point is incident with three lines.

2. NO NONTRIVIAL STRONG THREE-TERM RELATIONS

Suppose a,b,c are palette labels and

                    sigma_c=sigma_a sigma_b^(-1) sigma_a.             (3)

The slope identity gives c=a^2/b. Equating intercepts gives

                    c^2=a^2+a^3/b-ab.

After substituting c and multiplying by b^2/a (which is nonzero), this
is equivalent to

                    (a+b)(a-b)^2=0.                 (4)

Hence a=b or a=-b. The second possibility is excluded by A. The first
forces c=a=b and is the trivial relation. This includes exclusion of
the order-two degeneracy c=b with a!=b; it is the strong, rather than
merely three-distinct-elements, progression condition.

3. EXACTLY MINIMAL MULTIPLICATIVE ENERGY

For ordered palette labels a,b, the relative permutation is

       sigma_a sigma_b^(-1)(x)
           = (a/b)x + a^2-ab
           = r x + a^2(1-1/r),   r=a/b.               (5)

For a!=b, r!=1. Knowledge of the relative affine map determines r and
a^2. Since A contains no antipodal pair, it determines a uniquely, and
then b=a/r uniquely. Thus all d(d-1) nonidentity ordered differences
are distinct, and none is the identity. The identity has d ordered
representations, namely a=b. Therefore

            |S S^(-1)|=d(d-1)+1,
            E(S)=d^2+d(d-1)=2d^2-d.                  (6)

The last value is the minimum possible energy for d distinct group
elements: the identity necessarily contributes d^2 and the remaining
d(d-1) representations contribute at least one apiece. Consequently
E(S)/d^3->0 in this dense properly colored affine family, without any
unused-point padding or arbitrary completion freedom.

4. AN EXPLICIT NONRAINBOW C4, RETAINED FOR EVERY TOURNAMENT CHOICE

The four graph positions on rows -1,1 and columns 2,6 are

                      column 2       column 6
          row -1       color 2        color 3
          row  1       color 1        color 2.

Indeed sigma_2(-1)=2, sigma_3(-1)=6,
sigma_1(1)=2, and sigma_2(1)=6. These are distinct rows and columns for
p>=7, and 1,2,3 all belong to A.

All four positions are unique-line points of the palette. Their other
possible quadratic roots are respectively -1,-2,-2,-3, none of which
belongs to A. Thus no tournament choice ever deletes these four edges.
The color-2 diagonal repeats, so every member of the constructed family
violates the B condition. This is deliberately an obstruction to an
insufficient proof strategy, not a candidate counterexample to #810.

More generally, whenever t!=0 and a-t,a,a+t all belong to an antipodal-free
palette of these quadratic lines, the rows -t,t produce a rectangle
whose diagonal color is a and whose cross colors are a+t,a-t. Its four
positions are unique-line points within that palette. The relation is
an ADDITIVE progression of slope parameters, not the global permutation
progression (3). This makes the missing local information explicit.

5. LIMITATION OF A BOUNDED PENCIL COVER

The slopes in this palette are distinct, so a parallel pencil contains
at most one line. No three lines are concurrent, so a concurrent pencil
contains at most two. Covering the d lines therefore takes at least
ceil(d/2) pencils. The constant-support regime is not automatically
covered by the bounded-pencil argument in pass1-affine-bounded-deficit.txt.

The example does not rule out a method combining the full B condition
with affine geometry. It isolates the exact issue such a method must
use: local agreements of distinct global relative permutations and the
actual supports of the colors.
