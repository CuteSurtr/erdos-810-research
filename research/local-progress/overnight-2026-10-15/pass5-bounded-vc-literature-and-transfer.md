# Pass 5: bounded-VC support transfer and primary-source audit

15 October 2026. This note records a qualitative deduction from established
graphon and bracketing results, combined with the independently audited Pass 4
near-common-support theorem. Historical priority of this combined deduction
has not been established. It is an analytic proof, not a Lean certificate.

## Result and exact scope

Let \(p\) run through odd primes. Let \(A_p\subseteq\mathbb F_p^*\), with one
color for each slope, and let

\[
\sigma_a(x)=ax+\beta_p(a),\qquad x\in D_a\subseteq\mathbb F_p.
\]

The intercept function is arbitrary. Assume that the actual colored graph is
simple and proper and every four-cycle is rainbow. If the family of actual
row supports \(\{D_a:a\in A_p\}\) has uniformly bounded VC dimension, then

\[
\sum_{a\in A_p}|D_a|=o(p^2).
\]

More generally the same conclusion holds if the color–row incidence
bigraphs omit any fixed induced bipartite pattern, or differ in \(o(p^2)\)
entries from a family with that property. No polynomial intercept assumption,
common row ordering, or nesting is needed. This is a qualitative result; the
argument does not supply an explicit rate.

The Pass 4 input used below is uniform over all palettes, intercept functions,
and row sets: if \(D_a\subseteq X\) and

\[
R=\sum_{a\in A}|X\setminus D_a|=o(p^2),
\]

then \(\sum_a|D_a|=o(p^2)\). Its proof uses Solymosi's group-table theorem,
the published PRRW affine-energy theorem, and the crossed-reflection estimate.
See `pass4-arbitrary-intercept-common-support-theorem.txt` and
`pass4-arbitrary-affine-common-support-independent-audit.txt`.

## Primary inputs checked

   [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.4377);
   [published-book repository record](https://real.mtak.hu/111518/).
   Theorem 3.1 (PDF p6) supplies a pure bigraphon representative.
   Theorem 4.1 (PDF p12) says that a thin pure bigraphon is binary almost
   everywhere and has compact row and column neighborhood metric spaces.
   Here thin means that some fixed induced bipartite pattern has density zero.
   Lemma 4.7 (PDF p14) gives finite dual essential VC dimension of its row
   family. Proposition 4.11 and Corollary 4.12 (PDF p17) apply this directly to
   limits of nontrivial hereditary bigraph classes. These are statements about
   **bipartite induced patterns**, not ordinary induced graphs.

2. **Adams–Nobel, _Uniform approximation of Vapnik–Chervonenkis classes_,
   Bernoulli 18(4) (2012), 1310–1319, DOI 10.3150/11-BEJ379.**
   [Full primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4515);
   [author-hosted published PDF](https://nobel.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13591/2017/01/BEJ379.pdf).
   Corollary 1 (preprint PDF p5) gives finitely many \(\varepsilon\)-brackets
   for a separable VC class under a fixed probability measure. A countable
   class satisfies its separability hypothesis. Brackets have actual lower
   and upper measurable sets with width at most \(\varepsilon\).
   Lemma A (PDF p3) states finite primal VC dimension iff finite dual VC
   dimension. The earlier preprint 1007.4037 has the same needed bracketing
   conclusion; the later title and identifier above match the publication.
   The published PDF's metadata and opening statement were returned by
   search; its direct browser fetch failed. The full theorem and proof were
   read in the later primary preprint.

3. **Standard dense-bigraph limit compactness and induced-density
   continuity.** We use subsequential convergence in bipartite cut distance,
   with independent measure-preserving relabelings on the two parts. The
   Lovász–Szegedy text uses this limit framework and defines the bipartite
   induced densities in Section 2 (PDF pp4–5). The elementary upgrade from
   cut convergence to \(L^1\) convergence at a binary limit is proved below,

The new work in this note is the transfer argument connecting these inputs
to the existing near-common affine result. No new graphon, bracketing,
incidence, or exponential-sum theorem is claimed.

## Lemma: a positive thin bigraphon contains a positive complete rectangle

Let \(W:J_1\times J_2\to[0,1]\) be a thin pure bigraphon of positive edge
measure \(\delta=\int W>0\). We prove there are measurable \(U\subseteq J_1\)
and \(V\subseteq J_2\), both of positive measure, such that \(W=1\) almost
everywhere on \(U\times V\).

By Lovász–Szegedy, \(W\) is binary almost everywhere, its row neighborhood
metric is compact, and its row family has finite dual essential VC dimension.
Choose a countable family of rows \(x_1,x_2,\ldots\) dense in that metric.
Write \(C_i=\{y:W(x_i,y)=1\}\), choosing binary representatives.

There are countably many Boolean atoms generated by finite subsets of
the \(C_i\). Remove the union of all such atoms having measure zero. This
removes a null set only. On the remaining column space every nonempty atom
of every finite subfamily has positive measure. Consequently the finite
dual essential VC bound becomes an ordinary finite dual VC bound for this
countable family. Finite primal VC dimension follows by duality. This step
is necessary: a claim about essential VC dimension must not silently be
replaced by a pointwise VC claim for an uncountable family.

Apply Adams–Nobel with bracket width \(\varepsilon<\delta/4\). Obtain finitely
many measurable pairs \(L_i\subseteq H_i\) with
\(\mu_2(H_i\setminus L_i)\le\varepsilon\), covering all the \(C_j\).
In the \(L^1\) space of binary functions, the condition

\[
1_{L_i}\le f\le1_{H_i}\quad\text{almost everywhere}
\]

defines a closed subset: it is equivalent to the vanishing of the continuous
nonnegative functional

\[
\int_{L_i}(1-f)+\int_{J_2\setminus H_i}f.
\]

The finite union of these closed brackets therefore covers the closure of
the countable dense row family, hence all relevant rows. This use of
closure requires only almost-everywhere bracket containment, which is
exactly what the rectangle argument needs.

The set of rows having measure at least \(\delta/2\) has positive measure.
At least one bracket contains a positive-measure set \(U\) of those rows.
Measurability follows from the displayed continuous functional composed
with the measurable row map. For that bracket,

\[
\mu_2(L_i)\ge\delta/2-\varepsilon>\delta/4.
\]

Take \(V=L_i\). Almost-everywhere containment of \(V\) in every row in \(U\),
followed by Fubini, proves the rectangle assertion. The finite number of
brackets depends on this one limit object and measure. No bound uniform in
the original finite graphs is used.

## Lemma: cut convergence to a binary limit gives \(L^1\) convergence

Suppose \(0\le W_j\le1\), \(W\in\{0,1\}\) almost everywhere, and
\(\|W_j-W\|_\square\to0\) after relabeling. Approximate \(W\) in \(L^1\) by
a finite linear combination \(Q\) of rectangle indicators. Then

\[
\int (W_j-W)Q\to0,
\qquad
\left|\int (W_j-W)(W-Q)\right|\le\|W-Q\|_1.
\]

Thus \(\int W_jW\to\int W^2=\int W\). Also \(\int W_j\to\int W\).
The identity

\[
\|W_j-W\|_1=\int W_j+\int W-2\int W_jW
\]

now proves the claim. This is the particular stability property that fails
at fractional limits.

## Finite extraction, including fractional vertex cells

Suppose the desired sparsity conclusion fails along primes \(p_j\to\infty\),
so \(m_j\ge\delta p_j^2\) for some fixed \(\delta>0\). Put \(d_j=|A_{p_j}|\).
Then \(\delta p_j\le d_j\le p_j\). Represent the color–row incidence matrices
as bigraphons \(W_j\), normalized separately on their \(d_j\) colors and
\(p_j\) rows. Their edge measures are at least \(\delta\).

Pass to a cut-convergent subsequence. If the support class omits a fixed
induced bipartite graph, induced-density continuity makes its limit \(W\)
thin. For bounded VC supports, use the fixed shattering bigraph: one side
has \(v+1\) row positions, the other has all their subset patterns. That
pattern cannot occur when VC dimension is at most \(v\). Its distinct
neighborhoods ensure that a noninjective map cannot create a spurious
induced occurrence. The same limiting conclusion holds with \(o(p_j^2)\)
edits, since such edits are \(o(1)\) in the normalized matrix \(L^1\) norm.

The preceding lemmas give \(W_j\to W\) in \(L^1\), and sets \(U,V\) of
positive measures \(\alpha,\gamma\), with \(W=1\) almost everywhere on their
product. Therefore

\[
\int_{U\times V}(1-W_j)=o(1).
\]

The relabelings may cut individual finite-vertex cells; it would be wrong
to identify \(U,V\) immediately with subsets of finite vertices. For a
color cell \(I_a\), and row cell \(J_x\), define

\[
u_a=d_j\mu_1(U\cap I_a),\qquad
v_x=p_j\mu_2(V\cap J_x).
\]

Then \(0\le u_a,v_x\le1\),
\(\sum_a u_a=\alpha d_j\), \(\sum_xv_x=\gamma p_j\), and

\[
\sum_{a,x}u_av_x(1-W_j(a,x))=o(d_jp_j).
\]

Select the actual vertices

\[
A_j'=\{a:u_a\ge\alpha/2\},\qquad
X_j'=\{x:v_x\ge\gamma/2\}.
\]

Their sizes are at least \(\alpha d_j/2\) and \(\gamma p_j/2\), respectively.
Every missing incidence in their product contributes at least
\(\alpha\gamma/4\) to the weighted deficit. Hence the total omissions in
\(A_j'\times X_j'\) are \(o(d_jp_j)=o(p_j^2)\).

Retain only these colors and these rows. The actual graph is still proper
and B-colored, its affine maps retain their original field labels, and it
has at least \(c p_j^2-o(p_j^2)\) actual edges for a fixed \(c>0\). It is
now a near-common-support graph, contradicting the Pass 4 theorem.
Independent relabelings were used only to locate subsets, so no arithmetic
map or field-coordinate structure has been altered.

This proves the stated result.

## Stronger necessary condition: every fixed induced support pattern

The same argument gives a useful formulation without assuming bounded VC.
Fix \(\delta>0\) and a finite bipartite graph \(F\), with part sizes \(s,t\).
There are constants \(c=c(\delta,F)>0\) and \(p_0\) such that any graph in the
stated affine class with \(p\ge p_0\) and \(m\ge\delta p^2\) must have at
least

\[
c|A|^s p^t
\]

labeled induced copies of \(F\) in its color–row support bigraph.
Otherwise take a sequence with normalized \(F\)-density tending to zero;
its positive-density limit is thin, and the same contradiction applies.
If \(F\) has twins, the usual normalized homomorphism density and the
injective count differ by \(o(1)\), since both side sizes tend to infinity.

This says that a hypothetical dense affine counterexample must have
complicated **actual supports**, in the strong induced-pattern sense.
It does not assert that such dense examples exist, nor resolve arbitrary
affine supports or unrestricted Erdős #810.

## Why the direct ultra-strong-regularity argument was insufficient

Fox–Pach–Suk [Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.03745) gives
\(K\le c\varepsilon^{-(2d+1)}\) parts with most pairs of density near zero
or one. Applying the polynomial robust estimate separately to near-full
pairs can produce a summed error on the scale \(p^2\sqrt{K\varepsilon}\),
or a similar unfavorable dependence. The displayed bound on \(K\) alone
does not force that error to vanish. The present proof does not make the
invalid exchange of quantifiers needed by that naive argument: it takes
one limit, brackets there, and only then returns to the finite graphs.

An intermediate route also works for uniformly stable support bigraphs.
Chernikov–Towsner [Theorem B/4.15 and Definition 1.2(2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07870)
give strong stable regularity with any prescribed error function \(f\(K\)\)
of the eventual number of parts. The uniform near-common theorem gives a
finitary error modulus by contradiction; choosing \(f\(K\)\) beneath that
modulus permits a direct finite proof. Stability excludes a fixed induced
half-graph and is stronger than bounded VC. The graphon–bracketing route
above removes that additional restriction.

### Narrow prior-result comparison

A targeted search for the affine rainbow-four-cycle problem together with
VC dimension, actual supports, and induced support patterns did not produce
an exact primary-source match. That is search evidence only, not a proof of
priority. The precise differences from the inspected primary results are:

- [Solymosi, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133) addresses dense
  partial finite-group tables. It is an input to the near-common affine
  theorem through a pencil, and does not by itself cover an arbitrary
  intercept function in the affine group of order \(p(p-1)\).
- [PRRW, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03401) bounds affine
  coefficient-set energy. It supplies expansion in the Pass 4 proof,
  while the control of actual color-dependent supports is the separate
  issue treated here.
- Lovász–Szegedy and Adams–Nobel provide the limit structure and bracketing
  machinery used above. Their inspected statements do not address the
  rainbow-four-cycle affine coloring problem. The positive-rectangle
  extraction is an application of established methods, not a proposed
  new VC regularity theorem.

The new consequence recorded locally is therefore best described as an
audited application of those published inputs and the Pass 4 affine
near-common theorem. Its exact historical occurrence remains unverified.
The previously identified unavailable 2026 perfect-B-coloring paper remains
part of the broader novelty gap; no exhausted access route was retried.
