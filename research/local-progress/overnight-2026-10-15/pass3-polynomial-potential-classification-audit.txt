LOW-DEGREE SYMMETRIC CROSSED POTENTIALS: AN ELEMENTARY CLASSIFICATION
15 October 2026. Independent hand audit of the parent's candidate.
This is an exact algebraic lemma, not a density or removal theorem.

1. STATEMENT

Let k be a field of characteristic p>2. Let G(s,t),H(a,b) be
polynomials over k, with total degree of G at most p. Suppose the
following is a FORMAL polynomial identity in independent a,b,c:

 G(a+b,ac)-G(a+c,ab) = H(a,c)-H(a,b).                    (1)

Then there are univariate polynomials g,h,q and a scalar kappa such
that

 G(s,t) = g(s)+h(t)+kappa*s*t,
 H(a,b) = -g(a+b)+h(ab)+kappa*a^2*b+q(a).                (2)

Conversely, every pair of the form (2) satisfies (1).
The originally proposed assumption deg G<p/2 is therefore sufficient;
the proof actually permits deg G<=p. In characteristic zero the same
conclusion holds with no degree restriction.

The premise is a formal identity. This note does not infer it from
an identity only on F_p-valued points without sufficient separate
degree bounds. It also does not classify all four unrelated slot
potentials or nonpolynomial phases.

2. MIXED DERIVATIVE

Differentiate (1) once in b and once in c. Writing F=G_st gives

        a F(a+b,ac) - a F(a+c,ab) = 0.

Since the polynomial ring is an integral domain, cancel a:

              F(a+b,ac)=F(a+c,ab).                     (3)

3. A SHORT POLYNOMIAL ARGUMENT FOR CONSTANCY

Set c=0 in (3), and put f(x)=F(x,0). Then

                       F(a,ab)=f(a+b).                 (4)

If f is zero, comparison of coefficients in b in (4) gives F=0.
Otherwise let D=deg f, and write F(s,t)=sum_j F_j(s)t^j.
For every j>D, comparison of b^j gives a^j F_j(a)=0,
so F_j=0. Comparison of b^D gives

                  a^D F_D(a)=leading_coefficient(f).

If D>0 this is impossible: its left side is divisible by a and
its right side is a nonzero constant. Therefore D=0, all F_j with
j>0 vanish, and (4) forces F_0 to be constant as well. Thus

                            G_st=kappa.                (5)

This step works in every characteristic and needs no degree bound.
It avoids any claim about which affine transformations are generated
by the involutions (s,t) -> (a+t/a,as-a^2).

4. INTEGRATION AND THE PRECISE CHARACTERISTIC CONDITION

Write G=sum c_ij s^i t^j. For a mixed monomial with i,j>=1 and
i+j<=p, both i and j are nonzero in k. Therefore (5) forces every
mixed coefficient to vanish except possibly c_11=kappa. All pure
s or pure t monomials are absorbed into g and h. This proves the
first equation in (2). The same argument works for arbitrary degrees
in characteristic zero.

Substitution yields

 G(a+b,ac)-G(a+c,ab)
 = g(a+b)-g(a+c)+h(ac)-h(ab)+kappa*a^2*(c-b).

Let H_0(a,b)=-g(a+b)+h(ab)+kappa*a^2*b. Then (1) says

       [H-H_0](a,c)=[H-H_0](a,b).

Setting c=0 shows H-H_0=q(a), proving the second equation in (2).
Direct substitution establishes the converse.

Above the stated characteristic range, vanishing mixed derivatives
alone do not justify integrating into g(s)+h(t)+kappa st, because
mixed monomials with an exponent divisible by p can have zero mixed
derivative. No unrestricted positive-characteristic classification
is asserted here.

5. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE FOUR-SLOT SYSTEM

On a solution of

 a+b=d+e,  ac=de,  a+c=f+g,  ab=fg,

the polynomial potential relation reads

 H(a,b)-H(a,c)+G(d+e,de)-G(f+g,fg)=0.                   (6)

For G(s,t)=st and H(a,b)=a^2*b this is the cubic identity

        a^2*b-a^2*c+de(d+e)-fg(f+g)=0.

Within the stated symmetric low-degree ansatz, every potential is
the sum of a row-only term g(s), a column-only term h(t), this cubic
term, and an irrelevant color-only term q(a) in H. Thus the cubic
term is the only extra polynomial contribution in this ansatz after
removing the evident row, column, and color terms.

This limited classification does not provide an inverse theorem,
quantitative control of arbitrary bounded functions, or a mechanism
to transfer a hypergraph deletion back to original support pairs.
