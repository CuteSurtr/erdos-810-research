AFFINE PALETTES WITH REPEATED SLOPES: AN EXACT MOMENT AND A SPARSITY CLASS
2026-10-15, overnight pass 5, finite_audit agent.

Status: the moment calculation and its sparsity consequence independently
passed formal_audit's audit. No finite graph searches were used. These are
restricted-class results, not a solution of unrestricted Erdős #810; priority
has not been established.

1. STATEMENT

Let p range over odd primes. Let S be a set of distinct affine maps

                    sigma_(a,b)(x)=a*x+b,

with a!=0 and d=|S|<=C*p for a fixed C. Several colors may share a slope.
Write B_a={b:(a,b) in S}, v_a=|B_a|, v=max_a v_a. Define the weighted
multiplicative energy of the slope counts by

 T(S)=sum_(a1*a3=a2*a4) v_a1*v_a2*v_a3*v_a4.              (1)

All slope indices in this sum are nonzero field elements. Let X,Y subset F_p.
Select some of the eligible colored incidences

             (x,a*x+b), (a,b) in S, x in X, a*x+b in Y,

so that the actual graph is simple, properly colored, and every C4 is rainbow.
Let R count the eligible colored incidences that were not selected.

THEOREM. If T(S)=o(p^(7/2)) and R=o(p^2), then m=o(p^2).

In particular the conclusion holds if v=o(sqrt(p)), since

                         T(S)<=v*d^3.                   (2)

The completely eligible case has R=0. Taking Y=F_p includes common row
domains. The statement permits arbitrary intercept sets B_a; they need not
come from polynomial graphs or share a shape.

2. CROSSED FOURIER BLOCKS WITH MULTIPLICITY

For each (a,b) in S define

 tau_(a,b)(x,y)=((y-b)/a, a*x+b),
 U_(a,b) f=f composed with tau_(a,b),
 Q=sum_(a,b in S) U_(a,b).

Q is selfadjoint, each tau is an involution, and Q1=d1. Fourier characters
transform by

 e_(xi,eta) composed with tau_(a,b)
       =e_p(b*(eta-xi/a)) e_(a*eta,xi/a).

As before, the product xi*eta is invariant. For t!=0 the (p-1)-dimensional
Hermitian block has entries

 M_t(r,s)=sum_(b in B_(rs/t)) e_p((r-s)*b/(rs/t)).         (3)

The coordinate-axis block has norm to the fourth power at most

                         (p-1) E_aff(S),                (4)

where E_aff counts equal affine right quotients. This follows from the
permutation character of Aff(F_p): p-1 at identity, -1 at nonidentity
translations, and 0 at other affine maps, on the zero-sum representation.

3. EXACT FOURTH-MOMENT CLASSIFICATION

Expand tr(M_t^4) with cyclic Fourier indices r,s,u,v. Put

 a=rs/t, q=u/r, h=v/s;
 s=t*a/r, u=q*r, v=h*t*a/r.

The four palette slopes are a,qa,qha,ha. Call their intercept choices
b1,b2,b3,b4 in that order. The complete sum over the free nonzero r has
phase alpha*r+gamma/r, with

 alpha=(h*b1-h*b2+b3-b4)/(h*a),
 gamma=(t/q)*(-q*b1+b2-b3+q*b4).                         (5)

The total number of choices (a,q,h,b1,b2,b3,b4) is exactly T(S). Choosing
three palette points fixes the fourth slope, with at most v intercepts,
which proves (2).

We now count precisely when both coefficients in (5) vanish.

(i) q=h=1. All four slopes equal a, and the sole equation is
b1+b3=b2+b4. The contribution is

                          sum_a E_+(B_a).               (6)

(ii) q=1,h!=1. The two equations force b1=b2 and b3=b4.
The count is d^2-sum_a v_a^2. The case h=1,q!=1 has the same count.

(iii) q!=1,h!=1. The coefficient rows in the four intercept variables are
[h,-h,1,-1] and [-q,1,-1,q]. They are independent, and their kernel is spanned
by the constant vector and (a,qa,qha,ha). Thus the four palette points are
collinear, including all cases of repeated slopes. The first two points
have distinct slopes, so they determine a nonvertical line. If L_nv is the
maximum palette occupancy of such a line, choosing the fourth point on it
gives at most L_nv choices; the third slope and its intercept are then fixed.
Therefore this count is at most L_nv*d^2.

Let D_0 be the full degenerate count. We have proved

 D_0 <= (L_nv+2)d^2 + sum_a E_+(B_a).                    (7)

Every nondegenerate coefficient pair in (5) gives a complete Kloosterman
sum of absolute value at most 2sqrt(p); when exactly one coefficient is
zero, the sum is exactly -1 and obeys the same bound. Hence

 tr(M_t^4) <= p[(L_nv+2)d^2+sum_a E_+(B_a)]
                                      +2sqrt(p)*T(S).   (8)

For the classical Kloosterman input see Harcos, Theorem 1, page 1:
https://users.renyi.hu/~gharcos/weil.pdf .

4. THE ADDITIVE ENERGY TERM IS ALREADY PART OF AFFINE ENERGY

Within a fixed slope a,

 sigma_(a,b1) sigma_(a,b2)^(-1)(y)=y+b1-b2.

For each translation amount t its quotient multiplicity is at least
sum_a r_(B_a-B_a)(t). Squaring and summing, the nonnegative cross terms give

                      sum_a E_+(B_a)<=E_aff(S).          (9)

Thus the same-slope degeneracies are not a new unbounded term beyond the
previous affine-energy obstruction.

5. HEAVY B PALETTES HAVE FEW POINTS ON ANY LINE

Suppose every original color has at least eta*p actual edges, with eta>0
fixed. If a palette line contained epsilon*p colors, its original edges
would contain Omega(p^2) edges of a partial group table:

* On a vertical palette line a=a0, relabel the row by r=a0*x and keep
  the column s=y. The color is the additive difference b=s-r in F_p.
* On a nonvertical line b=u*a+w, relabel r=x+u,s=y-w. After deleting
  the possible edge at r=0 (at most one actual edge by simplicity), the
  color is the multiplicative quotient a=s/r in F_p^*.

In either case Solymosi's group-table rectangle theorem produces a genuine
rectangle with repeated opposite colors, contrary to B. Consequently the
maximum occupancy L_all over ALL palette lines is o(p), uniformly for
fixed eta. This step uses actual edges and permits arbitrary partial domains.

Source: Solymosi, Theorem 5, printed page 4,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133 .

6. AFFINE ENERGY AND THE SLOPE-ENERGY HYPOTHESIS

PRRW Theorem 2.1 gives

 E_aff(S) << sqrt(v)*d^(5/2) + L_all*d^2,                 (10)

provided v*d<=p^2. The energy convention in that source equals the one here
by rearranging a*b^(-1)=c*d^(-1) to c^(-1)*a=d^(-1)*b.
Source, Theorem 2.1, printed page 3:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03401 .

The hypothesis T(S)=o(p^(7/2)) implies v^4<=T(S), so v=o(p^(7/8)), in
particular v=o(p). Since d=O(p), the characteristic restriction in (10)
holds for all sufficiently large p. For a heavy B palette, Section 5 and
(10) now imply E_aff(S)=o(p^3).

Equations (8)-(9) show that every nonzero Fourier block has fourth moment
o(p^4), since sqrt(p)T(S)=o(p^4). The axis estimate (4) has the same bound.
Therefore the nonconstant operator norm of Q is

                             lambda=o(p).               (11)

7. TRANSFER TO ACTUAL ELIGIBLE EDGES AND PRUNING

Let E be the actual edge-point set. If e=(x,y) and tau_(a,b)(e)=(z,w) both
lie in E, then x,z belong to X and y,w belong to Y. The two closing
color-(a,b) incidences (x,w),(z,y) are therefore eligible. If both are
present, B forces the transition to be the unique actual-color fixed loop.
Every other transition can be charged to a missing eligible incidence.
Each such incidence is charged at most d times in either row position.
Consequently

 <1_E,Q1_E> <= m+2dR,
 <1_E,Q1_E> >= d*m^2/p^2-lambda*(m-m^2/p^2).             (12)

Suppose m>=epsilon*p^2 along a sequence. Discard colors with fewer than
eta*p actual edges, where eta=epsilon/(2C). At most d*eta*p<=epsilon*p^2/2
edges are lost. The retained graph has m'=Omega(p^2), d'=Omega(p), and all
its color classes are heavy. Its T, v, and R can only decrease. Sections
5-6 give lambda=o(p) for this palette. The second line of (12) is then
Omega(p^3), while its first line is at most p^2+2CpR=o(p^3). Contradiction.
This completes the theorem.

8. AN OBSTRUCTION TO DEDUCING THE SLOPE-ENERGY CONDITION FROM EXISTING DATA

The following is a proper, heavy, common-domain construction. It is NOT B.
It demonstrates that propriety plus L_all=o(p) and E_aff=o(p^3) do not by
themselves force T=o(p^(7/2)); the missing B information must be used more
deeply, or cancellation in (8) must be retained instead of termwise Weil.

For all sufficiently large primes put

 k=floor(log p), v=floor(p/(100k)),
 S={(a,b):1<=a<=k, 1<=b<=v}.

Let X be F_p with all intersection rows of pairs of distinct-slope palette
lines deleted. Such rows lie among

                 (b'-b)/(a-a'),  a!=a'.

There are at most (2k-2)(2v-1)<p/20 such rows. Hence |X|>19p/20, and the
full palette graph on X is proper: equal images at a common row would
force precisely a deleted intersection, while distinct intercepts at the
same slope never meet. Each class is heavy, d=kv=Theta(p), and m=Theta(p^2).

A vertical palette line contains v points; a nonvertical line contains at
most k points. Thus L_all=max(k,v)=o(p). PRRW implies

 E_aff << sqrt(v)*d^(5/2)+L_all*d^2=o(p^3).

But the slope counts are uniformly v on [1,k], so

 T(S)=v^4 E_times([1,k])
     >= (2k^2-k)v^4
      = Omega(p^4/(log p)^2),

which is much larger than p^(7/2). Only the two diagonal families in
multiplicative energy were used; no number-theoretic estimate or finite
computation is needed.

To verify explicitly that this is not a B counterexample, use slope 1 and
intercepts 1,2,3, which occur for large p. As |X|>p/2, some x,x+1 both lie
in X. The two rows x,x+1 and columns x+2,x+3 form a rectangle with colors
2,3 on its first row and 1,2 on its second. Thus a color repeats.

This construction shows failure of a particular auxiliary implication,
not failure of the desired B sparsity conclusion and not a lower bound
on the actual crossed-operator norm. Cancellation may make the true
fourth moment substantially smaller than the termwise upper bound (8).
