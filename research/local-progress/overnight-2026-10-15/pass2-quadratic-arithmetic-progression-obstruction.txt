QUADRATIC AFFINE SUPPORTS: THE SLOPE-AP SUBPATTERN IS INSUFFICIENT
15 October 2026. Exact algebra and an asymptotic construction; no finite
search, Lean certificate, or novelty claim. This note is a barrier to
one specific simplification, not a counterexample to #810.

1. ROOT-PAIR COORDINATES

For the quadratic palette sigma_a(x)=a*x+a^2 over an odd prime field,
represent an actual edge of color a by the pair (a,z), where

                         x=z-a,    y=a*z.               (1.1)

Let E be the set of these selected ordered pairs. Given an edge (x,y),
its possible completion colors are roots of t^2+x*t-y=0. For (1.1)
these are a and -z, so the only possible second representation is
(-z,-a). This identifies the actual properness constraint; it is NOT
symmetry under the transpose (a,z)->(z,a).

2. EXACT ARITHMETIC-PROGRESSION SUBFAMILY OF FORBIDDEN SQUARES

Let a,b,c be nonzero slopes with b=a+t, c=a-t, t!=0. The rows -t,+t
and columns a*c,a*b form a completed square with color matrix

                           a   b
                           c   a.

All four vertices are distinct on their respective sides because
2a*t!=0. The four edges are actual precisely when

                  (a,c),(b,a),(c,a),(a,b) all lie in E.  (2.1)

This can be checked directly from (1.1). It is a two-way star in the
directed root-pair graph, centered at the middle slope a of a 3-term
arithmetic progression. In particular, an E with no pair of opposite
directed edges automatically avoids every square in this subfamily.

The general B constraint is larger: for repeated color a and cross
colors b,c the locally determined square need not have b+c=2a. Thus
the implication from B to avoiding (2.1) is one-way.

3. DENSE HEAVY PROPER SUPPORTS AVOID ALL OF (2.1)

For each odd prime p put d=(p-1)/2 and h=floor(d/2). Use colors
a in {1,...,h}, and select exactly the root pairs

                 E={(a,z):1<=a<=h, a<z<=d}.             (3.1)

There are no reciprocal pairs in E, by the ordinary integer ordering
of these representatives, so no pattern (2.1) occurs.

This does define a proper simple edge-colored graph. For every
selected pair, its other possible color -z has representative p-z>d
and therefore lies outside the palette {1,...,h}. Hence no two
selected colors can assign the same graph edge. Each individual
color is a matching, since sigma_a is a nonconstant affine map.

Color a has d-a actual edges, at least d-h=(1/4+o(1))*p. The total is

  m=sum_(a=1)^h(d-a)=h*d-h(h+1)/2=(3/32+o(1))*p^2.      (3.2)

Thus the construction has a positive proportion of p colors, each
with a fixed positive support fraction, and positive graph density.
It satisfies the AP-subpattern avoidance exactly, not merely in
expectation or after removing a small exceptional set.

4. WHY THIS IS NOT B

For every prime p>=211, the following four selected pairs exist:

                   (3,34), (8,39), (1,102), (3,104).

They give rows 31,101 and columns 102,312 (reduced modulo p), with
color matrix

                            3   8
                            1   3.

All support inequalities in (3.1) hold: h>=52 and d>=105 for p>=211.
The rows are distinct; the columns differ by 210, nonzero modulo such
a prime. The repeated-color square is therefore genuine. Its cross
slopes have 8+1!=2*3, so it is outside the AP subfamily.

5. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE NEXT ATTEMPT

Ordinary Roth theory on slope triples only detects (2.1), which is
compatible with dense heavy supports. It cannot by itself resolve
arbitrary-support quadratic palettes. A successful argument has to
use the full coupled root-pair sum/product conditions, including the
non-AP triples that determine isolated local coincidences. The new
prime-field small-total-defect theorem addresses a different regime
through the total dual-incidence budget; it does not remove this
arbitrary-positive-support gap.
