INDEPENDENT CHECK OF THE QUANTITATIVE AFFINE DEFICIT THEOREM
15 October 2026. Finite-audit agent's independent hand check.

Target: pass1-affine-quantitative-audit.txt.
Verdict: PASS, including the improved exponent 1/12.

1. The exact incidence inequality d<=(r+1)L is valid. Its parallel-pair
   and finite-intersection categories partition all unordered pairs,
   and every omitted incidence is counted at most once in the sum of
   t_P-1 over intersection points.

2. If C=A B^(-1) A and A!=B, the rows x and z=A^(-1)B(x) give the
   completed rectangle A,B;C,A. Four actual-edge conditions exclude at
   most 4r choices of x because each relevant affine map is a bijection.
   Distinct affine maps A,B coincide at at most one point; this is also
   precisely the possible row-degeneracy z=x. Thus n>4r+1 ensures an
   actual nondegenerate forbidden rectangle. The strong progression
   convention correctly includes C=B and order-two steps.

3. Left-coset normalization preserves this exact noncommutative
   progression equation. The specified translation and concurrent-
   pencil cosets are correct. Their normalized ambient groups are
   (F,+) and the cyclic group F^*.

4. I independently read Schildkraut's Theorem 11.4 at
   https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15125v1#S11.SS2 . It states the bound
   |A|<=N exp[-Omega((log N)^(1/12))] for progression-free subsets of
   odd-order finite abelian groups. The source explicitly calls this
   a slight generalization of Bloom--Sisask Theorem 21, whose displayed
   formulation is cyclic. The audited note attributes this accurately.

5. In characteristic two, strong progression-freeness permits at most
   one additive-group element. For the multiplicative cyclic group
   of order M=n-1, the representatives 0,...,M-1 embed into odd cyclic
   order 2M+1 without introducing a new three-term progression: the
   relevant integer expression has absolute value at most 2(M-1).
   This covers even M and all torsion issues without assuming that
   F^* has odd order.

6. Combining the uniform pencil bound with d<=(r+1)L and m<=dn gives
   exactly m<=C(r+1)n^2 exp[-c(log n)^(1/12)]. The stated condition
   log(r+1)=o((log n)^(1/12)) both implies the required n>4r+1
   eventually and makes the density tend to zero.

This verifies the conditional theorem and its source use; it does not
verify the entire external Roth theorem from first principles, establish
historical novelty, or remove the affine and deficit hypotheses.
