QUADRATIC AFFINE PALETTES: AN EXACT ARITHMETIC SUPPORT CONSTRAINT
Overnight pass 2, 15 October 2026.
Hand proofs and a precise limitation; no finite graph searches.
No historical novelty claim and no general vanishing-density theorem.

SETTING

Let p be an odd prime. For a palette A subset F_p^* use the completed
color matching sigma_a(x)=a x+a^2, and retain arbitrary actual domains
D_a subset F_p. The graph is proper when every selected grid point has
at most one color. Assume additionally that A is antipodal-free:

                         A intersect (-A) is empty.

This is a useful test class; a general palette can be restricted to an
antipodal-free part, but that restriction alone proves no density bound.

1. A FAMILY OF FORCED POTENTIAL SQUARES

Let t!=0 and a-t,a,a+t all lie in A. On rows -t,t and columns
a(a-t),a(a+t), the completed palette contains the color matrix

                         a       a+t
                         a-t     a.                  (1)

The rows and columns are distinct, because a,t and 2 are nonzero.
Each of these four positions lies on only ONE palette line. Indeed
the two possible color parameters at (x,y) are the roots of

                         z^2+xz-y=0.

At the positions in (1), the alternative roots are -(a-t), -a, -a,
and -(a+t), respectively. All lie outside A. Consequently properness
never forces deletion of these four incidences, and resolving double
intersections of other palette lines does not affect them.

The B condition therefore imposes the exact support restriction

  1_Da(-t) 1_Da(t) 1_D(a+t)(-t) 1_D(a-t)(t)=0          (2)

for every such additive progression in the slope palette.
This is an ADDITIVE progression of slopes, not a global three-term
relation among the affine permutations.

2. EACH OMITTED INCIDENCE CAN HIT AT MOST TWO OF THESE SQUARES

Let T(A) count these nontrivial slope progressions, identifying t and
-t, which give the same geometric square. Different remaining choices
give different squares: their repeated color determines a, and their
row pair determines {t,-t}.

Fix a colored incidence (c,x), meaning the point (x,sigma_c(x)). If it
occurs on a square (1), there are only two possible centers:

 * c, if it is one of the repeated-color diagonal edges;
 * c+x, if it is a cross edge.

In either case the step is determined up to sign by x. If x=0 it
occurs in none of the nontrivial squares. Thus every incidence belongs
to at most two of the T(A) squares. To satisfy (2), the actual domains
must omit at least

                              T(A)/2                              (3)

incidences at unique-line points. Equivalently, the potential squares
have an incidence cover number at least T(A)/2.

There is also a collection of at least T(A)/5 pairwise edge-disjoint
potential squares. Make a graph whose vertices are the squares, joining
two when they share an edge. Each square has four edges and each edge
belongs to at most one other square, so this graph has maximum degree
at most four. A greedy independent set has size at least T(A)/5.

3. A SHARP PROPERNESS BASELINE, AND AN ADDITIONAL B LOSS

Write d=|A|. Any two distinct quadratic palette lines meet once, and no
three meet, since z^2+xz-y has at most two roots. Thus the union of the
completed lines has exactly

                         dp-binomial(d,2)

grid positions. This is the maximum edge count allowed by properness
alone: retain every unique-line point and choose one owner at every
double intersection.

The incidences counted in (3) are all unique-line points, disjoint from
the intersection loss already forced by properness. Therefore every
actual B-colored subgraph of this antipodal-free palette satisfies

                  m <= dp-binomial(d,2)-T(A)/2.        (4)

Formula (4) is a local-support constraint. It is not asserted to be
the best numerical bound, and it does not imply m=o(p^2).

4. DENSE PALETTES GIVE QUADRATICALLY MANY SUCH LOCAL CONSTRAINTS

For every fixed delta>0 there is c_delta>0 such that, if |A|>=delta p
and p is sufficiently large, then

                            T(A)>=c_delta p^2.        (5)

For completeness this follows from the usual finite Varnavides argument.
Choose a fixed integer M so large that every subset of {1,...,M} of
size at least delta M/2 contains a nonconstant three-term progression.
Such an M follows from Roth's theorem; one may use the quantitative
odd-cyclic input already audited in pass1-affine-quantitative-audit.txt.

For all x in F_p and t in F_p^*, average |A intersect
{x+t,...,x+Mt}|. The mean is at least delta M. Since every size is at
most M, at least a delta/2 fraction of these p(p-1) progressions have
at least delta M/2 marked positions. Each such progression contains an
A-progression. A fixed unordered three-term progression in F_p is
represented at most 2M^2 times in this counting: choose its orientation
and the two integer positions that determine the arithmetic step, and
then x,t are fixed. Thus (5) holds, for example with a sufficiently
small positive constant proportional to delta/M^2.

This proves that a dense antipodal-free palette forces an additional
positive fraction of missing UNIQUE-LINE incidences in any B support.
It identifies where the extra omissions must occur, rather than merely
counting unavoidable collisions at intersections of completed lines.

5. WHY THESE ADDITIVE TEMPLATES DO NOT SETTLE DENSE SUPPORTS

Take a prime p>240, put k=floor(p/10), and use

                   A={1,...,k},    D={1,...,k},
                   D_a=D for every a in A.

This is a proper coloring. Distinct lines a,b intersect only at the row
x=-(a+b). Its integer representative lies between p-2k and p-2,
which is outside D. The edge count is k^2=(1/100+o(1))p^2.

Yet D intersect (-D) is empty, so EVERY template (1), whose rows are
t and -t, already loses at least one row. Thus the entire family of
constraints (2) is satisfied in this positive-density proper example.

It is certainly not B. The following four selected positions are all
present for p>240:

                         column 52     column 112
             row 9        color 4       color 7
             row 24       color 2       color 4.

The identities are 4*9+4^2=52, 7*9+7^2=112,
2*24+2^2=52, and 4*24+4^2=112. All specified rows and colors lie in
D and A, and the two rows and columns are distinct modulo p.

This verifies a precise limitation: additive-progressions-on-rows
{t,-t} can all be absent while a dense properly colored quadratic graph
still has different repeated-color rectangles. A proof for arbitrary
supports must control more of the coupled four-pair sum-product
pattern. It cannot stop after proving (2) or its supersaturation (5).
The complementary root-oriented construction in
pass2-quadratic-arithmetic-progression-obstruction.txt gives another
explicit obstruction; its proof is not duplicated here.

6. THE COMMON-DOMAIN GAP REMAINS

With a common row domain D and palette A, properness is equivalent to

      D avoids -(a+b) for all distinct a,b in A.

A forbidden rectangle with diagonal color a, cross colors b,c, and
rows x,z satisfies

      b^2+bx=a^2+az,     c^2+cz=a^2+ax,
      a,b,c in A,       x,z in D,       x!=z.           (6)

The two equations can be solved rationally for x,z for generic a,b,c,
but positive density of A and D alone does not justify counting their
values as independent random field elements. The inspected rational
Roth theorems concern fixed rational shifts of a common additive base,
not this coupled map A^3->D^2. No valid mixing theorem or parametrization
resolving (6) is established in this note.
