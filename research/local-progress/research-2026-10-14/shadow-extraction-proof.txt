Full-shadow extraction and nonlinear corrections, 2026-10-14

STATUS AND SCOPE
These are independently derived mathematical deductions, checked adversarially
and by finite tests. Publication novelty remains unverified. They strengthen
exact comparisons with the Brown-Erdos-Sos extremal function, but do not settle
the quadratic-versus-subquadratic question in Erdos problem #810. The general
proofs below are written mathematical proofs, not claimed Lean formalizations.

Write g(N) for the maximum size of a simple 3-uniform (7,4)-free family on N
vertices, and g_lin(N) for the maximum restricted to linear families. Write
M(N) for the maximum number of edges of an N-vertex graph admitting at most N
colors with every C4 rainbow. A triple family is (7,4)-free if four distinct
triples always have union of size at least eight.

THEOREM A: FULL-SHADOW EXTRACTION

    M(N) >= 3 g_lin(N).

In fact the resulting coloring is proper. Let H be linear. Its 2-shadow has
an edge uv for every pair contained in a triple. The color of uv is the unique
third vertex w for which uvw belongs to H. Linearity makes the color unique,
and every original triple contributes three distinct graph edges. Hence the
shadow has exactly 3|H| edges on N vertices and uses only the original N
vertices as color labels.

It is proper: two incident edges uv and ux with equal color w would come from
triples uvw and uxw sharing the pair uw, contrary to linearity unless v=x.

To prove that every C4 is rainbow, use the following more general localization
lemma, which also supplies the nonlinear extension.

LOCALIZATION LEMMA
For an arbitrary (7,4)-free H, take its entire 2-shadow and, for each graph
edge, select any containing triple and color the edge by its omitted vertex.
Every nonrainbow C4 is supported either by:
  (i) two triples sharing a pair, whose union has four points; or
  (ii) three triples whose union has at most five points.

Proof. If the four selected triples are distinct, the four graph vertices and
at most three colors contain them all, contradicting (7,4)-freeness. Thus two
selected triples coincide. They cannot correspond to opposite cycle edges,
which together have four distinct endpoints. Relabel the cycle a-b-c-d-a so
that ab and bc both select abc. Their colors are c and a. Write the remaining
selected triples as cdx and day, so their colors are x and y. Colors are never
equal to an endpoint of the edge they color. The only possible repetitions in
the list c,a,x,y are consequently

    x=a, or y=c, or x=y.

If x=a and y=c, the selected triples are just abc and acd, case (i). In any
other case, the distinct selected triples number three and their union is
contained in {a,b,c,d,z} for a single additional point z, case (ii).

For linear H, neither (i) nor (ii) is possible: two linear triples span at
least five points, and three distinct linear triples span at least six. This
proves Theorem A.

STRUCTURAL FACTS USED BELOW
Join triples if their intersection has two points. Every connected component
of this overlap graph has at most three triples. Different nontrivial overlap
components have disjoint vertex supports. These facts and the basic 2N/5
subfamily deletion argument are prior art in Furedi, Section 8 (2012):
https://iuuk.mff.cuni.cz/~samal/DS/S0963548311000563a.pdf

More generally, every three-edge family spanning five points is an isolated
block: any other edge meeting its support would make four edges span at most
seven points. Three edges spanning four points must be the entire family.
Outside these isolated blocks, every nontrivial overlap component is therefore
a two-edge diamond abc,abd. No external triple meets its support in two
points: a third such triple would create an isolated three-edge block on at
most five points.

The outside pairs of all external triples meeting a diamond's four-point
support are pairwise disjoint. Otherwise the diamond and two external triples
would have union at most seven. Furthermore, a triple disjoint from the support
cannot contain both outside vertices of a triple that meets the support, for
the same reason.

THEOREM B: REWIRING IMPROVES THE LINEAR EXTREMAL COMPARISON

    g(N) <= g_lin(N) + floor(N/4), for N >= 5.

This is a statement about replacing edges, not a stronger linear-subfamily
deletion theorem. On each isolated three-edge/five-point block, replace its
triples by two linear triples on the same five points, losing one edge.
This preserves (7,4)-freeness: new and old supports are disjoint, and mixed
four-edge selections span at least eight points. Remove one edge from each
remaining two-edge diamond. All remaining triples are linear. If there are
a isolated five-point blocks and b diamonds, the supports are disjoint, so
5a+4b<=N and the loss a+b<=floor(N/4). The exceptional entire three-edge
family on four points can, when N>=5, be replaced by two linear triples on
five points, again losing only one.

DIRECT NONLINEAR SHADOW CONSTRUCTION
On each ordinary diamond abc,abd, the full shadow has five pairs. Its only
four-cycle has edges ac,bc,bd,ad. Delete any one of those four edges, retaining
four shadow edges. On an isolated three-edge/five-point block, first replace
the block by a three-edge path, for instance abc,abd,ade. Retain the six shadow
edges supplied by the linear pair abc,ade. This replacement is valid because
the block is isolated; any mixed four-edge selection still spans at least
eight points. Keep the full shadow of all other triples.

The localization lemma proves the resulting graph is rainbow-C4: every possible
bad cycle would lie inside one diamond or one isolated five-point block, and
the selected graphs in those supports have no C4 at all. An ordinary diamond
retains four graph edges; its coloring need not be proper. With a isolated
three-edge blocks and b diamonds the resulting graph has

    3|H| - 3a - 2b

edges. This already gives an additive O(N) correction to factor-three
extraction for arbitrary nonlinear H.

TWO EXTREMAL REPLACEMENT LEMMAS

(1) An extremal H has at most one isolated three-edge/five-point block.
Two such blocks have six edges on ten isolated vertices. Replace them by the
linear seven-edge (7,4)-free family
    457,349,248,189,367,058,016.
Its pair and four-edge conditions are independently checked in
linear10-seven-certificate.json and extremal-overlap-checks.json. This
replacement preserves all mixed four-edge conditions and gains one edge.

(2) An extremal H cannot contain both an isolated five-point block and an
ordinary diamond. Let T be the isolated five-point support and S the diamond's
four-point support. Replace the five local edges by the six row/column triples
of a 3-by-3 grid on S union T. This gadget is linear and (7,4)-free: any four
row/column triples have distribution 3+1, 2+2, or 1+3, and span at least eight.

The replacement is safe even though external edges can meet S. For three new
edges and one external edge, the union has at least 6+3-1=8 points. For two
new and two external edges, the new union has at least five points and the
two external edges supply at least four points outside S, giving at least
nine. The latter assertion follows from the two diamond boundary facts above.

For one new edge and three external edges, let r be the number of distinct
points of S used by the three external edges. If r=0 their union has at least
five points, by the original (7,4)-condition with any old local edge. If r=1
it has at least six points, using an old diamond edge containing that point.
If r=2 it has at least seven points: two external edges through distinct ports
have disjoint outside pairs; a third edge entirely contained in their six-point
union would either meet S and violate outside-pair disjointness, or avoid S
and contain both points of one outside pair. If r=3, all three external edges
meet S and their outside pairs are disjoint, giving nine points. The new edge
overlaps this external union in at most r points. In every case the full union
has at least eight points. We gained one edge, contrary to extremality.

THEOREM C: SHARPER NONLINEAR COMPARISON

    M(N) >= 3 g(N) - 2 floor(N/4), for N >= 5.

For N>=8, take an extremal H. If it has no isolated five-point block, its
diamonds number at most floor(N/4), giving the stated correction. If it has
one such block, it has no diamonds by replacement lemma (2), and the direct
construction loses only three graph edges. Since 2floor(N/4)>=4, this is even
better. The exceptional entire three-edge/four-point family is not extremal
for N>=8, since the four triples 012,234,456,670 are linear and (7,4)-free on
eight vertices. For N=5,6,7, g(N)=3 and the asserted lower bound is seven;
this follows from the already independently verified M(5)=7 and monotonicity.

OPTIONAL STRONGER STRUCTURAL OBSERVATION
For N>=8, if an extremizer has an isolated three-edge/five-point block T, its
complement is linear, by the two replacement lemmas. Choose an outside triple
abc, and label T by 1,2,3,4,5. Replace its three edges by a12,b23,c45, keeping
abc. The four local triples are linear and span eight points. All other edges
meet abc in at most one point. Mixed selections with three local edges span
at least 6+3-1=8; with two local edges, at least 5+5-2=8; with one new local
edge and three old edges, at least 3+6-1=8 because the old family is linear.
The resulting entire hypergraph is linear with the same number of triples.
Thus an extremizer with such an isolated block implies g(N)=g_lin(N), and
Theorem A then yields M(N)>=3g(N) for that case. This observation is not needed
for the stated uniform correction bound.

LIMITATIONS
These comparisons do not establish whether g(N) or M(N) is o(N^2). They do
not justify reversing a rainbow-C4 coloring into a (7,4)-free lift. In
particular, contracting a diamond is unsafe: the verified family
{012,013,245,367,468,579} becomes non-(7,4)-free after identifying tips 3 and 2.
