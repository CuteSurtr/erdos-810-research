Extremal overlap-component refinement, 2026-10-14

STATUS: A proved additive refinement for the extremal comparison, with an
independently checked finite replacement gadget. It does not change the
quadratic-versus-subquadratic issue in Erdos #810. Publication novelty has not
been established. The general replacement argument is a written proof; the
finite gadget is independently checked by exhaustive Python enumeration and
the successful final indexed Lean certificate.

Notation: g(N) is the maximum number of triples in a simple (7,4)-free
3-uniform hypergraph on N vertices. g_lin(N) imposes linearity as well.
Here (7,4)-free means every four distinct triples have union of size at least 8.

THEOREM: For every N >= 5,

    g(N) <= g_lin(N) + ceil(N/4).

Prior art: Furedi, "2-Cancellative Hypergraphs and Codes" (2012), Section 8,
proves the overlap-component argument giving a linear subfamily after at most
2N/5 deletions. Its ingredients include isolated three-edge components on five
points and disjoint supports for remaining two-edge overlaps. The improvement
here uses extremality plus a finite replacement, so it is not a sharper
deletion theorem for arbitrary input hypergraphs.
Source: https://iuuk.mff.cuni.cz/~samal/DS/S0963548311000563a.pdf

1. Isolated three-edge blocks.
If three distinct triples have union S of size 5, every other triple is
disjoint from S: otherwise those four triples have union at most 5+3-1=7.
If their union has size 4, no fourth triple exists anywhere at all.

2. Replacement gadget.
The following seven triples on vertices 0,...,9 form a linear (7,4)-free system:

    457, 349, 248, 189, 367, 058, 016.

The certificate lists all edges in linear10-seven-certificate.json. The Python
checker enumerates all 21 pair intersections and 35 four-edge unions.
ExtremalReplacementCertificate.lean is an attempted additional verification;
uniformity, edge count, and linearity passed, but the full quantified
(7,4)-free step exhausted resources and is not reported as a completed proof.

3. At most one isolated three-edge/five-point block in an extremizer.
Suppose two such blocks existed. Their disjoint supports use ten vertices and
their total edge count is six. Replace those six triples by the seven-edge
gadget from step 2. No remaining edge meets these ten vertices.

The resulting family is still (7,4)-free. Four new triples are safe by the
gadget certificate. Three new triples span at least six points by linearity,
so with an old triple they span at least nine. Two new triples span at least
five points and two old triples at least four, so they span at least nine.
One new triple has three points; three old triples span at least five points,
since a three-old-edge union of size four would already contradict the
original family together with any edge in either isolated block. Thus the
remaining mixed distribution spans at least eight points. We gained one edge,
contradicting extremality.

4. Count the deletions.
Form the graph on hyperedges joining pairs that share two vertices. Every
component has at most three edges. A three-edge component spans at most five
points. Supports of distinct nontrivial overlap components are disjoint.

An extremizer therefore has at most one three-edge component on five points.
If there is no such component, the number of two-edge components is at most
floor(N/4), and removing one edge from each makes the family linear.
If there is one, remove two edges from it and one from every two-edge component.
The deletion count is at most

    2 + floor((N-5)/4) = ceil(N/4).

The exceptional three-edge component on four points is the entire hypergraph;
at most two deletions suffice, and 2 <= ceil(N/4) for N >= 5.

Taking the maximum over linear subfamilies proves the theorem.

RELATED EXTREMAL PROJECTION REFINEMENT (N=2n >= 8).
For the balanced two-part projection write
    p = 3n / [4(2n-1)],
    q = n(n-1) / [4(2n-1)(2n-3)].
The earlier exact averaging identity has collision loss q times W, where
    W = (#pairs of codegree 2) + (5/2)(#pairs of codegree 3).
For an extremal H, the component argument now gives
    W <= max(floor(N/4), 5/2 + floor((N-5)/4)).
Consequently, setting c_n=1/2 for even n and c_n=1 for odd n,
    M(n) >= ceil(p*g(2n) - q*(n/2+c_n)), n>=4.
This improves the previous leading additive correction from about n/16 to
about n/32, but still does not affect the asymptotic question in #810.
