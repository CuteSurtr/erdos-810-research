Adversarial checks for a trivial stronger quarter-density bound
2026-10-15

STATUS
These checks do not prove a quarter-density bound. They identify two
plausible shortcuts and precisely what they actually establish. They also
show that the palette size cannot be discarded in endpoint estimates.
Publication comparison remains a separate literature task.

1. THE ENDPOINT BOUND e(S)<=k^2 IS SHARP WITHOUT A PALETTE BOUND
Let the vertices be a_1,...,a_k,b_1,...,b_k. Include the k edges a_i b_i,
all edges a_i a_j with i<j, and all edges a_i b_j with i<j. Give the k
matching edges a_i b_i one common color c. Give every other edge its own
fresh color. The graph has exactly

    k+binom(k,2)+binom(k,2)=k^2

edges. The coloring is proper. A nonrainbow C4 would have to use two
c-colored edges. For i<j, the only cross edges between {a_i,b_i} and
{a_j,b_j} are a_i a_j and a_i b_j, sharing the endpoint a_i. Neither pair
of disjoint cross edges needed for that C4 is present. Thus every C4 is
rainbow, and the endpoint inequality is attained exactly.

This construction uses k^2-k+1 colors. It does not refute a stronger bound
when the palette is only O(k), but does refute any palette-independent
improvement e(S)=o(k^2).

2. WHY A PALETTE-DEPENDENT o(k^2) ENDPOINT BOUND WOULD BE A BREAKTHROUGH
Starting with any proper rainbow-C4 graph G, attach one new leaf to every
vertex. Give all new leaf edges the same new color, absent from G. The
new coloring remains proper and every C4 remains in the old graph. The
new color class is a perfect matching spanning all 2|V(G)| vertices.
If every endpoint-induced graph of this type, with only O(k) colors,
had o(k^2) edges, applying that assertion here would make |E(G)|=o(|V(G)|^2).
Together with properification, this would solve the original asymptotic
Erdos #810 question. It is not a harmless application of triangle removal.

3. A DIRECT NEIGHBORHOOD/TRIANGLE BOUND GIVES 3/8, NOT 1/4
Let G be properly B-colored with q available colors. Fix v, put W=N(v),
and d=|W|. For each color c present at v, let vz be its unique edge at v.
If uw is an edge of color c inside W, neither u nor w is adjacent to z:
otherwise a C4 using uw and vz would repeat c. Thus the endpoints of
c-colored edges in G[W] lie among the nonneighbors of z inside W. There
are d-1-d_{G[W]}(z) such vertices.

For each of the q-d colors absent at v, its matching in W has at most d
endpoints. Summing endpoint counts over all colors gives

    2e(G[W]) <= sum_{z in W}(d-1-d_{G[W]}(z))+(q-d)d
               =d(q-1)-2e(G[W]).

Therefore

    e(G[N(v)]) <= d(v)(q-1)/4.

Summing over v gives an upper bound on triangles T:

    3T <= (q-1)*(2m)/4,
    T <= (q-1)m/6.

On the other hand, each edge uv has at least d(u)+d(v)-N common neighbors,
so

    3T >= sum_v d(v)^2-Nm >= 4m^2/N-Nm.

Combining these inequalities yields

    m <= N*(N+(q-1)/2)/4.

For q=N this is 3N^2/8-O(N), weaker than the audited recursive bound near
0.299225*N^2. Thus this natural triangle-supersaturation shortcut does not
supply a hidden quarter threshold.

4. FURTHER CAUTION
A claim that all proper B-colored graphs with O(N) colors have o(N^3)
triangles would itself imply the (7,4) Brown-Erdos-Sos conjecture: the full
shadow of a dense linear (7,4)-free triple family is properly B-colored and
contains a positive quadratic number of edge-disjoint triangles, so triangle
removal would force a positive cubic number of triangles. Such a claim should
not be treated as an automatic consequence of the coloring conditions.
