2. CONCAVITY
For A_C(r)=1/(1+Cr), the quantity used in the cross-cut derivative is
D(r)=1/r+A_C(r). With t=x(r-x),

    d^2/dx^2 [t/(1+D(r)t)]
      =-2/(1+D(r)t)^2-2D(r)(r-2x)^2/(1+D(r)t)^3.

One simple uniform certificate for C>=1 and r<=1 is

    D(r)t <= [r+r^2/(1+Cr)]/4 <=3/8.

It gives a negative second derivative for the full endpoint integrand:
2-4/(11/8)^2=-14/121<0. The sharper -14/25 certificate in the main proof
also checks, using a+b<=1. Both certify the required Jensen step.

The denominator-sum function is

    f_C(r)=r(1+Cr)/(1+(C+1)r),
    f_C''(r)=-2/[1+(C+1)r]^3<0.

Its concavity justifies replacing f_C(a)+f_C(b) by 2f_C((a+b)/2).
The loss expression decreases with the edge count, as in the previous
independent audit, so substituting the current F_A upper bound has the
claimed direction.
