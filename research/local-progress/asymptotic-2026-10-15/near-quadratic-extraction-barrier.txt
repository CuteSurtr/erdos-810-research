Near-quadratic obstruction to uniform B-to-C extraction
2026-10-15

STATUS
Complete written deduction using density Hales-Jewett. The proof includes
an elementary sphere construction of the needed progression-free sets.
No finite computation or unverified Lean assertion is used. The result is
an obstruction to a proposed reduction, not a new upper or lower bound
settling Erdos #810. Publication novelty remains unestablished.

THEOREM
There exists a sequence of properly edge-colored bipartite graphs G_j with
N_j vertices, at most N_j/2 colors, and

    m_j=|E(G_j)| = N_j^(2-o(1)),

such that every subgraph whose inherited coloring is C-colored has o(m_j)
edges. Here C means rainbow C4 and no alternating two-color four-edge path.
All the original graphs are B-colored: proper with every C4 rainbow.

The conclusion remains valid if every original color may be split into at
most K subclasses, for any fixed K. It also remains valid when every left
vertex and right vertex can be split into at most L and R copies respectively,
for fixed L,R, with retained edges assigned arbitrarily to endpoint copies.

Thus even the near-quadratic edge regime does not permit a density-independent
constant-fraction B-to-C reduction by deletion or bounded local splitting.
A fixed positive quadratic-density hypothesis remains outside this theorem.

1. A BASE GRAPH FROM A PROGRESSION-FREE SET
Let Q be odd and let S subset Z/QZ have at least two elements and satisfy

    b+c=2a, with a,b,c in S, implies a=b=c.

Let B(Q,S) have two disjoint vertex classes Z/QZ. Include xy exactly when
y-x belongs to S, and color xy by y-x. Each color is a perfect matching.
There are Q|S| edges and |S| colors.

If a C4 has a repeated color a, properness puts the equal-colored edges on
opposite sides. The other colors b,c satisfy b+c=2a. The property of S forces
all these colors equal, contradicting the distinct C4 vertices. Hence the
coloring is B.

For distinct a,b in S, the union of their two perfect matchings is a disjoint
union of even cycles of length 2*ord(a-b). The order is at least three because
Q is odd and a-b is nonzero. Thus it contains an alternating cycle of length
at least six, and therefore an alternating path of four edges on five vertices.

2. TENSOR POWERS AND DENSITY HALES-JEWETT
For a fixed base B=B(Q,S), its d-th bipartite tensor power has vertex classes
(Z/QZ)^d and color set S^d. Edges satisfy y-x in S^d coordinatewise. The same
progression-free calculation proves it is B-colored. Its parameters are

    N=2Q^d,  q=|S|^d,  m=(Q|S|)^d.

Let A=E(B), viewed as an alphabet of size M=Q|S|. The edge set of the tensor
power is naturally A^d. A combinatorial line in A^d gives a color-preserving
copy of the entire base B: outside the nonempty variable-coordinate set I,
all endpoints and colors are fixed; on I, the left endpoint repeats a base
left vertex, the right endpoint repeats a base right vertex, and the color
repeats its base color. Nonemptiness of I makes the endpoint and color maps
injective. In particular, the line contains the base's alternating four-edge
path.

Therefore a C-colored retained edge set must be combinatorial-line-free.
If c(d,M) is the largest line-free subset of an M-letter d-dimensional cube,
its size is at most c(d,M). Density Hales-Jewett states

    c(d,M)/M^d -> 0 as d -> infinity, for every fixed finite M.

For K color subclasses, partition retained edges by the extra subclass index.
Every class must be line-free, so at most K*c(d,M) edges survive. With L and R
endpoint-copy indices as well, the corresponding bound is LRK*c(d,M). A line
within one common index class still embeds the base and preserves its two
alternating colors and distinct vertices.

3. ELEMENTARY NEAR-FULL-SIZE PROGRESSION-FREE SETS
For an integer j>=4, put t=2^j, B=2t, and Q_j=B^j+1, which is odd.
For each vector v in {0,...,t-1}^j, encode it as the integer

    f(v)=sum_{i=0}^{j-1} v_i B^i.

There are at most j(t-1)^2+1 values of the squared norm sum v_i^2. Choose a
largest norm class V_j and let S_j={f(v):v in V_j}, viewed in Z/Q_jZ. Then

    |S_j| >= t^j / (j(t-1)^2+1).

Every encoded value is less than B^j/2. Consequently a congruence
f(v)+f(w)=2f(u) modulo Q_j is an equality of ordinary integers: both sides
lie in [0,B^j), shorter than a full residue interval. There are no carries in
either side in base B, since every digit sum and every doubled digit is at
most 2t-2<B. Hence v+w=2u coordinatewise. Since u,v,w have the same squared
norm, strict convexity (or the parallelogram identity) implies u=v=w.
Thus S_j is progression-free in the required strong sense.

Moreover,

    log_2 Q_j = j^2+j+o(1),
    log_2 |S_j| >= j^2-2j-log_2 j-O(1),

so log_{Q_j}|S_j| -> 1. This is the usual sphere idea behind Behrend's
construction, with all details needed here supplied explicitly.

4. DIAGONAL CHOICE
For each j, let M_j=Q_j|S_j|. By density Hales-Jewett, choose d_j sufficiently
large that

    c(d_j,M_j)/M_j^{d_j} < 1/j^2.

We may additionally choose d_j>=j and make N_j=2Q_j^{d_j} strictly increase.
Let G_j be the d_j-th tensor power of B(Q_j,S_j). Then

    log(m_j)/log(N_j/2)
      = log(Q_j|S_j|)/log Q_j
      = 1+log_{Q_j}|S_j| -> 2.

Thus m_j=N_j^(2-o(1)), while q_j=|S_j|^{d_j}<=Q_j^{d_j}=N_j/2.
Every inherited C-colored subgraph has fewer than m_j/j^2 edges, and every
fixed (L,R,K) refinement has fewer than LRK*m_j/j^2 edges. Both fractions
tend to zero. This proves the theorem.

5. WHAT THE RESULT DOES AND DOES NOT ELIMINATE
It eliminates a positive retained fraction independent of the input graph,
even when the input is proper B-colored, has O(N) colors, and has
N^(2-o(1)) edges. Bounded numbers of vertex/color copies do not repair it.

It does not eliminate a theorem with the hypothesis m>=epsilon*N^2 for a
fixed epsilon>0 and a retained fraction depending on epsilon. Our constructed
graphs have m/(N/2)^2=(|S_j|/Q_j)^{d_j}->0. It also does not eliminate
unbounded color refinement, arbitrary recoloring with O(N) total colors, or
transformations that do not preserve edges through bounded labelled copies.
In particular, no solution of the original asymptotic problem follows.
