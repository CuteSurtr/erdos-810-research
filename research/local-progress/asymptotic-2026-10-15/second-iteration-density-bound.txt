Second endpoint iteration for rainbow-C4 colorings
2026-10-15. Proof draft; novelty not established; not Lean verified.

RESULT. Let M(N) be the largest number of edges in an N-vertex graph whose
edges receive at most N colors and whose every four-cycle is rainbow. Then

                      M(N) <= (c+o(1)) N^2,

where c=0.30052603570643976... is the unique root in [1/4, 0.31] of

       32c^7 - 16c^6 + 4c^4 + 2c^3 - 2c^2 - 3c + 1 = 0.

This refines the first endpoint iteration's (sqrt(5)-1)/4 constant.
It is a constant upper bound, not the conjectured o(N^2) bound.

0. Reduction to proper coloring.
The companion properification-and-regularization.txt proves that deleting
o(N^2) edges makes any rainbow-C4 coloring proper, with a palette-independent
error. Thus it suffices to prove the result for proper rainbow-C4 colorings.
The reduction uses the classical triangle-removal lemma; a primary reference
for that lemma is Jacob Fox, A new proof of the graph removal lemma, Annals
of Mathematics 174 (2011), 561-579, introduction:
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2011/174-1/p17

1. Basic bipartite inequality.
For a proper B-colored bipartite graph with parts of sizes A,B, at most Q
colors, and E edges,

           E <= Q A B (A+B+1) / [Q(A+B)+AB].                 (1)

Proof: a color class of size K is a matching. Between the endpoints of two
of its edges there is at most one of the two possible cross edges, since
both would create a four-cycle with a repeated color. Its endpoint-induced
rectangle therefore has at most K(K+1)/2 edges. Adding the edges from these
endpoints to vertices outside gives endpoint degree sum at most
K(A+B+1-K). Summing over colors yields

 sum_v d(v)^2 <= (A+B+1)E - sum_color K^2
              <= (A+B+1)E - E^2/Q.

On the other hand sum_v d(v)^2 >= E^2/A + E^2/B. Rearrangement proves (1).
Empty parts or E=0 can be treated separately.

2. Second iteration in a bipartite graph.
Set Q=N, A+B=N. Write a=A/N, b=B/N, p=E/N^2, so a+b=1, and t=ab.
Unused colors may be added to reach Q=N. For a particular color class K,
split the graph into its matched endpoint rectangle and the two rectangles
from its endpoints to the outside. The endpoint degree sum is at most

 K(K+1) + B1(K,B-K,N) + B1(A-K,K,N),                        (2)

where B1 is the right side of (1). The first term counts the matched endpoint
rectangle twice, and the other two rectangles once each.

Define, for 0<=x<=s,

            g_s(x) = s x(s-x) / [s+x(s-x)].

After normalization, (2) is at most N^2 f(k)+O(N), uniformly in K,A,B, where
k=K/N and

                 f(k)=k^2+g_a(k)+g_b(k).

The bound follows directly from (1); its extra '+1' contributes only O(N).
For 0<s<=1, put z=x(s-x). Then

 g_s''(x) = -2s^2/[s+z]^2 - 2s^2(s-2x)^2/[s+z]^3
           <= -2/(1+s/4)^2 <= -32/25.

Therefore f''<=2-64/25<0 on [0,min(a,b)]. Jensen applies to the N color
classes (including empty classes). Since their normalized sizes sum to E/N,
their average normalized size is p. Thus

        sum_v d(v)^2 <= N^3 f(p)+O(N^2).

The lower degree bound gives, for t>0,

                 p^2/t <= f(p)+O(1/N).                    (3)

Now rewrite

 f(p)=p-p^2-p^2[(a-p)^2/(a+p(a-p))
                       +(b-p)^2/(b+p(b-p))].

Cauchy-Schwarz gives

 [(a-p)^2/(a+p(a-p))+(b-p)^2/(b+p(b-p))]
             >= (1-2p)^2/[1+p(1-2p)].

Consequently, along any sequence with positive limiting t,p, (3) implies

  p <= t R(p),  R(p)=(1-p+p^2-2p^3)/(1+p-2p^2).

Define

  T(p)=p/R(p)=p(1+p-2p^2)/(1-p+p^2-2p^3).

Let phi be the inverse of T on the interval needed for 0<=t<=1/4. Then the
second-iteration bipartite bound is

                   E/N^2 <= phi(AB/N^2)+o(1).             (4)

The error in (4) is uniform over all A+B=N and all such graphs. To justify
this without dividing an error by small p, suppose uniformity fails and
take a convergent subsequence of (a,b,p) in the compact feasible region.
If p tends to zero, (4) cannot fail by a fixed positive amount. Otherwise
a,b,t stay positive and the preceding limit argument applies.

3. Calculus needed for the inverse and Jensen.
The first-iteration bound (1) gives p<=t/(1+t)+o(1)<=1/5+o(1), so only
0<=p<=1/5 is relevant. Put D=1-p+p^2-2p^3. Direct differentiation gives

 T'(p)  = [1+2p-8p^2+8p^3]/D^2,
 T''(p) = [4-18p+30p^2+28p^3-72p^4+48p^5]/D^3.

On [0,1/5], D>0. The numerator of T' is at least 1-8/25>0.
The numerator of T'' is at least 4-18/5-72/625>0.
Also T(1/5)=28/103>1/4. Thus phi is defined, increasing, and concave on
[0,1/4], with phi(1/4)<1/5.

Finally T'<=2 on [0,1/5]. Indeed 2D^2 minus the numerator of T' equals

 C(p)=1-6p+14p^2-20p^3+10p^4-8p^5+8p^6.

The cubic Q(p)=1-6p+14p^2-20p^3 is decreasing on this interval because
Q'=-6+28p-60p^2<0, and Q(1/5)=1/5. Hence
C(p)>=1/5-8/3125>0. This proves phi'>=1/2.

4. Return to a general graph.
Fix a color matching of size K, and let S be its 2K endpoints. Between any
two matching edges there are at most two other edges, and those two must
share an endpoint: a disjoint pair would give a non-rainbow four-cycle.
Hence the graph induced by S has at most K^2 edges. The endpoint degree sum
is therefore at most 2K^2+e(S,V\S).

Apply (4) to the bipartite graph across this cut, on all N vertices with at
most N colors. For x=K/N the bound is

 N^2 h(x)+o(N^2),  h(x)=2x^2+phi(2x(1-2x)),  0<=x<=1/2.

The error is uniform by step 2, so it may be summed over N colors. Writing
z=2x(1-2x), the calculus in step 3 gives

 h''(x)=4-8phi'(z)+phi''(z)(2-8x)^2 <=0.

Jensen and sum_v d(v)^2>=4E^2/N now show, with rho=E/N^2,

                         4rho^2 <= h(rho)+o(1).

Every subsequential density limit therefore satisfies

                  2rho^2 <= phi(2rho-4rho^2).             (5)

If rho<=1/4 there is nothing to prove. On rho>=1/4, T(2rho^2) is increasing
and 2rho-4rho^2 is decreasing, so (5) has a unique upper boundary. At the
boundary, clearing the positive denominator in

             T(2rho^2)=2rho-4rho^2

and dividing by 2rho gives

           32rho^7-16rho^6+4rho^4+2rho^3-2rho^2-3rho+1=0.

Its relevant root is 0.30052603570643976..., giving the stated result.
The calculus domain causes no gap: phi(t)<1/5, so (5) already forces
2rho^2<1/5. Thus T is evaluated only on its verified domain.

What is and is not claimed.
The inequalities above are a proof of the displayed constant bound, subject
to independent review of this draft. The numerical root is descriptive;
the polynomial and monotonicity uniquely define the constant. No claim is
made that this improves the best published bound, since the literature
comparison is ongoing. Iterating further may improve the constant, but the
formal constant-density fixed point 1/2 for the bipartite bound does not
justify a global 1/4 bound: the palette-to-side ratio changes in the smaller
rectangles and a boundary layer can persist. No such limiting claim is made.
