Independent audit of the F_alpha recursive bipartite bound
2026-10-15

SCOPE
This audit verifies the endpoint decomposition, concavity, Cauchy step,
uniform-asymptotic interpretation, and transfer to the general graph bound.
The remaining one-variable minimization producing Phi(alpha) is being
independently audited by the other mathematical auditor. Nothing here is
claimed to be Lean-formalized or publication-new.

NORMALIZATION AND INDUCTION HYPOTHESIS
Use q as an AVAILABLE palette size, allowing unused colors. Let a,b denote
the two vertex-class sizes divided by q, with s=a+b<=1. Let u=m/q^2 and
p=ab. The claimed family is

    u <= F_alpha(a,b)+o(1),
    F_alpha(a,b)=ab*s/[s+ab*(1+alpha*s)].

The error is required to be uniform over properly B-colored graphs and
normalized sizes a,b with a+b<=1. Equivalently, the claim holds along every
sequence with q tending to infinity. The base alpha=0 has uniform error
at most 1/q, from the exact bipartite counting inequality.

All cut subgraphs must retain the SAME available palette q, including unused
colors. One must not replace q by each cut's possibly small actual number of
used colors and then assume that smaller parameter tends to infinity.
The unchanged-palette formulation is valid and avoids this issue.

ENDPOINT DECOMPOSITION
Consider a color class of size x*q. Its covered left/right endpoints each
number x*q. The bipartite induced graph between these endpoints has at most
k(k+1)/2 edges, where k=x*q. The sum of full graph degrees on the endpoints
is consequently bounded, after division by q^2, by

    x^2 + x/q
      + F_alpha(x,a-x) + F_alpha(x,b-x) + o(1).

Both cross rectangles have normalized total vertex count a or b, so fall
within the induction hypothesis. The uniform o(1) is important here because
there can be q color classes. Upon summing, each per-class error is multiplied
by q, and the final degree-square sum is normalized by q^3, leaving o(1).
The x/q affine errors also total only O(1/q) after normalization.

CONCAVITY OF THE ENDPOINT INTEGRAND
Write

    g(x)=x^2+F_alpha(x,a-x)+F_alpha(x,b-x),
    0<=x<=min(a,b).

For a fixed positive r in {a,b}, put t=x(r-x) and D=1/r+alpha. Then

    F_alpha(x,r-x)=t/(1+D*t),

and its second derivative is

    -2/(1+D*t)^2 - 2D*(r-2x)^2/(1+D*t)^3.

Since r<=1, 0<=alpha<=1/2, and t<=r^2/4,

    D*t <= (r+alpha*r^2)/4 <= 3/8.

Hence

    g''(x) <= 2-4/(11/8)^2 = -14/121 < 0.

If a or b is zero there are no edges, so that degenerate case needs no
calculus. Jensen is therefore valid, uniformly throughout the claimed range.
Padding to q colors and using sum_c x_c=u gives

    (s/p)*u^2 <= u^2+F_alpha(u,a-u)+F_alpha(u,b-u)+o(1).

The lower bound is Cauchy-Schwarz on the two vertex-degree sequences.

THE CAUCHY STEP
Ignoring an asymptotically vanishing error in a subsequential limit, write

    R = (a-u)^2/[a/(1+alpha*a)+u(a-u)]
      + (b-u)^2/[b/(1+alpha*b)+u(b-u)].

The preceding inequality becomes

    (s/p+1+R)*u <= s.

This algebra follows by expanding each F term as
u(r-u)-u^2*(1/r+alpha)*(r-u)^2/[1+(1/r+alpha)*u(r-u)].

Cauchy-Schwarz in Engel form gives

    R >= (s-2u)^2/
          [a/(1+alpha*a)+b/(1+alpha*b)+u(s-2u)].

The map r -> r/(1+alpha*r) is concave, so its two terms sum to at most
s/(1+alpha*s/2). Replacing the denominator by this upper bound yields

    R >= (s-2u)^2/[s/(1+alpha*s/2)+u(s-2u)].

All terms and denominators are nonnegative; for positive a,b the denominator
is positive. The induction hypothesis also yields

    u/s <= s/[4+s+alpha*s^2],

because F_alpha is increasing in p and p<=s^2/4.
These are precisely the inputs to the scalar minimization claimed by the
parent researcher. No sign reversals or hidden regularity assumptions occur.

UNIFORMITY OF THE ASYMPTOTICS
A rigorous formulation can proceed entirely by subsequences. If a claimed
uniform bound fails, take a violating sequence q->infinity and pass to
convergent subsequences of (a,b,u) in the compact feasible region. If a or b
tends to zero, u<=min(a,b) tends to zero and cannot create a fixed positive
violation. If u tends to zero the same is true. Otherwise all divisions above
are legitimate in the limit, and the uniform induction hypothesis applies
to every color-class cross rectangle before averaging.

For a fixed finite number of iterations this establishes a uniform o(1)
error. It does not supply a useful numerical error rate. If infinitely many
fixed iterations are used to approach a limiting coefficient, take the
limsup in q FIRST, and only then let the number of iterations tend to infinity.
No quantitative uniformity in iteration count is required in that order.

TRANSFER TO GENERAL PROPER B-COLORED GRAPHS
For a graph on q vertices and at most q colors, a class with x*q matching
edges covers 2x*q vertices. As audited separately, its induced graph has at
most x^2*q^2 edges. Its cut has part fractions 2x and 1-2x, so F_alpha gives

    h_alpha(x)=2x^2+z/[1+(1+alpha)*z],
    z=2x-4x^2,   0<=x<=1/2.

Writing c=1+alpha, its second derivative is

    h_alpha''(x)
      =4-8/(1+c*z)^2-2c*(z')^2/(1+c*z)^3
      <=4-8/(11/8)^2
      =-28/121 <0,

for 0<=alpha<=1/2. Thus summing endpoint degree bounds, applying Jensen,
and comparing with Cauchy-Schwarz yields in the limit

    4rho^2 <= h_alpha(rho),

where rho=m/q^2. For positive rho this is equivalent to

    4c*rho^3-2c*rho^2-3rho+1 >= 0.

The polynomial is strictly decreasing on [0,1/2] for 1<=c<=3/2:
its derivative is 12c*rho^2-4c*rho-3, whose maximum on this interval is at
an endpoint and is at most c-3<0. Hence the unique root in that interval
is a valid limiting density bound. Properification by o(q^2) deletions
transfers it unchanged to arbitrary rainbow-C4 colorings.

SCALAR MINIMIZATION: FOLLOW-UP CHECK
The other auditor independently confirmed the remaining minimization, and
its calculation also checks directly as follows. Put r=u/s and
A=1/(1+alpha*s/2). The Cauchy lower bound divided by s is

    (1-2r)^2/[A+s*r*(1-2r)].

This decreases in r: setting t=1-2r, the derivative with respect to t of
t^2/[A+(s/2)t(1-t)] has positive numerator 2At+(s/2)t^2. Substitute the
upper bound r<=s/D, where D=4+s+alpha*s^2, and put T=4-s+alpha*s^2.
The resulting lower bound is the reciprocal of

    K(s)=D^2/[(1+alpha*s/2)*T^2]+s^2/T.

The second summand has derivative s(8-s)/T^2>0. The logarithmic derivative
of the first is

    4(4-alpha*s^2)/(D*T)-alpha/(2+alpha*s).

For 0<s<=1 and 0<=alpha<=1/2, D<=11/2, T<=4, so this is at least
14/22-1/4=17/44>0. Thus K increases, and the lower bound is minimized at
s=1. Therefore

    R >= s*Phi(alpha),
    Phi(alpha)=(3+alpha)^2/
      [(5+alpha)^2/(1+alpha/2)+3+alpha].

This proves the claimed improvement F_alpha -> F_{Phi(alpha)}.

The fixed-point polynomial is

    2alpha^3+17alpha^2+35alpha-18=0.

Its derivative is positive on [0,1/2], and it has exactly one root alpha_*
in that interval. Writing Phi as

    (alpha^3+8alpha^2+21alpha+18)/(3alpha^2+25alpha+56),

its derivative has strictly positive numerator

    3alpha^4+50alpha^3+305alpha^2+788alpha+726.

Also Phi(alpha)-alpha is minus the fixed-point polynomial divided by the
positive denominator. Hence alpha_0=0 and alpha_{j+1}=Phi(alpha_j) increase
to alpha_*. Taking the N-limsup separately for each fixed j, then taking
j to infinity, justifies the limiting cubic coefficient without any uniform
error claim in j. No flaw was found in the complete recursive argument.
