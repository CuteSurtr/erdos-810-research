Recursive endpoint bound for rainbow-C4 colorings
2026-10-15. Complete proof draft, independently checked by multiple agents.
Not Lean verified. No assertion that the result improves published bounds.

THEOREM. Let M(N) be the maximum number of edges in an N-vertex graph with
at most N edge colors in which every four-cycle is rainbow. Then

                 limsup M(N)/N^2 <= rho_* = 0.29922502883245506...

Define alpha_* as the unique nonnegative root of

                 2alpha^3+17alpha^2+35alpha-18=0.

It lies in (0,1/2), and equals 0.42303632978613243.... The constant rho_* is
the unique root in [0,1/2] of

        4(1+alpha_*)rho^3-2(1+alpha_*)rho^2-3rho+1=0.

The limit is taken with the number of vertices. This is not an o(N^2) bound.

A. Properification.
The companion properification-and-regularization.txt proves that any
rainbow-C4 coloring on N vertices becomes proper after deleting o(N^2)
edges, uniformly in the palette size. The proof packs edge-disjoint
monochromatic two-edge paths. Each path's endpoints have a unique common
neighbor. On three copies of the vertex set the packed paths close to
edge-disjoint triangles with no other triangles, so the triangle-removal
lemma bounds the packing by o(N^2). Deleting both legs of an inclusion-maximal
packing eliminates all monochromatic adjacent edge pairs. Thus any constant
upper bound for proper B-colorings transfers to arbitrary B-colorings.
The classical external input is the triangle-removal lemma, e.g. Fox,
Annals of Mathematics 174 (2011), 561-579:
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2011/174-1/p17

B. Statement of the bipartite induction.
Normalize the palette size to N, and allow bipartite graphs whose parts have
sizes A,B with A+B<=N. Put a=A/N, b=B/N, s=a+b. For 0<=alpha<=1/2 set

 F_alpha(a,b)=ab s/[s+ab(1+alpha s)]       when s>0,
 F_alpha(0,0)=0.

Property U(alpha) means that every properly B-colored such graph, with at
most N colors, has at most

                 N^2 F_alpha(a,b)+epsilon_N N^2

edges, for some epsilon_N tending to zero uniformly over A,B and the graph.
Padding with unused colors is harmless. We prove U(0), and

 U(alpha) => U(Phi(alpha)),
 Phi(alpha)=(3+alpha)^2/[(5+alpha)^2/(1+alpha/2)+3+alpha].       (6)

C. Base case.
Let E be the edge count. A color class of size K is a matching. Among its
matched endpoints, the bipartite graph has at most K(K+1)/2 edges: each pair
of matching edges permits at most one cross edge, since two would make a
non-rainbow four-cycle. The sum of endpoint degrees is at most
K(A+B+1-K). Summing over N colors and using Cauchy-Schwarz gives

 E^2/A+E^2/B <= sum_v d(v)^2 <= (A+B+1)E-E^2/N.

Consequently

 E <= NAB(A+B+1)/[N(A+B)+AB]
   = N^2 F_0(a,b) + N ab/(s+ab).

The final coefficient is at most 1/4 for s<=1. This proves U(0), uniformly.
Empty parts and E=0 are immediate.

D. Endpoint decomposition and Jensen.
Assume U(alpha), with alpha in [0,1/2]. For a color matching of size K,
its endpoint degree sum is at most

 K(K+1)+N^2 F_alpha(k,b-k)+N^2 F_alpha(a-k,k)+2epsilon_N N^2,

where k=K/N. The first term counts edges between matched endpoints twice;
the other terms bound the two rectangles to outside vertices. Those
rectangles have total vertices B and A respectively, so U(alpha) applies.

Define h(k)=k^2+F_alpha(k,b-k)+F_alpha(a-k,k). This function is concave
on [0,min(a,b)]. To see this, for a fixed part size u in (0,1] write
z=k(u-k), d=1/u+alpha. Then

 F_alpha(k,u-k)=z/(1+dz),
 (d^2/dk^2)F_alpha(k,u-k)
       =-2/(1+dz)^2-2d(u-2k)^2/(1+dz)^3.

Here dz<=u(1+alpha u)/4<=3/8. Therefore

 h'' <= 2-4/(11/8)^2 = -14/121 < 0.

The normalized color class sizes have average E/N^2, since there are N
colors after padding. Jensen and the degree-square lower bound imply, with
m=E/N^2,

       m^2 s/(ab) <= h(m)+2epsilon_N+O(1/N).                (7)

The O(1/N) is uniform: the additional K term sums to E<=N^2.

E. The scalar improvement.
First work in a limit with a,b,m positive, so the errors in (7) vanish.
Write each F_alpha term as its complete rectangle minus a loss. Then

 h(m)=sm-m^2-m^2 L,

 L=(a-m)^2/[a/(1+alpha a)+m(a-m)]
       +(b-m)^2/[b/(1+alpha b)+m(b-m)].

Cauchy-Schwarz and concavity of u/(1+alpha u) give

 L >= R(s,m):=(s-2m)^2/[s/(1+alpha s/2)+m(s-2m)].           (8)

Thus (7) gives m <= ab s/[s+ab(1+R(s,m))].
The induction hypothesis itself bounds

 m<=F_alpha(a,b)<=s^2/[4+s+alpha s^2].                       (9)

The function R(s,m) decreases in m for 0<=m<=s/2. Indeed its derivative
has, apart from the positive squared denominator, numerator

 -(s-2m)[4s/(1+alpha s/2)+s(s-2m)] <=0.

Put D=4+s+alpha s^2 and B=4-s+alpha s^2. Inserting (9) into (8) gives

 R(s,m)/s >= B^2/[D^2/(1+alpha s/2)+s^2 B] = 1/K(s),

 K(s)=D^2/[(1+alpha s/2)B^2]+s^2/B.

For s in (0,1], K is increasing. The derivative of its second summand is
s(8-s)/B^2>0. The logarithmic derivative of its first summand is

       -alpha/(2+alpha s)+4(4-alpha s^2)/(DB).

Because alpha<=1/2, D<=11/2 and B<=4, this is at least
14/22-1/4=17/44>0. Therefore the minimum of 1/K(s) occurs at s=1, proving

                         R(s,m)>=s Phi(alpha).

Substitution gives m<=F_{Phi(alpha)}(a,b), as required.

F. Uniform errors and induction boundary cases.
The preceding limiting argument proves U(Phi(alpha)) uniformly. If not,
there would be an eta>0 and a sequence of counterexamples with normalized
edge counts m exceeding F_{Phi(alpha)}(a,b) by at least eta. Compactness
allows a convergent subsequence of (a,b,m) in the closed feasible region
0<=a,b, a+b<=1, 0<=m<=ab. Because F is nonnegative, the limiting m is at
least eta, so a,b,s are positive. Formula (7) then passes to the limit,
and U(alpha) gives (9). Sections D-E contradict the eta excess. Cases
m->0, a->0, b->0, or s->0 cannot produce a fixed positive excess.

This argument is repeated only finitely many times for each U(alpha_j).
No estimate uniform in the number of iterations is needed below.

G. The sequence and its limit.
Let alpha_0=0 and alpha_{j+1}=Phi(alpha_j). Algebra gives

 Phi(alpha)=(alpha^3+8alpha^2+21alpha+18)/(3alpha^2+25alpha+56).

Its derivative is positive for alpha>=0: after multiplying by its squared
denominator, the numerator is

             3alpha^4+50alpha^3+305alpha^2+788alpha+726.

The fixed-point equation is

                2alpha^3+17alpha^2+35alpha-18=0.

This polynomial is strictly increasing on [0,infinity), is negative at 0,
and positive at 1/2. Its unique root alpha_* is therefore in (0,1/2).
For 0<=alpha<alpha_*, Phi(alpha)>alpha, and monotonicity gives
Phi(alpha)<Phi(alpha_*)=alpha_*. Consequently alpha_j increases to alpha_*.
All induction parameters stay in the verified interval [0,1/2].

The first values are
 alpha_1=9/28,
 alpha_2=562185/1412516=0.3980025712983074...,
 alpha_3=0.4168344849338080... .

For every fixed j, U(alpha_j) holds. Take N->infinity first, and then
j->infinity. Continuity of F_alpha gives the bound with alpha_* for every
subsequential graph-density limit. Equivalently U(alpha_*) also follows
by choosing a fixed sufficiently large j for each desired error tolerance.

H. Global endpoint bound.
Now let G be a general proper B-colored graph on N vertices with <=N colors.
For a color matching of size K, let S be its 2K endpoints. Between two
matching edges there are at most two additional edges: three would include
a disjoint pair and yield a non-rainbow four-cycle. Thus e(G[S])<=K^2.
The endpoint degree sum is at most 2K^2+e(S,V\S).

Use U(alpha_j) on this bipartite cut. With x=K/N and z=2x(1-2x), the
normalized endpoint degree sum is at most

 H_alpha(x)=2x^2+z/[1+(1+alpha)z] + o(1),

uniformly in x. For alpha<=1/2 and 0<=x<=1/2,

 H_alpha''=4-8/[1+(1+alpha)z]^2
          -2(1+alpha)(2-8x)^2/[1+(1+alpha)z]^3
          <=4-8/(11/8)^2 <0.

Summing over N colors, Jensen, and sum_v d(v)^2>=4E^2/N therefore give,
for every subsequential density limit rho=lim E/N^2,

              4rho^2 <= H_{alpha_j}(rho).

For rho>0 this rearranges to

           4(1+alpha_j)rho^3-2(1+alpha_j)rho^2-3rho+1 >=0.

For any c=1+alpha_j in [1,3/2], the cubic 4c r^3-2c r^2-3r+1 is strictly
decreasing on [0,1/2]. Its derivative is 12c r^2-4c r-3, whose maximum on
this interval is max(-3,c-3)<0. The cubic is positive at 0 and negative
at 1/2, so it has a unique root there. Letting j->infinity gives rho<=rho_*.

As a fully rational finite-iteration alternative, alpha_2 gives

 rho <= 0.2997928854558638...,
 3949402rho^3-1974701rho^2-2118774rho+706258=0.

I. Limitations.
The bipartite proof and the proper global bound use elementary counting,
Cauchy-Schwarz, and one-variable calculus. Only transfer to nonproper
colorings needs triangle removal. The bound is an asymptotic constant; no
polynomial decay in N is obtained. The exact induction limit above belongs
to this chosen family of estimates and is not asserted to be optimal even
within the endpoint method. External novelty remains a literature question.
