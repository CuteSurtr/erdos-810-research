# Independent audit: definable palettes and residual cover independence

18 October 2026. This file records a written proof audit of [the finite-cover arithmetic construction](finite-cover-palette-descent.md) and the geometric disjointness argument developed with it. The component arguments below pass. The final graph statement and quantitative transfer must also use the potential-coset restriction and counting precautions stated below. Historical novelty is not determined here.

## 1. Finite witness and field-crossing checks

Tao's [Theorem 25](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.2894#S4) supplies the finite-intersection polynomial witness representation. Making generic leading coefficients invertible gives a genuinely finite witness variety over an open of the palette line. An infinite unary definable set forces an $F$-defined geometrically irreducible curve component: otherwise all rational points lie in boundedly many zero-dimensional intersections of conjugate geometric components. Lang–Weil then supplies a linear-size rational image contained in the palette. This proves the needed component extraction without assuming that the arithmetic Galois closure is regular.

The field-crossing construction was checked directly. With $L/F(a)$ the arithmetic Galois closure, constants $F_e$, and $H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F(V))$, the image of $H$ on $F_e$ is the full cyclic constant Galois group, because its fixed constants are exactly $F(V)\cap F_e=F$. Choose $\sigma\in H$ inducing Frobenius, let $M$ be its order, and enlarge constants to $F_M$. The fixed field of $(\sigma,\operatorname{Frob})$ in $LF_M$ has constant field $F$, contains $F(V)$, and has degree equal to the geometric monodromy group order. Its extension to $F_M$ is all of $LF_M$, since the cyclic fixed subgroup meets the geometric group trivially. Therefore it is a regular geometrically Galois cover dominating the witness curve.

Enlarging the constants before taking the fixed field is necessary and correctly included. The construction works even when the arithmetic cover is not Galois after descent over $F$; geometric Galoisness is what is needed later. The degree and genus remain bounded in the original witness complexity. This is a field-crossing argument of the type discussed by [Dèbes–Legrand, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.6154), not a new descent principle.

## 2. The Kummer quotient and residual inertia

Over algebraically closed constants of characteristic zero, let $G$ be the geometric group of the descended palette cover. Let $N$ be the normal subgroup generated by inertia over all finite nonzero palette values. The subgroup is invariant under arithmetic descent, so its quotient curve descends.

The quotient cover is connected and unramified over $\mathbb G_m$. It is therefore a cyclic power cover. Its unique points over zero and infinity are separately invariant under descent and hence rational. The descended genus-zero curve consequently admits a coordinate $w$ for which

```math
a=\tau w^n,\qquad\tau\in F^\times.
```

No assumption $n\mid(p-1)$ is necessary: the finite-field rational image is a coset of $(\mathbb F_p^\times)^n$, of index at most $n$.

The residual cover over the $w$-line has geometric group $N$. Its inertia groups at **all** finite nonzero $w$-values normally generate $N$. Every conjugate of an original nonzero inertia subgroup occurs above some such lifted value, and the Kummer map is unramified there. Using only one chosen lift of a branch value would be insufficient; using every lift gives exactly the required normal generation.

## 3. The three lifted colors and the correct rational comparison field

Let $q=v/u$ and use the established functions $F(q),r(q)$, so that

```math
u=a^3/F(q),\quad v=qu,\quad k=qru,
\qquad(a,b,c)=(a,ar,aqr).
```

Restrict the three potentials to one coset $\lambda(F^\times)^n$. Their witness covers can be chosen to carry designated functions

```math
u=\lambda\xi_u^n,
\qquad v=\lambda\xi_v^n,
\qquad k=\lambda\xi_k^n.
```

Adjoin $s^{3n}=G(u,v)/\tau^3$, and put

```math
a=\tau s^n,\quad
w_1=s,\quad w_2=s\xi_k/\xi_v,\quad w_3=s\xi_k/\xi_u.
```

Then $a=\tau w_1^n$, $b=\tau w_2^n$, and $c=\tau w_3^n$. The previous higher-root theorem gives an integral field after the separate potential covers and this additional root equation.

For each $i$, compare fields over the rational function field $K_i=\bar F(w_i,q)$. The two generators are algebraically independent. If $r_1=1$, $r_2=r$, and $r_3=qr$, then

```math
a=\tau w_i^n/r_i(q),
```

so all three potential functions are rational in $(w_i,q)$. Along every divisor $w_i=\theta\ne0$, the selected color is the nonzero constant $\tau\theta^n$. The nine explicit restrictions already checked in the polynomial-residue proof show that all three potentials and the other two colors vary nontrivially with $q$.

The bare divisor $w_i=\theta$ in this rational surface has multiplicity one. Every separate potential cover is unramified there, because the potential residue is nonconstant and hence not a branch value. The root equations needed to recover $s$ and the designated potential roots have unit radicands there and are unramified in characteristic zero. For every earlier palette cover, its lifted argument $w_j$ is also nonconstant in the residue field, since $w_j^n$ is the varying color $a_j/\tau$. Thus these covers are unramified at every valuation above the divisor as well.

This argument does **not** assume that the entire potential-cover compositum is regular over $\bar F(w_i)$. That assertion would need a separate proof and could introduce circular reasoning. The rational comparison field $K_i$ avoids it.

## 4. Galois intersection argument

Let $L_i/\bar F(w_i)$ be the residual palette-cover field. Its extension $L_i(q)/K_i$ is Galois with full group $N$, because $q$ is transcendental. Let $M$ be the compositum of the existing potential and earlier palette fields, and replace it by its finite Galois closure over $K_i$. This closure remains unramified at every divisor $w_i=\theta\ne0$: all conjugates are unramified there, and a compositum of unramified extensions is unramified.

The intersection

```math
J=M\cap L_i(q)
```

is a Galois intermediate extension of $L_i(q)/K_i$. At each divisor $w_i=\theta$, target inertia survives unchanged because the local parameter retains valuation one. Since $J$ is unramified there, every such inertia group lies in $\operatorname{Gal}(L_i(q)/J)$. Their normal closure is $N$ by Section 2. Hence $J=K_i$.

The two Galois extensions are linearly disjoint. Consequently adjoining the new residual palette cover preserves integrality and its full geometric group. Repeating this argument for all three colors proves the required simultaneous geometric integrality. It remains valid after arbitrary separate potential witness covers of the specified root-carrying type.

## 5. Necessary counting precautions

The counting universe must be the selected potential coset. In the positivity step, each potential-cell witness cover must carry the designated $n$th-root function. This is obtained by including that root equation among its witnesses before extracting a geometrically irreducible component. One must not adjoin a root to an arbitrary preselected witness component and silently assume the resulting component is defined over the finite field.

The relation on $(u,v)$ can be defined by existential quantification over all the displayed finite covers, with bounded complexity. Its restricted four-grid multiplicity is still a bounded definable function; the four-output map of $K$ is unchanged. The established regularity, two Cauchy–Schwarz steps, and finite-cell positivity argument therefore give the same quantitative error once the component has been chosen over the field. All projected witnesses must map into the original palette on the designated dense open, as guaranteed by the dominating field-crossed cover.

Counting graph configurations must count actual $(a,q)$ values, not the palette-cover or potential-root witnesses. Projection fibers are uniformly bounded, and every accepted pair supplies at least one actual configuration. Thus the old sixteen-incidence and $3p$-color multiplicity bounds are unchanged.

All complexity bounds are fixed-formula bounds. They may be ineffective, and the proof does not apply to arbitrary palettes whose descriptive complexity grows with $p$.

## 6. Final chain checked

The completed Sections 5–6 of the companion file incorporate these precautions and pass the full audit. Restricting the support to a largest $n$th-power coset loses at most a factor $n$, a fixed bounded quantity. The compatible lifted palette points form a subset of the existential relation used in the grid count, which is enough for positivity. The resulting lower configuration count is

```math
N\ge c h^3/p-Cp^{15/16}h.
```

The previously checked deletion transfer gives

```math
m\ll p^{63/32}+p^{4/3}(R+pd)^{1/3},
```

with constants depending on the fixed palette formula and a fixed lower density bound for the specified core. For an exact fixed-formula palette with no omissions, a hypothetical failure of a uniform bound forces the palette size to tend to infinity; the infinite definable ultraproduct supplies the witness cover above. A bounded palette instead has only $O(p)$ eligible edges. Thus the exact-palette power saving does not require an externally supplied numerical density lower bound.

This is a proved restricted-family extension within the written proof framework already used for the quadratic coset theorem. It relies on the old rational-map separate-cover theorem and counting adaptation, the finite-witness theorem, and the explicitly proved field-crossing/inertia argument. It does not supply a structure theorem for arbitrary dense palettes.
