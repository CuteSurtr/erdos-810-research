# Cubic perturbations with fixed-formula palettes or supports

18 October 2026. Both one-sided extensions, including the symmetric support theorem in Section 6, passed an [independent mathematical audit](cubic-perturbation-independent-audit.md). The spectral and Wiener bounds are stronger quantitatively under their respective hypotheses; this result permits either set to have arbitrary fixed definable complexity while leaving the other set unrestricted. It uses the descended finite-cover construction from [finite-cover-palette-descent.md](finite-cover-palette-descent.md). Historical novelty remains unverified.

## 1. Statement

Fix a ring-language formula $\varphi(a;\mathbf t)$ and a positive constant $\delta$. Let $A_0\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times$ be one of its parameter instances, with $|A_0|\ge\delta p$. Let $t\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, let $H\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ be arbitrary, and let $A\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times$ be the actual palette. Prescribe incidences

```math
(a,h)\longmapsto
\left(\frac h{a^2}-a-ta^2,\ \frac h a\right).
```

Retain any subset whose endpoint pairs have single-valued colors. Put $d=|A_0\setminus A|$ and let $R$ count omitted incidences only in $(A\cap A_0)\times H$. If $m$ is the total number of retained edges of all colors, then

```math
m\ll_{\varphi,\delta}p^{15/8}+p\sqrt{R+pd}.      \tag{1}
```

The constant is uniform over all formula parameters and nonzero $t$. For the exact palette $A=A_0$ with no omissions, $m=O_\varphi(p^{15/8})$ without a separately specified density lower bound. This still requires fixed formula complexity; arbitrary dense palettes are not covered. No four-cycle restriction is needed.

## 2. Palette cover and designated potential roots

Work first in a characteristic-zero nonstandard finite field $F$. For infinite definable $A_0$, the finite-witness and field-crossing argument already proved supplies a regular, geometrically Galois curve cover $Z\to\mathbb P^1_a$, defined over $F$, whose rational-point image on a designated open lies in $A_0$.

Let $N$ be the normal closure of inertia at all finite nonzero color values. Its quotient descends over $F$ and has a coordinate $w$ with

```math
a=\tau w^n,\qquad \tau\in F^\times.
```

The residual cover $Z\to\mathbb P^1_w$ has geometric group $N$, normally generated by its inertia groups at all finite nonzero $w$-values. The integer $n$ and the cover complexity are fixed finite quantities.

Restrict the potential support to a largest coset $\lambda(F^\times)^n$. When choosing a witness curve for any full-dimensional definable potential cell in this coset, append its root equation before extracting a geometrically irreducible component defined over $F$. Thus the two separate potential covers carry functions $x,y$ with

```math
u=\lambda x^n,\qquad v=\lambda y^n.
```

All such choices are finite covers of the respective potential lines. Restricting to the coset loses at most a factor $n$ in support size.

## 3. The quartic and its pole provide the common color coset

Put $q=v/u$. The collision surface is rational in $(a,q)$ and satisfies

```math
u=-q a^3\bigl(1+t(1+q)a\bigr),\qquad v=qu,
\qquad b=qa.                                      \tag{2}
```

The quartic cover of the $(u,v)$ plane remains geometrically integral after arbitrary separate potential covers. Indeed, its geometric monodromy is $S_4$, and its oblique simple-critical branch carries a transposition. All separate potential covers are unramified at that divisor, so the normal-generation argument in [cubic-perturbation.md, Sections 3–4](cubic-perturbation.md) gives disjointness.

Adjoin a root $w_a$ of $w_a^n=a/\tau$. To prove that this remains integral after the chosen potential covers, use the branch at infinity above $q=-1$. With $z=1/a$, equation (2) reads

```math
u z^4+qz+tq(1+q)=0.
```

Write $\varepsilon=q+1$. At $\varepsilon=0$, the root $z=0$ is simple: the derivative with respect to $z$ equals $-1$. Over the complete local field with residue field $\overline F(u)$, its lift is

```math
z=-t\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_{\varepsilon}(a)=-1.             \tag{3}
```

At this divisor $u$ remains a free nonzero residue and $v=-u$ varies nontrivially as well. Every separate potential cover is unramified, including the designated root covers. Consequently (3) survives with valuation $-1$ in the full potential-cover compositum. The polynomial $X^n-\tau/a$ is Eisenstein there, so adjoining $1/w_a$ and hence $w_a$ preserves geometric integrality. This works for arbitrary positive $n$, including composite $n$.

Set

```math
w_b=w_a\,y/x.
```

Then $q=(y/x)^n$, so both quotient coordinates are rational functions over $F$ and satisfy

```math
a=\tau w_a^n,\qquad b=\tau w_b^n.                  \tag{4}
```

Thus the two color maps lift compatibly to the same descended Kummer quotient. Merely requiring both colors to lie in a residue class would not provide these maps.

## 4. Adding the two residual palette covers

Fix $w_a=\theta\ne0$, so $a=\rho=\tau\theta^n\ne0$, and use the other color $b$ as the remaining rational coordinate. The two potentials restrict to

```math
u=-\rho^2b(1+t\rho+tb),\qquad
v=-\rho b^2(1+t\rho+tb).                            \tag{5}
```

They are nonconstant polynomials of degrees two and three. This remains true when $1+t\rho=0$, since $t,\rho\ne0$. The other color varies as well. Exchanging $a,b$ proves the corresponding statement on every divisor $w_b=\theta\ne0$.

For a selected color, compare fields over the rational surface $\overline F(w_a,b)$ or $\overline F(w_b,a)$. The complete root/potential field is finite over this surface. At every selected divisor $w_i=\theta\ne0$, all separate potential covers are unramified by (5), all existing root equations have unit radicands, and any earlier residual palette cover is unramified because its quotient coordinate is nonconstant there. The finite Galois closure of the current compositum is therefore unramified at these divisors.

The selected residual palette extension has group $N$. Its intersection with that Galois closure corresponds to a normal subgroup containing all inertia at finite nonzero $w_i$-values. Those inertia groups normally generate $N$, so the intersection is trivial. Thus the selected palette extension is linearly disjoint and preserves integrality. Repeat for the other color. The complete potential/color cover is geometrically integral and defined over $F$.

## 5. Binary count and deletion transfer

Define the bounded-complexity relation $E(u,v)$ by existence of a collision pair (2) whose colors are images of $F$-points of $Z$, excluding the finitely many designated exceptional fibres and $u=v$. It forces both colors into $A_0$. The compatible lifted points constructed above form a subset of this relation, which is sufficient for positivity.

Run binary algebraic regularity on the chosen potential coset. Every full-dimensional cell pair has positive relation density: append the potential roots to its witness curves and apply Sections 3–4, then Lang–Weil and bounded projection degree. The regularity discrepancy on arbitrary subsets is $O(p^{7/4})$. Splitting the support slice into two disjoint comparable parts and counting actual ordered collision pairs gives

```math
N\ge c h^2-Cp^{7/4},\qquad h=|H\setminus\{0\}|.   \tag{6}
```

Here root and palette witnesses are not separately counted. Each accepted potential pair supplies an actual ordered color pair, and all actual pairs are counted for the deletion bound. The fixed coset loss is absorbed in $c$.

As before, an absent color affects at most $2p$ ordered collision pairs. A specified omitted incidence affects at most four, because the other color obeys a nonzero quadratic equation. Every counted collision is destroyed by one of these deletions, hence

```math
N\le2pd+4R.
```

Equation (1) follows from (6) and $m\le p|H|$. Uniformity in the fixed formula, its parameters, the density lower bound, and nonzero $t$ follows by the usual characteristic-zero ultraproduct contradiction. For an exact fixed-formula palette, bounded finite palettes contribute only $O(p)$ edges, while a hypothetical violation forces an infinite definable ultraproduct and hence the witness construction. This proves the exact-palette assertion without a separate numerical density hypothesis.

The inherited analytic source is [Tao, *Expanding polynomials over finite fields of large characteristic, and a regularity lemma for definable sets*, Lemma 23 and Theorem 25](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.2894). The application-specific addition is the pole (3), which removes the color Kummer obstruction before the residual-cover disjointness argument. Historical novelty has not been established.

## 6. The symmetric fixed-formula support theorem

There is a second one-sided version with the same exponent. Let $H_0\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times$ be a parameter instance of one fixed ring-language formula, with $|H_0|\ge\delta p$. The palette $A\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times$ is now completely arbitrary. Let actual $H\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ be arbitrary, put $d=|H_0\setminus H|$, and let $R$ count omitted incidences only in $A\times(H\cap H_0)$. The total retained count, including arbitrary extra potentials, satisfies

```math
m\ll_{\varphi,\delta}p^{15/8}+p\sqrt{R+pd}.          \tag{7}
```

For an exact fixed-formula nonzero support and no omissions, this is $O_\varphi(p^{15/8})$ without a separately specified density lower bound. Adding the potential zero contributes at most $p$ further incidences. The following proof supplies the root and residual-cover checks; it does not infer symmetry merely by exchanging names in the previous argument.

### Support witness and compatible quotient roots

The same finite-witness and descent construction gives a regular geometrically Galois cover whose rational-point image lies in $H_0$. Its maximal quotient by finite nonzero inertia is

```math
h=\lambda\eta^n,
```

and the residual group $N$ is normally generated by all finite nonzero $\eta$-inertia. Restrict the palette to one multiplicative coset $\mu(F^\times)^n$. When selecting the separate color witness curves for full-dimensional regularity cells, include the equations

```math
a=\mu x^n,\qquad b=\mu y^n
```

before extracting the respective geometrically irreducible components. In the function field of their product, adjoin $\xi$ with

```math
\xi^n=u/\lambda,
\qquad u=-a^2b(1+t(a+b)).                            \tag{8}
```

This extension is geometrically integral. At the generic divisor $S=1+t(a+b)=0$, both $a$ and $b=-1/t-a$ are nonconstant and generically nonzero. Hence all separate color covers are unramified there. The function $u$ has valuation one, which persists in their compositum; $X^n-u/\lambda$ is Eisenstein at any lifted divisor. This proof also applies to composite $n$.

Since $v/u=b/a=(y/x)^n$, the two designated support-quotient coordinates are

```math
\eta_u=\xi,\qquad \eta_v=\xi y/x.                    \tag{9}
```

These are explicit functions over the original field, so they resolve the compatibility of the two power-map quotients.

### All nonzero support inertia survives

Select one potential, initially $u$, and compare the current field over the rational surface $\overline F(\eta_u,q)$, where $q=b/a$. The color $a$ satisfies

```math
\lambda\eta_u^n=-q a^3(1+t(1+q)a),\qquad b=qa.       \tag{10}
```

For every $\theta\ne0$, put $\rho=\lambda\theta^n\ne0$. The quartic extension (10) is unramified at the generic divisor $\eta_u=\theta$. Indeed, away from $q=0,-1$, its only repeated-root condition is

```math
(1+q)^3-\frac{27}{256t^3\rho}\,q=0.                 \tag{11}
```

This is a nonzero monic cubic polynomial in $q$ for every $\rho\ne0$. Thus the generic specialization is separable, even at the row value $\rho=1/(64t^3)$ where the earlier full-$S_4$ row theorem had an exception. The quartic's leading coefficient is a unit at the selected generic divisor, so the separability check gives unramifiedness of its splitting field there.

On the same divisor, both colors vary. If $a$ were constant, then $b$ would solve the fixed nonzero quadratic $\rho=-a^2b(1+t(a+b))$, contradicting the free transcendental parameter $q=b/a$. If $b$ were constant, the same equation would instead be a nonzero cubic in $a$, giving the same contradiction. Thus every input color cover and each chosen color root cover is unramified. The other potential is $v=q\rho$, and its designated quotient root has nonconstant $n$-th power. Hence any previously adjoined residual support cover is also unramified.

Take the finite Galois closure of the existing field over $\overline F(\eta_u,q)$. It remains unramified at every finite nonzero selected divisor. Its intersection with the residual support cover is a Galois quotient in which all those inertia groups vanish. Their normal closure is $N$, so the intersection is trivial. Adding the selected support cover preserves geometric integrality. Exchanging $u,v$ gives the same argument over $\overline F(\eta_v,a/b)$ for the second cover. Consequently the full color/support cover is geometrically integral and defined over $F$.

### Count and uniform transfer

Define the bounded-complexity binary relation on colors by existence of a collision whose two potentials are images of rational points on the support witness cover, excluding the diagonal colors and the finitely many prohibited fibers. In each full-dimensional pair of color cells in the chosen coset, the preceding compatible-cover argument and Lang–Weil give positive relation density. Binary algebraic regularity therefore yields an actual ordered collision count

```math
N\ge c|A|^2-Cp^{7/4},                               \tag{12}
```

with the bounded coset loss absorbed in $c$. Root witnesses are not counted with multiplicity. A missing potential affects at most $4p$ ordered configurations: for each first color the other color solves a quadratic, and the missing potential can occupy either ordered position. A fixed omitted incidence affects at most four. Consequently

```math
N\le4pd+4R.
```

Combining this with (12) and $m\le p|A|$ proves (7), including all extra actual potentials. The same fixed-formula ultraproduct argument as above makes constants uniform in the bounded parameter tuple and nonzero $t$. Bounded finite support instances contribute only $O(p)$ edges; an exact-support violation with unbounded size gives an infinite definable ultraproduct and the established witness construction. This proves the version without a separate density hypothesis.

The two one-sided theorems together say that either the palette or the nonzero support may have fixed formula complexity, while the other set is arbitrary. They do not cover both sets arbitrary at once. The separate low-Wiener-support theorem gives better exponents for some supports of growing multiplicative complexity, under different hypotheses.
