# Fixed-formula common potential supports with arbitrary palettes

18 October 2026. This is the symmetric counterpart of the [definable-palette extension](finite-cover-palette-descent.md). It uses the same previously established rational expansion and finite-cover machinery. No novelty claim or implication for unrestricted Erdős problem 810 is made.

## 1. Statement and hypotheses

Fix one formula $\varphi(h;\mathbf t)$ in the language of rings, with a fixed finite tuple of parameters. In each prime field let

```math
H_0=\{h\in\mathbb F_p^\times:
\mathbb F_p\models\varphi(h;\mathbf t)\}.
```

The parameters may vary with $p$; the formula and its complexity may not. Let $A\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times$ be arbitrary. A retained incidence $(a,h)$ represents the edge

```math
(h/a^2-a,\ h/a)
```

of color $a$. Assume the retained incidences define a simple proper edge coloring and every four-cycle is rainbow.

For the completed family, with every incidence in $A\times H_0$ retained, the total number of edges satisfies

```math
m\ll_\varphi p^{63/32}.
```

More generally, suppose $|H_0|\ge\delta p$ for a fixed $\delta>0$, let $H\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ be an arbitrary actual support, put $d=|H_0\setminus H|$, and let $R$ count omitted incidences only in $A\times(H_0\cap H)$. Then

```math
m\ll_{\varphi,\delta}
p^{63/32}+p^{4/3}(R+pd)^{1/3}.                 \tag{1}
```

Here $m$ may include incidences at arbitrary additional values in $H\setminus H_0$, and zero potential. In particular, $d=o(p)$ and $R=o(p^2)$ force $m=o(p^2)$. There is no definability hypothesis on $A$.

The proof gives a lower count for configurations whose three colors lie in $A$ and whose three potentials lie in $H_0$:

```math
N\ge c|A|^3/p-Cp^{15/16}|A|.                  \tag{2}
```

The core construction and all field extensions used for (2) are detailed below. The finite-witness extraction, arithmetic field crossing, and maximal Kummer-quotient descent are those proved in Sections 1–4 of the linked companion note.

## 2. Inverse colors as the three counting coordinates

Use the established parametrization

```math
Q=q^2+1,\quad B=q^2+q+1,\quad r=(q+1)/Q,
\quad F=-BQ/[q(q+1)],
```

```math
(a,b,c)=(a,ar,aqr),\qquad
(u,v,k)=(a^3/F,\ qa^3/F,\ qra^3/F).
```

Work on the dense open excluding the usual zero denominators and degenerate parameters. Put

```math
s=1/b,\qquad t=1/c,\qquad w=1/a.
```

The color relation $a(b+c)=b^2+c^2$ becomes

```math
w=K(s,t)=\frac{st(s+t)}{s^2+t^2}.             \tag{3}
```

Conversely, a nondegenerate triple satisfying (3) determines the original $(a,q)$, with $q=s/t$. Direct substitution gives

```math
\kappa(s,t):=k=-\frac1{w(s^2+st+t^2)},
\qquad u=k\frac t w,\qquad v=k\frac s w.       \tag{4}
```

Thus the counting map is exactly the old rational map $K$; only the additional definable restriction is changed.

## 3. A root cover surviving all three inverse-color covers

Work over algebraically closed constants of characteristic zero. The previously proved separate-cover theorem says that (3) stays geometrically integral after arbitrary separate finite dominant covers of the $s$-, $t$-, and $w$-lines.

Let $\omega$ be a primitive cube root of unity and take the divisor $D:t=\omega s$. Then

```math
w=K(s,\omega s)=\omega^2s.
```

All three inverse-color coordinates are nonconstant on $D$. Therefore the three separate covers are unramified at its generic point. The factor $s^2+st+t^2$ vanishes to order one there, and $w$ is a unit, so $\operatorname{ord}_D\kappa=-1$.

For every positive integer $n$ and nonzero scalar $\lambda$, the equation

```math
\xi^n=\kappa/\lambda                           \tag{5}
```

therefore stays irreducible after those covers: at a valuation above $D$ the radicand still has value $-1$. Equivalently, the reciprocal-root equation is Eisenstein at that valuation. This proves geometric integrality of the combined inverse-color covers and (5), for every $n$, without assuming that $n$ divides $p-1$ in the later finite-field applications.

## 4. Preparing the witness cover for the definable support

Let $F$ be a characteristic-zero nonstandard finite field. The witness and field-crossing lemmas give a regular geometrically Galois cover $Z\to\mathbb P^1_h$, defined over $F$, whose rational image on a designated dense open lies in $H_0$. Quotient by the normal subgroup generated by inertia at finite nonzero $h$-values. The quotient has a coordinate $\eta$ and an equation

```math
h=\lambda\eta^n,\qquad\lambda\in F^\times.     \tag{6}
```

The residual geometric group $N$ is normally generated by its inertia groups at all finite nonzero values of $\eta$. These assertions, including the arithmetic descent and the requirement to use all lifts of every branch value, are proved in the companion note. The degree $n$ and all complexities are bounded in the original formula.

Choose a largest slice of $A^{-1}$ in a coset $\mu(F^\times)^n$. Its size is at least $|A|/n$. The three counting coordinates $s,t,w$ will all be restricted to this coset. Whenever a finite witness curve is selected for a full-dimensional definable cell in this coset, include its root coordinate among the witnesses before selecting the geometrically irreducible component. The three curves consequently carry functions $x,y,z$ with

```math
s=\mu x^n,\qquad t=\mu y^n,\qquad w=\mu z^n.
```

Adjoin (5). By Section 3 the resulting field $M_0$ is geometrically integral. Define the three maps to the support cover's Kummer quotient by

```math
\eta_k=\xi,\qquad
\eta_u=\xi y/z,\qquad
\eta_v=\xi x/z.
```

Equations (4)–(5) give the actual identities

```math
k=\lambda\eta_k^n,\qquad
u=\lambda\eta_u^n,\qquad
v=\lambda\eta_v^n.                             \tag{7}
```

These designated rational functions are essential: residue-class membership alone would not choose maps into the three quotient covers over $F$.

## 5. Residual support covers remain independent

Let the constants now be algebraically closed, and fix one index $i\in\{u,v,k\}$. Put

```math
\psi_u=1/F,\quad\psi_v=q/F,\quad\psi_k=qr/F,
\qquad K_i=\overline F(\eta_i,q).
```

The two generators are algebraically independent, and $M_0$ is finite over $K_i$. Indeed,

```math
a^3=\lambda\eta_i^n/\psi_i(q),                \tag{8}
```

then $b=ar$, $c=aqr$, and all other coordinates are recovered through the finite inverse-color witness covers and their specified root functions. The original $\xi$ is recovered from $\eta_i$ and $x,y,z$ by the displayed formulas in Section 4.

Consider a generic divisor $\eta_i=\theta$, where $\theta\ne0$. The radicand in (8) is a unit at this divisor, so the cubic extension is unramified. All three colors are nonconstant in the residue field. To check this explicitly, the ratios color-cubed/potential are

```math
\begin{pmatrix}
F&F/q&F/(qr)\\
Fr^3&Fr^3/q&Fr^2/q\\
Fq^3r^3&Fq^2r^3&Fq^2r^2
\end{pmatrix}.                               \tag{9}
```

Every entry is nonconstant: since $F$ has growth $q^2$ and $r$ has growth $q^{-1}$ at infinity, the respective growth exponents are

```math
\begin{pmatrix}2&1&2\\-1&-2&-1\\2&1&2\end{pmatrix},
```

all nonzero. The selected potential is the nonzero constant $\lambda\theta^n$, so (9) proves the assertion for the colors and hence for their inverses. Therefore every separate inverse-color witness cover is unramified there. Its specified root equations have unit radicands and introduce no ramification either.

The other two potentials are also nonconstant at this divisor: their ratios to the selected one belong to $\{q,r,qr,q^{-1},r^{-1},(qr)^{-1}\}$, each nonconstant. Thus their quotient coordinates $\eta_j$ are nonconstant as well, because $\lambda\eta_j^n$ is nonconstant. Every previously adjoined residual support cover, evaluated at such an $\eta_j$, is unramified there. These facts prove that $M_0$, and then the compositum with all earlier residual covers, is unramified over $K_i$ at every valuation above each generic divisor $\eta_i=\theta\ne0$.

Let $L_i/\overline F(\eta_i)$ be the residual cover field, with group $N$. Adjoining the independent variable $q$ preserves that Galois group. Let $M$ be the existing compositum and replace it by its finite Galois closure $\widetilde M$ over $K_i$. This closure is still unramified at all the indicated divisors. The intersection

```math
J=L_i(q)\cap\widetilde M
```

is a Galois intermediate field of $L_i(q)/K_i$. Since it is unramified at every finite nonzero $\eta_i$-value, its corresponding normal subgroup contains all the residual inertia groups. They normally generate $N$, so $J=K_i$. The two Galois extensions are linearly disjoint. In particular, adjoining the selected residual support cover to $M$ preserves integrality and its full degree.

Repeating this argument for the three indices proves geometric integrality of the complete inverse-color/support-cover pullback. All the maps and designated roots were defined over $F$, so the integral variety is defined over $F$ as required for the arithmetic Lang–Weil count. No assertion that $M_0$ is regular over the one-variable field $\overline F(\eta_i)$ is needed.

## 6. Counting and deletion transfer

Define $E(s,t)$ to assert that the three potentials in (4) admit rational points on the designated open of $Z$. This is a fixed-complexity definable predicate, and it forces $u,v,k\in H_0$. The four-grid map remains the old map of $K$. Its generic finite-fibre count with the restriction $E$ is a bounded definable function, exactly as in the prior restricted-grid proof.

Run that proof inside the selected inverse-color coset. In the positivity step every full-dimensional input or output cell has an inverse-color witness curve carrying its root coordinate. Sections 3–5 show that the entire pullback of (3), (5), and the three support covers is geometrically integral. Lang–Weil therefore gives a positive quadratic count in every such cell. The same two Cauchy–Schwarz estimates and the same within-cell error $O(p^{15/16}\sqrt{|X||Y|})$ apply.

Take disjoint comparable subsets of the selected slice of $A^{-1}$ as the two inputs and that slice as output. Each accepted pair has $s\ne t$; it gives three distinct colors, because $K(s,t)=s$ or $K(s,t)=t$ forces $s=t$. Excluding the remaining fixed exceptional parameter locus costs $O(|A|)$ and is absorbed in the error. The fixed-factor loss from the largest coset slice yields (2).

Witness multiplicities are not counted as extra graph configurations: every accepted pair supplies an actual $(a,q)$, and distinct ordered inverse-color pairs give distinct such parameters. A fixed omitted potential value destroys at most $9p$ configurations: for each of its three roles and each $q$, its prescribed value gives a cubic equation for $a$, with at most three roots. The established bound for an omitted actual incidence is sixteen. Hence a retained B-coloring gives

```math
c|A|^3/p-Cp^{15/16}|A|\le N\le9pd+16R.        \tag{10}
```

If the error is at least half the main term, $|A|\ll p^{31/32}$. Otherwise $|A|^3\ll p(R+pd)$. Since the full actual graph has at most $p|A|$ incidences, these alternatives prove (1), including arbitrary extra potential values and the possible zero potential.

Uniform finite-prime constants for a fixed formula and density bound follow by the same ultraproduct contradiction used in the existing technical note. For the exact support $H=H_0$, a bounded finite support has $O(p)$ edges. Any sequence violating a uniform $O(p^{63/32})$ bound must therefore have $|H_0|\to\infty$; its fixed-formula ultraproduct is an infinite unary definable set, so the witness construction applies without a separate specified density parameter.

## 7. Scope and remaining gap

Together with the definable-palette theorem, this proves the same power saving when **either** the palette or the common potential support has fixed definable complexity, while the other set is arbitrary. Fixed-degree polynomial images, fixed-exponent residue conditions, and their fixed finite Boolean combinations are included. Degrees, parameter counts, and formula length growing with $p$ are excluded.

The main inputs remain Tao's algebraic regularity and expansion framework, the already proved six-slot geometry, and standard field crossing and inertia. This is an additional specialized consequence, with historical novelty unverified. It does not classify arbitrary dense palettes or supports, and does not supply a reduction from arbitrary B-colorings to this common-potential family.
