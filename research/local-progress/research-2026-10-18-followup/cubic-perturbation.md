# Cubic perturbations of the quadratic common-potential model

18 October 2026. Written proof for independent audit. The conclusion concerns a restricted family of simple colored graphs; it does not improve a general bound for Erdős #810.

## 1. Statement

Fix $t\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, with $p$ sufficiently large. For a nonzero color $a$ and a potential $h$, prescribe the bipartite edge

```math
(a,h)\longmapsto
\left(\frac{h}{a^2}-a-ta^2,\ \frac{h}{a}\right).
```

Equivalently, its endpoints satisfy $y=ax+a^2+ta^3$ and its potential is $h=ay$. Let $H\subseteq\mathbb F_p$ be arbitrary, let $A\subseteq\mathbb F_p^\times$ be the actual palette, put $d=|\mathbb F_p^\times\setminus A|$, and let $R$ count omitted incidences in $A\times H$. Suppose the retained incidences give a simple edge-colored graph: two distinct colors cannot be assigned to the same ordered pair of endpoints. Let $m$ be the number of retained edges. Then, uniformly in $t\ne0$,

```math
m\ll p^{15/8}+p\sqrt{R+pd}.
```

In particular, with the full nonzero palette and no omitted incidences, $m=O(p^{15/8})$. No four-cycle condition is needed. Properness within each color is automatic, since both endpoint coordinates are injective functions of $h$ for a fixed nonzero $a$.

## 2. Exact collision equations

Two distinct colors $a,b$ assign the same endpoints precisely at the intersection of their two lines. Subtraction gives

```math
x=-(a+b)-t(a^2+ab+b^2),\qquad
y=-ab(1+t(a+b)).
```

The respective potentials are

```math
u=ay=-a^2b(1+t(a+b)),\qquad
v=by=-ab^2(1+t(a+b)).                    \tag{1}
```

For $u,v\ne0$, put $q=v/u=b/a$. Then $b=qa$ and

```math
u=-q a^3\bigl(1+t(1+q)a\bigr).           \tag{2}
```

When $u\ne v$, the resulting colors are distinct. Thus every solution of (2) with $u,v\ne0$ and $u\ne v$ gives two incidences that cannot both be retained. A solution automatically has $a,b\ne0$.

## 3. Quartic monodromy and its oblique branch divisor

Work over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic zero. For generic $q$, the polynomial map

```math
a\longmapsto -q a^3\bigl(1+t(1+q)a\bigr)
```

has degree four. Its derivative is

```math
-q a^2\bigl(3+4t(1+q)a\bigr).
```

The branch value $u=0$ has a ramification point of index three at $a=0$, and its remaining point $a=-1/[t(1+q)]$ is unramified. The other finite critical point is

```math
a_c=-\frac{3}{4t(1+q)},\qquad
u_c=\frac{27q}{256t^3(1+q)^3}.
```

Its ramification index is two; its critical value is nonzero for generic $q$. The cover is connected because it is a degree-four map of rational curves. Its transitive monodromy subgroup of $S_4$ therefore contains a three-cycle and a transposition. The only transitive subgroups of $S_4$ containing a three-cycle are $A_4$ and $S_4$, and the transposition excludes $A_4$. Hence the geometric monodromy is $S_4$. This already holds after algebraically closing $k(q)$, so the Galois closure over $k(u,q)=k(u,v)$ also has group $S_4$.

In the $(u,v)$ plane the simple-critical branch is the irreducible divisor

```math
D:\quad (u+v)^3=\frac{27}{256t^3}\,uv.    \tag{3}
```

It is parametrized birationally by $q=v/u$ using $(u,v)=(u_c,qu_c)$. Both coordinates vary nontrivially, so this divisor is neither vertical nor horizontal. Its generic inertia group in the quartic Galois closure is generated by a transposition.

## 4. Irreducibility after arbitrary separate coordinate covers

Let $V\to\mathbb P^1_u$ and $W\to\mathbb P^1_v$ be arbitrary geometrically irreducible finite covers. Let $M$ be the function field of their product, finite over $K=k(u,v)$, and let $\widetilde M/K$ be its finite Galois closure. At the generic point of $D$, both $u$ and $v$ have nonconstant residues. Consequently the separate covers are unramified there: their branch values are constants on their respective coordinate lines. Their conjugates and compositum remain unramified, so $\widetilde M/K$ is unramified at $D$.

Let $L/K$ be the quartic Galois closure, with group $S_4$. The intersection $J=L\cap\widetilde M$ is Galois over $K$. Thus $J=L^N$ for a normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq S_4$. Since $J/K$ is unramified at $D$, the subgroup $N$ contains its inertia transposition. Every transposition is conjugate, and transpositions generate $S_4$, so $N=S_4$ and $J=K$.

The two Galois extensions are linearly disjoint. Hence the original quartic cover remains geometrically integral after pullback to $V\times W$. This statement is uniform over all separate covers of fixed finite complexity. The same geometric argument applies in every sufficiently large characteristic after bounded presentations are fixed.

## 5. Lower count for arbitrary support

Define $E_t(u,v)$ for nonzero distinct $u,v$ by existence of a field solution $a$ to (2), with $q=v/u$. This is a fixed-complexity ring formula; $t\ne0$ is a parameter. Binary algebraic regularity gives finite definable partitions of the two coordinate lines such that on every pair of full-dimensional cells the relation has density $\sigma_{ij}$ and discrepancy $O(p^{7/4})$ on arbitrary subsets.

Every full-dimensional cell pair has $\sigma_{ij}>0$. Indeed, choose geometrically irreducible finite witness curves defined over the field and mapping into the two cells. Section 4 makes the quartic pullback geometrically integral. Lang–Weil then gives $\gg p^2$ points, and bounded projection degree gives $\gg p^2$ accepted pairs in that cell pair. The diagonal and excluded coordinate zeros have only $O(p)$ points. A zero-density regularity cell pair would contradict this count. Since the number of cells is fixed and their positive densities are bounded away from zero, for arbitrary disjoint subsets $H_1,H_2$ of the nonzero support of comparable size,

```math
|E_t\cap(H_1\times H_2)|
\ge c|H_1||H_2|-Cp^{7/4}.                \tag{4}
```

Uniform finite-prime constants, including uniformity over $t\ne0$, follow by the standard ultraproduct contradiction for a fixed formula with parameters. Alternatively, the change of variables $(a,b,u,v)\mapsto(ta,tb,t^3u,t^3v)$ reduces the equations to $t=1$ exactly.

Let $h=|H\setminus\{0\}|$, split this set into two disjoint comparable parts, and count actual ordered collision configurations $(a,b)$ with the first potential in $H_1$ and the second in $H_2$. Every accepted pair supplies at least one such configuration, and at most four. Thus

```math
N\ge c h^2-Cp^{7/4},                    \tag{5}
```

after adjusting constants to cover bounded $h$.

The analytic input here is the binary regularity estimate in [Tao, *Expanding polynomials over finite fields of large characteristic, and a regularity lemma for definable sets*, Lemma 23 and Theorem 25](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.2894). The finite-witness and Lang–Weil positivity step follows the same framework as the earlier quadratic note; the additional geometric verification is Section 4.

## 6. Missing colors and incidences

A fixed absent color participates in at most $2p$ ordered collision configurations: it can occupy either color position, and the other color has at most $p-1$ choices. Thus missing colors account for at most $2pd$ configurations.

For a fixed omitted incidence $(a_0,h_0)$, if it occupies the first position, (1) gives

```math
h_0=-a_0^2b\bigl(1+t(a_0+b)\bigr),
```

a degree-two equation in $b$ with nonzero leading coefficient. There are at most two choices. The second position similarly gives at most two choices for the other color. Each omitted incidence therefore accounts for at most four configurations.

Every collision configuration has an absent color or an omitted incidence, since the retained coloring is single-valued on ordered endpoint pairs. Consequently

```math
N\le 2pd+4R.
```

Combining with (5) gives $h\ll p^{7/8}+\sqrt{R+pd}$. Finally $m\le(p-1)|H|\le p(h+1)$ proves the stated bound.

## 7. Scope

This shows that a nonzero cubic perturbation destroys the large full-palette supports available in the unperturbed quadratic model. The distinction is geometric: the new oblique branch divisor carries a transposition that normally generates the quartic monodromy group. The conclusion uses only single-valued simple colored realization, before any rainbow-four-cycle constraint. It is not a result for arbitrary palettes, arbitrary color functions, or unrestricted Erdős #810. Historical novelty has not yet been checked.
