# Small values of the rainbow-C4 extremal function (Erdős #810): outline

Working title: *Exact values and cyclic constructions for a rainbow 4-cycle problem of
Burr, Erdős, Graham and Sós.*

Source: `main.tex`. Tables are generated by `make_tables.py` from
`../bruteforce/results`, and every certificate is re-checked when they are built.
Literature notes are in `LITERATURE.md`.

## 1. Introduction
- The problem: M(n) is the largest e such that some n-vertex, e-edge graph has an
  n-colouring with every C4 rainbow. Equivalently, M(n) is the largest e with
  χ_S(n, e, C4) ≤ n [BEGS89].
- Erdős #810 asks whether M(n) ≥ εn². BEGS believed not.
- Known background:
  - the P4 analogue [BEGS89, Thm 6.3];
  - Sárközy–Selkow, whose result excludes C4 = K_{2,2};
  - the g(n;7,4) link;
  - Tao's finite-field Ajtai–Szemerédi remark.
- Tao's AlphaEvolve lower bounds for n ≤ 25 (forum, 11 Dec 2025), and his request for exact
  values via SAT.
- Contributions:
  1. M(n) for every n ≤ 12, by independent methods (Theorem 1).
  2. Improved lower bounds for 14 ≤ n ≤ 25 and new ones for 26 ≤ n ≤ 128 (Table 2).
  3. Cyclic orbit colourings:
     - the exact family optimum k_max(n) for n ≤ 41 (n ≠ 35);
     - k_max(4m) = m for 3 ≤ m ≤ 10;
     - the collapse of the number of n/4 solutions;
     - a proposition that linear and bounded piecewise-affine offset rules give o(n) orbits.

## 2. Preliminaries
- The conflict graph Q(G): G is n-good iff χ(Q(G)) ≤ n.
- Heredity: k-goodness is preserved under deleting edges or vertices.
- Trivial bounds: M(n+1) ≥ M(n) + 1, and ex(n, C4) ≤ M(n).

## 3. Exact values for n ≤ 12 (Theorem 1)
- Table 1 gives n, M(n), the number of extremal graphs up to isomorphism (n = 7..11) and the
  method.
- §3.1 Pruned orderly generation:
  - geng with a PRUNE hook that rejects intermediate graphs whose Q needs more than n
    colours; soundness via heredity;
  - two checkers;
  - validation against the unpruned runs and against Pólya counts.
- §3.2 Unpruned enumeration for (12, 41):
  - four independently written checkers;
  - totals equal the Pólya count 2,411,961,516;
  - for all but 753 graphs the checkers found a 13-clique in Q(G).
- §3.3 SAT with checked DRAT proofs:
  - the encoding;
  - symmetry breaking (max-degree vertex first, degree ordering, lex-leader rows, colour
    first occurrence), with its soundness argument;
  - cubes on the maximum degree;
  - (10, 31) and (11, 35) are fully certified; (12, 41) is certified for Δ ∈ {0..6, 10, 11}.
- §3.4 Observations:
  - the extremal counts 5, 45, 3, 2, 131;
  - at (11, 35), all but 2 of the 15,108,047 graphs are ruled out by a 12-clique in Q;
  - one of the 131 extremal graphs at (11, 34) needs only 10 colours.

## 4. Lower bounds for 13 ≤ n ≤ 128 (Table 2)
- Methods:
  - tabu search;
  - SAT with a prescribed semiregular automorphism (vertex orbits and colour orbits);
  - cyclic orbit colourings (§5).
- Comparison with AlphaEvolve for n ≤ 25.
- The trend in e/n² (0.28 → 0.17), with the caveat about search effort.

## 5. Cyclic orbit colourings
- Definition: vertices Z_n, D ⊆ [1, ⌈n/2⌉ − 1]; edge {i, i+d} has colour i + g(d).
- Lemma 2 (validity via closed walks through 0):
  - colour = offset + g(d);
  - two edges collide iff g(d_a) − g(d_b) ≡ o_b − o_a.
- Lemma 3 (multiplier action):
  - g′(e) = u·g(d), or e + u·g(d) when ud ≡ −e;
  - checked by proof and numerically.
- Table 3: k_max(n) exact for 12 ≤ n ≤ 41 (n ≠ 35), lower bounds up to n = 100.
- Observation:
  - k_max(4m) = m for m = 3..10;
  - the numbers of solution orbits at k = n/4 are 58, 165, 362, 285, 128, 59, 4 for
    n = 16, 20, …, 40;
  - no n/4 solution has been found for 44 ≤ n ≤ 100.
- Proposition 4: linear and bounded-height piecewise-affine rules give |D| = o(n), by
  Szemerédi plus Varnavides; b = 2 gives a 4-AP.
- Remarks:
  - coset rules give k ≈ p^{3/4} (experimental);
  - the doubling lift fails;
  - optimal solutions have no low-degree polynomial structure.

## 6. Other families (short)
- Sum colourings, which are o(n²) by Roth and connect to Tao's remark.
- Bipartite affine colourings over F_p (the repository's notes).
- The BEGS (7,4) construction.
- All are weaker than the cyclic family in our range.

## 7. Open problems
- M(13), which is at least 47; plain enumeration costs about 10⁴ CPU-hours.
- Is k_max(n) = o(n)?
- Is k_max(4m) < m for some m? (n = 44 is the first open case.)
- Certified (DRAT) proofs for M(12).
